# label - CBS Records

**CBS Columbia System** : CBS Records developed and offered its own distinct stereo system, known as the Columbia system. This proprietary solution was one of several attempts by record companies to establish a viable method for stereo record production. **Refusal to Release The Clash's Debut in North America** : CBS Records initially refused to release The Clash's self-titled debut album in North America for two years after its British release. Executives believed the record was "too raw, and abrasive, and crude, and political for American tastes" during the disco era. This decision forced North American fans to buy higher-priced British import versions, which astonishingly sold 100,000 copies, making it the biggest-selling import ever at the time, before CBS finally relented in 1979 with a "watered down version." ← Ongoing History of New Music > Joe Strummer： A Remembrance | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4m2Kx3ffJUQ&list=PLM8bxV0E1Gse5C3n1u-mkVSTZLL5AJICG ← cbs-records ← cbs-records

**Reluctance Over Double and Triple Albums** : CBS Records expressed reluctance to release The Clash's *London Calling* as a double album and *Sandinista!* as a triple album, due to concerns about manufacturing hassle and market price. **The Clash Signing Controversy (1977)** : CBS Records signed The Clash to a worldwide recording contract for 100,000 pounds on January 25, 1977, amidst a fierce bidding war. This deal was widely condemned by the punk community as a betrayal of the genre's anti-establishment ethos, with one fanzine even declaring "punk died the day the clash signed to CBS." **Worldwide CD Launch (March 1983)** : CBS Records participated in the big worldwide launch of the compact disc format in March 1983, releasing 16 albums on the new format. This included major titles like Michael Jackson's "Thriller," helping to establish the CD in the market. **Pressure on MTV for Black Artist Airplay (1983)** : CBS Records reportedly threatened to withhold all their music videos from MTV unless the channel played Michael Jackson's early 1983 hit "Billie Jean." This legendary move is credited with forcing MTV, which had largely refused to play videos by black artists, to begin regularly rotating clips by black musicians.
