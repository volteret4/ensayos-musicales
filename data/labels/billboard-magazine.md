# label - Billboard magazine

## curiosities
**Coiner of Genre Names** : Billboard magazine was responsible for coining the chart names "Rhythm and Blues" and "Country and Western." These terms were initially intended as titles for collections of genres, rather than single, distinct genres, but over time led to the development of specific musical styles associated with those names. **Evolution into Entertainment and Music (Early 1900s)** : The magazine's coverage expanded from general advertising to county fairs, circuses, vaudeville, and the gossip generated by these entertainment industries, thereby entering the entertainment sector. In the early 1900s, with the rise of sales of photographs and pre-recorded discs, "Billboard Advertising" began tracking which records were selling most, covering the jukebox industry, and later, radio airplay. "From there, the magazine moved on to covering county fairs, circuses, vaudeville, and Superlux show, and the kind of advertising those things did and the gossip those industries generated. This got the magazine into the entertainment industry. But then in the early 1900s, when sales of photographs and pre-recorded discs and records began to take off, Billboard Advertising started taking an interest in that." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TsiYLGGSJ2U ← billboard-magazine
**First Music Chart and Industry Leadership (1936-1950s)** : By the time of the Great Depression, Billboard was predominantly focused on music. It published its first music chart on January 4, 1936, followed by additional charts ranking America's best-selling records. By the 1950s, Billboard had become the definitive industry leader in tracking music sales. "And then on January 4th, 1936, the magazine published its first ever music chart. That was followed by more charts which ranked the best selling records in America. **Origin of Name and Founding (1894)** : Billboard magazine was established in 1894, years before the modern music industry, originally named "Billboard Advertising." Its initial purpose was to cover outdoor advertising, including the posting of bills, flyers, posters, and billboards, which were the primary means of advertising before radio and television.
