**Indie Label and R.E.M.'s Growth** : IRS Records was a larger indie label run by Miles Copeland, brother of Police drummer Stuart Copeland. R.E.M. released five albums on IRS between 1982 and 1985, growing to be one of North America's most respected alternative bands and pioneers of the college rock scene. However, by 1987, R.E.M. outgrew IRS's distribution and support capabilities, leading to their departure for a major label. "IRS distribution, their ability to get R.E.M. records into record stores, had gone as far as it could, and it was time to move on to someone who could get the records out there worldwide." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AymMybE33zU ← irs-records ← irs-records

**Quasi-Independent Label for New Wave Hits** : IRS Records was described as a "quasi-independent label" founded by Miles Copeland, which served as R.E.M.'s first label beyond their debut single. It had distribution through major labels like A&M Records and MCA but maintained an "indie-minded and scrappy" ethos. IRS was instrumental in generating hits for a variety of new wave acts, including The Go-Go's and Fine Young Cannibals, reflecting its success as "the little label that could" in the 1980s. **R.E.M.'s Independent Success** : R.E.M. achieved platinum-level success on IRS Records, an independent label with limited resources. While on IRS, their albums *Murmur*, *Reckoning*, and *Fables of the Reconstruction* were belatedly certified gold, and *Life's Rich Pageant* became their first album to initially receive gold certification. This independent success paved the way for their eventual signing with Warner Brothers.
