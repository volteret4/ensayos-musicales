# label - Fantasy Records

## curiosities
**Copyright Ownership of CCR Songs** : Fantasy Records, run by Saul Zaentz, came to own the copyrights for songs written by John Fogarty for Credence Clearwater Revival. This ownership was a result of John Fogarty giving them up to escape his contract with Zaentz, leading to an antagonistic relationship. **Transition from Jazz to Rock** : Fantasy Records was originally a jazz label that began crossing over to rock in 1964. They signed the band that would become Credence Clearwater Revival, renaming them The Gollywags and later Credence Clearwater Revival, in an effort to market them in the evolving music landscape. **Legal Battles with John Fogerty (1980s)** : Fantasy Records, particularly under the leadership of president Saul Zantz, was deeply entrenched in bitter and prolonged legal battles with John Fogerty throughout the 1980s. These disputes primarily revolved around the copyrights to Fogerty's song catalog and the rights to perform under the Creedence Clearwater Revival name, significantly impacting Fogerty's career and personal life. **Self-Plagiarism Lawsuit (1980s)** : Fantasy Records president Saul Zantz famously sued John Fogerty in the 1980s, alleging self-plagiarism. Zantz's novel theory was that Fogerty's 1985 solo hit, "The Old Man Down the Road," was too similar to Creedence Clearwater Revival's 1970 hit, "Run Through the Jungle," a song Fogerty wrote but which Zantz's label owned. The case was ultimately dismissed, highlighting the extreme nature of their acrimony. **Acquisition by Concord Music Group (Mid 2000s)** : In the mid-2000s, Fantasy Records was sold by its long-time president Saul Zantz to the Concord Music Group. This acquisition marked a turning point, as the new parent company subsequently reverted the rights to Creedence Clearwater Revival's catalog back to John Fogerty.
