# label - Universal Music Group

## curiosities
**Extensive Subdivision of Imprints** : The Universal Music Group acts as a "Russian nesting doll" of labels, comprising numerous divisions like Interscope Geffen-ANM, Capitol Music Group, Republic Records, Island Records, and many others, which are further subdivided into more imprints globally, including specific regional offices. "The main label is Universal. And under that, our division's known as Interscope Geffen-ANM, Capital Music Group, Republic Records, Island Records, Caroline Records, Isolation Network, Universal Music Group Nashville, Universal Music Latin entertainment, the Verge label group, Universal Music Enterprises, Eagle Rock Entertainment, Universal Music UK, Universal Music Hong Kong, Universal Music Japan, and Universal Music Sweden." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcADOuQUiAQ ← universal-music-group
**Major Label Status and Ownership** : Universal Music Group is identified as the largest of the three major multinational record corporations. It is owned by a French company called Vivendi and operates a vast network of sub-labels and divisions across the globe. "Universal, which is the biggest known by a French company called Vendee." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcADOuQUiAQ ← universal-music-group
**Acquisition of Dreamworks Records Contract (2006)** : The Universal Music Group absorbed Nelly Furtado's contract from the now-defunct Dreamworks Records label, which facilitated her signing with Timbaland's Mosley Music Group and her subsequent career reinvention. "When Nelly Fertado's contract with the now-defunct Dreamworks Records label was absorbed into the Universal Music Group, Tim Beland signed Fertado to his Mosley Music Group label and began producing an album for her." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Flip It and Reverse It Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Partnership with Mosley Music Group (2006)** : Universal Music partnered with Timbaland to launch his vanity label, Mosley Music Group, in 2006, contributing to his commercial resurgence and providing a platform for artists to reinvent their sound. **Decision Against Promoting "Happy" (2013)** : The studio and soundtrack label Universal chose not to promote Pharrell Williams' song "Happy" initially, despite the filmmakers' enthusiasm for it, because Williams already had two other songs topping the charts at that moment.
