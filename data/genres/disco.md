# genre - disco

## curiosities
**Billboard Chart Dominance in the 70s** : Disco music ascended to prominence on the Billboard charts by the end of the 1970s, effectively replacing Bubblegum Music as a leading genre. This rise signified a major shift in mainstream musical taste. **Ebbing Popularity (1980)** : By 1980, disco's popularity was ebbing, yet Blondie's "Call Me" showed how dance music could evolve and make a successful transition into the new decade. **Dominant in 1979** : 1979 was a year "full of disco," which made The Knack's "My Sharona" (a rock song) an unexpected number one and a point of contention among rock fans. **Dominance in Late 1970s** : Disco emerged as the dominant sound in both R&B and Pop by the late 1970s, leading to varying degrees of adaptation success among vocalists who rose to prominence in the 1960s. "By the late 70s, both R&B and Pop had moved on to the sounds of disco." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Say a Little Prayer Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4eec154465cd600c4d67
**Veterans' Adaptation Challenges** : Many 1960s vocalists struggled to adapt to the disco sound, with some, like Aretha Franklin, experiencing significant commercial and critical setbacks, while others, like Diana Ross, successfully pivoted. "Vocalists who had come up in the 60s had varying responses to disco, and some adapted better than others." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Say a Little Prayer Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4eec154465cd600c4d67
**Coming of Age (1974-1975)** : Disco music, characterized by lush club sounds, began its significant breakthrough on the charts around 1974 and 1975, signaling its maturity as a musical form. "Musically, it's signaled that disco had come of age." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Queen of Disco Edition Part 1 (Encore) | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/69d92e92fdeddc4b127158d5
**Extended Mix Popularization** : The "Love to Love You Baby" single and album were instrumental in reimagining disco as album-length music and significantly helped to popularize the concept of the extended mix, pushing the boundaries of traditional single lengths.
