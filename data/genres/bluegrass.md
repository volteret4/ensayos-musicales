**Foundations and Characteristics** : Bluegrass has its roots in mountain music and is a mixture of gospel, blues, and country. It is distinctly known for its use of the banjo and fiddle, as well as the beautiful close harmonies typically sung in trios or quartets. The fiddle music of Appalachia and the Ozarks forms part of its very foundation. "Bluegrass has its roots in mountain music. Like rock it's really a mixture of many other genres too." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 46 - The Chaos and Music of the Ozark Music Festival of 1974 | https://www.ftr70.com/ ← bluegrass
**New Influence in Alt Rock (Late 2000s)** : Bluegrass was considered as a potential new musical influence for the alt-rock crowd in the late 2000s, alongside folk. This experimentation emerged as rock entered a phase of innovation during its cyclical decline. "Could the industry maybe sell a form of folk and bluegrass influence music to the alt-rock crowd?" ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_yslM5oanRo ← bluegrass
**Roots Music Category** : Bluegrass is recognized as a type of roots music that needs space for expression and appreciation, alongside other traditional forms like hillbilly music. **Mainstream Exposure via Deliverance Soundtrack (1970s)** : Bluegrass music gained significant mainstream exposure through the chart-topping soundtrack to the 1972 Southern Gothic survival thriller *Deliverance*. The soundtrack, titled *Dueling Banjos*, featured the memorable banjo workout by Eric Weissberg and Steve Mandel, which reached number 2 on the Hot 100 in February 1973, serving as a "starter sampler" for the genre. "the chart-topping soundtrack to the Southern Gothic Survival Thriller Deliverance was to Bluegrass Music." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Hooked to the Silver Screen Edition Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Boosted by Deliverance Soundtrack (early 1970s)** : The *Deliverance* soundtrack was instrumental in boosting the profile of Bluegrass music in the early 1970s, serving as a precedent for how a film's music could highlight a genre, much like *O Brother, Where Art Thou?* did for Americana.
