**Rise via Ringtones - Mid-2000s** : The polyphonic ringtone industry contributed significantly to the rise of reggaeton, a genre that was emerging in Panama and Puerto Rico during the mid-2000s. Traditional radio chains initially refused to program reggaeton songs, but the genre found widespread popularity through reality shows like Gran Hermano and, especially, through polyphonic ringtones, as its sound was perceived as more natural for this format than rock music. **Emergence and Influence** : Reggaeton became a significant musical force in the early 2000s, building upon its late 1990s existence. Its global translation and the irresistible, adaptable Dem Bow rhythm can be attributed to the rhythm's Afro-diasporic origins, found in Afrobeats, Soca, and Dancehall. **Origin of Dem Bow Rhythm** : The Dem Bow rhythm, originally from Dancehall, has roots in Africa and is heard across various cultures, including Afrobeats and Soca. While "Dem Bow" is now a catch-all term for the driving beat in reggaeton, old-school producers note it refers to a very specific rhythm. "First of all, if you look at where the demo rhythm came from, and and demo is a very, you know, if you talk to one of some of the old school reggaeton producers, they'll tell you like, demo is one very specific rhythm that was used, and now it's kind of used as a catch-all term for what the reggaeton driving beat is, but it's not technically demo." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Bridge - Hits Don’t Lie | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Evolution from Underground** : The genre originated in Panama when Jamaican artists sent records to cousins who began rapping in Spanish over them. This evolved into what was called "Underground" in Puerto Rico, where elements like "malise" and Bachata guitars were added, solidifying its Latino identity around the mid-1990s and transforming it into what is now known as reggaeton. "it came from Panama. A lot of Jamaican artists were sending records to their cousins in Panama, and they started wrapping in Spanish over it, and then eventually it became what was called Underground in Puerto Rico." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Bridge - Hits Don’t Lie | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Misconception of Decline** : There was a widespread misconception around 2009-2010 that reggaeton had died. However, this was inaccurate, as sales for the genre remained strong; it was primarily media outlets that ceased paying attention. "There was this misconception that Raggedón had died in like 2009 and 2010.
