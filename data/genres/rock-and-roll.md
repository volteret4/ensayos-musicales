# genre - rock and roll

## curiosities
**Dominant Genre Emergence (Post-1956)** : December 4, 1956, is identified as a significant date marking the transition where rock and roll began to solidify as a dominant genre, distinct from rockabilly, gospel, and country music, which had previously been considered "the same kind of thing." Artists like Jerry Lee Lewis, who began their careers after this establishment, exemplify this shift. **Grammys' Foundational Disdain (1950s)** : The founders of the Grammys "hated rock and roll" and aimed to reward "anything but" it, considering it a "passing fad." Consequently, the initial Grammy categories completely excluded rock and roll, focusing instead on genres like jazz, classical, pop, country, Broadway, movie soundtracks, comedy, and children's music. "According to Henry Shipper's book Broken Record, the Inside Story of the Grammy Awards, Naras created the Grammys to quote, clean up and gentrify pop. The awards would reward artistry and excellence. Buzzwords for people like Frank Sinatra and Ella Fitzgerald, while paying no heed to top-selling rock and R&B." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > And the Grammy Goes to… Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4ef0154465cd600c4e3a
**Initial Grammy Aversion (1950s)** : The Recording Academy, founded in the 1950s, was initially comprised of industry figures who "hated rock and roll" and spent the Grammys' first decade actively avoiding it. This historical bias set a precedent for later resistance to new popular genres like hip-hop. "The recording Academy was founded in the 1950s by industry figures who heated rock and roll. **Grammy Recognition in 70s and 80s** : The Academy eventually began rewarding rock, R&B, and other forms of pop music, with the Grammys aligning more with popular tastes by the 1970s and 1980s.
