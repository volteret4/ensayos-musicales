**Characteristics and Reception** : Easy Listening, also known as "Isleysening" or "música ligera," is a genre of instrumental music designed to be relaxing. It incorporates elements from jazz, swing, Latin music, or folk, but unlike jazz, it is intended to remain in a secondary plane, not demanding the listener's full attention. This characteristic led many critics and listeners to question its artistic merit, often categorizing it as mere entertainment, despite the contributions of ingenious arrangers and directors like Martin Denny, Les Baxter, and Esquivel. **Definition and Purpose** : Easy listening music, as exemplified by the Andrew Loog Oldham Orchestra's arrangements of rock songs in the 1960s, involved "watering down" original tracks. This genre was specifically created to appeal to audiences who might be offended by rock and roll, making the music more palatable. "I mean, really water them down so people who were offended by rock and roll wouldn't be offended by these rock and roll songs." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AcADOuQUiAQ ← easy-listening

**Billboard Chart History** : Billboard magazine initiated its first easy listening chart in July 1961, designed to track a popular radio format that catered to older listeners or those disaffected by rock and roll. This "mellow music chart" went through various names, including "Middle Road singles" in the mid-1960s, and was a "chart juggernaut" in the early 60s. **Evolution to Adult Contemporary** : The easy listening radio format evolved from its 1960s prominence, undergoing name changes such as "Middle Road singles" in the mid-60s, before eventually converting to "adult contemporary" in 1979. This is the name by which "soft radio music designed to waft in the background" is commonly known today.
