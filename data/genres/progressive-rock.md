**Delayed US Chart Success** : Progressive rock bands, particularly from outside the US, sometimes experience a significant delay in their songs achieving commercial chart success in the American market. Jethro Tull's "Living in the Past" was a UK hit in 1969 but only reached the US Hot 100 in late 1972 after a commercial promotion as a lead single from a compilation album, having previously only been pushed to AOR stations. **Complex Album Structures in the 70s** : The 1970s marked a significant period for progressive rock, with bands successfully charting number one albums that featured highly complex structures, such as single, lengthy pieces of music broken into LP sides, often despite intentionally anti-commercial approaches. **Early Genesis Identity** : In its first iteration, fronted by Peter Gabriel, Genesis was a leading group in the progressive or prog rock movement of the early 1970s. Characterized by sprawling, quirky, and highly conceptual songs, often too long for a 45 RPM single, they were a major concert draw and pushed the genre's boundaries further than many contemporaries. **Era of Verbose Lyrics and Elaborate Titles** : The early 1970s progressive rock era was marked by bands like King Crimson, Yes, Gentle Giant, and Emerson Lake and Palmer, all performing songs with elaborate titles and absurdly verbose lyrics. Genesis, especially with Peter Gabriel's onstage antics, developed an even more devoted fandom for their live shows in this context.
