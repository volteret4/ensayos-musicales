# genre - Country music

## curiosities
**Authenticity and Struggle** : For country music, the concept of authenticity is deeply tied to the "music of struggle," almost reveling in hardship, which contrasts with rock's focus on escaping struggle. This emphasis on lived experience often led to skepticism towards privileged artists or those perceived as insincere. "Some genres do not permit veering too far away from this elusive idea of authenticity. Which for country is music of struggle, almost reveling in it, and for rock is escaping from it." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 35 - Cosmic American Music - 70s Country Rock | https://seventies.libsyn.com/ep-35-cosmic-american-music-70s-country-rock
**Drum Kit Ban** : For a long time, drum kits were banned from country music shows, including prestigious venues like The Grand Ole Opry. When Western Swing bands were eventually allowed to perform there, drummers were often forced to hide behind a curtain. "And for a long time, drum kits were banned from country music shows like The Grand Orlopry, and when they did finally relent and that Western Swing bands play there, they made the drum as hide behind a curtain." ← Episode 3： ＂Ida Red＂ by Bob Wills and the Texas Playboys | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zu4E9Y1oJA0
**Genre Segregation in Charts (1956)** : Despite Fats Domino's "Blueberry Hill" being listed among Billboard's top 30 country sellers for the year, it never appeared on the top 100 country charts in 1956, illustrating strict genre segregation. This incident marked the sole appearance by a black artist on any Billboard country charts in the 1950s, highlighting a disparity between audience purchasing habits and industry gatekeepers' recognition. "Blueberry Hill made Billbud's top 30 records of the year for country sales in his annual round-up, but it never appeared even on the top 100 country charts during 1956 itself." ← Episode 45： ＂Blueberry Hill＂, by Fats Domino | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VqIwZGJHgBg
**Geographic and Demographic Origins** : Country music originated in the rural areas of the Eastern US, particularly Appalachia, Kentucky, Tennessee, and Nashville. It was primarily created by white people living in these regions. "Country music was the music made by white people who lived in the rural areas of the eastern US, basically." ← Episode 3： ＂Ida Red＂ by Bob Wills and the Texas Playboys | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zu4E9Y1oJA0
**Skepticism Towards Rock Singers** : Some country singers and their fans were concerned that rock singers entering the country genre were insincere in their appreciation or were making fun of country music. The differing approaches to music-making, such as a rock artist's desire to "play what you feel" versus a country session player's preference for specific instructions, fueled this suspicion. "They were worried that the rock singers were not sincere about their appreciation for country. Their approach to making the music might have been a little different, too, which leads to questions about whether or not they were serious." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 35 - Cosmic American Music - 70s Country Rock | https://seventies.libsyn.com/ep-35-cosmic-american-music-70s-country-rock
**Ever-Shifting Definition** : The definition of country music is described as an "ever-shifting goalpost," frequently debated within the industry, especially concerning crossover artists and modern sonic elements like synthesizers and trap beats. **Nashville Industrial Complex** : This term is used to describe the established country music industry in Nashville, which has historically rebuffed artists who record country music but are perceived as outsiders, including Black artists and those attempting to cross over to pop. **SoundScan Impact on Charts** : The addition of SoundScan to Billboard's charts in 1991 significantly boosted the fortunes of country music on the pop charts. SoundScan's accurate tallying of music sales allowed genres that previously underperformed on pop charts to gain more accurate representation and higher positions. "Sound Scan boosted the fortunes of genres that had never done so well on the pop charts before, including country music." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Toppermost of the Poppermost Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4ef60c30a1408dc29358
**Mid-1970s "Popification" Debate** : The mid-1970s saw a significant internal debate within the country music community concerning the genre's perceived "popification." This discussion was heavily influenced by the crossover success of artists like Olivia Newton-John and was overtly highlighted by Charlie Rich's protest at the 1975 CMAs. "Riches' act of defiance read as a protest against the perceived popification of country music, especially after the recent Olivier Newton-John debate." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Country Roads and Summer Nights Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/698fd3ebd6c27a06bb92e1f2
