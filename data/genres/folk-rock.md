# genre - Folk Rock

## curiosities
**Grateful Dead's Shift to Folk Rock** : As the 1970s approached, The Grateful Dead transitioned from their 1960s psychedelic sound to a folk rock approach, characterized by concise songwriting, storytelling, stripped-down arrangements, and drawing from Americana traditions. "But as the 70s approached, they were taking more of a folk rock approach." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 59 - Bands and Booze on the Festival Express of 1970 | https://www.ftr70.com/ ← folk-rock
**Influential Sound** : The combination of jangly, reverb-heavy, treble-focused guitars and Everly Brothers-inspired harmonies directly influenced bands like The Byrds, Love, Big Star, Tom Petty, R.E.M., The Smiths, and The Bangles. "That combination of jangling, reverb, heavy trebligh guitars, and definitely brothers inspired harmonies is one that leads directly to the birds, love, big star, Tom Petty, R-E-M, the smiths, and the bangles, among many others." ← Episode 113： ＂Needles and Pins＂ by The Searchers | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=izC9ZBI0UHM
**Invention by The Searchers' "Needles and Pins"** : Folk Rock was inadvertently invented by The Searchers' arrangement of "Needles and Pins," which featured two electric guitars playing a riff simultaneously with reverb, creating an electric 12-string sound. "That record is the root of Django Pop and Folk Rock." ← Episode 113： ＂Needles and Pins＂ by The Searchers | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=izC9ZBI0UHM
**Post-1978 Rock Diversification** : Folk rock was identified as a specific sub-genre within mainstream rock discussions after 1978, contributing to the increasingly complex categorization of music. "People were now talking about metal and Prague and folk rock, and soft rock, and country rock, and blues rock, and of course, punk." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bYsV3ozbk4M ← folk-rock
**Offshoot of Rock and Roll** : Folk rock is identified as one of the significant offshoots of rock and roll that Tom Petty and Prince witnessed and synthesized as they came of age musically in the late 60s and early 70s. "They came of age musically in the late 60s and early 70s and therefore had the benefit of having witnessed the first wave of rock and roll and all of its offshoots, from rhythm and blues, to folk rock and singer songwriter pop, to hard rock and heavy metal." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Hit Parade - Le Petty Prince Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Sonny and Cher's Debut** : The genre was introduced by a "new folk rock duo who called themselves Sunny and Cher" in 1965, who achieved multi-week number ones. "There were multi-week number ones by the duo who called themselves The Righteous Brothers, and a new folk rock duo who called themselves Sunny and Cher." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Hits of the Year Edition Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**DJ Influence on Chart Success** : Folk rock songs can experience a significant "second chance hit" revival due to the proactive efforts of radio DJs. In 1969, The Young Blitz's "Get Together" received a boost after a DJ used it as background music and it was picked up for a public service announcement, leading to its reissue and a climb to the top five. "DJs are famous for this sort of proactive hit picking." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Second-Chance Hits Edition Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Genre Evolution - Socially Conscious Summer Hits** : As the rock era progressed into the 1960s, the concept of a "summer song" expanded beyond beach and surf themes to include folk rock hits like The Loven Spoonful's "Summer in the City" and The Rascals' "People Got to Be Free." These songs reflected the broader zeitgeist and social unrest, evolving the genre's role in summer culture. "The Vandellas dancing in the street, a number two hit in 64, was received as both a party record and a civil rights anthem." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Song(s) of the Summer Edition Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Term Origin with The Byrds** : The term "folk rock" was first used to describe The Byrds following their June 1965 number one folk rock cover of Bob Dylan's "Mr. Tambourine Man," marking a pivotal moment in music history that also brought about Dylan's transformation into a generator of hit singles for himself. "As it turned out, the arrival of folk rock, a term that was first used to describe The Birds, was what finally brought about Dylan's transformation into a generator of hit singles for himself." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Freewheelin’ Edition Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Invention in the 1960s** : Bob Dylan is recognized as an icon from the invention of folk rock in the 1960s.
