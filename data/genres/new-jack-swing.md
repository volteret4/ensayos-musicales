**Hybrid Genre Definition** : New Jack Swing is defined as a genre that hybridized R&B singing with Hip Hop production. It was highly popular and "taking over the charts" in the late 1980s and early 1990s through artists like Bobby Brown and Janet Jackson. "This was at a moment when New Jack Swing, the genre that hybridized R&B singing and Hip Hop production, was taking over the charts, both from male stars like Bobby Brown. **Dominance in Early 1990s R&B** : In the early 1990s, the R&B genre had evolved, with "new Jack Swing" becoming the prevalent sound. This shift in the musical landscape posed a challenge for artists like David Bowie, whose "Black Tie White Noise" album struggled to find a niche on radio as top 40 stations favored this and other club/alt pop sounds. "R&B was now new Jack Swing, and top 40 radio was playing more club music and alt pop." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Starman to Blackstar Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/696aa4d936ab0b5268aab02b
**Post-Invasion Synth Pop Model** : The rise of New Jack Swing, exemplified by Janet Jackson's 1986 *Control* album, offered a different model of synth-pop that gave synthesizers "more of a groove." This genre contributed to the evolving tastes that brought the second British invasion to a close.
