**Experimentation on "Michigan Left"** : Arkells explored new recording techniques and effects, such as "a gated reverb on a snare drum," during the month-long sessions for "Michigan Left" at The Bathhouse studio. This was a luxury not afforded during the budget-constrained recording of their previous album, "Jackson Square," allowing for more adventurous sound design. "we got really into like throwing things through delays or, you know, a gated reverb on a snare drum. **Accidental Discovery and 1980s Dominance** : The gated reverb effect, a hallmark of 1980s drum sounds, originated by accident during a recording session. It happened when Phil Collins was drumming in a room with natural reverb, and an additional talk-back microphone, fitted with a strong compressor and gate, inadvertently captured the drum's sound and its reverb before abruptly cutting off the decaying sound. This created a distinct, large, cavernous sound that was unnaturally clipped, most notably applied to the snare drum. "The gated reverb is a mistake. It happens by accident." "It actually makes the drum sound even bigger. But it's this unnatural thing where it sounds like you're in a cavern that gets cut off, it gets clipped." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Bridge - Monster Drums Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Hybrid Sound of Traditional and Digital** : The gated drum sound represents a unique hybrid, blending traditional drumming techniques with the possibilities offered by emerging digital audio technology in the 1980s. It combines the natural acoustics of a drum with electronic processing to sculpt and abruptly shorten the reverberant tail. **Accidental Signature Drum Sound** : The Gated Reverb drum sound was accidentally discovered by Phil Collins and producer Hugh Padgham during the recording of Peter Gabriel's 1980 album. It originated from a talk-back microphone placed near Collins's drum kit, which, when heavily compressed, created a loud, reverberant sound that was then abruptly clipped, resulting in a sound that was both "sharply contained and yet massive." This innovation became Collins's ultimate signature drum sound, profoundly influencing his own material and infecting that of other pop stars. "It came to be called the Gated Drum or Gated Reverb.
