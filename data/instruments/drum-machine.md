# instrument - Drum machine

## curiosities
**Evolution from Rhythm Machines (1960s-1970s)** : Rhythm machines, which initially offered pre-programmed standard beats like waltzes and fox-trots, evolved into drum machines in the 1960s and 1970s. Companies like Roland, Multivox, and Bentley began selling increasingly sophisticated devices that were particularly popular with new German electronic bands. "In the 60s and 70s, rhythm machines evolved into drum machines. **Threat to Drummers (1980s)** : The widespread adoption and advancement of drum machines in the 1980s, especially after Roland unveiled the TR808, made "flesh and blood drummers really got nervous." These analog drum machines had unique, inspiring sounds that challenged traditional percussionists. **Pop Experimentation in Early 1980s** : The period of 1981-1982 was a "good time for pop artists experimenting with primitive drum machines," with classic pop tracks emerging from inquisitive rock stars using gizmos from companies like Roland, Korg, and Linn. Darryl Hall's tinkering with the Roland Compurism produced "I Can't Go for That," which rivaled Phil Collins' "In the Air Tonight" for sonic influence, marking a significant technological shift in pop music production. "This, by the way, was a good time for pop artists experimenting with primitive drum machines." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Rock ’n Soul, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Early Use in Popular Recording** : An early version of the drum machine, then called a "rhythm box," was used by Sly Stone to build Little Sister's 1971 single "Somebody's Watching You." By some estimations, this made it the "first popular recording to derive its rhythm from a drum machine," predating its more widespread use in "Family Affair" later that year. "Sly built the track out of an early drum machine, then called a rhythm box." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Spirit of ’71, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Roland CR-78 (1978) - Early Programmable Unit** : The Roland CR-78, released in 1978, was one of the earliest pseudo-programmable drum machines. It served as a significant precursor to the more famous Roland TR-808 and was notably employed at the beginning of Phil Collins' "In the Air Tonight" to establish a sparse rhythmic foundation. "The Roland CR-78 drum machine, one of the earliest sort of pseudo programmable drum machines came out in 1978.
