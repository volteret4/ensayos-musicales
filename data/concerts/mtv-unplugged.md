# concert - MTV Unplugged

## curiosities
**Artist Dissatisfaction with Commercial Hype** : Many artists who participated ended up complaining to MTV because the network always generated a commercial "hype" and expectation that did not align with the artists' artistic intentions. **Coincident Performances by STP and Nirvana** : Stone Temple Pilots' MTV Unplugged episode was broadcast on November 17, 1993. Notably, Nirvana set up in the same studio to record their famous unplugged performance just one day after STP's broadcast. Both bands chose to cover "semi obscure songs by David Bowie" as part of their sets. "On Temple Pilots, performing for MTV with an unplugged episode broadcast on November 17, 1993, that by the way was one day before Nirvana set up in the same studio to record their famous unplugged performance." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r6vVw66-gmI ← mtv-unplugged
**Forced Collaborations** : MTV often pressured artists into collaborations and duets to generate spectacle, which sometimes lacked artistic meaning or seemed forced, such as Mariah Carey covering The Jackson 5. "la antivision represionó a montar a los artistas para hacer esos duetos y colaboraciones que generaba mucho espectáculo pero que a veces no tenían mucho sentido." ← Music Radar Clan > Por qué el MTV Unplugged de Nirvana NO es un mal álbum | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=U4CmYfakHkQ&list=PLBFijugu0fhAAIR-Ibh59aaU8VWg-SkLc
**Inconsistent Production and Authenticity** : The format was questioned for various reasons, including concerts that weren't truly unplugged, sessions recorded over multiple takes resulting in a mix of 4 or 5 different recordings, and instances like Annie Lennox's performance being a prior jazz festival concert rather than a dedicated Unplugged. **Kurt's Reluctance to Include Dave Grohl** : Prior to the taping, Kurt Cobain considered banning Dave Grohl from participating because he didn't think Dave was up to playing for the acoustic set. "And until the band actually took the stage for the taping of the MTV and Plug broadcast, Kurt had been thinking of banning Dave from the gig because he didn't think he was up to it." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cS8k3PYU_Eo ← mtv-unplugged ← mtv-unplugged
**Nirvana Performance (November 1993)** : Nirvana's performance on MTV Unplugged in November 1993 was likely an attempt to re-excite fans about the band. While it did achieve this, its significant impact was mainly realized after Kurt Cobain's death. "I think part of the reason Nirvana did the MTV Unplug performance in November of 1993 was to try to get people excited about the band again." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cS8k3PYU_Eo ← mtv-unplugged ← mtv-unplugged
**Positional Problematic** : From its inception, the MTV Unplugged series suffered from a "serious positioning problem." It was always an "odd mix" that never fully defined itself, blending "great glories" like Eric Clapton, Bob Dylan, or Annie Lennox (who delivered historical Unplugged performances) with "strange" ones from artists like Shakira or Ricky Martin. **Nirvana Performance (Late 1993)** : In late 1993, Nirvana taped an "acclaimed live performance on MTV's Unplugged," during which Kurt Cobain "creatively reimagined his compositions," including the early 1994 modern rock chart-topper "All Apologies." "In late 93, they taped and acclaimed live performance on MTV's Unplugged, in which Cobain creatively reimagined his compositions." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > All Apologies Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4ef5154465cd600c4fb2
**Influence on Grammys in the 90s** : The "unplugged fad" that "had taken MTV by storm" significantly influenced the Grammys in the early 90s. Eric Clapton was the "first major beneficiary" in 1993, winning six awards for his "Unplugged" album, and Tony Bennett later won Album of the Year in 1995 with his "MTV Unplugged" CD. "the unplugged fad that had taken MTV by storm". ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > And the Grammy Goes to… Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4eef154465cd600c4ddc
**Tony Bennett's Low-Charting AOTY Winner (1995)** : Tony Bennett's "MTV Unplugged" album became the lowest-charting Album of the Year winner in Grammy history to date, having barely scraped the top 70 on the Billboard 200. This win, amid competition from chart-topping artists like Green Day, TLC, and Snoop Dogg, highlighted the Academy's tendency to overlook cultural relevance. "Bennett's collection of great American songbook standards performed on the MTV unplugged stage had barely scraped the top 70 on the Billboard 200 during its 1994 chart run." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > And the Grammy Goes to… Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4eef154465cd600c4ddc
