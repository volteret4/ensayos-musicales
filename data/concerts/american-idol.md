**Cultural Event and Chart Impact** : In the 2000s, the launch of the televised singing competition "American Idol" became a significant cultural event, not only rising to number one in TV ratings but also generating "serious pop stars" who scored major chart hits. Finalists from its first five seasons all achieved Hot 100 number ones, influencing chart patterns. **Reopening #1 Debuts (2003)** : After a chart policy change in 1998 had made number one debuts harder to achieve, "American Idol" reopened the Hot 100 to instant chart-toppers, starting with Clay Aiken's "This Is The Night" in June 2003. For the next three years, the pattern involved finalists releasing singles with little radio airplay but massive first-week retail sales, leading to number one debuts. This marked the end of the "idol-driven chart toppers" and led to another period where number one debuts became dormant for a few years.
