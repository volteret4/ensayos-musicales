## albums
**Idlewild Soundtrack (2006)** : This companion album, officially Outcast's last CD, was released in late summer 2006 alongside the movie of the same name. It shipped in platinum quantities and debuted at number two on the Billboard chart, driven by pent-up demand for new Outcast music. However, it had the shortest chart run of any Outcast album, falling off the Billboard 200 in less than three months, and critics called it "over long and unfocused." **Speaker Box, Slash, The Love Below (2003)** : Released in late September 2003, this two-CD set was Outcast's first album not to have a portmanteau word as its title, instead essentially having two titles in one: Big Boi's *Speaker Box* and Andre's *The Love Below*. The album reflected its creation as a pair of solo albums fused together, showcasing the members' diverging musical interests. Arista Records strategically priced the set so retailers could discount it to approximately the cost of a single CD, encouraging the public to view it as an extended Outcast album. This tactic resulted in it becoming Outcast's first and only number one album on the Billboard 200 in October 2003, opening with over half a million first-week sales. The album won the Grammy for Album of the Year at the 46th annual Grammy Awards in 2004 and topped many critics' polls as the best album of 2003. "In fact, the album essentially had two titles in one with a slash in the middle, Speaker Box, Slash, The Love Below." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Shake It Like a Polaroid Picture Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4ee9d1ba84fb8f044632
**Speakerboxxx/The Love Below (2003)** : This double CD was Outcast's penultimate release and won Album of the Year at the 2004 Grammys. It was only the second hip-hop album to achieve this top prize. Similar to Lauren Hill's win, Andre 3000 and Big Boy were noted for singing as well as rapping on the album. Twenty years later, it remains the last hip-hop album of any kind to take home the general field Album of the Year Grammy. "In 2004, outcast one album of the year with their penultimate release, the double CD Speakerbox Beloved Below, an album on which, again, Andre 3000 and Big Boy were singing as well as rapping." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > And the Grammy Goes to… Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4eef154465cd600c4ddc
**Stankonia (2000)** : This album, which arrived in November 2000, featured an album cover photo depicting Big Boi and Andre 3000 in front of an all-black American flag, with Dre flexing his hands at the camera. It debuted to over half a million sales in its first week, landing at number two on the charts; it would have been number one if not for Jay-Z's *The Dynasty* released the same week. *Stankonia* was Outcast's biggest and most sonically expansive album to date, blending elements of funk, psychedelia, gospel, rock, and club music with hip hop. By February 2001, it had already achieved triple platinum status and eventually sold 4 million copies. "The album was Outcast's biggest and most sonically expansive to date, incorporating elements of funk, psychedelia, gospel, rock, and club music, all fused with hip hop." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Shake It Like a Polaroid Picture Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4ee9d1ba84fb8f044632

## songs
**B.O.B. (Bombs Over Baghdad) (2000)** : Released in 2000 ahead of the *Stankonia* album, this single was acclaimed as one of the most dazzling in rap history, running at a "frenetic 155 beats per minute." The song was Outcast's attempt to synthesize the rhythms of British drum and bass music, which had been popular in UK clubs throughout the late 1990s. Critics deemed it seminal and prophetic, with the Village Voice calling it "just about the damnedest bass track ever, an electro workout," and Pitchfork later naming it the best single of the 2000s for its "obliteration of the boundaries separating hip hop, metal, and electro." Despite critical acclaim, its cutting-edge nature and controversial titular phrase (even before the Second Persian Gulf War) limited its chart success, reaching only number 69 on the R&B songs chart and missing the Hot 100 entirely. "B-O-B, Bombs Over Baghdad, has been acclaimed as one of the most dazzling singles in rap history." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Shake It Like a Polaroid Picture Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4ee9d1ba84fb8f044632

**Hey Ya! (2003)** : Outkast's song "Hey Ya" inadvertently created a problem for the camera company Polaroid. The lyric that encourages people to "shake it like a Polaroid picture" led many to believe that shaking or waving their photos would help them develop faster. However, Polaroid had to issue a public statement clarifying that shaking or waving can actually damage the image quality. **Hey Y'all (2003)** : Composed by Andre 3000 on an acoustic guitar, this "strummy catchy power pop anthem" became Outcast's biggest hit ever, arguably not rap at all. Influenced by bands like The Smiths, The Buzz Cogs, and The Hives, it blended these elements into an "abulliant title complete with an exclamation point" that contrasted with its secretly heartfelt verse lyrics meditating on the meaning of love ("Separates always better when there's feelings involved... nothing lasts forever, then what makes love the exception."). It was issued to radio and music stores in September 2003 as a double-A-sided single alongside Big Boi's "The Way You Move." By Thanksgiving, it had overtaken Big Boi's track on the Hot 100, and three weeks later, two weeks before Christmas 2003, it topped the Hot 100, knocking out Ludacris's "Stand Up." "Hey Y'all" was a multi-genre juggernaut, appearing on Billboard's pop, R&B, adult, alternative rock, and Latin charts, and Slate magazine named it the number one song in the new American songbook of the 21st century. **Ms. Jackson (2000)** : This song became Outcast's first blockbuster pop single, topping both the Hot 100 and the R&B chart in the winter of 2001. **The Way You Move (2003)** : This "horn-inflicted funk love song," featuring an "infectious chorus" sung by Sleepy Brown, was Big Boi's half of a double-A-sided single released in September 2003, three weeks before "Hey Y'all." Although officially credited to Outcast, it represented Big Boi's "head-to-head" competition with Andre 3000. It initially scaled the charts faster than "Hey Y'all," reaching the top 10 in early November while "Hey Y'all" was still at number 15. For eight of the nine weeks that "Hey Y'all" was number one, "The Way You Move" held the number two spot. Finally, in February 2004, in its 21st week on the chart, it overtook "Hey Y'all" to reach number one. This made Outcast only the fifth act to replace themselves at number one and set a new record for the longest wait by a song at number two before rising to number one. **Idlewild Movie (2006)** : Outcast fulfilled a long-time dream by creating *Idlewild*, a movie released in late summer 2006, which reimagined Depression-era movie musicals for the hip hop era. Andre and Big Boi starred as Percival and Rooster, respectively, "a pair of Juke joint performers in a fantastical Georgia town called Idol Wilde in 1935." The film also featured acclaimed black actors such as Terrence Howard, Cicily Tyson, and Ben Verine, with dazzling dance sequences and songs that evoked big band music. It earned back its $10 million budget worldwide but received mixed reviews and had a short theatrical run.
