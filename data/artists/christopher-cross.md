## albums
**Christopher Cross (1979)** : This self-titled debut album was a major success, going "quintuple platinum" and spending "more than two years on Billboard's top LP chart." It swept the top four Grammys in 1981, including Best New Artist for Cross himself. Despite its popularity, its sweep became "exhibits A and B of the recording academies out of touchness" among rock critics in the 1980s. However, later generations' "rehabilitation of both Yacht Rock as music" makes the win "seem somewhat less egregious now." **Christopher Cross (1979)** : This self-titled debut LP, released in the closing weeks of 1979, featured a tropical green background and a pink flamingo on its cover, making it visually resemble a "Yacht Rock album." Produced by Michael Omartian, it extensively utilized "dozens of West Coast players" and led to Cross commanding the charts in 1980. **Grammy Award Sweep (1981)** : Christopher Cross became the "King of 1981's Grammy Awards" by sweeping the top four prizes: Record of the Year (for "Sailing"), Song of the Year (for "Sailing"), Album of the Year (for *Christopher Cross*), and Best New Artist. He was the last artist to achieve this Grammy sweep until Billy Eilish in 2020. "Cross had swept the night's top four prizes, record, song, and album of the year and best new artist." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > What a Fool Believes, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Direct Entry into Yacht Rock** : Unlike other artists who adapted to or helped build the Yacht Rock genre, Christopher Cross, a San Antonio, Texas guitarist and songwriter, had little history as a session player before signing with Warner Bros. in 1978. He was effectively "born career-wise into Yacht Rock." "You might say Cross was born career-wise into Yacht Rock." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > What a Fool Believes, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**LA Session Player Utilization** : Producer Michael Omartian convinced Warner executives to let Cross record his own material but insisted on using "the cream of LA's session players." This approach ensured his debut album was filled with the characteristic sound of West Coast musicianship.
