**Music Has the Right to Children (1998)** : Released in 1998, this album presented a stark contrast to the dominant electronic music of the 1990s, which was characterized by clean, digital production and rhythmic prominence. Boards of Canada instead utilized slightly detuned analog synthesizers, sampled old television jingles, and recordings of children playing, all processed with a "patina of dust and time." The album's sonic aesthetic evokes a sense of nostalgia for a pre-internet world and an eternal summer. Its cover art, an old, blurred photograph of a family seemingly on vacation with their faces erased, intentionally looks backward to a distorted, ghostly past. It is considered a masterpiece of hauntology, even before the term became widely popularized. "Sintetizadores analógicos ligeramente desafinados, viejas sintonías de televisión sampleadas, voces de niños jugando, todo ello procesado con una pátina de polvo y tiempo." ← El Género Musical Más Escalofriante de Internet.
