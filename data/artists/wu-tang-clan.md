**Enter the Wu-Tang (36 Chambers) (1993)** : This was the debut album of the group, which emerged in 1993 from Staten Island, New York. Its production, handled without significant resources or budget, resulted in a characteristic raw, underground sound, akin to an "underground demo tape from a cassette" rather than a super-production. The album was a musical revolution, particularly in its distinct sampling technique, utilizing deconstructed and broken samples from 40s, 50s, and 60s soul, funk, jazz, and classic soul, setting a new standard for hip hop sampling that influenced later artists like J.C. and Kanye West. The group had to resort to hustling drugs to finance the recording of this first album, as they received no money from RCA despite signing a landmark contract.
