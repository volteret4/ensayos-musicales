**Do They Know It's Christmas (1984)** : Released on December 7, 1984, the charity single "Do They Know It's Christmas?" quickly achieved immense success. It entered the UK charts at number one on December 15th and held that position for five weeks, selling over three million copies in the UK alone, making it one of the biggest-selling UK singles ever. **Do They Know It's Christmas (1984)** : This charity single was conceived by Bob Geldof and Midge Ure in the fall of 1984, motivated by a BBC TV report on the Ethiopian famine. They decided to write an original Christmas song to avoid royalty payments for pre-existing carols. The song adapted an earlier Boomtown Rats piece by Geldof called "It's My World," with a Christmas-themed melody by Ure. Recorded rapidly in late November 1984 at Sarm West Studios in Notting Hill, West London, it featured a procession of leading British pop stars, with specific solo showcases for many. Its structure innovatively packed the first half with solos before a mass choir closing refrain. The single was issued in early December, barely a week after recording, along with a music video and a making-of video. It sold over three million copies in the UK, topping the charts for five weeks, and remains one of the two best-selling singles in UK chart history. In America, it sold 2.5 million copies by 1985 and peaked on the Hot 100 in mid-January 1985, though its airplay was seasonally limited. ## curiosities
**Initial Idea for a Benefit Record** : The day after being deeply affected by the BBC documentary on the Ethiopian famine, Bob Geldof tentatively proposed the idea of a benefit record for Ethiopia at his record company's office. The suggestion was well-received by those present, setting the stage for the Band-Aid project. "he tentatively suggested that someone ought to put out a record for the Ethiopians. A benefit thing." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Somq2MoRi5Q ← band-aid
**Legacy and Impact of Band-Aid** : The Band-Aid project, initiated by the "Do They Know It's Christmas?" single, generated significant funds for charity and inspired further philanthropic efforts in the music world. Its success led to the monumental Live Aid concerts the following July, as well as other charity singles from Canada and the US. More broadly, it fostered a renewed sense that rock and roll possessed a conscience and that music could genuinely effect positive change in the world. "The money raised by the band-aid single, the live-aid concerts the following July, the other charity singles from Canada and the US, and most of all the sense that Rock and Roll did have a conscience and that music could make a difference." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Somq2MoRi5Q ← band-aid

(November 25, 1984)** : Just two weeks after Bob Geldof saw the BBC documentary on the Ethiopian famine, 25 of Britain's most renowned musicians assembled at Sarm Studios in London's Notting Hill area on November 25, 1984. As word spread, other notable artists such as Bono, David Bowie, and Paul Weller of The Jam, enthusiastically joined the effort, forming a supergroup of British talent.
