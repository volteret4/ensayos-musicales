**Do It ('Til You're Satisfied) (1974)** : This track from 1973 is rumored to have taken 80 hours to put together and may be the first-ever extended remix specifically created for the dance floor. It was carefully spliced together by Tom Molten on a Reebok's Reel to Real tape machine, adding eight or sixteen bars at various points to create a long, unbroken version of about five and a half minutes of perfect dance beats. "The track we're now hearing, BT Express, and Do It To Your Satisfied, is from 1973. **Do It Till You're Satisfied** : Tom Moulton's remix added a longer keyboard breakdown and nearly doubled the track's length from just over three minutes to almost six. As the single climbed charts, Moulton's mix became preferred by radio DJs, leading the record to reach number two on the Hot 100 and number one on the R&B chart in late 1974. BT Express reportedly fibbed to Soul Train host Don Cornelius, claiming they had recorded the track at that length, much to Moulton's anger. ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > We Invented the Remix Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Do You Like It** : This early 70s funk song was sampled, along with Lynn Collins' "Think (About It)," to form the basis of Janet Jackson's "Alright." These samples helped create the track's old-school soul record feel, modernized with early 90s production.
