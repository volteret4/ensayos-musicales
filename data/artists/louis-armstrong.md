**What A Wonderful World (1967)** : An orchestral pop ballad about the wonders of existence, "What A Wonderful World" was offered to Armstrong by producer Bob Thiele and songwriter George David Weiss. Armstrong provided his distinctive gravelly vocals without playing his signature trumpet. It became a UK number 1 hit in 1968 but flopped in America, bubbling under the Hot 100 at number 116. Two decades later, more than 16 years after his death, the song was revived by the 1987 Robin Williams film "Good Morning Vietnam." Reissued as a single in 1988, it entered the Hot 100 for the first time, climbing to number 32 and establishing itself as a modern-day standard. **UK Tour as Part of Exchange (1955)** : After a "one-in, one-out" agreement was established in 1955 between the American and British musicians' unions, Louis Armstrong was granted permission to tour the UK. This was contingent upon a reciprocal arrangement that allowed British trumpet player Freddie Randall to tour the US.
