## albums
**The Miseducation of Lauryn Hill (1998)** : This album made history by becoming the first hip-hop album to win Album of the Year, two years after Alanis Morissette's win. The album was described as being "as much a singing album as a rapping album," highlighting the Grammy Academy's historical reticence towards pure rap music. ## songs
**A Rose Is Still A Rose (1998)** : Hill wrote and produced this song for Aretha Franklin, envisioning it as wisdom an elder like Franklin might impart to a younger woman. She also sang on the chorus and subtly interpolated Edie Brickell & New Bohemians' "What I Am," ensuring the Brickell illusion was noticeable. "Lauren Hill called the track, A Rose Is Still A Rose. The song deftly pulled off several tricks at once." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Say a Little Prayer Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4eec154465cd600c4d67
**Doo Wop (That Thing) (1998)** : In the fall of 1998, Lauryn Hill made her celebrated solo debut with "Doo Wop (That Thing)," which launched straight to number one. This achievement came after the success of her former rap troupe, The Fugees, and solidified her status as a megastar with significant cultural clout. It was "widely heard as a diss against her former bandmate from the Fugees, Wyclef Jean," establishing Hill's independence and contributing to the album's classic and blockbuster status with its melodic, elegant, yet brutally frank tone. ## curiosities
**Marriage to Rohan Marley** : Lauren Hill, a singer, is married to Rohan Marley, the son of Bob Marley. "He's married to singer Lauren Hill." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=MmXDbXI6Sqk ← lauren-hill
**First Hip Hop Album of the Year Win** : Lauren Hill's "The Miseducation of Lauryn Hill" was the first hip-hop album to win Album of the Year at the Grammys in 1998. This win was considered a triumph but "had no co-tales," as it was another five years before another hip-hop album took the top prize, and both of the only two hip-hop AOTY winners (hers and Outcast's) were "fronted by non-rap singles."
