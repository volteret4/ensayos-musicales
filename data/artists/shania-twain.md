## albums
**Come On Over (1997)** : This album by Shania Twain achieved extraordinary success, selling 40 million copies worldwide and earning it the distinction of being the eighth best-selling album of all time. Its massive global sales positioned Twain as one of three Canadians with the biggest-selling albums in the 1990s, demonstrating the unparalleled international impact of Canadian music. **Come On Over (1997)** : The highly anticipated follow-up to "The Woman in Me" explicitly signaled its crossover ambitions through its title. Released in fall 1997, it became a global phenomenon, spinning off hits for nearly three years, from 1997 to mid-2000. It spawned 11 singles for Country Radio and set a record by spending 50 weeks at number one on the country albums chart, a distinction held for over two decades. Approximately half of its singles charted on the pop charts. Certified double diamond in America with over 20 million copies sold, and 40 million globally, it became the second-best-selling album by a female artist worldwide, trailing only Whitney Houston's "Bodyguard" soundtrack, realizing Mutt Lang and Shania Twain's goal of blending multiple genres. **Come On Over (1997)** : This 1997 album by Shania Twain is described as an "Uber Blockbuster," one of the best-selling albums in chart history. It was certified for sales of 20 million copies in America alone, a figure equal to Fleetwood Mac's *Rumours*. **Up! (2002)** : Twain's 2002 album, titled "Up!" (including an exclamation point), represented a significant cross-genre experiment. It was released in three distinct, color-coded versions: a green country CD, a red pop CD, and a blue international disc that featured a specific South Asian Bollywood-style flavor. Each track was remixed or re-recorded to cater to these different audiences. While "Up!" was Twain's first album to debut at number one on the Billboard 200 pop album chart and sold quickly initially, its final sales total was less than half of "Come On Over," and its singles did not perform as well, failing to achieve any country number ones or pop top tens. It marked the final collaboration between Twain and Lang. "It was issued in three versions, color-coded by genre, a green country CD, a red pop CD, and a blue international disc." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Pour Some Sugar on Me Edition Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/

**You're Still the One (1998)** : This song became the album's "blockbuster pop breakthrough" and was particularly autobiographical for Twain and Lang. Twain wrote it in response to "careerist naysayers" questioning their marriage due to their 17-year age difference, expressing themes of perseverance. Lang was deeply moved by the sentiment and composed the song's highly memorable melodic chorus. Released as a single in early 1998, it not only topped the country chart but also reached number two on the Hot 100, where it remained for nine weeks during the summer of 1998 (blocked from number one by Brandy and Monica's "The Boy Is Mine"). Globally, it achieved immense popularity, hitting the top 10 in nearly a dozen countries and solidifying "Come On Over" as an international blockbuster.
