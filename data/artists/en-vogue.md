## albums
**Born to Sing (1990)** : This was the quartet's debut album, whose title was described as "truth in advertising" due to the powerful voices of its four members. While it produced their debut hit "Hold On," it did not generate as many big pop hits as Wilson Phillips' debut album but was considered more forward-looking due to its use of rap tools, breakbeats, electro-funk keyboards, and samples. ## songs
**Free Your Mind (1992)** : This "Gonzo Roxon" featured lyrics against racism, sexism, and prejudice of all kinds. It was named after Funkadelic's 1970 jam, "Free Your Mind, and Your Ass Will Follow." En Vogue's version reached number eight on the Hot 100 and was nominated for a Grammy for Rock Vocal Performance. "Envog's Free Your Mind reached number eight on the Hot 100 and was even nominated for a Grammy for rock vocal performance." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Be My Baby-Baby-Baby Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4eed8e6dd12efb02c340
**Giving Him Something He Can Feel (1992)** : This sensual slow jam was a cover version by En Vogue, originally written by soul legend Curtis Mayfield and recorded by Aretha Franklin in 1976. En Vogue's rendition reached number six on the pop chart, surpassing Franklin's original pop performance. "A cover of the sensual slow jam giving him something he can feel, written by sole legend Curtis Mayfield and originally recorded by Aritha Franklin in 1976." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Be My Baby-Baby-Baby Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4eed8e6dd12efb02c340
**Hold On (1990)** : This New Jack Swing-style love song launched En Vogue in early 1990, immediately becoming a hit. It reached number one on the R&B chart and number two on the Hot 100. The main hook was notably borrowed from James Brown's 1974 funk classic, "The Payback." "Envogue launched in early 1990 with a song that broke them right away. The new Jack style love song Hold On, which reached number one on the R&B chart and number two on the Hot 100." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Be My Baby-Baby-Baby Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4eed8e6dd12efb02c340
**My Lovin' (1992)** : This was En Vogue's follow-up hit in 1992, generated by producers Denzel Foster and Thomas McElroy by sampling another riff from James Brown's "The Payback." The tart kiss-off to a trifling man reached number two in May 1992, spent nearly eight months on the Hot 100, and became Billboard's seventh biggest hit of the year. It established a sassy persona for the group and led to a crossover with a larger pop audience. "My Lovin' parentheses, you're never gonna get it. A tart kiss-off to a trifling man reached number two in May 1992." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Be My Baby-Baby-Baby Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4eed8e6dd12efb02c340
**Something He Can Feel (1992)** : En Vogue released their cover of Aretha Franklin's song "Something He Can Feel" in 1992, featured on their album "Funky Divas." This rendition came 15 years after Franklin's original hit. ## curiosities
**Gen Y Pop Preference** : En Vogue was cited as an example of the pop artists that Generation Y was into, underscoring the strong presence of R&B and pop acts that dominated the airwaves during Gen Y's formative years. "It was all spice girls and backstreet boys and puff daddy and on vogue." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=amHre9ZZFkU ← en-vogue
**Conception by Producers** : En Vogue was conceived by Oakland, California-based producers and songwriters Denzel Foster and Thomas McElroy. They aimed to recreate the spirit of early 1960s girl groups with modern production and post-Whitney Houston vocal talent. "Envogue was conceived by a pair of producers and songwriters from Oakland, California, Denzel Foster, and Thomas McElroy." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Be My Baby-Baby-Baby Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4eed8e6dd12efb02c340
**All Capable of Lead Vocals** : By design, all four women in En Vogue—Terry Ellis, Cindy Heron, Maxine Jones, and Dawn Robinson—were capable of singing lead, showcasing their powerful voices. "The four women who made up the group, Terry Ellis, Cindy Heron, Maxine Jones, and Dawn Robinson, came from all over the United States and all possessed powerful voices. Each was capable of singing lead. This was by design." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Be My Baby-Baby-Baby Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4eed8e6dd12efb02c340
**Maxine Jones as Only Oakland Member** : Despite being conceived by Oakland-based producers Denzel Foster and Thomas McElroy, only one member of the quartet, Maxine Jones, was actually from their home city of Oakland after thousands of auditions across the country. "Only one of the quartet, Maxine Jones, was even from their own city of Oakland." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Be My Baby-Baby-Baby Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4eed8e6dd12efb02c340
