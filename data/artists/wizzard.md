**I Wish It Could Be Christmas Every Day (1973)** : Recorded in the late summer of 1973, this song was Wizzard's entry into the Christmas chart competition against Slade. It featured classic Phil Spector-like sound elements including old-school saxophones, jingling bells, and "who-ing" female backing vocals. The single entered the UK chart at number 19 in December 1973, but ultimately stalled at number four, being overshadowed by Slade's "Merry Xmas Everybody" which outsold it by hundreds of thousands of copies. Despite this, it remains a UK holiday perennial. **Formation and Sound** : Wizzard was formed by Roy Wood, an early member and co-founder of the Electric Light Orchestra. The band's glam rock sound was distinctly retro, blending the 70s crunch of glam with the "wall of sound" and brassy pop style reminiscent of old Phil Spector singles, which made them a natural contender for a Christmas song. **Rivalry with Slade (1973)** : In 1973, Wizzard was Slade's major competition on the UK charts, with both bands having a legitimate claim as the most popular glam rock act. Their head-to-head Christmas single battle was significant, with Wizzard's "I Wish It Could Be Christmas Every Day" ultimately losing out to Slade's "Merry Xmas Everybody." Bassist Rick Price later commented on the disappointment, stating that without Slade, Wizzard "wouldn't have had a problem."
