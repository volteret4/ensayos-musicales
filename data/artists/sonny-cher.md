## songs
**Baby Don't Go (1964)** : Initially released in 1964, this single by Sonny and Cher was a regional hit on the West Coast but failed to gain national traction or chart on the Hot 100. Following the duo's national breakthrough with "I Got You Babe" in 1965, their old label, Reprise Records, reissued the song, which then became a big hit, reaching number eight on the Hot 100 within two months of "I Got You Babe" peaking. **I Got You Babe (1965)** : This song reached number 1 in the summer of 1965 and was one of the first major pop hits to explicitly address the "generation gap." Despite its counter-culture image, Sonny Bono was "already over 30 years old" when it was recorded, and the track was largely performed by "polished LA studio musicians," with Cher being the "only actual baby boomer performing" on the record. It was also featured in an episode of Mad Men. "Canadian shares I Got You Babe, which reached number 1 in the summer of 65, was one of the first big pop hits to explicitly address the generation gap." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Singing Nuns and Green Tambourines Edition Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
