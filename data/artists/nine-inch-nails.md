## songs
**34 Ghosts 4 (2008)** : An obscure instrumental track from the "Ghosts" album, not originally intended to be a hit due to its unconventional title. This track gained significant recognition in late 2018 when Montero Lamar Hill (Lil Nas X) transformed it with the help of a Dutch DJ into the basis for "Old Town Road." "This obscure Nine Inch Nails track became far less obscure in late 2018, when a young black man from Atlanta, 19-year-old Montero Lamar Hill, turned 34 Ghosts 4 with the help of a Dutch DJ into this." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Ride ’til I Can’t No More Edition Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Burn (1994)** : Trent Reznor contributed this song to the "Natural Born Killers" soundtrack, and it was among those accused by Mark Anofrio of copyright infringement. ## curiosities
**Extensive 12-inch Remixes (90s-Early 2000s)** : Nine-inch Nails frequently had their singles remixed in multiple ways for nightclubs during the 90s and early 2000s, catering to the alt-rock dance club scene. This extensive remix culture diminished significantly by the 2010s. **Nothing Records – Vanity Boutique Label** : Trent Reznor's Nothing Records was created by Interscope, a subsidiary of Universal, as a special vanity boutique label. This arrangement gave Trent Reznor extra creative control, allowing him to pursue, sign, develop, and produce talent independently. It exemplifies how major labels can establish indie-looking imprints for significant artists. "This gave Trent some extra creative control and allowed him to pursue, sign, develop and produce talent on his own." ← Ongoing History of New Music > Definition (from the vault) | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ft8nruhWCas&list=PLM8bxV0E1Gse5C3n1u-mkVSTZLL5AJICG
**Innovative Industrial Artist** : Trent Reznor, performing under the moniker Nine Inch Nails, was a featured industrial artist at the first Lollapalooza in 1991. His music was described as "innovative, danceable, yet thrashing hard rock." **"Old Town Road" Sample Source** : A banjo sound from Nine Inch Nails was sampled and used as the "enveloping hook" in Lil Nas X's "Old Town Road." This alternative rock sample was integrated into what became a country and hip hop genre-bending hit, showcasing an "in theory, a banjo" sound that contributes to its minor key involvement. "I mean, with the caveat that I am not a musicologist and I don't overstep my bounds, I would say that the nine inch nails sample, but is in theory, a banjo. That is a very enveloping hook." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Bridge - Can’t Tell Me Nothin’ | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
