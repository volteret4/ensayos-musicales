## curiosities
**Influence on Punk and Post-Punk** : Although Jonathan Richman and the Modern Lovers never achieved significant record sales, they were highly influential to early punk bands and many post-punk groups such as the Sex Pistols, R.E.M., Violent Femmes, Dinosaur Jr., The Cars, The Pixies, and the Ramones. This impact stemmed from Jonathan Richman being a big Velvet Underground fan. "Jonathan Richmond and the Modern Lovers never sold a lot of records, but they were very important to the early punks and many of the post-punk bands like the Sex Pistols and REM and the Violent Femmes and Dinosaur Jr. and the Cars and the Pixies and the Ramones." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dDcD8kg0wEk ← jonathan-richman-and-the-modern-lovers
**Anti-Hippie Image** : Richman deliberately bucked the trends of his era by wearing his hair short when most male rockers sported long hair, openly reveling in his anti-hippie stance and embracing a distinct individuality. "He was also someone who at a time when every male rocker and every male rock fan wore their hair long, but this guy, nope, he wanted to wear his hair short. He was vehemently anti-hyppy and he reveled in being different." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Cy5SCQV0HAY ← jonathan-richman-the-modern-lovers ← jonathan-richman-the-modern-lovers
**Contemporary Powerpop Sound** : Recorded in 1972, their song "Roadrunner" is noted for sounding "every bit as modern powerpop as anything by the strokes or slone, the new pranographers, the Danny Warhol's, Jimmy Eat World, and even the Go-Ghosts, and the knack and blondie and the Romantics," demonstrating its ahead-of-its-time quality. **Groundwork for Punk Rock** : The stripped-down songs of Jonathan Richman and The Modern Lovers were instrumental in laying the groundwork for punk rock, with their music frequently classified as proto-punk, meaning it predated and helped define the genre. "His stripped down songs help set the groundwork for punk rock.
