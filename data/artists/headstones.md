**2003 Breakup Due to Addiction** : The band broke up in 2003 at Hugh Dillon's behest because he recognized that the band's atmosphere was enabling his addiction, and he feared for his life. Despite the band being their livelihood, manager Bernie Brin and the other members understood the gravity of the situation. "So the band breaks up when? 2003." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zBd13KtYlAA ← headstones
**Benefit Concert for Randy Kwan's Son** : The band played a reunion benefit concert at the Sound Academy for Randy Kwan's son, Hunter, attracting 2500 attendees. This gig was the Headstones' first performance after an eight-year hiatus and a way to honor Randy's memory and support his child. **Discovery and Signing with MCA** : The Headstones were discovered by Cam Carpenter and Tony Wanamaker, who secured them a showcase at the X-ray and Ultra Sound Club. A pivotal moment was the unexpected appearance and endorsement of Gord Downie and Paul Langlois from The Tragically Hip (who were also signed to MCA), which swayed MCA to sign the Headstones despite initial concerns about their "dangerous" image. "I remember when MCA and Cam Carpenter, you know, signed us at ultrasound, they were like, yeah, is it they're a little dangerous yet." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y7kmkWJ54ww ← the-headstones
**Early Reputation and Sound** : The Headstones were once known as the "scariest band in Canadian music," described as intense, self-destructive, and high-energy. The band's singer, Hugh Dillon, stated that the group's preferred self-description was "furious," highlighting their unique, unpredictable, and ferocious playing style that he likened to a high-energy hockey team. "Once upon a time, the headstones were the scariest band in Canadian music." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y7kmkWJ54ww ← the-headstones
**Intense Touring and "Second Album Syndrome"** : Following their debut, The Headstones maintained a grueling touring schedule, playing between 120 and 160 shows a year during the 1990s. This relentless pace led to the "second album syndrome" for "Teeth and Tissue," as songs were often written and recorded hastily on the road, with less time for full development. "I think we've been on the road so much for that pre that record picture of health. And then after it, how many days, I was just, they were doing like 120 shows a year or something like that during the whole 90s, 160 the first, the first year." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y7kmkWJ54ww ← the-headstones
**Kingston Origins and Early Formation** : The core members originated from Kingston, where they were part of a vibrant music scene that also produced The Tragically Hip. The band's genesis involved a group called "Sean Penn Atenshry" in the mid-1980s, which included Hugh Dillon, Randy Kwan, Mark Gibson, and Frontini, before Trent Carr joined and transformed their sound, leading to The Headstones' formation. "So how the headstones come out of Kingston? What year are we talking about? 89? No, you came from Kingston earlier than that. It was like 80s." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y7kmkWJ54ww ← the-headstones
**Live-Centric Songwriting Approach** : The band's songwriting philosophy prioritizes live performance, ensuring that songs can "stand up live" and genuinely resonate with an audience. This focus dictates what makes it into their setlists and onto new albums, as they believe insincerity would be detected by the crowd or fellow band members. "The songs had in my mind had to stand up live. People had to be able to, you could tell if they uncross their own." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y7kmkWJ54ww ← the-headstones
**Platinum Debut and Studio Challenges** : Their debut album, "Picture of Health," achieved platinum status instantly, which was a significant surprise for the band members. They admitted to feeling "totally green" and overwhelmed in the studio environment, and later questioned some of the choices made by producers and managers at that time. "Again, platinum out of the gate. Was that a surprise to you or did you? Oh, very much so a surprise." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y7kmkWJ54ww ← the-headstones
**Subconscious Noodling Songwriting Method** : The band's songwriting often begins with Trent Carr or Tim White "noodling" subconsciously during live performances or jams. Hugh Dillon actively listens for these spontaneous musical ideas, isolating specific notes or riffs that he finds unique and then developing them into full songs, rather than deliberately trying to write "hits." "I'll hear them subconsciously noodle. And that's when I stop everybody and what's that?" ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=y7kmkWJ54ww ← the-headstones
