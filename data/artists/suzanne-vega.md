**Tom's Diner (1987)** : This song was used by the Fraunhofer Institute in Germany as a crucial test case during the perfection of the Moving Pictures Expert Group Layer 3 (MP3) compression algorithm. Researchers selected it because it was a very pure acapella recording, which allowed them to easily detect distortions and glitches in the compression if the algorithm was not absolutely perfect. They meticulously listened to tens of thousands of versions of the song to fine-tune the mathematical processes for optimal audio quality in compressed music files. "When the Fronthoffer Institute in Germany was working on perfecting moving pictures expert group Layer 3, they used a song called Tom's Diner by Suzanne Vega as a test case. The reason they picked this song was that it was a very pure accapella recording, and unless the algorithm was absolutely perfect, you could hear distortions and glitches in the compression." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=TsiYLGGSJ2U ← suzanne-vega
**Tom's Diner (1987)** : This song was penned by Suzanne Vega in 1981 while at Tom's Restaurant, located at 2880 Broadway in New York City, a location also famously featured on the television show "Seinfeld." Its unique characteristics—being a pure a cappella recording with almost no reverb—made it an ideal reference track for the Fraunhofer Institute in Germany during the development of the MP3 compression algorithm. Engineers on the project meticulously listened to the song "tens of thousands of times" as they worked to compress the digital audio file to one-tenth its original size without introducing audible glitches, ultimately achieving a very good result. "Now I played that song because it was used as a reference track when the Fraunhofer Institute in Germany was working on creating the MP3." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Zniri9uSlc ← suzanne-vega
**Tom's Diner (1987)** : This two-minute a cappella song served as a "gold standard" for Bernhard Grill and the Fraunhofer team during MP3 compression testing. Its isolated human voice, devoid of psychoacoustic masking, presented a unique challenge for the algorithm. Early compression attempts were disastrous, with the sound described as "rats scratching all through the song," leading the team to listen to it thousands of times for refinement. The song was also suggested by the Fraunhofer team for inclusion in the blind listening tests during the international audio encoding competition in Stockholm in January 1990. "Grill's gold standard was this 1987 song from Suzanne Vega, Tom's diner." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=v7DXXu_1fvM ← suzanne-vega ← suzanne-vega
