**Producer for Wave** : Todd Rundgren, known as a "pop guy" rather than a punk producer, produced Patti Smith's 1979 album *Wave*. His involvement contributed to the album's smoother, more melodic sound, departing from the raw punk aesthetic of her earlier work. "It was produced by Todd Rungren who was not punk but he was more of a pop guy." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 57 - Punk and New Wave at New York's CBGB | https://www.ftr70.com/
**Self-Funded "Bat Out of Hell" Production** : As the producer for Meat Loaf's debut album *Bat Out of Hell*, Rock and Roll Hall of Famer Todd Rundgren discovered that Meat Loaf did not actually have a record deal with RCA as he initially believed. Convinced that the album "needs to be made," Rundgren personally agreed to pay for its recording. "The producer was rock and roll, Hall of Famer Todd Rungren, who ended up agreeing to paying for the recording of Bad Out of Hell himself. Because originally he thought that Meatloaf had a record deal with RCA, but he didn't. So Rungren's like, well, this needs to be made. So I'll pay for it." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 42 - Rock Operas of the 70s | https://seventies.libsyn.com/ep-42-rock-operas-of-the-70s ← todd-rundgren

**Eclectic Musician and Producer** : A musician and studio wizard, Todd Rundgren is known as an eccentric hitmaker. He achieved success with his landmark 1972 double album *Something/Anything?*, which he mostly played himself, and his spin-off band Utopia. He built a reputation as a truly eclectic producer, working with diverse bands from Badfinger and The Band to The New York Dolls and Grand Funk Railroad. "Todd Rungren was still coming off the success of his landmark hit 1972 Double Album, Something Anything, most of which he played himself, as well as his successful spin-off band Utopia." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Turn Around, Bright Eyes, Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Crucial Role in Bat Out of Hell Production** : Rundgren produced *Bat Out of Hell*, a project he initially found hilarious but then embraced, famously telling Steinman and Meat Loaf, "so what's the big problem? We just recorded, and that's it." He fronted the album's costs when the original label backed out, arranged vocals, played guitar, and ingeniously emulated a revving motorcycle sound for the title track using only his guitar and an overblown amplifier. "Todd Rungren would invite Steinman's team up to his usual workplace at Bearsville Studios in Woodstock, New York." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Turn Around, Bright Eyes, Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Amused Reaction to Meat Loaf/Steinman Audition** : When Jim Steinman, Meat Loaf, and Ellen Foley auditioned their songs for Todd Rundgren in New York City, he reportedly looked amused and laughed at the "ripe and rollicking tracks" like "Paradise by the Dashboard Light," seemingly finding the songs hilarious before agreeing to produce them. "Rungren laughed. According to both Steinman's and Foley's recollections, Rungren seemed to find the songs hilarious." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Turn Around, Bright Eyes, Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
