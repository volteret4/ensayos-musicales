## albums
**Private Dancer (1984)** : This album was a "stellar" nominee for Album of the Year at the 1985 Grammy Awards, which recognized music from 1984 and late 1983. It ultimately lost to Lionel Richie's "Can't Slow Down." "among the stellar album of the year nominees were, Prince's Purple Rain, Cindy Loppers She's So Unusual, Bruce Springsteen's Born in the USA, and Tina Turner's Private Dancer." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > And the Grammy Goes to… Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4ef0154465cd600c4e3a
**Tina Turns the Country On! (1974)** : Released in 1974, this was Tina Turner's first solo album and was a country record. The decision to make a country album was influenced by her then-husband Ike Turner, who aimed to expose her talents to a wider audience. The album largely consisted of covers and received mixed critical reviews, yet earned her a Grammy nomination in an R&B category, not country. "her first solo album released in 1974 was a country record Tina turns the country on." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 53 - More than Charley Pride - African Americans in 70s Country | https://www.ftr70.com/

## songs
**The Best (1989)** : A song written by Holly Knight. "She wrote... the best for Tina Turner." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZcF5JMhBj2M ← tina-turner
**GoldenEye (1995)** : Tina Turner performed the title theme for the 1995 James Bond film "GoldenEye," Pierce Brosnan's first movie as Bond. The song was written by Bono and The Edge of U2, making it essentially a U2 song "disguised as a Tina Turner performance." It became a decent hit in various parts of the world, including the UK Top 10 and Top 5 in several European countries. "The producers approached Tina Turner to do the theme and then she was connected with Bondo in the edge of U2 to write the song so they did." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=K0pgJ7XAlpo ← tina-turner
**Help Me Make It Through the Night (1974)** : Tina Turner covered "Help Me Make It Through the Night" on her 1974 album "Tina Turns the Country On!" The song was written and originally recorded by Kris Kristofferson in 1970, with Sammi Smith having the biggest commercially successful cover in 1971. Turner's rendition, while "wonderful," was noted for being "still bluesy," which may have influenced its Grammy nomination in an R&B rather than a country category. "This is Tina Turner with her cover of Help Me Make It Through The Night." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 53 - More than Charley Pride - African Americans in 70s Country | https://www.ftr70.com/
**What's Love Got To Do With It (1984)** : This song was Tina Turner's first and only Hot 100 chart-topper, achieving number one status in the late summer of 1984. Despite her background in rhythm and blues, Turner preferred to classify herself as a rock singer, and the song's verses featured elements of reggae or calypso music. Its broad appeal was evident as it charted across Billboard's pop, R&B, rock, dance, and adult contemporary charts. The track was initially offered to and recorded by a white British new wave band. "What's Love Got To Do With It" : this is a song for everyone." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > What’s 1984 Got to Do with It Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4ef5154465cd600c4fa3

She was part of a wave of pop veterans who scored major hits that year by reinventing themselves.
