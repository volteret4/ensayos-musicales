## songs
**Drive Daddy Drive** : A "hard R&B number" recorded by Little Sylvia, more in the style of Little Esther, released as the B-side of the "I Went to Your Wedding" single. This showcased a different, less coherent sound in her early career, failing to make her a star. ## curiosities
**Discovery of The Sugar Hill Gang** : Sylvia Robinson discovered Big Hank, a member of The Sugar Hill Gang, through a tip from her son at Krispy Krust Pizza in New York. She then recruited the other two members by driving around and asking rappers if they were interested in recording rap over the music of Chic's "Good Times." This unconventional method led to the formation of the group and the recording of "Rapper's Delight" in just 14 minutes. "Legend has that Sylvia discovered Big Hank on a tip from her son at Krispy Krust Pizza in New York." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 6 - The 70s Roots of Hip Hop | https://seventies.libsyn.com/ep-6-the-70s-roots-of-hip-hop ← sylvia-robinson
**Founder of Sugar Hill Records** : Sylvia Robinson founded Sugar Hill Records with the intention of recording a rap record. She faced challenges in finding rap artists willing to be recorded, as rap was primarily viewed as a live performance art, and many doubted its commercial viability on vinyl. Her perseverance led to the discovery and recording of The Sugar Hill Gang. "Sylvia Robinson knew she wanted to record a rap record, but she was having a really hard time finding a rap artist who would agree to be recorded." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 6 - The 70s Roots of Hip Hop | https://seventies.libsyn.com/ep-6-the-70s-roots-of-hip-hop ← sylvia-robinson

**Lead Guitar and Backing Vocals for Ike & Tina Turner** : On Ike & Tina Turner's hit "It's Gonna Work Out Fine," Sylvia played the lead guitar part and joined The Ikettes on backing vocals, making the record essentially a Mickey & Sylvia production with Tina Turner as guest vocalist. "Sylvia played the lead guitar part while Mickey did the spoken Ag vocals, which Ag would do live. **Pivotal Figure in Hip-Hop** : Sylvia Robinson is described as becoming to hip-hop what Sam Phillips was to rock and roll. In 1979, with financing from her husband Joe Robinson's gangster friend Morris Levy, she was more responsible than anyone from the first wave of rock and roll stars for recognizing the commercial potential in hip-hop. "Because Sylvia Robinson, who had been little Sylvia, and the woman calling her lover boy, became to hip-hop, what Sam Phillips was to rock and roll." ← Episode 49： ＂Love is Strange＂ by Mickey and Sylvia | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=FkyLPVvPbcI
