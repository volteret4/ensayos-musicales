**Sweet Dreams (Are Made of This) (1983)** : This R&B in Synthpop Clothing hit is one of the most iconic songs of the second British invasion, with estimates suggesting it has been covered, sampled, or interpolated around 130 times. Its music video, featuring Annie Lennox in drag, Dave Stewart playing a computer keyboard, and cows in a conference room, was considered "MTV Gold." The song took 17 weeks to climb the Hot 100, waiting out The Police's "Every Breath You Take" for four weeks at number two before taking the number one spot the first week of September 1983. **Sweet Dreams (Are Made of This) (1983)** : This number one hit by Eurythmics, now considered classic pop and "adult contemporary wallpaper," initially seemed "weird" upon its release in 1983. Its mastery of melody, rhythm, and arrangement was evident, but nothing else on the radio sounded like it. Distinctive features included chili lyrics about power and abuse, Annie Lennox's unusual pronunciation of "this" as "these," synthesizers cycling around the melody to create a lurching rhythm, and an electronic string solo on the bridge. The music video was also transgressive, showing Dave Stewart playing a computer keyboard as a musical instrument and Annie Lennox in drag, singing deadpan in a corporate conference room while cows marched around a conference table. "The weirdness, the transgressiveness, this was what made Sweet Dreams great pop." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Flip It and Reverse It Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
