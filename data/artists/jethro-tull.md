## albums
**A Passion Play (1973)** : Following the successful "geeky gambit" of *Thick as a Brick*, this 1973 album also consisted of one long track, broken into two LP sides, and similarly topped the album chart. This solidified Jethro Tull's brand as "heady, mystical, but uncompromising Prague rockers," for which consumers rewarded them. "A passion play was also one long track, broken into two LP sides, and it two topped the album chart in 1973." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The ACDC Rule, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Aqualung** : This album marked Jethro Tull's breakthrough in America, becoming their fourth studio LP and their first to crack the US Top 10, peaking at number seven in about a month. Its title track became an album-oriented rock radio standard, famous for its memorable guitar riff. *Aqualung* remains Jethro Tull's top global seller and is certified triple platinum in America, building on a US following cultivated through touring and two prior LPs that reached the Top 20. "Jethru Tull finally cracked the US top 10 with their fourth studio album, Aquila, whose title track became an album-oriented rock radio standard thanks to one very memorable guitar riff." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The ACDC Rule, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Living in the Past (1972)** : This compilation album, released in late 1972 in the United States, used the track "Living in the Past" as its lead single and title track. This commercial promotion, unlike the earlier AOR-only push, finally brought the song onto the Hot 100. "It was not until late 1972 that this Jethro Tull song was commercially promoted in the US as the lead single and title track of a compilation album, also titled Living in the Past." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Second-Chance Hits Edition Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Thick as a Brick (1972)** : This 1972 LP was innovatively conceived and recorded as a single 44-minute piece of music, titled simply "Thick as a Brick," and divided into two parts to fit the sides of a vinyl LP, with no individually titled songs. It fully embraced progressive rock rather than conventional album rock, and in an "inside joke," frontman Ian Anderson claimed to have co-written it with a fictitious eight-year-old schoolboy named Gerald Bostock. Despite this "affected anti-commercialism," and thanks to the fanbase built by *Aqualung*, the album hit number one on the album chart in just three weeks. "Thick as a brick, Jethru Tull's 1972 LP contains no individually titled songs. It was conceived and recorded as one 44-minute piece of music, titled Simply Thick as a Brick, and broken into two parts to accommodate the sides of a vinyl LP." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The ACDC Rule, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/

## songs
**Living in the Past (1969)** : In 1969, this flute-inflected progressive rock song was a number three hit in Jethro Tull's native UK. However, in the United States, it was only pushed to AOR stations as a promotional single. It didn't reach the Hot 100 until late 1972 when it was commercially promoted as the lead single and title track of a compilation album, eventually peaking at number 11. ## curiosities
**Anti-Streaming Stance** : Jethro Tull refuses to have any of their discography on Spotify, and generally opposes any digital support, including digital downloads and YouTube. They believe that their albums are global components that should be heard as a whole, and any fragmentation or partial listening out of context destroys the integrity of their work. "Ellos entienden que sus discos son parte de un componente global, que debe escucharse como parte de un todo, y cualquier cosa que ayude a fragmentar eso y escuchar un trozo fuera de contexto les parece realmente cargarse su obra." ← Music Radar Clan > Artistas que no están en Spotify y por qué | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RQYV60lqU8Y&list=PLBFijugu0fhAAIR-Ibh59aaU8VWg-SkLc ← jethro-tull ← jethro-tull
**Protection of Artistic Integrity** : The band actively pursues its rights on YouTube, not primarily for royalty concerns, but to prevent individual song fragments from being heard outside the context of the full album. Their singer has stated that vinyl is the perfect format, requiring listeners to engage with half an hour of music at a time, preventing arbitrary skipping. "Su cantante de hecho en varias entrevistas ha dicho que para él la música perfecta es en minilo, porque tienes que tragar medio ahora primero, medio ahora después y que no puedes coger tu un trozo y escuchar los suelto." ← Music Radar Clan > Artistas que no están en Spotify y por qué | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RQYV60lqU8Y&list=PLBFijugu0fhAAIR-Ibh59aaU8VWg-SkLc ← jethro-tull ← jethro-tull
**Extended Suite Pioneer** : Pioneered filling an entire LP side with 15-20 minute "uber songs" composed of movements, featuring elaborate rock combos with multiple guitar and keyboard solos, jazzy instrumental breakdowns, and string arrangements. This concept, along with bands like Pink Floyd, Genesis, and Yes, was later brought to dance music by Donna Summer and her producers. "a whole LP side would be filled by these uber songs composed of movements by such bands as Pink Floyd, Genesis, Jethro Tull, and Yes." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Hit Parade - The Queen of Disco Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
