**Bat Out of Hell (1977)** : Recording took a few weeks by early 1976 at Bearsville Studios in Woodstock, New York. Todd Rundgren produced, fronted costs, arranged vocals, played guitar, and emulated a revving motorcycle sound with his guitar and amplifier. Jim Steinman wrote all songs, asked Rundgren to emulate vintage Phil Spector sound on "You Took the Words Right Out of My Mouth," and directed Ellen Foley to sing "Paradise by the Dashboard Light" like *West Side Story*. It features E Street Band members Max Weinberg (drums) and Roy Bittan (keyboards). Finding a label took a year; Warner Brothers rejected it. Steve Popovich of Cleveland International Records signed them, distributed by Epic Records (who had originally rejected it). Released in October 1977, it was a slow-grower, debuting at number 185 and charting for half a year. Television appearances, including Meat Loaf's *Saturday Night Live* performance in March 1978 and a 9-minute video on UK's *The Old Grey Whistle Test*, propelled its success. It became one of the longest-charting albums in UK history (522 weeks), trailing only Fleetwood Mac's *Rumours* and Pink Floyd's *Dark Side of the Moon* among studio albums. In the US, it peaked at number 14, was certified platinum in 1978, quadruple platinum in 1984, and diamond by 1994, with 14 million copies sold in America and reportedly over 40 million worldwide.
