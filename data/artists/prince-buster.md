## albums
**Al Capone (1967)** : Cecil Bustamante Campbell — known by his stage name Prince Buster — was a pivotal figure in the creation of ska. Together with guitarist Jerry Johnson, he moved the beat emphasis from the 1 and 3 to the 2 and 4, stressing the offbeat. This change in syncopation, made in 1962, was the spark that created ska as a distinct genre. Prince Buster was also the source of the name for Madness — their name came from an old Prince Buster song. "Cecil moved the beat emphasis from the 1 and the 3 to the 2 and the 4. In other words, the off beat was stressed. This change in syncopation was the spark and SCA was born." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=VxWp7HrT8oE ← prince-buster

## curiosities
**Assistant to Coxsone Dodd** : Prince Buster initially worked for Coxsone Dodd as an assistant, running one of Dodd's many sound systems. He later became a significant figure in his own right. **Black Nationalist and Rastafari Adherent** : Prince Buster was a staunch black nationalist and a follower of the Rastafari religion. This conviction fueled his desire to create a purely Jamaican music, distinguishing him from rivals like Dodd and Reid who viewed their productions as R&B substitutes. "Vince Buster, on the other hand, by this point was a full-fledged black nationalist and wanted to make a purely Jamaican music. Buster was, in particular, an adherent of the Rastafar Avalidium." ← Episode 114： ＂My Boy Lollipop＂ by Millie | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kf-DtqoOsRI

**Incorporating Rasta Rhythms into Ska** : For his single "O'Carolina," Prince Buster brought in five drummers from the Rasta Nyabinghi tradition, including the influential Count Ossie. This marked the first time Rasta's sacred music rhythms were integrated into Jamaican R&B, contributing to Ska's development. "he brought in five drummers from the Rasta Niabinghi tradition, most notably Count Osie, who became the single most influential drummer in Jamaica, to record on the folks for the single O'Carolina, incorporating the rhythms of Rasta's sacred music into Jamaican R&B for the first time." ← Episode 114： ＂My Boy Lollipop＂ by Millie | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kf-DtqoOsRI
