**Metal Box (1979)** : *Metal Box* is considered a masterpiece and one of the biggest ruptures in music history. It featured incredible bass lines from Jah Wobble, acting as both rhythmic pulse and melodic motif, creating physical and heavy lows described as "the human heartbeat." Keith Levene, freed from traditional rhythm and melody duties, played dislocatedly, producing crystalline and kaleidoscopic sounds. **Metal Box (1979)** : For *Metal Box*, Public Image Ltd fully embraced the methodologies of disco and Jamaican dub, using the recording studio as an instrument in itself. They radically processed sounds, employed echo chambers, and incorporated immense empty spaces, treating silence as equally important as noise. "Abrazaron del todo la metodología de la música disco y del tapjamaicano, o sea que usaban el estudio de grabación como si fueran instrumentos más." ← La traición que salvó al punk. **Metal Box (1979)** : The metallic box packaging of *Metal Box* served as a symbol of a radical break from the punk scene that had dominated two years prior. Its release marked the birth of something new, rejecting punk's rules, limits, and dogmas, and forging a new path for music. **Metal Box (1979)** : Released in the winter of 1979, *Metal Box* was presented not as a conventional record but as a metallic 16mm film canister, devoid of band photos. **Public Image: First Issue (1978)** : Public Image Ltd launched their first album in 1978, referred to in the transcript as "Versyshu," generating high expectations as it was Lydon's new band and he was still considered the face of punk by many. **Chaotic New York Concert (May 15, 1980)** : Public Image Limited (P.I.L.), led by Johnny Lydon (formerly Johnny Rotten), agreed to play two nights at the Ritz in New York for a $12,000 fee, despite internal squabbles, drug abuse, and money problems. The band was in no shape to play, lacking a drummer (the sixth one quit) and a bass player (who had just walked out). They quickly hired a 65-year-old jazz drummer from a Broadway music shop. Instead of performing live, P.I.L. pantomimed along to their records, hidden from the audience by a large screen with their silhouettes projected by bright white lights. The 1,500 people in the audience were kept waiting in the pouring rain for over an hour, then discovered the band wasn't playing, and Johnny Lydon began taunting and insulting the crowd. This led to a rain of garbage, a storming of the stage, and the destruction of all equipment, with the gig ending after just 25 minutes. "The gig was killed after just 25 minutes." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=AymMybE33zU ← public-image-limited ← public-image-limited

**Reunion Motivated by Album Anniversary and Personal Tragedy (2009)** : In the summer of 2009, Johnny Lydon decided to revive Public Image Limited. This decision was influenced by the 30th-anniversary re-release of their influential "Metal Box" album, and also by emotional factors: the loss of his father to cancer in 2008 and the recurrence of his brother's cancer. These events reminded him of his mother's death during PiL's active period, which had inspired their song "Death Disco," suggesting "unfinished business." "The actual announcement came out on September the 7th of 2009. That was one reason. Another was a little more emotional." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DJGFUlQTUhQ ← public-image-limited
