**Toto IV (1982)** : This was Toto's fourth album, released in 1982, and featured the singles "Africa" and "Rosanna." "Africa was a single from Totos' fourth album back in 1982." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yuA9Xor8vfw ← toto
**Toto IV (1982)** : This album, which went "triple platinum," produced the number two hit "Rosanna" and the number one hit "Africa." It won Album of the Year at the 1983 Grammys, with "Rosanna" also taking Record of the Year. Like Christopher Cross's sweep, Toto's wins were highly criticized in the 1980s rock press as examples of the Academy's disconnect. However, the later "rehabilitation of both Yacht Rock as music and Africa in particular among millennials and Gen Z make these wins seem somewhat less egregious now." "And Toto's Toto 4, which took album of the year at the 1983 Grammys, as well as record of the year for Rosanna." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > And the Grammy Goes to… Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4ef0154465cd600c4e3a

## curiosities
**Collaboration on "Beat It"** : Three members of Toto contributed to Michael Jackson's song "Beat It," with Steve Lukather specifically playing the main guitar riff. "In fact, three members of Toto play on Michael Jackson's beat it." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KtdIMNoHqA8 ← toto ← toto
**High-Caliber Session Musicians** : The band was comprised of some of the best session players in the world; for instance, guitarist Steve Lukather played the guitar on Michael Jackson's "Beat It" (excluding Eddie Van Halen's solo), and Jeff Porcaro was the drummer. "Totos was made up of some of the best session players in the world." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yuA9Xor8vfw ← toto
**Original Artists of Covered Songs** : Toto are the original artists of the songs "Africa" (1982) and "Rosanna," both of which were covered by Weezer, largely due to fan requests. "Weezer's version of Africa, the total hit from 1982." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LYRPxtP61JM ← toto
**Reaction to Weezer's "Africa" Cover (2018)** : The original band Toto was "absolutely thrilled" about Mary Klym's campaign and Weezer's subsequent cover of "Africa." Steve Lukather even sent Mary Klym a video message, with Ringo Starr humorously "cooking around" in the background. "And total, the original band behind the song, is absolutely thrilled about what Mary Clem of Cleveland did for the song." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yuA9Xor8vfw ← toto
**Reputation as a "Corporate Rock Band"** : Toto was known as a "corporate rock band," a term for projects intentionally designed to be mainstream and sell as many records as possible. This made them "highly uncool" to a segment of the rock audience, particularly the alt-rock crowd, who felt they were "not supposed to like Toto." "Toto was what we used to call a corporate rock band." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yuA9Xor8vfw ← toto
**Shared Tour with The Ramones (January 26, 1979)** : In a bizarre pairing reflecting major labels' misunderstanding of punk, The Ramones were chosen to open shows for Toto on a tour. A notable instance occurred on January 26, 1979, in Lake Charles, Louisiana, where the bill also included Foreigner and Blue Oyster Cult. "the Ramones were picked to open shows for Toto." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=XjTWcy8RHEs ← toto ← toto
**Steve Lukather's Guitar Solo (1981)** : Steve Lukather of Toto contributed a "scorching guitar solo" to Olivia Newton-John's hit song "Physical," which was highlighted as one of the elements contributing to its massive success. "It has a scorching guitar solo courtesy of Steve Lucat there from Toto." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 40 - Honestly Loving Olivia Newton-John | https://seventies.libsyn.com/ep-40-honestly-loving-olivia-newton-john
**Grammy Win Controversy (1983)** : Toto's wins for "Toto IV" (Album of the Year) and "Rosanna" (Record of the Year) at the 1983 Grammys were highly controversial among 1980s rock critics, who cited them as evidence of the Recording Academy's "out of touchness." Despite the criticism, the album was popular, achieving triple platinum status and producing major hits. "If you read The Rock Press during the 1980s, in any piece critiquing the Grammys, and there were many, Christopher Cross's sweep and Toto's wins were exhibits A and B of the recording academies out of touchness." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > And the Grammy Goes to… Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4ef0154465cd600c4e3a
