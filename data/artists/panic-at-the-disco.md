## albums
**A Fever You Can't Sweat Out (2006)** : Panic! at the Disco recorded their debut album, "A Fever You Can't Sweat Out," concurrently with Fall Out Boy's promotion of "From Under the Cork Tree." Released in the winter of 2006, the album achieved significant commercial success, going platinum by July of that year, demonstrating their rapid rise and crossover appeal to a pop audience. **Death of a Bachelor (2016)** : This album marked Panic! At The Disco's first-ever number one album. With the project having transitioned to a solo endeavor for Brendon Urie, he approached the vocals as if performing on a Broadway stage, reflecting his inherent theatrical singing style. "2016's death of a bachelor found uri singing as if he was on a Broadway stage it was panics first ever number one album." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > I Write Sins, Not Tragedies, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Pray for the Wicked (2018)** : With the release of this album, Brendon Urie fully converted the Panic! At The Disco project to "pure pop." This strategic shift led to the album spawning "High Hopes," which became the band's biggest hit ever. "when uri came back with a new panic at the disco album 2018's pray for the wicked he had fully converted the project to pure pop." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > I Write Sins, Not Tragedies, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/

## songs
**High Hopes** : This "exuberant" song became Panic! At The Disco's biggest hit ever. Its success followed Brendon Urie's decision to fully convert the band's project to pure pop with the album "Pray for the Wicked." "scored panics biggest hit ever the abulliant high hopes." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > I Write Sins, Not Tragedies, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**I Write Sins Not Tragedies - Signature Hit & VMA Winner** : "I Write Sins Not Tragedies" became Panic! at the Disco's signature hit, a pop crossover success that reached number seven on the Hot 100 and number two on Billboard's Mainstream Top 40 radio chart. The song featured a "baroque title," baroque instrumentation built around a "cello played pizzicato style," and a "crashing punk chorus" with a lyrical hook famously cribbed from a teenager's diary: "haven't you people ever heard of closing the goddamn door." Its memorable circus-themed wedding music video, shot for approximately $30,000, surprisingly won MTV's Video of the Year in an upset over Madonna, Shakira, and the Red Hot Chili Peppers, one year after Green Day won the same prize. "it became panic at the disco's signature hit reaching number seven on the hot 100 and number two on billboards mainstream top 40 radio chart." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > I Write Sins, Not Tragedies, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**The Only Difference Between Martyrdom and Suicide Is Press Coverage (2006)** : "The Only Difference Between Martyrdom and Suicide Is Press Coverage" was Panic! at the Disco's debut single, released in the winter of 2006. This song, with its characteristically wordy title, reached number five on the modern rock chart in just two months, showcasing the band's immediate impact. "The only difference between martyrdom and suicide is press coverage was the debut single by Panic at the disco." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > I Write Sins, Not Tragedies, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
