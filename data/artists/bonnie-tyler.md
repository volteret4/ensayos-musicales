**Total Eclipse of the Heart (1983)** : This massive hit was written and produced by Jim Steinman, who had refined and reused its irresistible melody for over a decade, including in his college musical *The Dreaming* and the 1980 film *A Small Circle of Friends*. They recorded it with many of Steinman's favorite players from the *Bat Out of Hell* sessions, including Roy Bittan (piano) and Max Weinberg (drums) from Bruce Springsteen's E Street Band, and vocalist Rory Dodd, whose falsetto was so prominent it was almost a duet. The song debuted at number 75 on the Hot 100 in July 1983 and took a dozen weeks to reach number one, knocking out Billy Joel's "Tell Her About It" in the first week of October 1983. It stayed at number one for four weeks, giving Jim Steinman his first ever number one hit. The song's lyrics and "glossy, bonkers, and much ridiculed music video" (featuring Tyler as a teacher/headmaster of a boys' prep school where the boys are possessed) are seen as the ultimate expression of the Jim Steinman aesthetic: "no logic, all fun." In 2020, Tom Bryan described it as an "extinction-level event rendered in musical form." "Debuting on the Hot 100 at number 75 in July of 1983, Bonnie Tyler's total Eclipse of the heart took a dozen weeks to reach number one." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Turn Around, Bright Eyes, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
