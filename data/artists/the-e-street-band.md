## songs
**Hold On (1978)** : This was an indie single released by Street Band on Logo Records in 1978. It was described as "awful," leading DJs at the BBC to flip the record over to its B-side. "They released an indie single on logo records in 1978 called Hold On. And frankly Hold On was an awful song." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zUQ2vZzmRiI ← street-band
**Toast (1978)** : This song, found on the B-side of the "Hold On" single, is a spoken-word piece with a Cockney-type accent about making a piece of toast. The band reportedly came up with it on stage to stretch for time and later decided to include it on the B-side of their single. Its release coincided with a series of national strikes in England, including one by bakers, making bread scarce. This context helped the obscure B-side climb to number 18 on the British charts that fall. The band even performed it live on "Top of the Pops." The song became both the group's biggest hit and its biggest liability, leading to audiences expecting a quirky English outfit and instead finding a "loud, fairly conventional rock band," which "destroyed their credibility completely." "Inside two of the single was a song about the simple pleasures of making a piece of toast. Apparently, the guys in Street Band had come up with a song on stage during a moment when they needed to stretch for time. And when it came time to record their single, they decided to slap this jam on the B side." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zUQ2vZzmRiI ← street-band

## curiosities
**Band Breakup** : After the success and subsequent liability of "Toast," Street Band released a half dozen more "straight ahead rock and roll singles" which were "miserable failures." The band eventually broke up for good in 1979 and "descended into obscurity," save for the single song "Toast" and keyboardist Paul Young's subsequent solo career. "And Street Band broke up for good in 1979 and descended into obscurity." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=zUQ2vZzmRiI ← street-band

**Impact of "Toast" on Band Credibility** : The unexpected success of "Toast" significantly damaged Street Band's credibility. Audiences who came to see them, expecting a quirky English outfit based on the song, were instead met with a loud, conventional rock band, leading to a disconnect that hurt their career. "When people came out to see Street Band, they expected some quirky English outfit and said they got this loud, fairly conventional rock band. **Nebraska Album Origins** : Bruce Springsteen's 1982 album "Nebraska" was notably recorded without the E Street Band, as it originated from demos that Stevie Van Zandt convinced Springsteen to release as a standalone album. This decision resulted in a stripped-down, introspective sound distinct from their usual collaborations. **Instrumentation for "Born in the USA" (Song)** : The E Street Band's contribution radically transformed the "Born in the USA" demo into an anthem, punctuated by Roy Bittan's six-note synthesizer riff (interpolated from Springsteen's melody) and Max Weinberg's cracking drums, which anchored the song at a martial tempo, sounding "like a rifle firing." "Punctuated by Roy Bitton's six note synthesizer riff, a riff that he interpolated from Springsteen's own melody, the song was slowed to a martial tempo, anchored by Max Weinberg's cracking drums like a rifle firing." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Tramps Like Us, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Patti Scialfa's Entry and Return** : Patti Scialfa joined the E Street Band as a vocalist four years prior to the start of the "Born in the USA" tour. After a hiatus, she rejoined Bruce Springsteen for his 1988 tour, during which their relationship developed, leading to their marriage three years later. "On that 88 tour, Springsteen invited back the vocalist who'd joined the E Street band four years earlier, just before the start of the born in the USA tour, Paddy Skjalfa." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Tramps Like Us, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
