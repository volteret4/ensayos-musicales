**Beyoncé (2013)** : A self-titled album released by Beyoncé on Friday, June 10, 2013, as a surprise. This unconventional Friday release date, before the establishment of the global release day, gave her a strategic head start over other artists for chart sales, as it allowed her first chart week to include Friday, Saturday, Sunday, and Monday, thereby artificially inflating her initial sales figures. ## songs

**Break My Soul (2022)** : This song was a chart-topper in the summer of 2022, reaching number one on the Hot 100. It alluded to Robin S's "Show Me Love" remix by specifically using the same Korg keyboard preset that DJ Stonebridge employed, rather than directly sampling or interpolating the melody. Despite this, songwriting co-credit was controversially awarded to Alan George and Fred McFarlane, the writers of the original, non-hit version of "Show Me Love," rather than Stonebridge, illustrating a perceived flaw in copyright law. "You may recall, break my soul from the summer of 2022 when it went to number one on the Hot 100. Pay particular attention to that bubbly keyboard sound." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > If You Love Sting, Set Him Free Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4ef88e6dd12efb02c671
**Crazy in Love (2003)** : This song, featuring Jay-Z, was the "incontestable" winner of the 2003 Billboard Song of the Summer and formally launched Beyoncé's solo career. It was built from a Shy Lights horn sample, with every aspect of its production evoking summer: a "sweaty beat" that dominated July and August, lyrics about a "love like a fever," and Jay-Z's rap about feeling "crazy and ranged." The accompanying video notably featured dancers "jiggling and twerking in tank tops and short shorts," as Beyoncé asserted her role as "pop's new queen." "Everything about this production evoked summer, the sweaty beat that dominated July and August of 2003, the lyrics about a love like a fever, J. Z's rap about feeling quote crazy and ranged." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Song(s) of the Summer Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4ef50c30a1408dc292e4
**Daddy Lessons (2016)** : Beyoncé performed this "Texas celebrating country and western indebted" song at the 2016 CMA Awards. She was joined by country trio The Chicks for the performance. This cultural event was part of a percolating conversation around the history of the black cowboy that the #yeehawagenda later elevated. "the cultural debate surrounding the 2016 CMA Awards performance by Beyoncé who was joined by country trio The Chicks to perform her Texas celebrating country and western indebted daddy lessons." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Ride ’til I Can’t No More Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f078e6dd12efb02ce88
**Irreplaceable (2006)** : Co-written by Ne-Yo and Stargate, this semi-acoustic classic remains Queen Bey's longest-lasting number one hit, spending ten weeks at the top in late 2006 and early 2007. This sassy torch song famously addresses a breakup scenario and defeated Rihanna's "Umbrella" (featuring Jay-Z) for the 2007 title. "To this day, Queen Bee's longest lasting number one hit remains this semi-acoustic classic from songwriters Neo and Stargate, the one that tells you where you can pick up that box of your things." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Hits of the Year Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f078e6dd12efb02ce81
**Single Ladies (Put a Ring on It) (2008)** : This song was the "only exception" where Beyoncé was connected to an all-genre Grammy prize, taking the Song of the Year award in 2010. However, the award went to its songwriters, not to Beyoncé herself as an artist.
