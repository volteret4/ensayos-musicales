## albums
**Labor of Love (1983)** : This album was an affectionate homage to UB40's influences, consisting entirely of covers of ten songs the group grew up with by reggae artists like Jimmy Cliff, The Wailers, Boy Friday, and Winston Groovy. It went gold in America by the summer of 1986, three years after its release, signifying half a million US sales, despite never climbing higher than number 39 on the Billboard album chart. In 1988, following the unexpected success of "Red Red Wine," newly pressed copies of the album on CD and cassette were released, and it reached a new 1988 peak of number eight on the Billboard album chart and ultimately went platinum. **Labor of Love 2 (1989)** : This album followed the success of "Red Red Wine" and featured remakes of big, unmistakable pop classics rather than hardcore reggae deep cuts. **Red Red Wine (1983)** : Originally recorded for their "Labor of Love" album, this song was UB40's version of a Tony Tribe track, which they didn't realize was a Neil Diamond original. They slowed the tempo to a "swaying lover's rock" and added a toast-style rap break by Astro. It became their first UK number one in September 1983. In America, it initially peaked at number 34 in March 1984. In 1988, a Phoenix radio programmer, Guy Zapoleon, rediscovered it, leading to a viral spread on US radio. A&M Records re-released the single on cassette (Cassignle) and CD, and it re-entered the Hot 100 on August 13, 1988, ultimately reaching number one for the week ending October 15, 1988. It spent a dozen weeks in the top 40 during its second run. "Red Red Wine was a true trans-lantic hit. **Band Formation and Name (1978)** : The group formed in late 1978 in Birmingham, a multicultural British city with a large Jamaican diaspora, and consisted of eight members, half white and half black. They named themselves after the government paperwork (Unemployment Benefit, Form 40) for those living on unemployment benefits. "UB40 formed in late 1978, a little less than a decade after Tony Tribe hit single." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Hit Parade - Red, Red Wine Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Breakthrough and UK Success (Late 1970s - Early 1980s)** : UB40 got their break when Chrissie Hynde of The Pretenders saw them playing in a pub and offered them an opening slot. They quickly found success in England, catching the wave of two-tone ska and other new wave/reggae blends, scoring three straight UK top 10 singles by the end of 1980 and eight straight UK top 40 singles in their first three years. "UB40 got their break when Chrissy Hind saw them playing in a pub and gave them a slot as an opener for her band, the pretenders." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Hit Parade - Red, Red Wine Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**US Chart Struggle and Album Sales (1984-1986)** : Despite their UK success, UB40 initially had a spotty history on the US charts. While "Red Red Wine" peaked at number 34 and "I Got You Babe" at number 28, they weren't setting the colonies on fire. However, their "Labor of Love" album quietly sold well, going gold in America by 1986 despite a relatively low album chart peak. **"Red Red Wine" Rediscovery and Chart Topping (1988)** : UB40's US label, A&M Records, was promoting their new single "Breakfast in Bed" in 1988 when Guy Zapoleon, a program director for KZZP FM in Phoenix, rediscovered "Red Red Wine" from the band's catalog. Zapoleon decided to put the five-year-old track into rotation on his station's "Party Patrol" show, leading to an overwhelming number of requests. The song went viral across other US pop stations, effectively killing the momentum of "Breakfast in Bed," and forcing A&M to switch its promotional focus to the older track. "And that's when everything changed for UB40, thanks entirely to one of those programmers." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Hit Parade - Red, Red Wine Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Benefit from "Would Have Been, Should Have Been" Fad (Late 1980s)** : UB40 was the band that benefited most from the "would have been, should have been" fad initiated by Guy Zapoleon. Their career trajectory, which had seen them become a reggae pop covers band with moderate success, dramatically shifted after "Red Red Wine" topped the charts. This led to a subsequent album of covers, "Labor of Love 2," which brought them their biggest career hits. "But the act that benefited most from the would have been should have been FAD was the band that started at all in 1988, UB40." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Hit Parade - Red, Red Wine Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
