## songs
**I'll Never Smile Again (1940)** : This single topped Billboard's very first national list of best-selling retail records in July 1940. It was credited to Tommy Dorsey and his Orchestra, with Frank Sinatra and The Pied Pipers receiving small print credit for the "vocal refrain" on the 78 RPM record label, though Billboard's chart only listed Tommy Dorsey. ## curiosities
**Big Band Leader** : Tommy Dorsey was a significant band leader during the Big Band era, leading large orchestras in which he was the central figure. His musicians and singers were employees, following his precise instructions. "These were orchestras of ten or more people led by a big name, Paul Whiteman, Tommy Dorsey, Benny Goodman, and so on." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=V7WjNR1ujhk ← tommy-dorsey
**Distinction from Thomas Dorsey** : Mentioned to clarify that he is a different person from Thomas Dorsey, the inventor of gospel music, specifically a white bandleader. "This is not the same man as the white band leader Tommy Dorsey, who we've mentioned a couple of times earlier." ← Episode 5： ＂This Train＂ by Sister Rosetta Tharpe | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=II-hDnwUgT8
**Host of "Stage Show"** : Tommy Dorsey, a swing band-leader, co-presented the "Stage Show" TV program with Jimmy Dorsey, which was one of Elvis Presley's early television appearances. "a program that was presented by the swing band-leaders Tommy and Jimmy Dorsey." ← Episode 38： ＂Heartbreak Hotel＂ by Elvis Presley | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ES35PYeAwfs
**Impact of Musicians' Union Strike** : Tommy Dorsey's band, as an instrumental ensemble on a major label, was unable to record any new music for two years during the Musicians' Union Strike of 1942-44 due to the dispute over royalties. However, his former vocalist, Frank Sinatra, could still record, backed by vocal groups. "Tommy Dorsi's band couldn't record anything, but Dorsi's old vocalist Frank Sinatra could, backed by a vocal group instead of instrumentalists." ← Episode 2： ＂Roll Em Pete＂ by Big Joe Turner | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=thpMb1A_fAs
**Pre-Rock Era Frontman** : Tommy Dorsey was a prominent big band leader in the pre-rock era, akin to a star DJ of today. Despite Frank Sinatra being the main reason for the song's popularity, Dorsey was considered the front-line artist, even though he didn't sing. "Band leaders were to the pre rock era, what star DJs are to today.
