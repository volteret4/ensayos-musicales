**Adult Education (1984)** : This song, released by American duo Daryl Hall and John Oates, featured a blend of guitars, synthesizers, and "clatter," mirroring the soundscapes created by British groups like Culture Club. It was climbing into the top 10 on the same week that Culture Club's "Miss Me Blind" was also charting, demonstrating American artists were "taking notes" from the British invasion. ## curiosities
**Credibility with African-American Musicians/Fans** : Despite being white artists, Hall & Oates earned considerable credibility among African-American musicians and fans, a testament to the palpable Philly Soul influences present in their songs like "Sara Smile" and "She's Gone." "Hall and Oates do have credibility with African-American musicians and fans." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 37 - Philly Soul and the Sound of the 70s | https://seventies.libsyn.com/ep-37-philly-soul-and-the-sound-of-the-70s
**Philly Soul Roots Despite New York Move** : Even though Hall & Oates declined a contract with Philadelphia International Records and opted to relocate to New York for a different musical direction, their music consistently retained strong "Philly Soul DNA" and roots, demonstrating the lasting impact of their origins. "But the roots are still there." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 37 - Philly Soul and the Sound of the 70s | https://seventies.libsyn.com/ep-37-philly-soul-and-the-sound-of-the-70s
**Compilation Album Naming Risk** : The duo's decision to title their compilation "Rock and Soul Part One" illustrates the inherent risk of naming a greatest hits album with a "Part One" or "Volume One," as they scored more hits but never released a "Rock and Soul Part two." "It's always a risk to name a greatest hits compilation volume one or part one." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Raise Your Glass Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f708e6dd12efb02f1ac
