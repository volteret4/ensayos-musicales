**We Are the World (1985)** : This anthem was co-written by Michael Jackson and Lionel Richie, who were both at the peak of their careers, following the inspiration of Band-Aid's charity single. Recorded on January 28, 1985, at A&M Studios in Hollywood, hours after the American Music Awards, it gathered approximately 50 recording artists under the production of Quincy Jones, who famously instructed them to "check your egos at the door." The song structurally adapted the Band-Aid template but featured a traditional chorus, deployed early by Jackson and Diana Ross, which recurs as a gospel-style refrain. With a running time of seven minutes and five seconds, it nearly doubled the length of "Do They Know It's Christmas?" and featured 19 American soloists in the first three minutes, plus five bonus solos later. It was the first single in US history to be certified multi-platinum for sales of four million, with global sales estimated at over 20 million. The song dominated radio airplay, became a national event, and topped Billboard's Hot 100 for four weeks starting April 13, 1985, being both the top seller and most reported airplay record. It won Record of the Year and Song of the Year at the 1986 Grammys. "We Are the World wasn't just a best-selling single. ## curiosities
**Motown Label Misconception** : A common but incorrect belief is that "We Are The World" was edited and released by Motown Records. This misconception likely arose because the song was composed by Lionel Richie and Michael Jackson, both prominent Motown stars, and produced by Quincy Jones, a key figure associated with Motown at the time. Despite the song's initial idea being conceived at the Motown house, it was ultimately released by Columbia Records due to economic and resource-related considerations. **Quincy Jones's Grammy Apology for "Egos" Sign (1986)** : Upon accepting the Record of the Year Grammy for "We Are the World" in 1986, Quincy Jones publicly apologized for the "check your egos at the door" slogan, stating it was ultimately unnecessary, as the humanitarian cause itself sufficiently motivated the teamwork and love exhibited by everyone that night. "When the song won record of the year at the 1986 Grammy Awards, Jones once again took the podium. And the Grammy goes to We Are the World, Quincy Jones, magician. If I'm going to be presumptuous enough to speak for this great group, I'd like to say to thank them and also apologize that the slogan, keep your eagles at the door, ever got out of hand because it was never necessary. The cause itself, I think, was significant enough to command the teamwork and the love and the giving that everybody gave that night. And we're happy that we were able to share it." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > I Wanna Rock with Q. Edition Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
