**Baby Do You Want A Bump** : This single was the first released under the Boney M name, created by Frank Farian as a "made-up group." It became a hit in Holland and Belgium, prompting Farian to recruit actual performers to form a "real group" for touring and further recording. **Rasputin (1978)** : This 1978 single was a major hit across Europe, reaching number one in Germany and Austria and number two in the UK, though it never charted on the Hot 100 in America. Named after the 19th-century Russian mystic Grigoriy Rasputin, the song features mythical stories of his powers, a tempo suggesting a Russian Prizadka dance, and a rhythm guitar hook played on balalaikas, despite no actual Russian involvement in its creation by Germans and an Austrian, with Jamaican British co-lead vocals. It has experienced a resurgence, becoming the subject of a global TikTok challenge in 2021, and a 2021 remix by British DJ Majestic made the top ten of Billboard's dance and electronic songs chart. "Rasputin, or perhaps I should say Ra Ra Rasputin, given the way the word is sung, was a hit across Europe back in 1978, including number one in Germany and Austria and number two in the UK." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Blame It on the Feign, Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/

**Formation as a "Real Group"** : After the single "Baby Do You Want A Bump" became a hit in Holland and Belgium, Frank Farian transformed Boney M from a made-up group into a "real group." He recruited West Indian vocalists: three British women (Liz Mitchell, Marcia Barrett, Maisie Williams) and Bobby Farrell from Aruba, all people of color. "After the single was a hit in Holland and Belgium. Farion turned Bony M into a real group." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Blame It on the Feign, Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Lip-syncing and Uncredited Vocals** : The "slippery" aspect of Boney M's lineup was that only Liz Mitchell and Marcia Barrett actually sang on the recordings, alongside Frank Farian himself, who never appeared on stage. Frontman Bobby Farrell mimed Farian's distinctive "very tutonic German accented vocals," and Maisie Williams, a former model, also lip-synced on stage. "What was more slippery was only two of these members, Liz Mitchell and Marsha Barrett actually sang on Bony M recordings." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Blame It on the Feign, Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Remarkable European Hit Streak** : Boney M, a "Euro-British Caribbean formula," achieved a remarkable streak of hits. They scored numerous number one hits in Frank Farian's native Germany and were "amazingly popular" in the United Kingdom, achieving nine straight UK top ten hits, which was a "special delight" for Farian. "Daddy Cool kicked off a remarkable streak of hits for Bony M." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Blame It on the Feign, Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Limited US Success** : In stark contrast to their immense popularity in Europe and the UK, Boney M found limited success in the United States, cracking the American Top 40 only once with "Rivers of Babylon" (at number 30), which was also their last US hit. Their sound was perhaps "a little too exotic for US tastes," an irony given Frank Farian's later success in America with Millie Vanille. "Bony M might have been just a little too exotic for US tastes. They cracked the American top 40 only once." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Blame It on the Feign, Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Foreshadowing Millie Vanille's Performance Style** : Observing Bobby Farrell's "maniacally" disco dancing in Boney M's early TV appearances, encouraged by Frank Farian to flail his limbs, revealed the "seeds" of what Millie Vanille's energetic, almost manic, performance style would become over a decade later.
