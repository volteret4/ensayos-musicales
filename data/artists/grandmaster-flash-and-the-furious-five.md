**The Message (1982)** : Released in 1982, "The Message" is widely considered "one of the most influential recordings of the rock era." It reached number four on Billboard's R&B chart but a modest number 62 on the Hot 100, reflecting the "glass ceiling" for early rap hits on pop radio despite its profound lyrical gravitas and "lusher sonic backdrop." Its influence is evident in covers or interpolations by diverse artists including Duran Duran, Stiff Little Fingers, Depeche Mode, and Puff Daddy. **White Lines (1983)** : This "immortal single," though co-credited to Grandmaster Flash, did not actually involve him and was conceived by Melle Mel and Sylvia Robinson as a new wave crossover hit. Built on a "clever, catchy interpolation" of Liquid Liquid's "Cavern," it was a smash on New York pop and dance stations but only peaked at number 47 on Hot Black singles and entirely missed the Hot 100. "This immortal single co-credited to Grandmaster Flash even though he did not actually take part, did not make the R&B top 40, peaking on Hot Black singles at number 47, and it missed the Hot 100 entirely." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Hit Parade - The Def Jams Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/

**"The Message" - Pop Chart Underperformance** : Despite its monumental influence, "The Message" achieved only modest commercial success on pop radio, peaking at number four on Billboard's R&B chart but a surprisingly low number 62 on the Hot 100 in November 1982. This underperformance underscored the significant "glass ceiling" for rap hits on pop charts during the early 1980s. **"White Lines" - Flash's Non-Participation** : Grandmaster Flash received co-credit for the influential 1983 single "White Lines," even though he "did not actually take part" in its creation. This detail highlights the often complex and sometimes disputed credit attribution practices in early hip-hop productions.
