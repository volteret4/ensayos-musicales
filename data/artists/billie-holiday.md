## songs
**Strange Fruit (1939)** : "Strange Fruit" was a landmark 1939 anti-lynching song that was banned in some places by racists who considered it too incendiary. Activists sent copies to Southern senators who refused to outlaw lynching. It created the template for anti-racism and civil rights protest songs that followed. "Strange Fruit, the 1939 anti-lynching song from Billy Holiday that was banned in some places by racists who considered it to be too incendiary." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dCelQ8Zj_cg ← billie-holiday
**Strange Fruit (1939)** : Holiday's most famous performance, "Strange Fruit," a highly political and inflammatory song about lynching, was rejected by her label, Columbia, and her producer, John Hammond, due to its controversial nature. Due to Columbia's refusal, Billy Holiday turned to Milt Gabler, a politically left-leaning friend and founder of Commodore, who negotiated a special deal with Columbia to produce and release the song on his Commodore label. It sold over a million copies, became arguably the most important recording in music history, and was named "Song of the Century" by Time magazine in December 1999. "That recording sold over a million copies and became arguably the most important recording in music history." ← Episode 4： ＂Choo Choo Ch'Boogie＂ by Louis Jordan | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DFZnwvsXGJM

**High School Portrayal** : Nora Jones portrayed Billie Holiday in a black history program during high school, an experience she described as "amazing." She auditioned by singing "Fidenmello" (likely "Fine and Mellow") and performed "Strange Fruit" for the main show. Nora actively tried to sound like Billie, and the director, Nedra James, provided spiritual guidance on embodying the character's life and story. **Outlier in Malismal Use** : Billie Holiday was one of the few outliers among early 20th-century singers who would use a little malisma to ornament her sound, demonstrating a departure from the widely accepted "one note per syllable" standard, using it more frequently than Ella Fitzgerald. "There were a few outliers. Billy Holiday and Ella Fitzgerald, for example, would both use a little Malisma, Holiday more than Ella, to ornament their sound." ← Episode 32： ＂I Got A Woman＂ by Ray Charles | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0oeI9f2x_fo
**"Strange Fruit" and Political Activism** : "Strange Fruit," a song about lynching, became Billy Holiday's most famous and politically charged performance. Its controversial nature led to its rejection by her main label, Columbia, but its eventual release by Milt Gabler on Commodore Records made it a historic success and a symbol of protest, evidenced by Rebecca Ferguson's attempt to perform it at Donald Trump's inauguration.
