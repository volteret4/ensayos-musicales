**Homework (1997)** : This album, released approximately 20 years before the transcript's context, is considered a major turning point in 90s electronic music and music in general. It is a cult object and revolutionary because it went against the prevailing trend of complex, fast, and genre-blending electronic music of the mid-90s, returning to the slow, simple tempos characteristic of 80s techno. The album drastically reduced electronic music to its minimal expression, utilizing fewer than 30 sounds in its creation. It was initially not conceived for mass consumption, leading Virgin Records to print only 50,000 CD copies, as it was thought to be a niche album for professional rave culture, with the vinyl edition being considered more important. However, it quickly sold 650,000 CDs, exhausting the initial run immediately and prompting massive production. The album lacks a thematic or argumentative thread, being a compilation of material produced by two artists from the rave culture, eventually gathered into two vinyl discs. While its immediate influence in the late 90s and early 2000s was not substantial, as electronic music continued its complex path, its impact became evident around 2007 with the "second wave" of modern electronic music, showing significant influences on subsequent groups. ← Music Radar Clan > Daft Punk： Como el Homework revolucionó la electrónica | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=myhlWJ6DQpw&list=PLBFijugu0fhAAIR-Ibh59aaU8VWg-SkLc ← daft-punk ← daft-punk
