## albums
**Cracked Rear View (1994)** : This album was packed with songs the band had recorded for their independent EPs, including all three future hit singles. Its production was handled by Don Gehman, known for his work with John Mellencamp and R.E.M. Initially, expectations were modest, with Atlantic Records hoping it would sell as well as The J-Hawks' album. Despite internal skepticism and a desire by Atlantic's head of A&R to shelve it as "irreleasable," it was issued in July 1994. After initial slow sales outside the South, it was almost dropped, but a performance on the *Late Show with David Letterman* dramatically boosted its profile. It went gold by mid-December 1994, platinum by February 1995, rose to number one on the Billboard 200 in May 1995 (its 44th week), and went quadruple platinum. By the end of 1995, it was Billboard's number one album of the year with 12 million certified sales, outperforming records by Garth Brooks, Boyz II Men, TLC, and Pearl Jam. As of 1996, it had sold 16 million copies, and with streaming factored in by the late 2010s, it was recertified at 21 times platinum, making it the ninth highest certified album in chart history according to the RIAA. **Kootchippop** : This EP was recorded by the band using money from a small settlement after a record deal with J.R.S. Records fell through in Los Angeles. The title, which the band members now admit was "dopey," was inspired by a term comedian Shirley Hemphill coined for female genitalia in a thong bikini, which "seemed funny at the time." Despite its title, *Kootchippop* sold remarkably well regionally, shifting 25,000 copies across the states where Hootie played clubs, fraternities, and sororities, a stunning figure for a self-released CD. **Hold My Hand (1995)** : This song was the band's first hit, breaking into the top 10 on Billboard's Hot 100 at number 10 in the week ending February 18, 1995, after a slow, steady climb. It kicked off a year of mega-stardom for the band. The song was brought to the band by drummer Jim Sonefeld, who was its primary songwriter and whose previous bands had turned it down. To Sonefeld, the song was about "compassion in a cruel world," while Darius Rucker interpreted it as a protest song about "love in the face of hatred," given his experiences with racial epithets in college. For the re-recording on *Cracked Rear View*, Atlantic Records secured David Crosby to provide backing vocals on the chorus. ## curiosities
**Anti-Nirvana Vibe and Organic Radio Success** : Hootie & the Blowfish were perceived as having an "anti-Nirvana" sound or vibe during their rise in the mid-1990s. This appeal contributed to the organic success of their "I Go Blind" cover on radio, as DJs in cities like Boulder or Denver picked it up and fans requested it widely. **Cover of "I Go Blind" by 5440** : Hootie & the Blowfish covered 5440's song "I Go Blind." Although they had recorded it, it was cut from their first album. It later appeared as a B-side on a cassette single and was licensed for the "Friends" soundtrack. They specifically requested that it not be released as a single from the soundtrack. "They had recorded I Go Blind. It didn't make their first record. It was cut. **No US Airplay Royalties for Cover Songs** : As an American artist playing a cover on American radio, Hootie & the Blowfish did not receive radio airplay royalties for "I Go Blind." These royalties are typically paid only to the composers and publishers of the original song. Their only benefit came from mechanical royalties (sales) of the "Friends" soundtrack or the cassette single. "if you are an American artist on American radio and you're playing a cover, you don't reap any radio airplay royalties from that because it only goes to the composers." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W9gdKha0PLg ← hootie-the-blowfish
**Positive Relationship with 5440** : The band maintained a very positive relationship with 5440 following the success of "I Go Blind."
