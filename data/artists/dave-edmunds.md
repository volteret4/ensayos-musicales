**King of Love (1990)** : In 1990, Dave Edmunds released this song as a 10-inch single. While the A and B sides played at the standard 45 RPM, a unique version of "King of Love" on the A side required the turntable speed to be flipped to 78 RPM, making it one of the last known performances to release a song on a 78 RPM record. "This vinyl record featured that song on the B side and on side A. Both played at 45 RPM, which is exactly the speed they were supposed to play. Looked the record over, and there was another version of King of Love, but this time you had to flip the speed to 78, which you gotta admit, it's kinda cool." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Epz5Sn151bk ← dave-edmunds

## curiosities
**Avatar of Roots-Oriented Rock** : Edmonds quickly became recognized as the "avatar" of the roots-oriented approach to rock music. He was a central figure at the "crux" of the pub rock scene and went on to produce albums for several bands within this scene, including Ducks Deluxe and the American band Eggs Over Easy. **Formation of Rockpile** : He formed an on-again, off-again band called Rockpile with Nick Lowe, named after Edmonds' 1972 LP. While the band played gigs more frequently than they recorded, they were instrumental in infusing retro rock elements into what would eventually become the New Wave sound.
