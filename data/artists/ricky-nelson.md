## songs
**Garden Party (1972)** : Written by Nelson after he was booed off stage at Madison Square Garden in October 1971 for performing country rock instead of his earlier rock and roll hits. The lyrics reflect on the audience's expectation for his 1950s image and music, and his decision to prioritize his own artistic path over pleasing everyone. The song includes lines about playing old songs, not looking the same, and the adage "you can't please everyone so you got to please yourself," as well as a reference to Johnny B. Goode. **Garden Party (1972)** : This top 10 hit, reaching number six in 1972, was Rick Nelson's last major success and was written in a "fit of pique." Despite its mellow sound, it functions as a diss record, expressing Nelson's anger at his audience's poor reception at a nostalgic multi-act concert at New York's Madison Square Garden in the fall of 1971. Nelson, performing as Rick Nelson, felt that the "hippie crowd" sat on their hands and even booed him, leading him to storm off stage and write the song, which contains coded references to other acts like Chuck Berry ("Johnny Be Good") and backstage attendees like John Lennon ("Yoko's Walrus") and George Harrison ("Mr. Hughes"). "It's a catchy song with very petty lyrics. And it was Rick Nelson's last big hit, reaching number 6 in 1972." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Here’s the Beef Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4ef3154465cd600c4ef5

**Booed at Madison Square Garden** : In October 1971, Rick Nelson and his Stone Canyon Band were booed by fans at Madison Square Garden during a "Rock and Roll Spectacular" (renamed from "Rock and Roll Revival"). Fans, who expected his 1950s hits and image from "Ozzie and Harriet," rejected his country rock sound, long hair, boots, and bell-bottom jeans. Nelson believed the booing was directed at him for his changed musical direction and appearance.
