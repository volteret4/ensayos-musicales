**Rio (1977)** : In the summer of 1977, Mike Nesmith filmed a promotional video for this single on a beach in Los Angeles, California. He invested thousands of his own money into the project, convinced there was a market for narrative-driven videos within the music industry. The video deliberately incorporated a narrative, used images to complement the song, and featured innovative editing, making it stand out when broadcast on television music shows. Although the song itself did not achieve the chart success he hoped for, this pioneering video was a crucial step towards a new way of enjoying music and eventually led to the creation of the MTV Network. **Early Video Pioneer** : In the summer of 1977, at 35 years old, Mike Nesmith was actively creating a promotional video for his single "Rio" in Los Angeles, despite encountering snickering and mockery from crew members who deemed it an "absurd project." **Pop Clips Program Concept** : In the summer of 1979, then 37 years old, Mike Nesmith pitched his program idea, "Pop Clips," to Warner Executive John Lack in New York City. The concept was to showcase artists from around the world through their music videos, with the intention that these clips would be introduced by "video jokies" (VJs), mirroring how disc jockeys play music on the radio. Nesmith had been turned down by every other network before John Lack agreed to buy the program for Nickelodeon. "The idea is to showcase artists from around the world via their music videos." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Introducing History Daily - The Launch of MTV | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Creative Differences with John Lack** : While working with John Lack on developing a 24-hour music channel, significant creative differences emerged, particularly regarding the choice of channel anchors. John Lack preferred traditional DJs to introduce the music videos, envisioning established voices that audiences would know and trust. In contrast, Mike Nesmith advocated for a more experimental approach, pushing for new video jokies and edgy stand-up comedians. These disagreements ultimately proved insurmountable, leading the two men to part ways as their artistic sensibilities and commercial ambitions diverged.
