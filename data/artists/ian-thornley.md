## curiosities
**Anecdotes with Ahmet Ertegun** : During Big Wreck's first iteration, Ian Thorneley had experiences with Ahmet Ertegun, co-founder of Atlantic Records, including a dinner at his office where Ertegun heard Big Wreck's music and shared stories about bands like the Rolling Stones and Led Zeppelin. Ertegun was described as a small Turkish man with a perfectly manicured mustache, often accompanied by "Amazonian gorgeous women." "Did you actually go for dinner with Ahmed Erdogan? Yeah, I mean, I was brought up to his office by Craig Calman." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QIA7V4PxCdM ← ian-thorneley
**Connection with Chad Kroeger** : Ian Thorneley has worked extensively with Chad Kroeger of Nickelback, considering him a close friend and family member. However, he acknowledges that Kroeger's successful working methods differ significantly from his own, leading to external pressures from those trying to replicate Kroeger's success. "But having said that, he, he, I, that I've worked a lot with the guy and he's like, he's like a family member and he's a very close guy and very close friend." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QIA7V4PxCdM ← ian-thorneley
**Early Business Exploitation Experience** : During the period with the band Thorneley, Ian encountered individuals in business who attempted to take advantage of him, leading to a significant phone conversation in 2005 or 2006 where his artistic and personal needs were dismissed. This experience taught him about the harsh realities of the music industry. "And the quote was, I don't give a fuck about your child." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QIA7V4PxCdM ← ian-thorneley
**Financial Worries and Motivation** : Despite his success, Ian Thorneley consistently experiences financial worries, especially with family responsibilities. This serves as a significant self-motivating mechanism, extending beyond purely artistic concerns to the practical needs of supporting his family. "I have, I have 11 month old at home, you know, and he's, he eats a lot of food. He eats more than I do." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QIA7V4PxCdM ← ian-thorneley
**First Velvet Revolver Consideration (Pre-Weiland)** : Before Scott Weiland joined Velvet Revolver, Ian Thorneley was considered as a potential singer. He received a CD with three instrumental tracks to write over, but he didn't pay much attention to it as his first Thorneley record was about to be released. "I remember getting a CD right before the first Thorneley record came out. I got a CD with three cuts on it, just instrumental tracks." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QIA7V4PxCdM ← ian-thorneley
**Live Wardrobe Malfunction** : During a Thorneley tour show in Edmonton or Calgary, possibly on Halloween, the power repeatedly went out due to running too many amps. While Ian was engaging the audience with jumps and antics, his pants split open in the crotch, revealing his anatomy due to not wearing underwear. His guitar tech, Shayne, attempted to tape him up, and a crew member named Timmy retrieved new pants, allowing him to finish the show. "And during one of these jumps, my pants split open in the crotchal area. And these days I didn't wear underwear." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QIA7V4PxCdM ← ian-thorneley
**Post-Big Wreck Version 1.0 Career Transition** : After Big Wreck's initial disbandment around 2001-2002, Ian Thorneley moved back to Toronto and found work as a session player. He contributed to albums by artists such as Nickelback, Sarah Harmer, and Stephen Fearing. "After big rec version 1.0 broke up, Ian moved back to Toronto where he found work as a session player." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QIA7V4PxCdM ← ian-thorneley
**Protecting Artistic Vision** : Ian Thorneley demonstrated a strong conviction in protecting his artistic output, which he likened to protecting his child. This conviction allowed him to resist manipulation and commercial pressures, distinguishing him from many musicians who might compromise for a living. "Now, what's, what I'm finding interesting is that this is your child, your baby. Yeah. And the conviction that you have in protecting your baby is not something that you see in a lot of musicians..." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QIA7V4PxCdM ← ian-thorneley
**Reason for Rejecting Velvet Revolver Frontman Role** : Despite the musical chemistry and positive vibe during rehearsals, Ian Thorneley ultimately did not join Velvet Revolver. The band sought a "peacock" frontman in the vein of David Lee Roth or Scott Weiland, a role that didn't align with Ian's preference to play guitar on stage. "I'm not that comfortable doing the whole, you know, front guy tambourine thing. It's just not for me." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QIA7V4PxCdM ← ian-thorneley
**Second Velvet Revolver Consideration and Rehearsals (Post-Weiland)** : After Scott Weiland's departure, Ian was again approached by Velvet Revolver. Slash himself called Ian to send over material, and Ian subsequently rehearsed with the band in Los Angeles, experiencing Slash's full touring rig and observing the band members as "real dudes." "The phone call came from slash. I was driving back from a gig or something. Oh, the phone rings and it's slash." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QIA7V4PxCdM ← ian-thorneley
**Shared "Guitarist First" Affliction with Miles Kennedy** : Ian Thorneley notes a long-standing connection with Miles Kennedy, lead singer for Slash's solo band and Alter Bridge. They share the characteristic of being "guitar players first and singers second," although Kennedy has since embraced the frontman role without a guitar. "Oddly enough, like Miles, who sings with slash all the time, the, the mild and I go way back and, and we suffer from the same affliction where guitar players first and singers second." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QIA7V4PxCdM ← ian-thorneley
**Berklee College of Music Admission** : Ian Thornley was admitted to the Berklee School of Music in Boston after submitting a 4-track demo featuring his compositions and various instrumental parts. His prior musical training, including piano studies with jazz musician Darwin Grosse, contributed to his foundational understanding of harmony and theory, which likely aided his admission. "I just, I had put together, you know, I had a little foretrack thing and I had written a bunch of compositions and, and don't know all these parts, put them all together and just sent those down." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z9QChV543aQ ← ian-thornley ← ian-thornley
**Berklee Experience and Departure** : Ian attended Berklee for three years but did not graduate. He realized that pursuing a degree often led to careers in teaching or session work involving chart reading, which didn't align with his aspirations for creating his own music. By his third year, he focused on taking courses with professors from whom he could gain practical insights. "I decided after, I think after two years, I had decided like I don't want to really, I'm not here to graduate." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z9QChV543aQ ← ian-thornley ← ian-thornley
**Cabbagetown Gig Experience** : As a 13-year-old, Ian Thornley filled in as a keyboard player for a band with a singer named Chrissy (a cousin). The band needed a replacement keyboardist for a gig at the Cabbagetown band after their previous player bailed. Despite his young age, he looked the part and managed to play the "pretty straightforward music" effectively. "I used an ironing board as a keyboard stand." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z9QChV543aQ ← ian-thornley ← ian-thornley
**Country Music Gig** : After his student visa ran out, Ian was denied entry into the US and got a gig as a country guitar player for a few months in Canada. While he found the experience "great" in terms of general musicianship, he quickly realized that country guitar was "a different language" and not his forte, despite being an accomplished guitarist in other genres. "I am not a country guitarist." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z9QChV543aQ ← ian-thornley ← ian-thornley
**Early Electric Guitar Influences** : Ian Thornley quickly acquired an electric guitar, a delay pedal, and a whammy bar, eager to emulate his influences. He was particularly obsessed with Bruce Cockburn's song "If a Tree Falls" and also practiced U2's music, using the gear to achieve similar sounds. "I was obsessed with if a tree falls or, yeah, if a tree falls. And that that, he used the whammy bar and the delay pedal." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z9QChV543aQ ← ian-thornley ← ian-thornley
**Early Musical Obsession** : Ian Thornley became obsessed with music from a very young age, starting around four or five years old. He began his musical journey on piano after a family friend had one, showing him a few things that he quickly picked up. His mother, who was also very musical and wanted to learn piano, decided they would both take lessons together around the age of five. "I've been obsessed with music from a very young age, from about four or five." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z9QChV543aQ ← ian-thornley ← ian-thornley
**First Recording Experience - Phase One** : As a teenage keyboardist in the band Tula, Ian went to Phase One studio to record his synth parts. This was an eye-opening and significant experience for him. "Went up to phase one and recorded and did on the synth parts." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z9QChV543aQ ← ian-thornley ← ian-thornley
**Guitar Inspiration from Dan** : While working a summer job at Sounds Interchange in the dub room, Ian befriended Dan, a talented guitar player. Dan played Zappa riffs and songs like Led Zeppelin's "Stairway to Heaven," which deeply impressed Ian and ignited his passion for the guitar. This experience made him realize he could recreate the music he loved with accuracy, leading him to fully embrace the instrument. "once I figured out that correlation of how it came pretty quickly, came pretty easily, but I think the real bug was being able to recreate things that I'd heard and things that I loved." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z9QChV543aQ ← ian-thornley ← ian-thornley
**Roxbury Living Experience** : While attending Berklee, Ian moved into the old Tremont Towers in Roxbury, Boston, with Brian Doherty and Dave Henning. This was an eye-opening and "scary" experience for a kid from Toronto, as they were living in a rough neighborhood. He quickly learned to "shut up, keep my head down and get where I was going, especially if it was dark." "it was scary is what it was. It was scary." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z9QChV543aQ ← ian-thornley ← ian-thornley
**Teenage Keyboard Whiz** : Up until about age 14, Ian played piano and synthesizers in pop bands around Toronto. He used a Juno 60 or 106, a Roland, and a Korg, and even an ironing board as a keyboard stand for gigs. He was considered a "whiz kid, keyboard kid" and would sometimes open sets with Bach's Toccata and Fugue in D minor before transitioning to a reggae vibe. "I was a synth guy." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z9QChV543aQ ← ian-thornley ← ian-thornley
**Transition from Keyboards to Guitar** : Ian stopped playing keyboards and piano after his mother became sick and subsequently passed away, a correlation he realized much later. For about a year and a half to two years, he gravitated towards athletics, cycling, and basketball. His father bought him an acoustic guitar, which he initially put in a closet after learning only a couple of chords. "that's really when I stopped playing keyboards and piano and all that." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=z9QChV543aQ ← ian-thornley ← ian-thornley
