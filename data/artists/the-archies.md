**Sugar Sugar (1969)** : This song, co-written by Jeff Barry and Andy Kim, was rejected by The Monkees, leading Don Kirshner to create The Archies as a fictional band to record it. Tony Dante sang all vocal parts, with his voice multi-tracked to create the illusion of a full band. Released around Memorial Day 1969, it initially struggled for radio play until Kirshner convinced a DJ in Texas to spin it, leading to a surge of requests. The song rapidly climbed the Billboard Hot 100, reaching number 41 in early August, then 24, 14, and finally number 3 by the end of August 1969, where it was behind Johnny Cash's "A Boy Named Sue" and The Rolling Stones' "Honky Tonk Women." "Sugar Sugar" then unseated "Honky Tonk Women" from the number one spot and remained there for a month, famously preventing Creedence Clearwater Revival's "Green River" from reaching number one. This track is described as having a good hook, simple lyrics, and a syncopated rhythm, and is noted for molding the taste of a generation of preteens. **Sugar Sugar (1969)** : This song was the Billboard year-end number one for 1969, by "The Archies," who were fictional comic book characters from CBS's "The Archie Show." The band itself was composed of real-life session musicians created to promote the cartoon. It became a massive hit, topping the Hot 100 for four weeks and selling 3 million copies in 1969 alone, with these sales helping it secure the year-end title over strong contenders.
