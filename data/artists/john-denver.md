**Take Me Home, Country Roads (1971)** : This song, recorded in the winter of 1971, moved Denver closer to country music, despite being a pop smash that reached number two on Billboard's Hot 100 in the summer of 1971. Its origin traces to December 1970 when Denver met Bill Danoff and Taffy Nivert (Fat City) in D.C., and the trio collaboratively wrote its bridge in an all-night session. They debuted it to a five-minute standing ovation at D.C.'s Cellar Door Club. Released as a single in the spring of 1971, it debuted at number 99 and took over four months to reach number two, prevented from reaching number one by the Bee Gees' "How Can You Mend a Broken Heart." This song established John Denver as a solo artist and defined his persona as earnest, folky, and homespun with a talent for melody. Surprisingly, it only reached number 50 on the Hot Country Singles chart, as the country audience was not initially very interested.
