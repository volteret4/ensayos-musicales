## songs
**Sun City (1985)** : Steve Van Zandt (Bruce Springsteen's guitarist) was disgusted that performers including the Beach Boys, Cher, Rod Stewart, Kenny Rogers, Elton John, and Queen were accepting money to perform at Sun City, a whites-only resort in the Bantustan of Bophuthatswana, South Africa, effectively breaking the international boycott against apartheid. Together with producer Arthur Baker (a favorite of New Order), he created "Sun City" under the name Artists United Against Apartheid. Participants from rock included Bono, Bob Dylan, Peter Gabriel, Ringo Starr, Lou Reed, Bob Geldof, Pat Benatar, Clarence Clemons, Jackson Browne, Keith Richards, Ronnie Wood, Joey Ramone, and Peter Garrett of Midnight Oil. Hip-hop contributors included Run DMC, African Bambaata, DJ Cool Herc, Grandmaster Melle Mel, Curtis Blow, and the Fat Boys. Also contributing were Jimmy Cliff, George Clinton, Herbie Hancock, Bonnie Raitt, Hall and Oates, Gil Scott-Heron, and Miles Davis. The single premiered at the United Nations, raised over a million dollars for anti-apartheid efforts, and was a big hit in Canada, Australia, the Netherlands, and the UK. In America it reached only number 38, with many radio stations refusing to play it due to its criticism of President Reagan's "constructive engagement" policy toward South Africa. **Sun City (1985)** : This critically acclaimed multi-artist charity record was released towards the end of 1985, spearheaded by Little Steven. It served as a symbolic protest against South African ethnic separatism (apartheid) and specifically aimed to make it socially unacceptable for Western artists to perform in Sun City, a segregated resort in Baputaswana. The song featured an intriguing and diverse array of music stars, including rappers, punks, and jazz greats, along with artists who had previously participated in first-wave charity singles like Bruce Springsteen, Hall & Oates, Bob Dylan, and Bono. Despite only reaching number 38 on the Hot 100 and being largely ignored by pop radio programmers, it achieved strong MTV play and was hailed by critics, topping the 1985 Village Voice Pazz & Jop Poll. Its fundamental mission of making Sun City an unacceptable performance venue was considered a success. ## curiosities
**Anti-Apartheid Mission** : The project "Artists United Against Apartheid" and its song "Sun City" were created as a direct protest against South African ethnic separatism. More specifically, it aimed to draw a clear line against Western artists performing in Sun City, which was a segregated resort located in Baputaswana. "On a narrower scale, it drew a line in the sand against Western artists performing in Sun City, a segregated resort in Baputaswana." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Hit Parade - The Charity Megasingle Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Bono's Politically Pointed Solo** : In "Sun City," Bono delivered a "politically pointed, rageful solo showcase." This performance was seen as an attempt to "neutralize his infamous thank god it's them star-turn" from the earlier Band-Aid single, showcasing a different artistic and political stance. **Critical Acclaim and Mission Success** : "Sun City" was widely hailed by critics and tastemakers, topping the 1985 Village Voice Pazz & Jop Poll and having its accompanying album place in the poll's top five. Little Steven later pointed out that the song succeeded in its fundamental mission of making it socially unacceptable for artists to perform in Sun City, despite its modest commercial chart performance.
