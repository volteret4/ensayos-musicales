**Rapper's Delight (1979)** : This song became the first mainstream hip hop hit, marking the beginning of hip hop's dominance in popular music. Kenny Arnold, an Iowa Hawkeyes basketball player, was known to play the song on repeat for hours in 1980. The track was essentially rapping over the bassline of Chic's "Good Times." While the quick wordplay was known in live rap performances, this was unprecedented in mainstream music. The rapping is described as tame compared to later hip hop. The three members, initially naive to the music industry, were recruited by Sylvia Robinson, who found Big Hank at Krispy Krust Pizza and the others by driving around asking rappers to record over "Good Times." The song was recorded in 14 minutes. Sylvia Robinson mailed records to black radio stations and pushed for airplay, which led to listener demand and the birth of the hip hop industry. Music writer Eric Reese stated that "Rapper's Delight" was a "tear in the veil" and a "standard" for hip hop's success when companies were hesitant to finance rap artists or labels. It broke financial barriers and inspired rappers to develop their own styles. "Now what you hear is not a chest on the rapid to the beat. **"X Factor Style" Formation** : The Sugar Hill Gang was a prefabricated rap collective of three men from Englewood, New Jersey, assembled by soul singer and budding mogul Sylvia Robinson. Signed to her Sugar Hill Records label, the group consisted of Michael "Wonder Mike" Wright, Henry "Big Bank Hank" Jackson, and Guy "Master G" O'Brien. **Lifting Rhymes and Baselines** : "Rapper's Delight" is infamous for incorporating rhymes from contemporary rapper Grandmaster Kaz and its hook from Chic's 1979 hit "Good Times." Although the baseline was replayed rather than directly sampled, its clear debt to Chic's Bernard Edwards resulted in him and Nile Rodgers being co-credited as songwriters within months of its release. **Melody Debate in Early Rap** : The Sugar Hill Gang's "Rapper's Delight" had plenty of actual music supporting the rapping, including a stolen bass line from Chic's "Good Times." However, the Sugar Hill team wanted the rap to be noticed as distinct from the music, essentially baking the debate over whether rappers should be melodic right into the genre's first hit. **"Good Times" Bass Line Recreation** : At the insistence of Sugar Hill Records mogul Sylvia Robinson, the "Good Times" bass hook was meticulously replayed by a studio bassist (either Bernard Roland or Chip Sheeran) to provide a sturdy backdrop for the MCs' rhymes, indicating a deliberate effort to recreate the original. This act later led to Bernard Edwards and Nile Rogers receiving writing credits and royalties.
