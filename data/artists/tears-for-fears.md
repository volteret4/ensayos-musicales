## songs
**Change (1983)** : This song, part of Tears for Fears' initial "icy but catchy synth pop" output, was a top 10 UK hit but only climbed to number 73 in America. "They had launched with IC but catchy synth pop in 1983 and generated a string of top 10 UK hits. But in America Tears for Fears couldn't climb higher than number 73 with 1983's Change." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The British Are Charting Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f7a154465cd600c7ae7
**Everybody Wants to Rule the World (1985)** : Roland Orzabal and his co-writers deliberately aimed this song "squarely for the American market," with singer Curt Smith calling it "Drive Music." It was a more upbeat take on themes of global power, reminiscent of Eurythmics' "Sweet Dreams," and resulted in another number one hit for the duo. "Two years later, Roland Orzabal and his co-writers aimed squarely for the American market with a song that singer Kurt Smith called Drive Music." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The British Are Charting Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f7a154465cd600c7ae7
**Mad World (1982)** : This single was released in Fall 1982 (September) and became an "across the board hit," popular in new wave, alternative rock, and pop genres. Roland Orzabal wrote the song, with Curt Smith, the bass player, performing the vocals. "A single called Mad World was released the previous fall. This would be September 1982. And it became an across the board hit." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yuA9Xor8vfw ← tears-for-fears
**Mad World (1982)** : Tears for Fears' 1982 single, which featured lyrically dark themes but was characterized by pinging synthesizers and a driving, "pepier" tempo. Twenty years later, it was famously covered by Michael Andrews and Gary Jules for the "Donnie Darko" soundtrack, reimagined as a spooky, slowed-down "bummer cover." ## curiosities
**BBC Archive Recordings (Multiple Years)** : Tears for Fears is mentioned as one of the many artists who made recordings for the BBC. These sessions were crucial for artists to gain airplay during periods when the BBC had "Needle Time" restrictions, which limited the broadcast of commercially released music. "Even from the Beatles to Bob Barley to Joy Division to Tears for Fiers and literally thousands of other artists were invited to make records at the BBC so they could get around the whole Needle Time thing." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ak6OuOUkRrQ ← tears-for-fears ← tears-for-fears
**Influence of Arthur Janov's Primal Scream Therapy** : Roland Orzabal was deeply interested in psychology and psychiatry at the time, particularly fascinated by the teachings of Arthur Janov, the developer of primal scream therapy. This therapeutic approach encourages patients to delve deep into their psyches to uncover the root of their issues, and was also embraced by figures like John Lennon and Yoko Ono. "Roland was deep into psychology and psychiatry at the time. He was particularly fascinated by the teachings of Arthur Janov, the guy behind primal screen therapy." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yuA9Xor8vfw ← tears-for-fears
**Saluted on Teal Album** : Tears for Fears was among the artists whose songs were saluted (covered) on Weezer's Teal Album. "So Tears for Fierce." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=LYRPxtP61JM ← tears-for-fears
**Targeting the American Market** : After their earlier struggles to chart high in the US, Tears for Fears deliberately composed "Everybody Wants to Rule the World" to appeal to the American market. "Two years later, Roland Orzabal and his co-writers aimed squarely for the American market with a song that singer Kurt Smith called Drive Music." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The British Are Charting Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f7a154465cd600c7ae7
