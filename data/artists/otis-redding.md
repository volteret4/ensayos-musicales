## songs
**Respect (1965)** : The original version of "Respect" by Otis Redding, released prior to Aretha Franklin's more famous cover. Redding's version was itself a top 40 hit, though it is not as widely recognized today as Franklin's reinterpretation. "his prior version was even a top 40 hit, but it's not remotely as famous today as the reboot." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Gotcha Covered Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f1d64fe6d21276e0fab
**Respect (1965)** : Otis Redding wrote and recorded this song in Muscle Shoals in 1965. His version portrayed a man demanding deference and dignity from his partner after a long day of work and enduring life's indignities. It went top five on Billboard's R&B chart in 1965 and cracked the pop top 40. "Written by Otis Redding and recorded by him in Muscle Shoals in 1965, respect was about a man demanding deference and dignity from his partner when he gets home from work after a long day of earning a living and enduring life's indignities." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Say a Little Prayer Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4eef8e6dd12efb02c3c9

## curiosities
**B-Side Hit** : Otis Redding's first release on Stax was initially unimpressive to Jim Stewart, who considered the A-side "a fun but unoriginal little Richard sound alike." However, DJs soon flipped the record, and the B-side became a hit, launching Redding's legendary career. "But soon DJs flipped the record, and it was the B side that became the hit. The Otis Redding would never again be thought of as just Johnny Jenkins's driver" ← Episode 105： ＂Green Onions＂ by Booker T.and the MGs | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cw6xJG8E_w0
**Influence on Early Tragically Hip** : The Tragically Hip's early setlists largely consisted of blues rock standards, including material from Otis Redding. "Early stuff from the stones, material from the doors, the yard birds, Marvin Gaye, Otis Redding, Ben Morrison, that kind of thing." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dnz_Ycd_q7s ← otis-redding
**Plane Crash Death (1967)** : Otis Redding died on December 10, 1967, when his small plane crashed into Lake Monoma near Madison, Wisconsin. "Otis Redding died on December 10th, 1967, when his small plane crashed into Lake Monoma near Madison, Wisconsin." ← https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3eGBnwPWRGo ← otis-redding
**The "Driver" Who Became a Star** : Otis Redding initially arrived at Stax as Johnny Jenkins's driver, not as a scheduled artist. Due to a favor owed by Jim Stewart to promoter Joe Galkin, Redding was given an impromptu recording opportunity. "Nobody was interested, but Galkin wore Jim Stewart down, and he agreed to listen to this person who he just thought of as Johnny Jenkins's driver." ← Episode 105： ＂Green Onions＂ by Booker T.and the MGs | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cw6xJG8E_w0
**Aretha Franklin's Cover** : Otis Redding famously remarked live on stage that Aretha Franklin "took away from him and made permanently hers" with her transformative 1967 cover of "Respect." Franklin's version flipped the gender and added new sections, turning it into a feminist anthem. "It was, as Redding himself would later say, live on stage, the song Aritha Franklin took away from him and made permanently hers." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Say a Little Prayer Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4eef8e6dd12efb02c3c9
**Muscle Shoals Connection** : Otis Redding was one of the artists recording with the Muscle Shoals Rhythm section for Atlantic Records by the mid-1960s, solidifying the studio's reputation for soulful recordings. "By the mid-60s, Atlantic had a lot of artists recording with the Muscle Shoals Rhythm section, from Pickett to Percy Sledge to Otis Redding." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Say a Little Prayer Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4eef8e6dd12efb02c3c9
