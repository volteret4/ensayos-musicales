**Come on Feel the Noize (1973)** : This March 1973 UK number one smash was a loud, brash, and catchy glam rock anthem. Slade were known for misspelling their song titles, and this one was spelled "Cum On Feel The Noize" (C-U-M, Noize with a Z). It was later covered by the 80s hair metal band Quiet Riot, making it familiar to some American audiences. **Merry Xmas Everybody (1973)** : This glam rock single was released in 1973 simultaneously with Wizzard's "I Wish It Could Be Christmas Every Day," igniting a head-to-head competition for the UK Christmas Number One. Slade's single won, topping the UK chart and officially becoming the UK Christmas Number One for that year. **Merry Xmas Everybody (1973)** : This holiday classic reached the top of the United Kingdom's official charts company Singles Chart on December 15, 1973, becoming a quintessentially British hit. The song's origin stems from bassist Jimmy Lea being challenged by his mother-in-law in 1973 to write a song that sold better than Bing Crosby's "White Christmas." Lea combined a verse melody from his shower with an old chorus melody vocalist Noddy Holder had from 1967, and Holder completed the lyrics with a hopeful message for recession-hit Britain. Despite being a Christmas song, it lacks traditional Christmas sonic signifiers like chiming bells or children's choruses, and notably ends with a heavy metal scream. It debuted at number one and outsold Wizzard's competing Christmas single by hundreds of thousands of copies, making it a perennial UK holiday classic that has never appeared on the US Hot 100. "Merry Christmas everybody. debuted on the UK chart all the way up at number one." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Christmas is All Around Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Squeeze Me, Please Me (1973)** : A number one follow-up to "Come On Feel the Noize" in 1973, this song was described as possibly even louder. Consistent with Slade's quirky style, half of its title was misspelled with K's, double U's, and Z's, similar to their previous hit. **US Market Performance** : Slade never broke big in America, with their only top 20 hit being "Run Run Away" in 1984. In contrast, they were megastars in their home country a decade earlier during the height of glam rock, scoring half a dozen UK number one hits from 1971 to 1973. "They never broke big here." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Christmas is All Around Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Song Title Misspellings** : Slade frequently misspelled the titles of their songs, a characteristic element of their identity. Examples include "Cum On Feel The Noize" (with C-U-M and a Z for noise) and "Squeeze Me, Please Me" (with K's, double U's, and Z's). This practice was also applied to their Christmas hit, "Merry Xmas Everybody." "Slayed like to misspell the titles to their songs." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Christmas is All Around Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Origin of Merry Xmas Everybody** : The band's creation of "Merry Xmas Everybody" was surprisingly indirect, sparked by bassist Jimmy Lea's mother-in-law in 1973 who sarcastically suggested he write something like Bing Crosby's "White Christmas" because it sold better than any of Slade's hits. Annoyed, Lea combined existing melodic ideas with Noddy Holder's lyrics, which aimed to uplift Britain amidst a recession and miner strike. "if one band member's mother-in-law hadn't said something snippy, they might never have written Merry Christmas everybody." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Christmas is All Around Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
