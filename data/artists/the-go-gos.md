**We Got the Beat (1980)** : This song, The Go-Go's biggest hit, spent three weeks at number 2 on the Hot 100 in April 1982. Written by Charlotte Caffey, it was inspired by the opening beat of The Miracles' 1966 hit "Going to a Go-Go," a song that had also inspired The Go-Go's name and that they had considered covering. The original 1980 version, issued as a single by UK pub and punk rock label Stiff Records, brought the band underground credibility in the UK and became a dance hit in America as an import, reaching number 35 on Billboard's Club Play Chart in the summer of 1980. Its rousing live performance clip received frequent rotation on MTV, contributing to its chart inevitability. The song peaked behind Joan Jett's "I Love Rock 'n Roll." "That's the Go-Gos, the five woman rock group from Los Angeles, California, with their smash We Got The Beat. Their biggest hit spent three weeks at number 2 in April of 1982." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Hit Parade - The Silver Medalists Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/

**"Our Lips Are Sealed" Music Video** : The music video for "Our Lips Are Sealed" was a low-budget affair, featuring The Go-Go's cruising through Beverly Hills in a red 1960 Buick LeSabre convertible (rented or bought from "Rinterrek"). The video, which showed them stopping at iconic LA spots and jumping into the Electric Fountain at Wilshire and Santa Monica, effectively portrayed a vibrant, young Gen Xer lifestyle in Los Angeles. "The video for our lips are sealed. It was really simple. There was no high budget special effects or anything like that. It was just the gogos cruising around Beverly Hills in a red 1960 Buick with Sabre convertible that they took or bought from Rinterrek because they wanted something that was older, something that was fun to ride around in for the shoot." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 58 - WKRP in Cincinnati | https://www.ftr70.com

**Classification Debate** : Despite being an all-female band with harmonized vocals and retro influences, music historians typically do not classify The Go-Go's as a traditional "girl group." Emerging from the late 1970s LA punk scene, they played their own instruments and wrote their own songs, striving for respect as a rock band among their male peers. They preferred to be known as a rock band that happened to be all women rather than a "girl group." "But music historians generally don't classify the Go-Go's, who emerged from the late 70's LA punk scene and fought to earn the respect of their male peers as a girl group of the likes of the charelles or the Shangra-Las." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Be My Baby-Baby-Baby Edition Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
