## albums
**Catch Bull at Four (1972)** : Released in November 1972, this album topped the chart, becoming Cat Stevens' only number one LP, despite its material being described as pleasant but "not as memorable" as its predecessors. It was named after one of Buddhism's ten bulls of Zen and featured Asian imagery on its LP. Its piano-based song "Sitting" stalled at number 16 on the Hot 100, and another single, "Can't Keep It In," did not chart at all. By 1972, Cat Stevens had reportedly run out of his backlog of folk pop material and wrote these songs "from the road." It ultimately sold about one-third as much as *T for the Tillerman* and *Teaser and the Firecat*. "But Katz Stevens was clearly on an unstoppable hot streak. **T for the Tillerman** : This album was Cat Stevens' first US top 10 album, reaching number eight in April 1971. Its success established his introspective folkpop style in America and prompted him to quickly record a similarly styled follow-up. "Wild World came from T for the Tillerman, Kat Stevens' first US top 10 album. It reached number 8 in April 1971." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The ACDC Rule, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Teaser and the Firecat (1971)** : Released just six months after its predecessor *T for the Tillerman* peaked, this album featured a whimsical cartoon-like cover and gentle folkpop melodies, consciously designed to look and sound like a companion album. It was packed with hits, including "Peace Train" (a number 7 hit) and "Morning Has Broken" (a number 6 hit, an adaptation of an early 20th-century Christian hymn), helping it reach number 2 on Billboard's album chart in December 1971. This album is certified triple platinum. "Peace Train came from teaser and the firecat, released just six months after its predecessor peaked.
