## songs
**Spinning Wheel (1969)** : This song was written by David Clayton Thomas two years before he joined Blood, Sweat & Tears, and was initially rejected in his native Canada for being "too jazzy" and hard to sell. It became a perfect fit for the band, noted for its "horns forward jazz" style, and was one of the biggest hits of 1969. The lyrics discuss letting go of troubles, not conforming, and allude to the 1960s psychedelic movement, without being overly intellectual or vacuous. **Band's Musical Identity** : Al Kooper originally created the band with a vision of it being a rock band, but other members perceived it as a jazz band. **Controversial Cold War Tour (1970)** : In the summer of 1970, during the height of the Cold War, the band embarked on a tour of communist countries including Poland, Romania, and Yugoslavia, after the US State Department offered Canadian David Clayton Thomas permanent residency in the United States in exchange for their participation. The band was surprised by censorship and directives to play more jazz and less "rhythm" because it was deemed to be "inciting rioting." This chart-topping status made them one of the highest paid acts on the festival bill. **Omission from Woodstock Film and Soundtrack** : The band was notably absent from both the Woodstock film and its accompanying soundtrack album. Their manager refused to allow them to be filmed unless they were paid in advance, leading to their exclusion.
