**You're So Vain (1972)** : This song, released in 1972, became one of pop's most famous and best diss records, rising to number one in January 1973. Carly Simon has maintained mystery about the full identity of the "you" it targets, though she clarified it's not about ex-husband James Taylor or Mick Jagger (who sings backup). She later revealed that the middle verse, describing the vain man taking advantage of a naive singer, is about actor Warren Beatty. The song's genius lies in its meta-diss nature, explicitly stating, "I bet you think this song is about you," and its immense popularity, sending her album "No Secrets" to number one. ## curiosities
**Mystery of "You're So Vain" Target** : Carly Simon has famously kept the full identity of the "you" in her song "You're So Vain" a closely guarded secret for over half a century. While she's ruled out James Taylor and Mick Jagger, she did reveal that one verse refers to Warren Beatty, but not the entire song. The executive who paid $50,000 at a charity auction to learn the secret was sworn to secrecy.
