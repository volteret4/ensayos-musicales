**24K Magic (2016)** : Bruno Mars's third album, released a year after "Uptown Funk" completed its chart run, deepened his exploration of 80s electro-R&B tropes and featured boastful lyrics reminiscent of West Coast hip-hop. Despite its lead single charting slightly lower than previous debuts, the album proved to be a mainstay, lodged in the top 10 for months, and ultimately won Album of the Year at the 2018 Grammys, seen by some as a comprehensive homage to the 80s black boy band New Edition. **Uptown Funk (2014)** : A mega-blockbuster hit officially credited to Mark Ronson featuring Bruno Mars, who was acknowledged as the song's "animating force." The song began with Mars's improvisation in an LA studio, involved extensive sessions across studios in six cities (Memphis, New York, London, Vancouver, etc.), featured three different horn sections (including Mars's band The Hooligans and Mark Ronson collaborators The Dap-Kings), required over 100 takes, and famously had Philip Lawrence singing the bass line due to recording difficulties. Released in November 2014, critics lauded its recreation of turn-of-the-80s electro-funk and R&B, noting numerous references to artists like James Brown, The Sequence, Kool & The Gang, George Kranz, Rick James, George Clinton, Zapp (featuring Roger Troutman), and The Gap Band, who successfully sued for a belated songwriting credit due to similarities with their 1979 hit "Oops Up Side Your Head." It spent 14 weeks at number one on the Hot 100 in early 2015, becoming the second longest run in chart history at the time, and earned the Grammy for Record of the Year.
