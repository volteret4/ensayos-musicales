# instrument - CB Radio

## curiosities
**1976 Craze** : America in 1976 experienced a widespread craze for Citizens Band (CB) radio, which originated from truckers using them during the 1973 oil crisis to communicate about gas, speed traps, and for general conversation to combat monotony. **FCC Regulations and Overload** : CB radios operated on airwaves with strict rules, including not using real names and especially no swearing, which the FCC enforced rigidly. At one point, about half a million people applied for a CB license with the FCC every month, overwhelming the agency to the point where they ceased trying to keep up with applications. "At one point about half a million people applied for a license with the FCC every month. **Transition to Mainstream Use** : Initially a vital tool for truck drivers, CB radios rapidly moved into family station wagons, allowing parents to communicate about "smokies" (police), gas, or simply to join in the fun. Their popularity stemmed from the appeal of 24/7 communication, entertainment, and information, similar to modern pocket computers. **Trucker Culture and Specialized Lingo** : Citizen's Band (CB) radios were a staple for truckers, leading to the development of a unique subculture and an extensive, specialized language. This lingo included "handles" (nicknames like "Rubber Duck" or "Big Ben"), 10-codes for communication, specific terms like "Shakey Town" for Los Angeles due to earthquakes, "Smokies" for state troopers resembling cartoon bears, and "double nickels" for the disliked 55 mph speed limit. This coded communication allowed truckers to warn each other and coordinate actions. "Truckers constructed a whole culture around CB radios... Truckers developed an entire CB specific language." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Decoder Ring - We Got Ourselves a Convoy | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Coordination of Resistance Against Speed Limits** : Truckers effectively utilized their CB radios to organize resistance against the 55 mph national speed limit. They broadcast warnings about upcoming speed traps and formed "convoys"—tightly packed groups of trucks—to collectively drive faster, with interior trucks shielded from detection. This transformed the CB radio into a tool with significant political implications. "Speaking in this code, truckers used their radios to coordinate resistance to the double nickel speed limit. **"Convoy"-Induced CB Radio Craze** : The immense success of CW McCall's song "Convoy" ignited a widespread CB radio craze, causing sales to skyrocket not only among truckers but also within the general public, who were captivated by the song and its unique terminology. By 1979, projections indicated that approximately half of all cars and trucks on national highways would be equipped with CB units. "The initial success of Convoy went way beyond record sales. **CW McCall Signature CB Model** : In the year following the release of "Convoy," between 400,000 and 500,000 new CB radios were sold each month. Among these was a specific CW McCall signature model, highlighting the extensive reach and commercial impact of the craze.
