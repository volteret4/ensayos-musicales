# instrument - Vocoder

## curiosities
**First Use by Kraftwerk on Autobahn** : The vocoder was included for the first time by Kraftwerk on their album "Autobahn," specifically for processing voices. This marked a significant step in their evolution towards a fully electronic sound and a departure from traditional vocal techniques. "incluyeron por primera vez un poco de er para procesar las voces." ← Por qué KRAFTWERK es la banda más influyente de la historia？ | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wWOBez9Cqhs
**Vocals and Sound Effects (Late 1990s)** : The vocoder was a common studio effect utilized in late 1990s Eurodance. It served to "smurf" (pitch-shift) vocals, generating distinctive sonic textures, and was often paired with flanger effects for added impact. "Y las canciones se llenaron de subidones y efectos de estudio como el flanger o el bocoder para pitufar las voces." ← La gran mentira detrás del Eurodance | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=_F34L8wfrdo
**Usage in Country/Pop Crossover** : Vocoder effects are used in Kacey Musgraves' album "Golden Hour," which is fundamentally a country album but incorporates pop and Americana elements, including influences reminiscent of Daft Punk. This demonstrates the instrument's role in cross-genre musical integration. **Afrika Bambaataa's "Planet Rock" Use** : The vocoder was used to filter Afrika Bambaataa's "behind the beat rapping" on the seminal Electro track "Planet Rock," contributing to its innovative sound and impact on 1980s R&B, rap, and dance production. "behind the beat rapping by Bombata filtered through a vocoder." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Point of No Return Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Early Rap Era Voice Synthesizer** : At the dawn of the rap era in the 1980s, the vocoder, an early voice synthesizer, was employed by electro-funk groups like Zapp on hits such as "More Bounce to the Ounce," in a fashion similar to T-Pain's later use of auto-tune. "in the 80s at the dawn of the rap era, the Vicoder, an early voice synthesizer, was employed in a fashion very similar to T-Pains by the electro-func group Zap on hits like more bounce to the outs." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Say My Name, Say My Name, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Zapp's Influence in Hip-Hop** : Zapp's distinctive vocoder sound became highly "beloved in hip-hop circles," leading to its integration into mainstream hip-hop hits by artists like Tupac and Dr. Dre. "The zap sound of Vicoder became so beloved in hip-hop circles that in 1995, Tupac and Dr. Dre invited Zap's Roger Troutman to throw some Vicoder-style talk box over their smash California love." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Say My Name, Say My Name, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
