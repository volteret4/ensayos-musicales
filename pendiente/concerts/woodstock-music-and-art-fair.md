**Codifier of the Music Festival Idea** : Although not the first of its kind, Woodstock "codified the idea of the music festival" and "quite literally set the stage for multi-act concerts in decades to come." Its influence is seen in subsequent festivals such as the Us Festival, Live Aid, Lollapalooza, and Coachella. **Impact on Recorded Music Business and Pop Charts** : The festival had a significant "knock-on effect" on the recorded music business and pop charts. While the quality of live music varied, recorded music (both live recordings from the concert and studio recordings by exposed artists) was an "unqualified success," with many performers dominating the charts a year or more after the "Aquarian Exposition." **"Woodstock" Film Release and Impact (1970)** : The three-hour documentary film "Woodstock," directed by Michael Wadleigh and famed for its "trippy and then innovative use of split screen," reached theaters in March 1970, more than seven months after the concert. This film was the primary way most Americans experienced Woodstock in the absence of social media and video sharing, and it helped make Jimi Hendrix's national anthem performance famous. **"Woodstock, music from the original soundtrack and more" Album (1970)** : A few weeks after the film's release, a "smash album" titled "Woodstock, music from the original soundtrack and more" was released. This three-LP set, which was "wavy and expensive," topped the Billboard album chart for a month during the summer of 1970, serving as both a movie soundtrack and a festival keepsake. It contained "more," including performances not in the movie or even on the actual Woodstock stage, like an Arlo Guthrie performance recorded at the Troubadour. "This album topped the Billboard album chart for a month during the summer of 1970, remarkable given that it was a three LP set which made it wavy and expensive. **Artists Omitted or Self-Excluded from Film/Soundtrack** : Several artists, particularly superstars, were either omitted from or requested to be left out of the "Woodstock" movie and soundtrack due to their dissatisfaction with their own performances.
