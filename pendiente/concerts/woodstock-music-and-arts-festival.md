# concert - Woodstock Music and Arts Festival

## curiosities
**Commemorated by Song (1970)** : The Woodstock Music and Arts Festival, held in 1969, was commemorated by the song "Woodstock," written and recorded by Joni Mitchell and later a hit for Crosby, Stills, Nash and Young in early 1970. The song captured the essence of the festival, even though Mitchell herself did not attend. **Joni Mitchell's Absence (1969)** : Joni Mitchell was invited to play at Woodstock but did not attend, advised by her manager David Geffen to do a television show instead due to travel concerns. This "infamous decision" meant a significant voice of her generation missed its signature live music event. "However, Mitchell was writing about a concert she herself never attended. In a now infamous decision, she had been invited to play the festival, but didn't even go to Upstate New York on the advice of her manager, David Geffen." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Building a Herstory Edition Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Graham Nash's Inspiration for "Woodstock" (1969)** : Graham Nash, then dating Joni Mitchell and a member of Crosby, Stills, Nash and Young, attended Woodstock and recounted his "Aquarian experience" to Mitchell. His stories inspired her to write the song "Woodstock." **Lack of Women Performers (1969)** : Out of the 32 acts that played Woodstock, only five were women or included women: Janis Joplin, Melanie, members of Sly and the Family Stone, Jefferson Airplane, and Joan Baez. This highlighted a significant gender imbalance at the "signature live music event of her generation." "Out of the 32 acts who played Woodstock, only five were women or even included women.
