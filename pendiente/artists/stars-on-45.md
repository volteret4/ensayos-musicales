## songs
**Medley (1981)** : This medley, featuring various Beatles songs, was the third number one hit to be written by Lennon/McCartney but not performed by The Beatles, topping the charts in 1981. It is noted for its "cheerful sacrilege" and its effective use of collective memory, acting as a "presage" to the sampling and DJ music that would follow, drawing comparisons to Grandmaster Flash's "On the Wheels of Steel" and Girl Talk. The medley combines "little bits and bobs of these other songs," primarily "beloved Beatles chestnuts," and while some transitions are "a little awkward," it "mostly works." The song begins with a "catchy" straight-up disco segment before transitioning into the Beatles pastiche. Despite initial perceptions of being "tacky" and "guileless," its forward-thinking approach to sampling became more appreciated over time. "there's a sort of cheerful sacrilege to that song that I find pretty enjoyable." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Bridge - Nostalgic for Number Ones | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Medley Intro Venus, Sugar Sugar, No Reply, I'll Be Back, Drive My Car, Do You Want to Know a Secret?, We Can Work It Out, I Should Have Known Better, Nowhere Man, You're Going to Lose That Girl, Stars on 45 (1981)** : This track was a disco medley of mostly Beatles songs, created by Dutch studio musicians (Bach Mayes, Hans Vermuleb, Opie Hoistens) trying to sound like The Beatles. It originated from an unauthorized French Canadian DJ bootleg. Willem Van Kuten legitimized the idea, commissioning Yop Egermont and Martin Duser to re-record the segments using sound-alike artists. The medley includes snippets of The Shocking Blue's "Venus" and The Archies' "Sugar Sugar," along with eight Beatles songs, all spliced together "analog style" over an "insidious, infectious clap track." The intro, an original melody, features pigeon-English lyrics that give the song its name and command dancers to "boogie like disco" but "not to forget the oldies." It topped the Dutch charts in February 1981, achieved number one or two positions internationally, and reached number one in America, where it was issued with a 41-word title. **Stars on 45 Medley (1981)** : This hit became the third and, to date, last single with John Lennon and Paul McCartney songwriting credits to top the Hot 100. It is characterized as pure, campy Euro disco, a genre that only occasionally caught on in America. Despite being released in 1980, it took off globally in early 1981 and reached number one that year, two years after Disco's peak, during the height of the disco backlash. The medley features a distinctive clap beat and a novel repackaging of familiar baby boomer hit parade elements, making it an ultimate hit for record buyers during the 1981 aerobics craze. It also explicitly mentions "Disco" in its opening line. "Stars on 45's medley was, of course, recorded months before Lennon's death, but the fact that the song only took off globally in early 1981, a year after its release can clearly be attributed to the kitschy reinvigoration of Lennon's legacy." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Without The Beatles | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/

## curiosities
**Origin from Bootleg Mix** : The concept for Stars on 45 originated from an unauthorized 12-inch dance single called "Let's Do It in the 80s Great Hits" by French Canadian DJs Michel Gendro and Paul Recher. This bootleg blended current disco tracks with older songs, including original recordings of Beatles songs, all mixed over a clapping beat. "What was remarkable about Let's Do It in the 80s Great Hits was that it lurched from very current Disco tracks by the likes of the Gap Band or Lips Incorporated to songs that were at least a decade old, all mixed over a clapping beat." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Without The Beatles | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Willem Van Kuten's Inspiration** : Willem Van Kuten, owner of the copyright to The Shocking Blue's "Venus," discovered the unauthorized DJ 12-inch containing his song and a long Beatles medley. Rather than suppressing it, he was inspired to create a legitimate, authorized medley using "sound-like artists" to replicate the original hits. "Van Kouten was more charmed than angered by the unlicensed Montreal 12-inch record, and he was also inspired, particularly by the long Beatles medley in the middle of the record." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Without The Beatles | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Dutch Studio Musicians** : Van Kuten enlisted Yop Egermont and Martin Duser, who recruited established Dutch singers for the project. Bach Mayes (from Smile) provided a John Lennon impression, Hans Vermuleb (from Sandy Coast) took on Paul McCartney's vocals, and Opie Hoistens (from Rainbow Train) was a "passable" George Harrison. "These singers impersonations of the Fab Four were not precise, but fairly respectable." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Without The Beatles | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Analog Splicing and Clap Track** : The re-recorded segments of various songs were spliced together using analog techniques and combined with an "unremittingly chipper clap track" that distinctively dated the song to 1980, reflecting the disco era's production style. "The tracks that Egermont's team re-recorded were then spliced together, analog style, against an unremittingly chipper clap track." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Without The Beatles | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**"Unhip Homage to 70s-Style Disco"** : The song's intro, an original melody, explicitly instructed listeners to "boogie like disco" while also commanding them "not to forget the oldies," creating a surreal mashup of late disco-era production with classic 60s songs. "What is amusing about stars on 45, however, is its unhipp-omage to 70s-style disco." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Without The Beatles | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Two Versions and Global Success** : Egermont and Duser initially created a nearly 10-minute version of Stars on 45, but Dutch DJs preferred the four-minute Beatles segment. In response, a tighter, sub-five-minute mix focused on the Beatles segment was released on a 45 RPM single, topping Dutch charts in February 1981 and achieving number one or two in England, Australia, Germany, and Spain before its US release. "Dutch DJs began to focus on the four-minute Beatles segment. In response, Egermont and Duser came up with a tighter, sub-five-minute mix that focused on the Beatles segment and fit on one side of a 45 RPM single." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Without The Beatles | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Longest Song Title on Billboard Hot 100** : For its US release, a subsidiary of Atlantic Records called Radio Records, fearful of litigation, issued the song with a 41-word title listing every covered song. This remains the longest title of a hit in Billboard Hot 100 history to reach any position, let alone number one. "This remains the longest title of a hit in Billboard Hot 100 history in any position, let alone number one." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Without The Beatles | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Interruption of Kim Carnes' Reign** : "Stars on 45's Medley" improbably reached number one in June 1981, briefly interrupting Kim Carnes' "Bette Davis Eyes," which was shaping up to be the Song of the Summer and Top Hit of the Year, spending five weeks at number one before being displaced, then returning for another four weeks. "In between, it was interrupted by this crazy collection of Dutch beetle imitators." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Without The Beatles | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Medley Craze Catalyst** : Stars on 45's popularity was so significant that it sparked a widespread medley craze in the music industry. Over the subsequent 18 months, numerous kitschy medleys flooded the charts, with some even mimicking Stars on 45's signature relentless clap beat. This phenomenon showcased the industry's tendency to imitate successful ideas. "Indeed, Stars on 45 proved so popular it even spawned a FAD of its own, a medley craze." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Without The Beatles | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Unique Timing Jackpot** : The producers of Stars on 45 achieved significant chart success due to exceptionally fortuitous timing, specifically coinciding with the global outpouring of grief following John Lennon's assassination in December 1980, and the concurrent aerobics craze in America in 1981. This unique convergence of events allowed their medley, recorded months prior, to resonate deeply with the public. "And the stars on 45 producers hit the ultimate timing jackpot in the saddest way possible." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Without The Beatles | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Post-Craze Decline** : By the summer of 1982, follow-up medleys released by Stars on 45, as well as by Meco, failed to chart on the Hot 100, signaling the clear end of the medley craze. Despite this, Stars on 45 had already secured a follow-up hit, preventing them from being classified as a one-hit wonder. "By the summer of 1982, follow-up medley's by both Meco and the Stars on 45 missed the hot 100, and it was clear the medley craze was done." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Without The Beatles | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Chinsy Clap Beat Deftness** : Compared to medleys quickly assembled by other artists like The Beach Boys and The Beatles to capitalize on the craze, Stars on 45's distinctive "chinsy sounding clap beat" appeared more skillfully executed. This was primarily because other medleys often lacked a unified beat, leading to awkward and jarring song-to-song juxtapositions. "They actually made Stars on 45's chinsy sounding clap beat seem a bit more deft." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Without The Beatles | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
