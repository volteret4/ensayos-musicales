## songs
**Worried Man Blues (1930)** : This old folk song was recorded by The Carza family in 1930. Its lyrics, including "the train arrived 16 coaches long," became part of the "floating lyrics" commonly used by blues singers, later serving as a basis for Junior Parker's "Mystery Train." "That song actually dates back to the old folk song, Warwickman Blues, which was recorded in 1930 by the Carza family." ← Episode 33： ＂Mystery Train＂, by Elvis Presley | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DSHTmHy3Jo0

## curiosities
**Big Name Act on Old Dominion Barn Dance** : The Carter Family was among the "big name acts" that performed on the Old Dominion Barn Dance, a tier below the Louisiana Hayride and Grand Ole Opry in the country music hierarchy. "The Barn Dance had some big name acts, the Carter family." ← Episode 40： ＂Drugstore Rock 'n' Roll＂ by Janis Martin | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0i2p9TelN0
**Forgotten by 1952** : Despite being very popular in the 1920s, The Carter Family was largely forgotten by 1952, when Harry Smith compiled his "Anthology of American Folk Music." "by 1952 even the most popular act, like Blind Lemon Jefferson or The Carter Family, were largely forgotten." ← Episode 97： ＂Song to Woody＂ by Bob Dylan | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3DufLVcZyc4
**Influence on Robert Johnson's Musical Philosophy** : Bob Dylan acknowledged that many of Woody Guthrie's songs he loved were rewrites of old Carter Family songs, providing a justification for Robert Johnson's reinterpretation of other blues artists' material. "many of the woody got these songs he loved, were rewrites of old Carter family songs, so if Johnson was rewriting Lee Roy Car songs that was fair enough." ← Episode 97： ＂Song to Woody＂ by Bob Dylan | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3DufLVcZyc4
**Influence on Woody Guthrie** : Woody Guthrie's early career as a country singer was inspired by The Carter Family, among other 1920s greats. "Guthrie had started out as a country singer, singing songs inspired by the Carter family, Jimmy Rogers, and other 1920s greats." ← Episode 97： ＂Song to Woody＂ by Bob Dylan | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3DufLVcZyc4
**AP Carza's Songwriting and Repurposing** : AP Carza, the patriarch of the family, was an important songwriter of his generation. He also had a practice of finding old folk songs, repurposing their lyrics, and then crediting himself as the writer. "AP Carza, the family patriarch, was one of the most important songwriters of his generation. But he would also go out and find old folk songs, that he would repurpose, and credit himself with having written." ← Episode 33： ＂Mystery Train＂, by Elvis Presley | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DSHTmHy3Jo0
**Founding Figures of Country Music** : The Carza family, alongside Jimmy Rogers, were pivotal in defining what country music is.
