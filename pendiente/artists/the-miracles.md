**Shop Around (1960)** : This song, written by Smokey Robinson, became Motown's first major hit, reaching number two on the pop charts and selling a million copies. Its success was achieved after Barry Gordy insisted on a new, faster arrangement for a third version of the song, recorded in the middle of the night. **Shop Around (1960)** : This was the breakthrough hit not only for the Detroit quintet, The Miracles, but also for Motown Records, released on the Tamla label, Barry Gordy's first Detroit imprint. It became Motown's first million-selling single and the first smash written by The Miracles' lead singer and songwriter William "Smokey" Robinson Jr., who co-wrote it with Barry Gordy. The original 1960 "Detroit version" was bluesy, sluggish, and dominated by a thumping tambourine, with Robinson's eager but slightly hoarse vocal. Barry Gordy, dissatisfied with its performance a couple of weeks after release, ordered an overnight rearrangement at 3 AM into a pepier version with big sonics, a heavier bass line, and brushed drums. This final version debuted on the Hot 100 just before Christmas 1960, topped the R&B chart in January 1961, and reached number 2 on the Hot 100 in February 1961. It was held from the number 1 spot by Lawrence Welk's "Calcutta." Had it gone all the way, it would have been the first pop number 1 for both Robinson and The Miracles, and for Motown. "The breakthrough hit not only for Detroit quintet, the miracles, but also for Motown Records, released on the Tamilar label." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Hit Parade - The Silver Medalists Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
