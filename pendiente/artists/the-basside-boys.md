## songs
**Macarena (1995)** : In the spring of 1995, Power 96 FM DJ Jam and Johnny Carre Day approached record producers Carlos De Arza and Mike Treyi (who operated under the name The Basside Boys, derived from their studio Basside Music) to create an English remix of Los Del Rio's "Macarena." The radio station, which primarily played English songs, stipulated that the track needed English elements to be considered for airplay. Carlos De Arza spent approximately 90 minutes researching existing versions, listening to the original, and then creatively adding English lyrics from Macarena's perspective, along with various samples and a guide track. "The ask was, they want you to do what you do to these things and put something in English on it. **Macarena (1995)** : Carlos De Arza did not alter the existing instrumentation of the "River Famix" (Los Del Rio's electronic remix) but instead rearranged its elements and inserted new English verses from Macarena's point of view. He composed the lyrics in roughly 90 minutes, describing them as a "nursery rhyme." He then enlisted his friend Patty Alfarro to record the female vocals, mirroring his guide track. Additionally, he incorporated an isolated laugh sample from the B-side of a Situation 12-inch record, which he felt encapsulated Macarena's sound, and a spoken word sample ("I am not trying to seduce you") from a remix of George Michael's "Too Funky," unknowingly sourced from "The Graduate" movie. "If I were to add all the time that I worked on this project, I would say maybe 90 minutes from research to edit to writing to recording vocals." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > One Year - Hey Macarena! | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Macarena (1995)** : The Basside Boys' remix was submitted to Power 96 FM at the end of June 1995 and immediately entered rotation. The station's request lines "exploded" with calls, and the song quickly became a local sensation in Miami, despite Carlos De Arza's initial belief that listeners would "get tired of it." By early October, the record had sold 150,000 copies, with people dancing to it in diverse settings from gay bars to Girl Scout meetings and operating rooms, solidifying its status as a "perfect mutation" and "ultimate Macarena." ## curiosities
**Studio Name Origin** : The name "Basside Boys" for Carlos De Arza and Mike Treyi's remixing duo came directly from their studio, "Basside Music." When their "Macarena" remix began to gain traction, Power 96 FM would announce, "Oh, here's a new one. The Basside Boys did this," effectively establishing their moniker. "My studio is called Basside Music. So when the record starts playing, oh, here's a new one. The Basside Boys did this. And so that's where that name comes from." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > One Year - Hey Macarena! | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Copyright Infringement and Resolution** : After the Basside Boys' remix achieved national popularity, Marcia Records, Los Del Rio's label, contacted Carlos De Arza regarding copyright infringement. The label's lawyer initially threatened cease-and-desist orders to halt radio play. However, due to the remix's immense popularity, they instead struck a deal: the record company retained the rights, released the remix on CD, and gave the Basside Boys "a small cut of the proceeds," acknowledging the remix's cultural impact and the impracticality of removing it from the airwaves. "The label did not shut down the bass-eyed boys. Given how popular the remix was, it would have been crazy to pull it off the air.
