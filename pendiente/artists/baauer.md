## songs
**Harlem Shake (2013)** : This track became an instant smash largely due to Billboard's new fan video rule, which factored in fan-created YouTube videos. What made it a hit was not an official video from Baauer—who only had a bare-bones clip of the single's artwork—but rather the widespread fan participation. The week it hit number one, the track was heard in YouTube videos 103 million times, setting a one-week streaming record that would later be broken by Lil Nas X. This phenomenon established an important precedent for "Old Town Road" as the first internet meme to directly create a number one hit. **Harlem Shake (2013)** : This viral phenomenon, an EDM track by DJ Baauer, became an instant number one Hot 100 chart-topper in the very first week Billboard officially added YouTube data to its chart formula in 2013. It achieved this with very little radio airplay and only modest download sales, largely thanks to some 100 million video views. The smash was primarily driven not by Baauer's official video, but by hundreds of viral fan videos featuring "people in gangs of dozens doing the silly pelvic thrusting oddly white people beloved Harlem Shake dance." **Harlem Shake (2013)** : This "fluke hit" by Philadelphia DJ Baauer was a novelty song and a quasi-instrumental hit. Its debut at number one was fueled by a Billboard policy change in 2013 that began counting YouTube views for the Hot 100, leading to over 100 million video views in its first week. Baauer (transcribed as Bauer) had never touched the Hot 100 before this "freak number one debut." As of 2025, it receives no radio spins, as "radio was never a major part of the whole Harlem Shake phenomenon."
