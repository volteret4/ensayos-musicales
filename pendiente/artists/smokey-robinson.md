## curiosities
**Primary Producer for The Marvelettes** : After "Please Mr. Postman," Smokey Robinson became the primary writer and producer for many of The Marvelettes' records, and later took over their production entirely, strategically shifting lead vocals to Wonder Young. "But while those records did okay on the R&B chart, they didn't have much success on the pop charts, mostly getting to around number 50. At one point Motown started to wonder if they needed to change things up a little. They put out a single by the group with Gladys and Wonder singing a dual lead, and with the group joined by Motown's in-house backing vocal group The Andantees. The record was put out under the name The Darnells, but was unsuccessful. Unfortunately for them, they missed the chance at a really big hit. Holland, Doge and Holland had written a song for them, but Gladys didn't like it. She thought it was too simplistic, and so they took it to the group who were still known with him Motown as the no-hit Supremes. We'll be looking at Where Did I Love Go in more detail next year. Eddie Holland did co-writer hit for them with Norman Whitfield though, though it wasn't a monster hit like Where Did I Love Go. It did give all the girls a chance to have a solo spot, a rarity for them. They took them back into the top 30 and made the top 5 on the R&B chart. It would be the last hit that they would have with George Anna in the group though. She'd been diagnosed with sickle cell anemia as a child, and the constant strain of touring made her more ill. The tours had been a shock for all of them to be honest. Their first major national tour was the first Motown review in 1962, a tour with a lineup that seems preposterously good these days. All of Motown's major acts and several acts that weren't yet major but soon would be were on the same bill.
