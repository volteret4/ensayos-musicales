She successfully pressured the label for this credit, becoming the first act credited for a sample on a number one hit and appearing in Marky Mark's music video. **Love Sensation (1980)** : This 1980 club smash by gospel-trained Loleatta Holloway contained the explosive vocal sample used for the chorus of Marky Mark and the Funky Bunch's 1991 number one hit "Good Vibrations." It had previously been featured in uncredited samples on other hits by artists like Samantha Fox and Black Box ("Ride On Time"). ## curiosities
**Pioneer of Featured Sample Credit** : Loleatta Holloway became the first act to receive full artist credit for a sample on a number one hit with Marky Mark and the Funky Bunch's "Good Vibrations" (1991). After experiencing "Martha Wash style brush off" on earlier hits, she successfully pressured the label for an above-the-line credit, also appearing in the music video. "Holloway became not only the first act given full artist credit for a sample on a number one hit. She was also the first chart-topping hook singer, a crown that would have already gone to Martha Wasch if CNC Music Factory had done right by her." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Feat. **New Template for Female Hook Singer** : Loleatta Holloway, by securing credit for her sample on "Good Vibrations," along with Martha Wash's struggles, established a new 1990s template for the female hook singer. This model involved a power singer providing the sweet, melodic hooks for primarily rap-driven tracks. "In essence, Martha Wasch and Lolita Holloway were setting a new 90s template for the female hook singer, the inverse of the featured rapper model, Al-Araqim and Bobby Brown, where friends and she ain't worth it had been primarily sung with a featured rap." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Feat.
