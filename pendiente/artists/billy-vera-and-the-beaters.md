## songs
**At This Moment (1981)** : A soulful saloon ballad penned by veteran LA singer Billy Vera in the 70s, it was first issued as a single in 1981 and briefly charted, peaking at number 79 on the Hot 100. Five years later, an NBC producer discovered it for *Family Ties*. **At This Moment (1986)** : After being discovered by an NBC producer for *Family Ties* and appearing twice on the show, Billy Vera had to convince Rhino Records for months to reissue the out-of-print recording. Following its third appearance on the show in Fall 1986, the re-released single re-entered the Hot 100 and climbed, reaching number one in January 1987 after a dozen weeks. ## curiosities
**Discovery by NBC Producer (1986)** : In 1986, an NBC producer looking for a love theme for Alex P. Keaton on *Family Ties* (played by Michael J. Fox) happened upon Billy Vera and The Beaters performing "At This Moment" in an LA nightclub, leading to the song's licensing and subsequent re-release. "an NBC producer happened to catch Vera and his band The Beatles in an LA nightclub." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > A Deal with the TV God Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Unprecedented Public Reaction** : Following its appearances on *Family Ties*, NBC reported an unprecedented volume of letters and phone calls from viewers inquiring about "At This Moment," indicating a massive public response to the song. "NBC says they have never received so many letters and phone calls about one song." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > A Deal with the TV God Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Catalyst for TV-driven Hits** : Radio consultant Sean Ross stated in a *Billboard* article that "At This Moment" played a significant role in initiating the trend of TV resurrecting songs, establishing a new route to radio for tracks that did not necessarily align with contemporary radio programming. "I think at this moment had a lot to do with starting the trend because it established a route to radio for songs that weren't of a piece with what else was on the radio." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > A Deal with the TV God Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
