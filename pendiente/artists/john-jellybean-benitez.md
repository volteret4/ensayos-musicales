# artist - John Jellybean Benitez

## genres
- Freestyle

## instruments
- Latin percussion
- Synthesizers

## songs
**Crazy for You (1985)** : Jellybean Benitez produced this 1985 number one hit for Madonna. It was a "full on ballad" that ingeniously applied "the tools of freestyle in the service of a synthesized torch song," showcasing the versatility of freestyle production techniques. **Lucky Star (1984)** : Following the success of Shannon's "Let the Music Play," Jellybean Benitez remixed Madonna's "Lucky Star" (an eventual number four hit) from her debut album to infuse it with a "freestyle vibe," indicating the genre's growing influence on mainstream pop production. "Jelly Bean Benitez went back and remixed tracks from Madonna's debut album that he hadn't worked on, like the eventual number four hit Lucky Star, to give them more of a freestyle vibe." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Point of No Return Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**The Mexican (1984)** : Featuring vocals by Jenny Han, this track was a number one club hit for Jellybean Benitez in the fall of 1984. A cover of a 70s rock song by British band Babe Ruth, it prominently featured Latin percussion, further linking freestyle to its Latin roots, distinguishing it from "Holiday" or "Let The Music Play." **Sidewalk Talk (1984)** : This top 40 pop hit, co-written by Madonna (who also sang the chorus), eventually reached number 18. It demonstrates Jellybean Benitez's continued success and collaboration within the pop sphere. ## curiosities
**Freestyle Producer Icon** : John "Jellybean" Benitez, a descendant of Puerto Rican immigrants from the Bronx, is "virtually synonymous with freestyle" and known for his innate ability to arrange and mix records with the genre's characteristic "pinging synthesizers and Latin polyrhythms." He was Madonna's boyfriend and produced her breakthrough hit "Holiday." "John Jelly Bean, Benitez...is virtually synonymous with freestyle. A descendant of Puerto Rican immigrants from the Bronx, Benitez innately knew how to arrange and mix a record with the pinging synthesizers and Latin polyrhythms freestyle became known for." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Point of No Return Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
