# artist - Jule

## labels
- Atlantic Records

## albums
**Pieces of You (1995)** : This album was a massive success for Jewel, succeeding "beyond anyone's imaginings." It took about 18 months for its first hit, "Who Will Save Your Soul," to break, and the album was still generating top 40 hits over two and a half years after its release in the final weeks of 1997. To make its top 10 hits more pop- and radio-friendly, all were remixed from their original album versions, creating a discrepancy for CD buyers. ## songs
**Foolish Games (1997)** : Initially released as track 2 (the B-side) of the CD single for "You Were Meant for Me" in late 1996, "Foolish Games" was a ballad from Jewel's *Pieces of You* album. Over the summer of 1997, a remixed version appeared on the platinum-selling soundtrack for the film *Batman and Robin*, generating heavy pop radio airplay. Billboard then agreed to make "Foolish Games" the A-side of the existing Jewel single on the chart, dated September 13th, 1997. This flip-flop combined two separate top 10 hits into one "mega chart run," staying on the chart for a then-record 65 weeks until February 1998. "At the time, Foolish Games was filler on the single. **Who Will Save Your Soul (1996)** : This was the first hit from Jewel's 1995 album, *Pieces of You*, finally reaching number 11 on the Hot 100 in August of 1996, approximately 18 months after the album's release. **You Were Meant for Me (1996)** : Released in the closing weeks of 1996 as Jewel's second single, following "Who Will Save Your Soul," this tender ballad was initially the A-side of a CD single. It rode the Hot 100 for 41 weeks, peaking at number 2 in spring 1997, held from the number 1 spot by hits from Puff Daddy and The Notorious B.I.G. By September 1997, it was still at number 25 and heavily played on radio. Its B-side, "Foolish Games," eventually overtook it. ## curiosities
**Chart Longevity Record (1998)** : Jewel's combined single of "You Were Meant for Me" and "Foolish Games" stayed on the Hot 100 for a record-breaking 65 weeks, finally falling off in February 1998. This was the longest run in Hot 100 history at the time, although it was surpassed just seven months later by LeAnn Rimes's "How Do I Live?"
