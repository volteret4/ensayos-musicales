## songs
**Oh Freedom** : This post-Civil War spiritual, arranged by Odetta, featured a specific line that deeply inspired Sam Cook: "And before I'll be enslaved, I'll be buried in my grave, oh home to my Lord and be free." Folk luminary Joan Baez famously covered Odetta's arrangement of this song at Dr. King's March on Washington in 1963. "Among those inspired artists was folk luminary Joan Baez, who covered this Odetta arrangement of the post-Civil War spiritual, Oh Freedom, at Dr. King's March on Washington in 1963." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Don’t Know Much About History, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/

**Influence on Bob Dylan** : Hearing Odetta's first album in a record shop inspired a profound musical epiphany in Bob Dylan, leading him to sell his electric guitar, buy an acoustic, and immediately learn her entire repertoire. "he had an epiphany and a record-shop listening booth listening to an album by the folk singer Odetta." ← Episode 97： ＂Song to Woody＂ by Bob Dylan | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3DufLVcZyc4
**Voice of the Civil Rights Movement** : Odetta was often called "the voice of the Civil Rights Movement" and was hailed by Dr. Martin Luther King, Jr. as "The Queen of American Folk Music." She served as a profound inspiration to numerous folk, gospel, soul, and pop artists throughout the 1950s and 1960s, including Sam Cook and Joan Baez.
