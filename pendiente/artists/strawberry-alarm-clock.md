## songs
**Incense and Peppermints (1967)** : This song was initially an instrumental track for a band then called The Sixpence. College student John Carter provided lyrics as a freelance gig and recruited an uncredited vocalist for the recording. The band considered it a B-side and it was released on the flip side of "The Bird Man of Alkatrash." However, local radio stations in Los Angeles began playing "Incense and Peppermints" instead of the A-side, leading to a change in promotional focus and the band's name to Strawberry Alarm Clock. It became a number one hit in 1967. The band scored one more top 40 hit before disbanding in 1969. ## curiosities
**Band Name Origin** : The band was originally called The Sixpence. In 1967, multiple Baroque rock bands used "Sixpence" in their names, prompting a change. "Strawberry Alarm Clock" was chosen randomly, adapted from The Beatles' song "Strawberry Fields Forever." "Strawberry Alarm Clock was chosen at random, adapted from the Beatles song Strawberry Fields Forever." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Hit Parade - The B-Sides Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Vocalist Anonymity** : The vocalist on "Incense and Peppermints" was a friend of the band, recruited by lyricist John Carter, who was "hanging around the studio and was never seen again." The band thought so little of the B-side song that they didn't mind having an outside vocalist. "Carter recruited a vocalist on the track who wasn't actually part of the six pence. **B-side Origin and Name Change** : The band originally called themselves "The Six Pents" until a hastily recorded B-side called "Incense and Peppermints" caught on at radio stations, prompting them to choose the "groovier band name" The Strawberry Alarm Clock.
