## songs
**Can't Hold Us (2013)** : Following "Thrift Shop," this inspirational, psych-up pop jam became their second number one hit. It featured vocals by another black singer, Ray Dalton, further highlighting the trend of white acts leading hip-hop-influenced pop hits. "Within weeks of their first number one, Maclamore and Lewis scored a second. **Thrift Shop (2013)** : This song featuring Wanz was the Billboard year-end number one for 2013, topping the Hot 100 for six weeks. It is debated whether it's a novelty record or a witty satire of hip hop's label-obsessed culture. As "white rappers," their status carried "its own baggage." It accumulated enough chart data early in 2013 to win the year-end title, despite Robin Thicke's "Blurred Lines" spending twice as many weeks at number one. "Some claim Macklemore's ode to bargain shopping is a witty dismantling of hip hop's label obsessed culture." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Hits of the Year Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f078e6dd12efb02ce81
**Thrift Shop (2013)** : This novelty rap hit, about finding stylish clothes in second-hand stores, became the top song of 2013. It featured a hook sung by 51-year-old R&B singer Mike Wanz (Mike Wands-Wandsley). "The top song of the year was Maclamore and Ryan Lewis's Thrift Shop, a novelty rap hit about looking for fly threads at second-hand clothing stores." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Rolling in God’s Royal Uptown Road Edition. | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/

## curiosities
**Best New Artist Grammy Win (2014)** : In 2014, Macklemore & Ryan Lewis won the Best New Artist Grammy, defeating a "murderer's row" of nominees including Kendrick Lamar, Kacey Musgraves, James Blake, and Ed Sheeran. "Or, lest we forget 2014, when a murderer's row of Kendrick Lamar, Casey Musgraves, James Blake and Ed Sheeran were all defeated by best new artist winners, Maclamor and Ryan Lewis." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Blame It on the Feign, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**White Act Dominance (2013)** : The success of Macklemore & Ryan Lewis contributed to a significant trend in 2013 where, for the first time in the Hot 100's history, an entire year passed with no number one hits led by African Americans. This occurred despite charts incorporating more diverse genre data. "In other words, at a time when the Hot 100 had more data across genres, factoring into it than before, songs led by white acts were coming out on top." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Rolling in God’s Royal Uptown Road Edition. | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
