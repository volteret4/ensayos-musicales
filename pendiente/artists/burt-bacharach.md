## curiosities
**Hit-Making Machine** : Burt Bacharach, alongside his songwriting partner Hal David, was described as a "hit making machine" due to his consistent success in writing popular songs for various artists. "The guy was a hit machine." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 28 - Soundtracks of the 70s | https://seventies.libsyn.com/028-soundtracks-of-the-70s-12221-5-45-pm
**Collaboration with Hal David** : Bert Bacharach and Hal David were a renowned songwriting partnership who wrote numerous hits for various artists, but their collaboration with Dion Warwick was unique. They considered her their muse, crafting songs specifically for her distinctive voice. "Within months, Bacarack and David began writing songs uniquely suited to Dion Warwick's self-assured voice, including Don't Make Me Over." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Killing Me Softly Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Split and Lawsuit with Dion Warwick** : Bacharach and David split up shortly after the beginning of the 1970s. This professional separation led to Dion Warwick having to sue them to resolve her contract with the songwriting duo. "She kind of had to, when her longtime mentors, songwriting duo Bert Macarach and Hal David, split up just after the start of the 70s, Warwick had to sue them to resolve her contract with the super smooth pair." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Killing Me Softly Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**1971 Split with Dionne Warwick (1971)** : Burt Bacharach and Dionne Warwick had not recorded together since 1971. Their reunion around 1985, facilitated by Carol Bayer Sager for the song "That's What Friends Are For," saw their "old grudge turned out to be water under the bridge." "Remember, Bert and Dion had not recorded together since 1971. Sager brought them back together and their old grudge turned out to be water under the bridge." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Killing Me Softly Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Disappointment with Rod Stewart's "That's What Friends Are For" (1982)** : Burt Bacharach, as co-writer of "That's What Friends Are For" with Carol Bayer Sager, was "greatly disappointed" when Rod Stewart's 1982 version of the song for the film *Night Shift* failed to become a hit. "Stewart's version of the song was not a hit, which greatly disappointed the song's co-writers, veteran songsmith Carol Bayer-Sager and her then husband, Bert Bacarack." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Killing Me Softly Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**"On My Own" Collaboration Process (1986)** : As a co-writer and producer of "On My Own," Burt Bacharach felt the track "cried out for a male vocalist accompaniment" after Patti LaBelle had recorded her solo version. This led to Michael McDonald recording his part remotely, resulting in a number one hit without the vocalists meeting during the recording process.
