## curiosities
**New Jack Swing Pioneer (Mid-Late 1980s)** : Teddy Riley, a New York songwriter-producer, achieved fame in the mid-to-late 1980s by pioneering New Jack Swing. This subgenre merged R&B and rap production styles, making hip hop palatable for pop and black radio, and subsequently dominated the charts. By 1989, Riley "all but owned New Jack Swing." "Teddy Riley's claim to fame in the mid to late 80s was pioneering the subgenre new Jack swing, a blend of R&B and rap production styles that made hip hop palatable on pop and even black radio." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Flip It and Reverse It Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Move to Virginia Beach and Scene Catalyst (Early 1990s)** : In the early 1990s, the Harlem native Teddy Riley moved to Virginia Beach, Virginia, seeking to escape the "rough and tumble" of New York City. He established a small studio in a residential neighborhood, which proved to be a catalyst for the entire Virginia Beach music scene, previously non-existent, despite the presence of young, raw local talent. "What makes Teddy Riley pivotal to this story is what he did in the early 90s, the native of Harlem, looking to get away from the rough and tumble of New York City moved to Virginia Beach, Virginia, setting up a small studio in a residential neighborhood." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Flip It and Reverse It Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Mentorship of Future Hitmakers (Early 1990s)** : Riley's Virginia Beach studio became a hub for local teenagers, including the future members of SBI and The Neptunes (Pharrell Williams, Timothy Mosley, Melvin Barcliff, and Chad Hugo). He allowed them to "hang around his studio," and he discovered The Neptunes, whom he later signed, recognizing their potential as a "great production team." **Mastermind of New Jack Swing** : Teddy Riley was a key figure and "mastermind" behind the New Jack Swing trend that dominated popular African American music in the late 1980s. He produced Keith Sweat's hits and led the successful R&B vocal group Guy, taking this genre "to the bank" in 1987 and 1988, and also produced Bobby Brown's number one smash "My Prerogative."
