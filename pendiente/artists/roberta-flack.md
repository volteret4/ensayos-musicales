**Killing Me Softly With His Song (1973)** : The origin of this song came from Laurie Lieberman's impressions of a Don McLean performance, which her songwriters Norman Gimbel and Charles Fox turned into a song. Roberta Flack discovered Lieberman's version on a TWA flight in 1972 and felt compelled to record it due to its title and her classical background, believing she could add something unique. Her full title version, unlike The Fugees' "Just Killing Me Softly," was met with acclaim. Marvin Gaye, after hearing her perform it at his late 1972 concert, urged her to record it immediately, certain it would be a smash. Released officially in January 1973, it rapidly ascended the Hot 100, hitting #1 in just five weeks. It topped the chart in March 1973, the same week Flack accepted the Record of the Year Grammy for "The First Time Ever I Saw Your Face," and would go on to win Record of the Year itself in March 1974, making Flack the first artist to win the prize two years in a row. She found success by incorporating disco instrumentation into slow or mid-tempo songs rather than focusing on dance tracks, similar to Dionne Warwick's approach. **Donnie Hathaway's Suicide (1979)** : Roberta Flack's career path became "bumpier and much sadder" after her duet partner, Donnie Hathaway, committed suicide in January 1979. Hathaway, a gifted but troubled singer, was plagued with crippling depression and paranoid schizophrenia and had been recording with Flack for another album of duets that he would not live to see released.
