**Boogie Woogie Piano Discovery** : Young Barry Gordy Jr. quickly figured out how to play the C, F, and G chords, enabling him to start playing Boogie Woogie Piano by ear. **Boxing vs. Music Career Decision** : Gordy paralleled Jackie Wilson's early career dilemma, initially unsure whether to become a musician or a boxer like his hero Joe Lewis, until he observed concert posters showing band leaders working every night and looking young, while boxers worked sporadically and looked old, leading him to choose music. "He knew what he was going to do, and it wasn't boxing." ← Episode Sixty-Four： ＂Reet Petite＂ by Jackie Wilson | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BYU-H4NvnmE
**Childhood Piano Instruction** : Born the seventh of eight children to a poor Detroit family, Gordy was taught piano rudiments by an uncle who tried to teach him scales, arpeggios, and music reading, but Gordy, easily bored, soon discovered how to play simple triad chords by playing the first three notes of an arpeggio together. "An uncle, who tried to get him to learn to play in the proper manner, learning scales and arpeggios and how to read music." ← Episode Sixty-Four： ＂Reet Petite＂ by Jackie Wilson | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BYU-H4NvnmE
**Collaboration with Billy Davis and Sisters** : Gordy began collaborating on songs with Billy Davis and his sister Gwen, who was dating Davis, agreeing to credit any individually written songs to the group and pool their earnings, with their primary goal being to write songs for Jackie Wilson. "Anything any of them wrote on their own would also get credited to them as a group, and they'd pool whatever they got, and they were going to write songs for Jackie Wilson." ← Episode Sixty-Four： ＂Reet Petite＂ by Jackie Wilson | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BYU-H4NvnmE
**Conflict with Nat Tarnapol** : Gordy and Billy Davis insisted they should write both sides of Jackie Wilson's singles to get a fair share of royalties, especially as they were effectively producing the sessions without credit or pay, leading to a disagreement with manager Nat Tarnapol, who felt Wilson didn't need them. **3 AM Inspiration for "Shop Around"** : After two unsuccessful versions, Barry Gordy had a sudden realization at 3 AM that The Miracles' "Shop Around" needed a new, faster arrangement. His immediate action to call Smokey Robinson and get the group into the studio led to the song becoming a number two pop hit and a million-seller. **Evolution of Motown's Slogan** : Barry Gordy initially coined the Motown slogan "create, make, and sell" to reflect his goal of producing great records that sold widely. However, recognizing the financial challenges of unpaid distributors, he revised it to "create, sell, and collect," emphasizing the crucial importance of securing payment. **Signing The Primettes (The Supremes)** : Motown staff widely believed that Barry Gordy signed The Primettes (later The Supremes) more due to his attraction to member Diane Ross than for the group's talent, despite their initial lack of success.
