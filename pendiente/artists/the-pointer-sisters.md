## albums
**Energy (1978)** : The Pointer Sisters' debut on Richard Perry's Planet Records, this album saw a reinvention of their sound, leaning more rock-adjacent while maintaining soulful and danceable elements. It included covers of Fleetwood Mac's "Hypnotized" and Stephen Stills' "As I Come of Age," and its single "Fire" became a huge hit, pushing the album to number 13 and earning it gold certification. "On their Planet Records debut energy, Richard Perry made The Pointer Sisters more rock adjacent, while still keeping them soulful, even danceable." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Yes We Can Can Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f6364fe6d21276e3fdd
**The Pointer Sisters (1973)** : This self-titled debut album on Blue Thumb Records featured the four sisters posing in 1920s flapper outfits, reflecting the classic pop harmonies and jazz style present on many tracks, including their cover of Lambert, Hendricks & Ross's "Cloud Burst" and their original jazzy "Jada." It gradually climbed the Billboard chart in summer 1973, reaching just outside the top 40, and eventually peaked at number 13 on the album chart following the success of "Yes We Can Can." "On the cover of their self-titled debut album, the four pointer sisters Anita, Bonnie, June and Ruth posed seated around a table in 20 style flapper outfits with stylish hats and wrap dresses. "Atlantic records, which signed the pointer sisters in 1971. **Fairy Tale (1974)** : Written by sisters Anita and Bonnie Pointer, "Fairy Tale" was inspired by Anita's heartbreak over a married DJ. The song is described as "100% in the country vein" with "beautiful harmonies" and prominent pedal steel guitar. Their own band refused to record it, leading the sisters to record with Nashville session musicians. Released as a single in the summer of 1974, it reached number 13 on the Billboard Hot Country Chart and also received Top 40 radio airplay. "Fairy Tale" earned the Pointer Sisters a Grammy Award for Best Country Performance by a Group or Duo in 1975, making them the only African American women to win a country Grammy until Beyoncé in 2024. "Made it to number 13. And so it was popular enough on the country charts that it got some airplay on top 40 radio too." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 53 - More than Charley Pride - African Americans in 70s Country | https://www.ftr70.com/
**Fairy Tale (1974)** : This twangy country breakup song, written by Anita Pointer with Bonnie Pointer, featured full-on fiddle and pedal steel. Initially a B-side, it gained traction when DJs flipped the record, leading Blue Thumb to partner with a Nashville label for country radio promotion. It reached number 37 on Billboard's Hot Country Songs Chart, number 13 on the Hot 100, and number 13 on Easy Listening, earning them a Grammy in 1975 for Best Country Performance by a Duo or Group with Vocal, making them the only black act ever to win that category. "A breakup song written from the point of view of a wronged woman, Fairy Tale was full on fiddle and petal steel country." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Yes We Can Can Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f6364fe6d21276e3fdd
**Fire (1979)** : A cover of an unreleased Bruce Springsteen song, produced by Richard Perry, which strategically capitalized on its bluesy structure and amplified its soul with a rock arrangement to create a fundamentally pop and massive hit. Released in late 1978, it took 16 weeks to climb to number 2 on the Hot 100 in winter 1979, holding that position for two weeks, and also helped a Bruce Springsteen song reach number 14 on the R&B chart. "Fire was a canny choice for The Pointers. They keyed into its bluesy structure and amped up the soul while Perry backed them with a rock arrangement." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Yes We Can Can Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f6364fe6d21276e3fdd

## curiosities
**Arkansas Roots and Country Upbringing** : The Pointer Sisters, hailing from Arkansas, grew up immersed in country music. They listened to it at home, on the radio, and in church, considering themselves "country people." "We were country people. Anita Pointer said." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 53 - More than Charley Pride - African Americans in 70s Country | https://www.ftr70.com/
**Band's Refusal to Play Country** : The Pointer Sisters' own band refused to record "Fairy Tale," with their drummer stating, "I didn't join this group to play country music." This led the sisters to record the song with Nashville session musicians. "Their drummer got up and left and said I didn't join this group to play country music." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 53 - More than Charley Pride - African Americans in 70s Country | https://www.ftr70.com/
**Experience with Racism in Nashville** : Upon arriving in Nashville for an event, the Pointer Sisters were mistakenly taken to the back of the venue, with staff assuming they were there to work in the kitchen. They were not there for work but to perform. "The staff thought that they were there to work the kitchen. Nope. They were not. They were there to sing this." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 53 - More than Charley Pride - African Americans in 70s Country | https://www.ftr70.com/
**Grammy Award for Country Performance (1975)** : The Pointer Sisters won a Grammy Award for Best Country Performance by a Group or Duo in 1975 for their song "Fairy Tale." They held the distinction of being the only African American women to win a country Grammy for 50 years until Beyoncé's win in 2024. "Fairy tale earned the pointer sisters a Grammy Award... for best country performance by a group or duo in 1975." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 53 - More than Charley Pride - African Americans in 70s Country | https://www.ftr70.com/
**Grand Ole Opry Performance (1974)** : The Pointer Sisters were invited to perform at the Grand Ole Opry in 1974, four years after Linda Martell's debut, following the success of their hit "Fairy Tale." "Four years after Linda Martell's appearance at the Grand Ole Opry, the pointer sisters were invited to play there." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 53 - More than Charley Pride - African Americans in 70s Country | https://www.ftr70.com/
**Inspiration for Country Music Entry** : Their venture into country music was sparked by heartbreak. Anita Pointer's relationship with a DJ ended when she discovered he was married, inspiring her and sister Bonnie to write "Fairy Tale" in the country genre. "Anita was dating a DJ and he neglected to tell her that he was married. Thus, their love that she thought they had was a fairy tale." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 53 - More than Charley Pride - African Americans in 70s Country | https://www.ftr70.com/

**Oakland, California Origins** : The Pointer Sisters were a family vocal group from Oakland, California, where sisters Ruth (b. 1946), Anita (b. 1948), Bonnie (b. 1950), and June (b. 1953) were born. "a family vocal group from Oakland, California, dropped four straight top ten hits all in 1984." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Yes We Can Can Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f6364fe6d21276e3fdd
**Strict Religious Upbringing** : Raised by Sarah and Elton Pointer in a strict religious household, their father, Elton Pointer, was a pastor at the West Oakland Church of God, and the girls were initially only encouraged to listen to and sing gospel music. "They were all born in Oakland, California, Ruth in 1946, Anita in 48, Bonnie in 1950, and Baby June in 1953, to Sarah and Elton pointer who raised them in a strict religious household." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Yes We Can Can Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f6364fe6d21276e3fdd
**Formative Bay Area Gospel Scene** : The Bay Area's gospel music scene was highly influential for the sisters, with June and Bonnie participating in the Northern California youth choir, the same ensemble that produced the Edwin Hawkins Singers' "Oh Happy Day." "by the late 1960s Kernodal writes, The West Coast had become the epicenter of a new wave of music experimentation that would shift the sound and cultural context of black, sacred music during the latter part of the 20th century." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Yes We Can Can Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f6364fe6d21276e3fdd
**Arkansas Country Music Influence** : During yearly visits to Prescott, Arkansas, the Pointer Sisters were exposed to country music through a local radio station, with Anita Pointer recalling listening to artists like Hank Williams and Willie Nelson and appreciating the "short story format" of country songs. "There, the pointer sisters were exposed to country music. Anita pointer later recalled, quote, I only remember listening to one Arkansas radio station. **Andrews Sisters Harmony Inspiration** : The intricate harmony arrangements of the Andrews Sisters, particularly on songs like "Boogie Woogie Bugle Boy" and "Don't Sit Under the Apple Tree (With Anyone Else But Me)," significantly inspired The Pointer Sisters, who found they could imitate this pre-rock style. "The intricate harmony arrangements of the Andrews sisters who turned songs like the Boogie Woogie Bugleboy and don't sit under the apple tree with anyone else but me into standards." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Yes We Can Can Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f6364fe6d21276e3fdd
**Lambert, Hendricks & Ross Vocalese and Interracial Influence** : The Pointer Sisters were particularly inspired by the 1950s vocal trio Lambert, Hendricks & Ross, who pioneered the intricate jazz singing style known as vocalese and were notable for being a co-ed and interracial group. **Evolution of Group Lineup (Late 60s - 70s)** : Bonnie and June were the primary singers in the late 1960s, then second-oldest sister Anita joined to form a trio, and by the 1970s, oldest sister Ruth made them a quartet. "At first, Bonnie and June, the two youngest sisters, were the primary singers. By the late 60s, they were joined by second oldest sister Anita to form a trio. **West Coast Backing Vocal Troupe** : Around the early 1970s, the Pointer Sisters became a highly sought-after backing vocal troupe, described as a "west coast version of the Sweet Inspirations," providing vocals for a diverse mix of rock and R&B acts. "It was around this time that the pointers became a well-regarded backing vocal troupe, a kind of west coast version of the sweet inspirations." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Yes We Can Can Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f6364fe6d21276e3fdd

**Unique Multi-Genre Top 40 Success** : According to a recent obituary of Anita Pointer, the group holds the distinction of being the only act to have landed top 40 hits on all five of Billboard's country, R&B, pop, dance, and adult contemporary charts, a rare combination for any act, particularly a Black vocal group. "Despite recording some truly indelible hits, the pointer sisters do not enjoy the same status as such rock and roll hall of fame inducted girl groups as Martha and the Vandela's, The Ronets, or The Go-Gos.
