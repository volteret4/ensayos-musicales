**Feliz Navidad (1970)** : Feliciano wrote this original song on Christmas Eve in LA, feeling homesick for his traditional Puerto Rican holiday celebrations. It became a Latin Christmas standard, effortlessly catchy and expressing universal sentiments in both Spanish and English. It was a slow-grower, not appearing on any Billboard chart in the 1970s. In the 2020 holiday season, it reached a new Hot 100 peak of number six, fueled by 54,000 terrestrial radio spins and nearly 100 times more Spotify streams than his hit "Light My Fire." ## curiosities
**Comparison to Marvin Gaye's Anthem** : Ironically, the night before Jose Feliciano's controversial rendition of the Star-Spangled Banner at the World Series, Marvin Gaye had sung the anthem at the request of Tigers Broadcasting legend Ernie Harwell. Gaye, known for his Motown sound, sang his version "straight" rather than in his characteristic style, in stark contrast to Feliciano's interpretation that upset many. "The irony of people being upset about Feliciano's rendition of the Anthem is that Marvin Gaye sang at the night before and at the request of the Tiger's Broadcasting legend Ernie Harwell." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 52 - 70s Christmas Music | https://www.ftr70.com/
**Early Life and Discovery** : Jose Feliciano was born in Puerto Rico in 1945, one of 12 children, and began playing the ukulele at age three. After his family moved to New York, he was performing in coffee houses in Greenwich Village by the age of 15, where he was discovered by a music executive from RCA. "Jose Feliciano was born in Puerto Rico in 1945. He was one of 12 children. He started playing the ukulele when he was three. **Star-Spangled Banner Controversy (1968)** : At Game 5 of the World Series in Tiger Stadium, Detroit, Jose Feliciano performed a unique rendition of the Star-Spangled Banner in 1968, which was deemed "not traditional enough" by some, with Carlos Santana even calling it "too black." This performance caused significant backlash, leading American Legion clubs to call for a law dictating how the song should be sung, and Feliciano was controversially labeled a communist during the Cold War era. This event reportedly led to a period where he struggled to get radio play in America and had to perform in other countries, though sources debate if he was truly blacklisted, as he still secured a TV special in 1969 and played to packed houses. "I had to leave America and play in other countries because I wasn't getting any radio play after I did the Star Spangled Banner." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 52 - 70s Christmas Music | https://www.ftr70.com/
