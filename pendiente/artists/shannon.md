**Let the Music Play (1983)** : Released in the summer of 1983, this song was ubiquitous by 1984 and became freestyle's "galvanizing hit" and first Top 10 hit, peaking at number 8 on the Hot 100 in February 1984, surpassing Madonna's "Holiday." Composed by teenage Puerto Rican producer Chris Barbosa, it featured a "blazing rhythm track" with gated snare (inspired by Arthur Baker/John Roby/Afrika Bambaataa), breakbeat/Latin salsa syncopation, and Roland TR-808 synth sounds. Lyrics, ghost-written by Ann Godwin, focused on club culture and romantic dance-floor dynamics. "Shannon's Let The Music Play...pushed past Madonna's holiday to become freestyle's first top 10 hit." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Point of No Return Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/

## curiosities
**One-Hit Wonder Status** : Despite its significant impact as the "Big Bang moment" for freestyle, Shannon's "Let the Music Play" was her only song to reach the American Top 40, making her a pop one-hit wonder. "Shannon would never return to the American top 40." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Point of No Return Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Early Career** : Brenda Shannon Green, who performed as Shannon, was raised in Washington, D.C., and worked as a bookkeeper in New York City. Prior to her hit, she had sung with jazz ensembles and R&B troupes, contributing an R&B foundation to her club-attitudinal vocals on "Let The Music Play."
