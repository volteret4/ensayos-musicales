## songs
**Unchained Melody (1965)** : The Righteous Brothers issued what is considered the definitive version of "Unchained Melody" in 1965. This recording, primarily featuring Bobby Hatfield, reached number four on the Hot 100 and was a very big hit. "In the summer of 1965, the righteous brothers Unchained Melody single reached number four on the Hot 100, a very big hit." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Second-Chance Hits Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4ef18e6dd12efb02c42e
**Unchained Melody (1990)** : Due to the song's prominent feature in the 1990 film "Ghost," "Unchained Melody" surged back onto the charts, but with a catch. The Verve label reissued the original 1965 version on vinyl 45 (reaching number 13, fueled by radio), while Bobby Hatfield re-recorded a new version for Curb Records (reaching number 19, selling a million copies on cassette and CD) due to royalty issues. This resulted in The Righteous Brothers competing against themselves with two versions in the Top 20 simultaneously. "By the fall of 1990, the righteous brothers were competing against themselves on the charts." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Second-Chance Hits Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4ef18e6dd12efb02c42e
**You've Lost That Lovin' Feelin' (1965)** : Hall and Oates released a cover of this song by The Righteous Brothers, marking their first single that was not written by either of them. It went on to become a still-recognized radio staple and reached number 12 in November 1980. "Hall and Oats's take on the righteous brothers, you've lost that love and feeling. Still, a radio staple to this day rose to number 12 in November 1980." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Rock ’n Soul, Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**You've Lost That Lovin' Feelin' (1965)** : This 1965 number one smash had cinematic depth added by producer Phil Spector. Despite the song being their breakthrough, the duo reportedly found working with Spector to be "heated" and avoided him for the rest of their career. "Phil Specter added cinematic depth to the 1965 number one smash you've lost that loving feeling." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Turn Around, Bright Eyes, Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
