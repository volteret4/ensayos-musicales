## albums
**E. 1999 Eternal (1995)** : This album spawned hits like "First of the Month" and the smash "Tha Crossroads," leading it to go quadruple platinum. It cemented Bone Thugs-n-Harmony's influential style of combining speedy raps with sweet R&B harmonies, making previous quibbles about their genre classification moot due to its massive commercial success. ## curiosities
**Pioneers of Sing-Rapping** : Bone Thugs-n-Harmony are arguably the "patient zero" of the sing-and-rap "infection." They took Das EFX's speedy style and added actual melody, which was initially controversial among unforgiving hip-hop purists who did not even consider them rappers. However, fans, the music industry, and the group themselves considered their syncopated bounce a form of rap, and MTV later called them the "most melodic hip hop group of all time." "Bone Thugs in Harmony are arguably the patient zero of the sing and rap infection." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Say My Name, Say My Name, Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Toasting Influence** : Some contemporary observers argued that Bone Thugs-n-Harmony were not so much rapping or singing as they were "toasting," a chanting style adapted by Jamaican DJs from African griots and made essential in reggae and dancehall music. This connection highlights the diverse international influences on their unique melodic rap style. "Some argued at the time that Bone Thugs were not so much rapping or exactly singing as toasting." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Say My Name, Say My Name, Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Cross-Genre Collaborations** : By the late 1990s, Bone Thugs-n-Harmony were appearing on the soundtrack to a Batman movie, and by the early 2000s, they even rapped alongside Phil Collins, demonstrating their widespread mainstream appeal and willingness to collaborate across vastly different genres.
