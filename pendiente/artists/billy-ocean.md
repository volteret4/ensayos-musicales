**Caribbean Queen (1984)** : A number one pop and R&B smash hit for Billy Ocean, which was helmed by R&B producer Keith Diamond. This success prompted the Drive team to seek a single that could follow up on its chart performance. **Caribbean Queen (No More Love on the Run) (1984)** : This song brought Trinidadian singer Billy Ocean back from the "pop wilderness," becoming a number one hit and holding the top spot for two weeks in the fall of 1984. Originally titled "European Queen," the American label requested a version with "Caribbean" for the USA release. Bathed in disco strings, it offered further evidence of America's love for dancefloor bangers. Collaborating with new producer Keith Diamond, the song featured a "sleek, urbane electro-funks sound" indebted to Michael Jackson's Thriller era, particularly the "suppleness of Jackson's Billie Jean." "The new number one song in the land is by Billy Ocean, not European, not American, not African, but Caribbean Queen." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > What’s 1984 Got to Do with It Edition Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Get Out Of My Dreams, Get Into My Car (1988)** : A danceable, exuberant, and kitschy song produced and co-written by Mut Lang. It was a comeback hit for Billy Ocean after a string of mid-80s hits, rising to number five on the pop chart and just weeks away from scoring another number one. The sound was characterized as "Clattery Dance Pop with Husky Soul Vocals." **Get Out Of My Dreams, Get Into My Car (1988)** : Produced and co-written by Mutt Lang, this song became his first number one hit on both the Hot 100 and Billboard's R&B chart in April 1988, predating Def Leppard's "Love Bites" by five months. Lang conceived most of the song's idea and presented it to Billy Ocean a couple of years after their earlier collaborations. "Get out of my dreams, get into my car, was mostly Mutt's idea." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Pour Some Sugar on Me Edition Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Get Out Of My Dreams, Get Into My Car (1988)** : The song's hook was partly inspired by "Back In Your Door," a forgotten track by Roman Holiday that Lang had written for the 1984 film "Teachers." Lang integrated the lyric "I'm your non-stop miracle, I'm your man" from that song's pre-chorus with the funky style of The Isley Brothers' 1973 hit "That Lady," resulting in an infectious dance-pop track. The BBC expressed concern that the title could be perceived as sexually suggestive, leading Billy Ocean to record an edited version with fewer grunts for the UK market, although the original version aired in America. It reached number three in the UK. "It was partially based on the hook of a song Mutt had previously written for the soundtrack to the 1984 movie Teachers, a forgotten track by the British new wave rock band Roman Holiday called Back in Your Door." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Pour Some Sugar on Me Edition Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/

**Loverboy (1985)** : Produced by Mut Lang, teaming with Keith Diamond and Ocean, this single took Billy Ocean in an entirely different direction with a sleek, space-age sound. It fused the dance pop Billy Ocean had been doing with the synth rock Mut Lang had developed for other artists like Def Leppard and The Cars. The accompanying video fittingly looked like an extension of the George Lucas's Star Wars universe, and the song reached number two on the Hot 100 in early 1985.
