## albums
**Girl You Know It's True (1989)** : This US album was pushed to a stunning 6 million in US sales by January of 1990 and spent seven weeks at number one on the album chart between 1989 and 1990. Its liner notes, specifically those of the American edition, listed Rob Pilatus and Fabrice Morvan as vocalists, which made Arista Records liable in a class action lawsuit. ## songs
**All or Nothing (1990)** : This track, originally the title track from the European version of the album and also included on the American LP, reached number four on the Hot 100 the same week Millie Vanilley won their Grammy. It was the last single issued from "Girl You Know It's True" and the final Millie Vanilley single overall. The chorus melody was found to be identical to Blood, Sweat and Tears' 1969 hit "Spinning Wheel," leading to a copyright lawsuit by David Clayton Thomas, which was settled. **Blame It On The Rain (1989)** : Originally intended for the family pop group The Jets by songwriter Diane Warren, the song was given to Frank Farian by Arista Records President Clive Davis after The Jets declined it. It became Millie Vanilley's third consecutive number one hit and their second to go platinum, spending two weeks at the top of the Hot 100 in November 1989. The main verse vocal was performed by Brad Howe, joined by John Davis on the chorus. This song is arguably the most enduring Millie Vanilley hit and gave them a stamp of industry legitimacy. ## curiosities
**Founding and Deception** : Millie Vanilley was Frank Farian's 1980s project, building on his earlier experiments with non-singing frontmen in his 1970s Europop group, Boni M. Rob Pilatus and Fabrice Morvan were hired as the non-singing frontmen, initially scoring a string of massive US hits while keeping the vocal deception a secret. "Ferrien's 80s project, fronted by non-singing frontman Rob Palatus and Fabrice Morvon, was scoring a string of massive US hits." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Blame It on the Feign, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Bristol Connecticut Incident (July 21, 1989)** : At a Club MTV tour date in Bristol, Connecticut, in front of thousands of fans, the backing track supplying Rob Pilatus and Fabrice Morvan's vocals got stuck in a loop. Rob Pilatus, mortified, ran offstage but was coaxed back by host Downtown Julie Brown. Although the incident was captured on tape and became "damning evidence," it was not their immediate downfall, as the public revelation of their deception wouldn't occur for over a year. "On July 21st, 1989, at a date on the club MTV tour in Bristol, in front of thousands of screaming fans, the backing track, supplying Rob Palatus and Fabrice Morvon's vocals got stuck in a loop." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Blame It on the Feign, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Fame Going to Their Heads** : The growing celebrity of Rob Pilatus and Fabrice Morvan led to hubristic statements. In an infamous Time magazine article, Rob Pilatus claimed musical superiority over legends like Bob Dylan, Paul McCartney, and Mick Jagger, declaring himself "the new modern rock and roll" and "the new Elvis." "In one infamous time magazine article, a heubristic Rob Palatus told interviewer Jay Cox, quote, musically we're more talented than any Bob Dylan or Paul McCartney." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Blame It on the Feign, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**American Music Awards Win (1990)** : Millie Vanilley won the Favorite Pop/Rock New Artist award at the American Music Awards, a prize determined by an industry cabal at the time. They defeated Hard Rock band Living Color and the George Harrison-fronted supergroup The Traveling Wilburys. "Millie Vanilley took home the favorite pop rock new artist award, defeating Hard Rock Band Living Color, and the George Harrison fronted Supergroup the traveling wheelbarrows." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Blame It on the Feign, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Grammy Award Win (1990)** : The group famously won the Best New Artist Grammy, an award that ultimately brought about their downfall. This was their only Grammy nomination that night, as none of their hit songs or their smash album were nominated in any genre categories. Their win was largely attributed to their significant sales momentum (6 million albums compared to 4 million combined for other nominees) and multiple number one hits. "The fact that Millie Vanilley were in the category at all probably had something to do with the hit they had right around voting time. **Pushback from Rob and Fab (1989-1990)** : Behind the scenes, Rob Pilatus and Fabrice Morvan began pushing back against Frank Farian, stating they could not maintain the charade and desired to sing on the next Millie Vanilley album. Along with studio rapper Charles Shaw, they periodically threatened to expose the deception, though Shaw later recanted his claims under Farian's pressure. "Rob and Fab were finally pushing back. They were telling Fary and they couldn't keep up the ruse. **Frank Farian's Revelation (November 1990)** : Having had enough of the internal struggles, Frank Farian decided to unmask Millie Vanilley himself before anyone else could. He issued a press statement revealing the deception, claiming the record company was unaware and he had never informed them. "Finally, in November of 1990, Fary and himself had had enough. **Grammy Revocation (November 1990)** : Following Frank Farian's revelation, Michael Greene, president of the Recording Academy, announced that the Best New Artist Grammy would be taken back. The Grammy committee ultimately decided to vacate the prize entirely for 1990, not awarding it to any of the other nominees: Neneh Cherry, Indigo Girls, Tone Loc, or Soul II Soul. **Press Conference and Grammy Return (November 20, 1990)** : Rob Pilatus and Fabrice Morvan, accompanied by a lawyer, held a highly publicized press conference in Los Angeles to confront reporters. They asserted that they, not Frank Farian, ended Millie Vanilley because they wanted to sing on their records and were unable to break their contract. They named Brad Howe and John Davis as the real singers and publicly returned the Grammy. "Rob Polatus and Fabrice Morvon accompanied by a lawyer held a press conference that was a feeding frenzy of reporters and photographers." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Blame It on the Feign, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Class Action Lawsuit** : Arista Records settled a class action lawsuit filed by buyers of the Millie Vanilley album in the United States. This liability stemmed from the fact that only the American edition's liner notes, unlike the European "All or Nothing" album, explicitly listed Rob Pilatus and Fabrice Morvan as vocalists. Approximately 10 million US buyers were eligible for a refund, though few took advantage of the offer. **Career Comparison to John Cazale** : Millie Vanilley had an abnormally short career, lasting barely two years from boom to bust, but an unusually consistent chart streak with all five of their singles reaching the US Top 5. This unusual ratio is compared to actor John Cazale, who only appeared in five feature films, all of which were Best Picture Oscar nominees. **Post-Scandal Influence** : The rap-informed dance pop style pioneered by Millie Vanilley proved enduring, influencing chart-topping music in the early 90s, particularly hip-hop permutations. They notably made the "Ashley's Roachclip" beat safe for pop crossover and arguably opened Top 40 radio playlists to more melodic rap, like Naughty by Nature's "O.P.P." "But the Millie Vanilley approach, that rap-informed dance pop style, that proved far more enduring." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Blame It on the Feign, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
