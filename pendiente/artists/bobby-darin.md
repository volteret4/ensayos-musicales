**Mac the Knife (1959)** : Bobby Darin's jazzy murder ballad that famously topped the Hot 100 for nine weeks in the fall of 1959. By this time, the song, originally from the 1928 German proto-musical "The Three Penny Opera," had already been recorded countless times and established itself as a pop standard. **Wear My Ring (1957)** : Warren Kosouto, identified as the future Bobby Darin (misspelled as "Bobby Damien" in the transcript), co-wrote the ballad "Wear My Wings." This song was released as the B-side to Gene Vincent's "Lotta Lovin'." ## curiosities
**Co-Writer of "I Want Elvis for Christmas" (as Walden Kosoto)** : Bobby Darin, then known as Walden Kosoto, co-wrote the novelty song "I Want Elvis for Christmas." He later changed his name and became a significant figure in music. "a young man named Walden Kosoto, who would soon change his name to Bobby DARREN." ← Episode 40： ＂Drugstore Rock 'n' Roll＂ by Janis Martin | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=l0i2p9TelN0
**Pioneering Apollo Performance (Pre-1957)** : Bobby Darin played the Harlem Apollo before The Crickets, disproving the claim that The Crickets were the first white act to perform there. "Bobby Daring had played there before them, and I think so had the jazz drummer Buddy Rich and maybe one or two others." ← Episode 74： ＂It Doesn't Matter Any More＂ by Buddy Holly | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6unib5T2mDQ
**Early Best New Artist Winner** : Bobby Darin was an early recipient of the Best New Artist prize, which was launched in the early 1960s as a way for the Academy to acknowledge young performers whose music aligned with voters' preferences. "Early winners of the prize included Bobby Darren, Robert Goulet and Tom Jones." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > And the Grammy Goes to… Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4ef0154465cd600c4e3a
