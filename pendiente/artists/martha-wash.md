## songs
**Everybody Everybody (1990)** : Martha Wash provided "explosive vocals" for Italian house producers Black Box on this song, which reached number 8 on the US chart in 1990. This volatile performance was what she later recreated for C+C Music Factory on "Gonna Make You Sweat." "One year later, Martha Wosh provided even more explosive vocals for Italian house producers Black Box, and reached number 8 on the US chart with everybody everybody." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Feat. **Gonna Make You Sweat (Everybody Dance Now) (1990)** : Martha Wash sang all of the melodic hooks, including the iconic "everybody dance now," on C+C Music Factory's number one hit. Despite her pivotal contribution, she was uncredited and later sued the group for fraud, deceptive packaging, and commercial misappropriation. ## curiosities
**Singer for Black Box** : Martha Wash was the uncredited singer on Black Box's hit "Everybody, Everybody" and two subsequent Top 40 hits. She eventually sued the producers and label over her lack of credit and won a large settlement. "Famed vocalist Martha Wasch was the singer on everybody, everybody." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Blame It on the Feign, Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/

**Lawsuit Against C+C Music Factory** : After her iconic vocals on "Gonna Make You Sweat (Everybody Dance Now)" were lip-synced by Zelma Davis in the music video and she remained uncredited, Martha Wash sued C+C Music Factory for "fraud, deceptive packaging, and commercial misappropriation." She effectively set a new template for female hook singers fighting for recognition. **New Template for Female Hook Singer** : Martha Wash, along with Loleatta Holloway, set a new 1990s template for the female hook singer. This model involved a power singer providing melodic hooks for primarily wrapped songs, reversing the "featured rapper" model. "In essence, Martha Wasch and Lolita Holloway were setting a new 90s template for the female hook singer, the inverse of the featured rapper model, Al-Araqim and Bobby Brown, where friends and she ain't worth it had been primarily sung with a featured rap." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Feat.
