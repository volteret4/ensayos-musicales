## albums
**3 Years, 5 Months & 2 Days in the Life Of... (1992)** : This debut album emerged as a cross-cultural smash and, unlike Sir Mix-A-Lot's album, generated multiple follow-up hits. By early 1993, it had achieved triple platinum status and was the best-selling rap album of any kind since MC Hammer's success; by the mid-90s, it went quadruple platinum. The album also topped the 1992 Pazz & Jop Critics poll, making it only the second rap album to do so, after Public Enemy's "It Takes a Nation of Millions to Hold Us Back" in 1988. **Tennessee (1992)** : Arrested Development's debut single, it began with a warped sample from Prince's 1988 hit "Alphabet Street," for which Prince later charged a hefty fee. The song then veered into a sonically expansive evocation of the deep South, serving as a soul-bearing reminiscence about the Black Experience Post-Slavery, inspired by the passing of Speech's grandmother and brother, and concluded with a spirit-lifting vocal performance by Dionne Farris. It won near-universal acclaim, topped critics' polls across both rock and rap media as the year's best single, and was a huge hit, reaching number one on Billboard's R&B chart in July 1992 and number six on the Hot 100 that same month. "A RESTED DEVELOPMENT's 1992 debut single, Tennessee, kicked off with a warped sample of Prince, taken from his 1988 hit Alphabet Street." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Oh. My.
