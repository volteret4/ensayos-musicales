## songs
**All Cried Out (1986)** : The third single from their debut album, this heartbroken piano ballad was transformed into a female-male duet between Lisa Lisa and Full Force members Paul Anthony and Lucien Boleget-Lue George, breaking the group into the top 10 with a peak at number 8. It was a traditional pop ballad with booming syn drums, showing their sonic versatility and willingness to move away from freestyle for chart success. "All Cryed Out finally broke Lisa Lisa into the top 10, peaking at number 8." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Point of No Return Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Can You Feel The Beat** : This song's entire chorus was interpolated as a breakdown in Nina Sky's 2004 number 12 hit "Move Ya Body". It was also interpolated in the Black Eyed Peas' 2020 collaboration with Maluma, "Feel The Beat," despite that track having no other sonic connection to freestyle. "Nina Skye quote the entire chorus of Lisa Lisa and Colt Jam's Can You Feel The Beat as a breakdown in the middle of the song." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Point of No Return Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Head to Toe (1987)** : Broadly classified as a freestyle song, this track is more accurately a hybrid that applied elements of freestyle production and 80s syncopation to a sound that resembled 60s Motown, specifically Diana Ross and the Supremes. It reached number one on the Hot 100 for the week ending June 20, 1987, making history as freestyle's first ever number one pop hit. "When it reached number one on the Hot 100 for the week ending June 20, 1987, it earned a place in history as freestyle's first ever number one pop hit." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Point of No Return Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**I Wonder If I Take You Home (1985)** : This 1985 hit is a quintessential example of the freestyle movement. It bluntly addresses a teen girl's dilemma about sleeping with her boyfriend, being significantly more explicit in its theme than earlier songs like The Shirelles' "Will You Love Me Tomorrow?", despite lacking curses or overt sex talk. The song features a pulsating, quantized backing track, contrasting with earlier orchestral styles. "Lisa Lisa and Colt Jam's 1985 hit, I wonder if I take you home. A quintessential example of the freestyle movement we are going to discuss in this episode is about bluntly a teen girl fretting over whether she should sleep with the boy she's dating." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Point of No Return Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Lost in Emotion (1987)** : Following "Head to Toe", this smash hit leaned even more heavily into the Motown sound and less into freestyle, characterized by synthesizer-run zylafeng and kongas. It topped the Hot 100 in October 1987, contributing to the platinum success of their album "Spanish Fly". "Lost in Emotion was another smash, topping the hot 100 in October of 87." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Point of No Return Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/

## curiosities
**Formation and Multicultural Identity** : Lisa Lisa and Cult Jam was formed by the Brooklyn rap crew Full Force, envisioning a "multi-cultural black and Latin group." They auditioned Lisa Velis and hired Alex Spanador Mosley (guitarist/bassist) and Mike Hughes (drummer/keyboardist) to form the trio, a structure that aided their marketability on pop radio and MTV. "Full Force were a Brooklyn rap crew, and they were the ones who formed Lisa Lisa and Colt Jam as a multi-cultural black and Latin group." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Point of No Return Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**1987 Crossover Success & Genre Dilemma** : In 1987, Lisa Lisa and Cult Jam achieved the biggest crossover success for any freestyle act, topping both the Crossover 30 chart and, for the first time, the Hot 100. This raised questions about whether they were moving away from pure freestyle to achieve such mainstream appeal. "the biggest small sea crossover of 1987 by any freestyle act came from Lisa Lisa and cult jam who not only topped the new crossover 30 chart but even in a first topped the hot 100." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Point of No Return Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Pop Potential Maximization** : The Full Force production team strategically maximized Lisa Lisa's pop potential by subtly referencing freestyle elements without creating full-on freestyle jams, a trend that aligned with the pervasive influence of freestyle in 1987. "Full force had figured out how to maximize Lisa Lisa's pop potential, allude to freestyle without creating full on freestyle jams." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Point of No Return Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Evolving Away From Freestyle** : Despite Full Force's efforts to cross Lisa Lisa over to pop audiences, the group moved almost entirely away from freestyle on their third album, "Straight To The Sky." Later, on her subsequent album, Lisa Lisa sang over hip house beats provided by producers from C+C Music Factory, further illustrating her shift in sound. "Even Lisa Lisa and Colt Jam, which full force was always trying to cross over with pop audiences, and almost entirely away from freestyle." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Point of No Return Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Current Activity** : Lisa Lisa remains active as a performer, participating in successful concert tours since the mid-2010s, often packaged with other vintage freestyle acts for audiences who remember the 80s. "Lisa clearly isn't giving up on the genre." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Point of No Return Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
