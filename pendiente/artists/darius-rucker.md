## albums
**Back to Then (2002)** : Darius Rucker began recording this contemporary R&B album in 1999. Initially rejected by Atlantic Records, who considered it to have "no hits" and not fitting their R&B promotion strategy, Rucker eventually released it in Summer 2002 on Hidden Beach, Jill Scott's neo-soul imprint, distributed by Epic Records. The album debuted and peaked at number 127 on the Billboard 200 and was off the chart in two weeks, with none of its singles charting on Black Radio or the R&B chart. It featured a duet with Jill Scott and is described as a "charming anomaly" in his catalog, a "little hippie R&B record" that struggled due to its genre ambiguity and changing R&B market trends. "Back to then, debuted and peaked on the Billboard 200 at number 127 and it was off the album chart in just two weeks." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > A Little Love and Some Tenderness Edition Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Learn to Live (2008)** : Produced by Frank Rogers, this album marked Rucker's official entry into country music and was his debut on Capitol Nashville. Rucker co-wrote nearly every track, collaborating with songwriters like Clay Mills, Chris DuBois (transcribed as DuWa), Rivers Rutherford, and an "still relatively unknown" Chris Stapleton (on "Forever Road"). It debuted at number one on the Top Country Albums chart on October 4th, 2008, and went platinum by March 2010. The album generated four massive country hits, including three consecutive number ones: "Don't Think I Don't Think About It," "It Won't Be Like This For Long," and "Alright," plus a number three hit, "History in the Making." "Rucker worked solidly within the Nashville system, producing his country debut album Learn to Live with established producer Frank Rogers, who'd worked with country hit makers like Brad Paisley, Trace Adkins, and Josh Turner." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > A Little Love and Some Tenderness Edition Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/

**Wagon Wheel (2013)** : This 2013 number one hit for Darius Rucker has an interesting backstory: Bob Dylan wrote an unfinished fragment called "Rock Me Mama" in 1973; Ketch Secor of Old Crow Medicine Show added new verses, gave it a two-step tempo, and released it as "Wagon Wheel" in 2004, sharing 50-50 publishing with Dylan. Rucker's version was an "across-the-board smash," topping Billboard's Country Airplay Chart and Hot Country Songs (which by then included digital data), and reaching number 15 on the Hot 100, his highest solo pop chart position. It was certified for 3 million downloads by 2014 and 11 times platinum (sales and streams) by 2022. "The Rucker chart topper that pop crossover fans are likely to know was his 2013 number one, which has an interesting backstory." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > A Little Love and Some Tenderness Edition Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/

## curiosities
**Grand Ole Opry Induction (2012)** : Darius Rucker was inducted into the Grand Ole Opry in 2012. He is the only African American musician to be permitted membership in the Opry since Charlie Pride in 1967. "Darius Rucker...was inducted into the Opera in 2012." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 53 - More than Charley Pride - African Americans in 70s Country | https://www.ftr70.com/
**Early Musical Eclecticism** : Darius Rucker, born in 1966 outside historic Charleston, consistently defied stereotypes regarding the types of music a young Black man would listen to, developing a truly eclectic array of influences. As a child, he loved The Beatles, his parents' soul records (Al Green, Gladys Knight, Otis Redding), and later, hard rock bands like Kiss. In high school, he sang Kenny Rogers songs and listened to Boston, Journey, and Kansas, stating, "I have always liked what I liked." "Rucker, born in 1966, just outside of historic Charleston, had always defined the stereotype of what types of music a young black man would listen to." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > A Little Love and Some Tenderness Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f6f64fe6d21276e433e
**High School Suspension for Music Taste** : While in high school, Darius Rucker was once suspended for a day for driving into the school's parking lot with his Boston cassette playing at full blast. This incident highlights his early, defiant individuality and commitment to his diverse musical preferences, even when they went against perceived norms. "He was once suspended for a day from high school for driving into the school's parking lot with his Boston cassette at full blast." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > A Little Love and Some Tenderness Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f6f64fe6d21276e433e
**Discovery by Mark Bryan in College** : As a sophomore at the University of South Carolina, Darius Rucker first caught the attention of freshman and budding guitarist Mark Bryan when Bryan overheard Rucker singing Billy Joel's "Honesty" in the dorm shower. This impromptu performance sparked their collaboration and the eventual formation of Hootie & the Blowfish. "Tim Summer... tells the story of how Darius Rucker, a U of SC sophomore, first caught the ear of freshman and budding guitarist Mark Bryan. **Profound Influence of R.E.M.** : Darius Rucker first heard R.E.M.'s "South Central Rain" on MTV while in high school, an event he describes as a "moment" that "blew my mind" and "changed the way I heard music." From that day forward, R.E.M. became everything he wanted to be, shaping his musical aspirations and leading him to learn and play their songs throughout high school and college. "That band instantly became everything I wanted to be. **Experiences with Racial Epithets in College** : During his college years, Darius Rucker frequently endured racial epithets at the fraternity houses and bars where he and his predominantly white bandmates (Mark Bryan and Dean Felber) played. He relied on his bandmates to support him during these difficult moments, an experience that influenced his interpretation of songs like "Hold My Hand" as a protest against hatred. "Rucker had already spent college enduring racial epithets at the frat houses and bars he and his white bandmates played, and he relied on Mark and Dean to have his back." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > A Little Love and Some Tenderness Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f6f64fe6d21276e433e
**Later Career Genre Shift to Country** : After Hootie & the Blowfish's moment passed at the end of the 1990s, Darius Rucker faced a unique challenge in finding a new genre for his voice. He eventually found a new home as a chart-topper in the 21st century in country music, a form of "Southern Heritage music" that had not incubated a new Black solo singer in decades. This shift successfully brought many of his 90s fans along and exemplifies his ability to defy genre pigeonholes. "Amazingly, in the 21st century, Darius Rucker found himself a chart-topper all over again, in a completely different genre, and he brought many of the fans who loved his 90s work along for the ride." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > A Little Love and Some Tenderness Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f6f64fe6d21276e433e

**Historical Country Chart Double Triumph (2008)** : On October 4th, 2008, Darius Rucker achieved a double triumph on the Billboard charts: his single "Don't Think I Don't Think About It" rose to number one on Hot Country Songs, becoming the first country number one by a Black soloist since Charley Pride in 1983 (a 25-year gap). In the same week, his album "Learn to Live" debuted at number one on the Top Country Albums chart. This occurred just one month before Barack Obama was elected as the first Black president in American history, adding to its cultural significance. "On the Billboard charts dated October 4th, 2008, Darius Rucker pulled off a double triumph." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > A Little Love and Some Tenderness Edition Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Sustained Country Success** : Darius Rucker's country chart conquest was not a fluke; his debut album "Learn to Live" generated four massive country hits, including three consecutive number ones. Over his first decade as an official country singer, he scored seven number one country hits out of a dozen top tens, establishing himself among the top tier of country hitmakers alongside Kenny Chesney, Toby Keith, and Rascal Flatts. "Anyone who expected Rucker's country chart conquest to be a fluke were disabused of that notion right quick." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > A Little Love and Some Tenderness Edition Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Broad Appeal and Genre Blending** : Darius Rucker's career, particularly through a song like "Wagon Wheel," is seen as a metaphor for his ability to supersede genre. His music, originally based in folk and rock, transformed into Americana, appealing to both country and pop listeners, mirroring his own multi-genre identity.
