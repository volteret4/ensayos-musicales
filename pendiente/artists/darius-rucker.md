## curiosities
**Grand Ole Opry Induction (2012)** : Darius Rucker was inducted into the Grand Ole Opry in 2012. He is the only African American musician to be permitted membership in the Opry since Charlie Pride in 1967. **Early Musical Eclecticism** : Darius Rucker, born in 1966 outside historic Charleston, consistently defied stereotypes regarding the types of music a young Black man would listen to, developing a truly eclectic array of influences. As a child, he loved The Beatles, his parents' soul records (Al Green, Gladys Knight, Otis Redding), and later, hard rock bands like Kiss. In high school, he sang Kenny Rogers songs and listened to Boston, Journey, and Kansas, stating, "I have always liked what I liked." "Rucker, born in 1966, just outside of historic Charleston, had always defined the stereotype of what types of music a young black man would listen to." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > A Little Love and Some Tenderness Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f6f64fe6d21276e433e
**High School Suspension for Music Taste** : While in high school, Darius Rucker was once suspended for a day for driving into the school's parking lot with his Boston cassette playing at full blast. This incident highlights his early, defiant individuality and commitment to his diverse musical preferences, even when they went against perceived norms. "He was once suspended for a day from high school for driving into the school's parking lot with his Boston cassette at full blast." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > A Little Love and Some Tenderness Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f6f64fe6d21276e433e
**Discovery by Mark Bryan in College** : As a sophomore at the University of South Carolina, Darius Rucker first caught the attention of freshman and budding guitarist Mark Bryan when Bryan overheard Rucker singing Billy Joel's "Honesty" in the dorm shower. This impromptu performance sparked their collaboration and the eventual formation of Hootie & the Blowfish. "Tim Summer... tells the story of how Darius Rucker, a U of SC sophomore, first caught the ear of freshman and budding guitarist Mark Bryan. Mark overheard Darius singing Billy Joel's honesty in the dorm shower." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > A Little Love and Some Tenderness Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f6f64fe6d21276e433e
**Profound Influence of R.E.M.** : Darius Rucker first heard R.E.M.'s "South Central Rain" on MTV while in high school, an event he describes as a "moment" that "blew my mind" and "changed the way I heard music." From that day forward, R.E.M. became everything he wanted to be, shaping his musical aspirations and leading him to learn and play their songs throughout high school and college. "That band instantly became everything I wanted to be. From that moment forward, there was nothing more important in my life than REM." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > A Little Love and Some Tenderness Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f6f64fe6d21276e433e
**Experiences with Racial Epithets in College** : During his college years, Darius Rucker frequently endured racial epithets at the fraternity houses and bars where he and his predominantly white bandmates (Mark Bryan and Dean Felber) played. He relied on his bandmates to support him during these difficult moments, an experience that influenced his interpretation of songs like "Hold My Hand" as a protest against hatred. "Rucker had already spent college enduring racial epithets at the frat houses and bars he and his white bandmates played, and he relied on Mark and Dean to have his back." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > A Little Love and Some Tenderness Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f6f64fe6d21276e433e
**Later Career Genre Shift to Country** : After Hootie & the Blowfish's moment passed at the end of the 1990s, Darius Rucker faced a unique challenge in finding a new genre for his voice. He eventually found a new home as a chart-topper in the 21st century in country music, a form of "Southern Heritage music" that had not incubated a new Black solo singer in decades. This shift successfully brought many of his 90s fans along and exemplifies his ability to defy genre pigeonholes. "Amazingly, in the 21st century, Darius Rucker found himself a chart-topper all over again, in a completely different genre, and he brought many of the fans who loved his 90s work along for the ride." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > A Little Love and Some Tenderness Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f6f64fe6d21276e433e
