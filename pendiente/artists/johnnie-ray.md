## curiosities
**Early 1950s Success** : Johnny Ray achieved a string of hits in the very early 1950s in the US, with his biggest hits coming in 1951. His main period of success in the UK was 1956 to 1958. "in the US, his biggest hits came in 1951, and he had a string of hits in the very early 50s." ← Episode 97： ＂Song to Woody＂ by Bob Dylan | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3DufLVcZyc4
**Influence on Bob Dylan** : Ray's music was the first to captivate a young Robert Zimmerman (Bob Dylan), though music historian Clinton Heylin incorrectly suggested Dylan might have confused him with Johnny Ace, mistaking the timeline of Ray's US and UK success. "He was first captivated by Johnny Ray." ← Episode 97： ＂Song to Woody＂ by Bob Dylan | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3DufLVcZyc4
**Managed by Al Green (Mobster)** : Johnny Ray was one of the biggest names in Whitepop music at the time and was managed by local mobster Al Green. "He managed Johnny Ray, one of the biggest names in Whitepop music at the time." ← Episode Sixty-Four： ＂Reet Petite＂ by Jackie Wilson | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BYU-H4NvnmE
**UK Tour Requirement (Early 1950s)** : When Johnny Ray toured the UK in the early 1950s, he was required to employ British musicians as a condition of performing, due to the mutual protectionist agreement between the American and British musicians' unions. "Similarly, when Johnny Ray had toured the UK in the early 50s, he'd had to employ British musicians." ← REUPLOAD Episode 71： ＂Willie and the Hand Jive＂ by Johnny Otis | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ez6BCUlI6Rg
**Pre-Rock Teen Idol (Early 1950s)** : Known as John Alvin Ray, he was the quintessential pre-rock teen idol of the early 1950s, preceding Little Richard and Elvis Presley as 50s teenagers' favorite singer. "John Alvin Ray, better known as Johnny Ray, was the ultimate pre rock teen idol of the early 50s." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Mighty Real Edition Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Musical Style** : His arrangements leaned towards jazz and blues, and his vocals towards traditional pop, earning him recognition as a precursor to rock and roll. "And though his arrangements, he'd closer to jazz and blues and his vocals toward traditional pop, Ray is widely considered a precursor to rock and roll." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Mighty Real Edition Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Bisexual Identity and Arrests** : Biographers widely understand Johnny Ray to have been bisexual. He was married only once and briefly, and faced multiple arrests for propositioning male lovers, including undercover police. "Johnny Ray is now widely understood by his biographers to have been bisexual." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Mighty Real Edition Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Posthumous References in Pop Music** : Johnny Ray's name was invoked in the lyrics of two number one hits from the 1980s: Dexys Midnight Runners' 1983 chart-topper "Come On Eileen" and Billy Joel's 1989 number one hit "We Didn't Start the Fire," securing his place in pop history despite not being able to be fully himself during his lifetime. "He's name checked in the first verse of the 1983 chart topper Come On I Lean by Dexie's Midnight Runners." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Mighty Real Edition Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**1950s Queer Chart History** : Johnny Ray is mentioned as an artist from the 1950s who is part of the "decades of queer chart history." His inclusion highlights the early, often implicit, presence of LGBTQ artists in popular music. "But a tour through these decades of queer chart history from Johnny Ray and Little Richard in the 1950s to Kim Petrus and Chapel Rhone in the 2020s shows the rainbow arc of history bending ever upward." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Mighty Real Edition Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
