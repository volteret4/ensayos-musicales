**Masterjam (1979)** : Produced by Quincy Jones, who reunited the "warring factions" of Rufus and Chaka Khan amidst Khan's rising solo stardom. The album's sound departed from the "Rufus of old," adopting "Quincy's brand of smooth yacht soul." It featured the R&B chart-topper "Do You Love What You Feel," which helped prolong the band's career. ## songs
**Ain't Nobody (1983)** : This track became Rufus's last hit and is arguably considered their "tour de force." Originating from keyboardist David "Hawk" Wolinski experimenting with the Lindrum drum machine and a sequencer, it created an "off-kilter and strangely enveloping" rhythm, with a monster kick drum doubled by John Robinson. It reached number one on the R&B chart in October 1983 and number 22 on the Hot 100 two months later, remaining their most played radio track. "It was Rufus' last hit, and arguably their tour de force." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Killing Me Softly Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**At Midnight (My Love Will Lift You Up) (1977)** : This song with Chaka Khan reached number 1 on the R&B chart and number 30 on the pop chart. It exemplified the group's sound gradually "edging away from funk and closer to disco bit by bit," with the band still billed as "Rufus featuring Chaka Khan." "As on their 1977 number 1 R&B, number 30 pop hit at Midnight, My Love Will Lift You Up." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Killing Me Softly Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/

"That was recorded by the American breed in 1968, which became Rufus, which then, yes, becomes Rufus of Rufus and Chaka Khan." ← For the Record - The 70s > Ep. 2 - Countryish Music of the 1970s (Encore) | https://www.ftr70.com/
**Chicago Band Pivots to Funk** : Rufus, a Chicago band fronted by Chaka Khan, finally broke big in 1974 after pivoting their sound from rock towards pure funk.
