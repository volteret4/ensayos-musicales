**Macarena (1993)** : Antonio Romero and Rafael Ruiz, performing as Los Del Rio, met in 1962 in Dos Hermanas, Spain, and started playing music together at age 14. In 1992, while on tour in Venezuela, they improvised a song inspired by a flamenco dancer. They initially named the girl in the song "Madalena" and wrote lyrics about her moving her body and having a good time, fantasizing about shopping and moving to New York, and having multiple boyfriends while her main boyfriend, Vitorino, was away. The name was quickly changed to "Macarena" because it was the name of the Virgin Mary celebrated in Seville and also one of the group members' daughters. The song was honed during rehearsal the next day with musicians and recorded back home in Seville at a famous Flamenco studio. This version features hand clapping, percussion, and acoustic flamenco guitar strumming, with the "hey, Macarena" part appearing at the end. They did not consider the song remarkable during its creation. "Madalena initially was the name of the girl. **Macarena (1993)** : Los Del Rio debuted the Macarena at Seville's Big Spring Fair in 1993. The fair, which includes tents for parties, food, and music performances, saw the song rapidly gain popularity, with attendees talking and playing it everywhere by the fair's end, signaling to the band that they had their biggest hit to date. Although inspired by a dancer, no choreography existed for this original version. It became Spain's song of the summer, an infectious, up-tempo anthem, a massive and unexpected accomplishment for the duo, who had traditionally appealed to an older, regional Spanish audience.
