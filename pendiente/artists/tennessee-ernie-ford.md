**Sixteen Tons (1955)** : This big hit from Tennessee Ernie Ford became a song Johnny Cash "fell in love with" when it was released. Cash became a lifelong fan of its writer, Miles Travis, and the imagery in "Sixteen Tons" likely served as an inspiration for the central metaphor of "I Walk the Line." "That song was quite possibly inspired by a line in 16 Tums, the big hit from Tennessee Ernie Ford that year which Cash fell in love with when it came out and which made Cash a lifelong fan of his writer, Miles Travis." ← Episode 37： ＂I Walk The Line＂ by Johnny Cash | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=1uhw6KjgSME
**Sixteen Tons (1955)** : Tennessee Ernie Ford first performed this 1947 country song by Merle Travis on his five-day-a-week NBC daytime TV show. He only decided to record it when his label, Capitol Records, insisted on needing another single. Thanks to its exposure on television, "Sixteen Tons" became the fastest-selling single in recording industry history to date, selling a million copies in just three weeks. "He first performed 16 tons, a 1947 country song by Merle Travis on his TV show, and only thought to record it when his label, capitalized records, insisted they needed another single." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > A Deal with the TV God Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
