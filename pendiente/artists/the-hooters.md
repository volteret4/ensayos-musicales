## curiosities
**Contribution to Cyndi Lauper's Album** : Rick Chertoff, who produced Cyndi Lauper's debut album "She's So Unusual" and was also working on The Hooters' debut, incorporated elements of their sound into Lauper's record. Keyboardist Rob Hyman and guitarist Eric Bazilian formed the "backbone" of Lauper's studio band for the album. "Chertoff, at the time, was working on the debut album by an up-and-coming Philadelphia band called The Hooters. Lauper liked elements of The Hooters sound, and so the groups Rob Hyman and Eric Bazzillion became the backbone of her studio band for the album." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Be the One to Walk in the Sun, Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Songwriting Collaboration with Cyndi Lauper** : Keyboardist Rob Hyman contributed the phrase "a suitcase of memories" to Cyndi Lauper, which she used as a foundation to build the song "Time After Time." He subsequently co-wrote the hit song with her. "Keyboardist Rob Hyman had a phrase Cindy liked, a suitcase of memories that she thought she could build a song around." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Be the One to Walk in the Sun, Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Guitarist's Role on "Money Changes Everything"** : Eric Bazilian, The Hooters' guitarist, was instructed by Cyndi Lauper and producer Rick Chertoff to emulate the "tough guitar sound" of The Clash's "London Calling" for Lauper's cover of "Money Changes Everything," as his initial playing style was deemed too Dylanesque. "Cindy felt the band was playing in a very dated Dillonesque style, so she told guitarist Eric Bazzillion and producer Rick Chertoff to go and listen to the clash." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Be the One to Walk in the Sun, Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Guitarist's Role on "Girls Just Want to Have Fun"** : Eric Bazilian was asked by Cyndi Lauper to adjust his "white reggae style" playing towards "more Motown-esque riffs" for the arrangement of "Girls Just Want to Have Fun," a contribution that helped define the song's transformative sound. "Lopper asked him to keep the reggae feel but shift his playing toward more motown-esque riffs." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Be the One to Walk in the Sun, Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
