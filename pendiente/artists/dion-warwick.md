## curiosities
**Musical Upbringing and Influences** : Dion Warwick was born into the family that produced The Drinkard Singers, a gospel vocal troupe led by her aunt Sissy Houston (Whitney Houston's mother). While she grew up singing in church, she is not strictly a gospel, jazz, or soul singer, but rather "exophisticated." Ella Fitzgerald and Sarah Vaughn were clear influences. She emerged from the Brill Building environment of early 60s post-Elvis Presley, pre-Beatles urban pop. "Warwick was born into the same family that produced the drinker singers. The gospel vocal troupe, led by Whitney's mother and Dion's aunt, Sissy Houston." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Killing Me Softly Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**The Bacharach/David Muse** : After Bert Bacharach was impressed by her vocals on The Drifters' "Mexican Divorce" (on which she sang backup with The Drinkard Singers affiliates), he introduced her to lyricist Hal David. Within months, Bacharach and David began writing songs uniquely suited to Warwick's self-assured voice, including "Don't Make Me Over" and "Anyone Who Had A Heart," effectively making her their muse. "Within months, Bacarack and David began writing songs uniquely suited to Dion Warwick's self-assured voice, including Don't Make Me Over." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Killing Me Softly Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Pop vs. R&B Crossover Skill** : Dion Warwick often faced the "knock" that she was more pop than R&B, partly due to her sophisticated interpretations. However, her "code switching skill" allowed her to achieve #1 R&B hits (like "Walk On By" and "Reach Out For Me") simultaneously with her pop success, and her soulful interpretations of Bacharach-David songs were later celebrated and reinterpreted by R&B artists like Luther Vandross. "However, this reputation for pop smoothness, the Lied Warwick's code switching skill, her reach out for me was also a number 1 R&B hit." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Killing Me Softly Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Interpretation of White Artists' Songs** : More than any other Black female singer of the 1960s, Dion Warwick is credited with "decoding" the interpretation of white artists' songs for a multi-racial audience, as exemplified by her cover of Herb Alpert's "This Guy's In Love With You" as "This Girl's In Love With You." "So you could fairly argue that more than any black female singer of the 60s, Deon Warwick decoded the interpretation of white artists' songs for a multi-racial audience." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Killing Me Softly Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Post-Bacharach/David Career Reboot** : After her long-time mentors Burt Bacharach and Hal David split in the early 1970s, Warwick sued them to resolve her contract, necessitating a career makeover. For a few years, she even added an 'E' to her last name (Warwicke). Her reboot was solidified by teaming with Philadelphia producer Thom Bell, a progenitor of Philly Soul, who produced her hit duet "Then Came You" with Philippe Wynne of The Spinners.
