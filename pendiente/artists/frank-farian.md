## albums
**Blind Before I Stop (1986)** : Farian produced this 1986 LP for American singer Meat Loaf during Meat Loaf's "Steinman-less wilderness years," a period of estrangement from his mentor Jim Steinman. The album sold poorly and generated only one single, a duet with John Parr, which did not chart on the Hot 100 and barely missed the UK top 30. ## songs
**Baby Do You Want A Bump** : This single marked Farian's first hit using a "made-up group" name, Boney M. It interpolated Prince Buster's 1964 Ska/Rocksteady hit "Al Capone" and became a hit in Holland and Belgium, leading Farian to form Boney M into a real touring and recording group. "This single was called Baby Do You Want A Bump, and this time for an artist's name, Frankie Ferrien decided to hide behind a made-up group that he called Boney M." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Blame It on the Feign, Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/

## curiosities
**Birth Name and Early Career** : Born Frans Reuter in Kern, Germany, Farian initially changed his name to Frankie Ferrien. He worked as a cook before pursuing music, attempting to release records under Frankie Ferrien, including covers of US and UK hits, but found no success until the mid-1970s. "The man born Frans Reuter in Kern, Germany had already changed his name to Frankie Ferrien. **Creator of Boney M and Millie Vanille** : Farian was the mastermind producer, musician, and pop impresario behind both the 70s Euro Disco act Boney M and the 80s Dance Pop duo Millie Vanille. His vision and methods, including the use of uncredited vocalists and lip-syncing frontmen, characterized both projects. **Continued Career Despite Fraud** : Despite being the mastermind behind "pop music's most famous fraud" (Millie Vanille), Farian was not run out of the music business. Instead, he continued to produce and co-write with Dance Pop acts for decades, with his productions still being heard on older Dance Pop radio stations and streaming mixes into the 2020s.
