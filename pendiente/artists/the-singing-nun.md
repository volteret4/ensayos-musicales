## songs
**Dominique (1963)** : This French-language song by a Belgian nun was the number 1 hit on December 14, 1963, placing it directly above The Kingsmen's "Louie Louie" on the charts. Its popularity in France was partially attributed to the word "Nika" in its chorus, which was a French swear word meaning "to fornicate." **Dominique (1963)** : An "acoustic pop song in French," this hit was credited to a "Belgian woman in a holy sister's habit." It reached number one on Billboard's Hot 100 during the week ending December 7th, 1963, shortly after the sudden death of the 35th U.S. president. It is recognized as "one of the 1960s oddest chart toppers." **Dominique (1963)** : This French-language song, written by Decker about St. Dominique (founder of her order), was the highlight of an LP originally intended as a gift for convent visitors. It quickly scaled the Hot 100, cracking the top 10 the week President John F. Kennedy was killed, and became number one two weeks later, providing comfort during a national tragedy. ## curiosities
**Obscenity Controversy Parallel** : The Singing Nun's hit "Dominique" became popular in France partly because its chorus contained "Nika," a French swear word for "to fornicate," creating a parallel to the imagined obscene lyrics controversy that fueled "Louie Louie's" success. "The singing nunsong had a chorus that went to Dominica, Nika, and one of the reasons it had become popular was that in France, but not in Belgium where she lived, Nika was a swear word, and expletive meaning to fornicate." ← Episode 106：＂Louie Louie＂ by the Kingsmen | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=S3agGJsxjpE
**Blocked Streisand's Album (1963)** : The Singing Nun prevented Barbara Streisand's "The Second Barbara Streisand Album" from reaching number one on the charts in the fall of 1963. Streisand has openly admitted to still holding a grudge about this chart-blocking feat. "Do you know who kept me from reaching number 1? The singing Nanda Manikani, Kasson Alain, and something like that? People just couldn't get that catchy refrain out of their heads, I guess. **Monastic Origins** : Jeanne-Paule Marie Decker, a 30-year-old from Belgium, joined the Fishermont monastery where her fellow sisters nicknamed her "Sister Smile." In the convent, she played guitar, sang, and wrote songs, eventually being encouraged by her sisters to record them. "Decker's was a 30-year-old from Belgium who had joined the Fishermont monastery. **Accidental Commercial Success** : Initially, Decker and a group of nuns recorded an LP at a local Brussels studio as a gift for convent visitors. However, the label was so impressed they offered to press thousands of copies and distribute them across Europe, leading to commercial release. "So, a group of the nuns went to a local Brussels studio and asked if they could record an LP as a gift to give the convent's visitors. The label was so taken with the songs they offered to press thousands of copies and distribute them across Europe." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Singing Nuns and Green Tambourines Edition Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**U.S. Release and Eerily Timed Hit** : An American label executive heard "Dominique" and offered to release the album in the U.S. under the name "The Singing Nun," also issuing "Dominique" as a single. The record debuted on the Hot 100 in early November 1963 and reached number one two weeks after President John F. Kennedy's assassination, resonating as a "needed sense of wholesomeness" during national grief.
