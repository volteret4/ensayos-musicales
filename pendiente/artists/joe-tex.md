## songs
**Baby You're Right (1961)** : Joe Tex wrote this song, and both he and James Brown recorded it. However, only James Brown achieved a hit with it, reaching number two on the R&B chart and number 49 on the pop chart in 1961. This track, while not directly sparking the rivalry, predates their more explicit musical feud. **Skinny Legs and All (1967)** : This was a "funky chatty" top 10 hit for Joe Tex in 1967. Years after a violent incident involving James Brown, Tex claimed in interviews that Brown had tried to sabotage the chart rise of this song. James Brown later co-opted the main lyric of this song for his 1969 hit "Mother Popcorn." ## curiosities
**Rivalry with James Brown** : Joe Tex had a long-standing rivalry with James Brown, stemming from their parallel careers as soul stars and personal entanglements. This rivalry included musical "potshots" in interviews and on record, as well as a violent incident. "Before we leave the 60s, I would be remiss if I didn't talk about the rivalry on wax, on stage, and even with fisticuffs between Soul Singer, Jo Tex, and Godfather of Soul, James Brown." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Here’s the Beef Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4ef3154465cd600c4ef5
**Cape Routine Parody and Violence (1963)** : At a joint live show in 1963, Joe Tex performed an onstage parody of James Brown's famous cape routine, theatrically tangling himself in the cape, which the crowd recognized as a jab at Brown. This infuriated Brown, who later confronted Tex at an after-show party, brandishing two shotguns and firing them. Seven people were reportedly injured, though Tex himself was not present. Brown's management allegedly paid "hush money" to cover up the incident.
