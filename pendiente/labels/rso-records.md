# label - RSO Records

## curiosities
**Robert Stigwood's Label** : RSO Records was the label founded by Robert Stigwood, which released much of the Bee Gees' breakthrough disco material and related artists. **White Label Release Tactic** : For the single "Jive Talkin'," RSO Records sent it to radio stations as an unmarked white label to prevent DJs from prejudging the song based on the Bee Gees' then "commercially poisonous name." "Heaging its bets, RSO records, Robert Stigwood's label, sent this song to radio stations in an unmarked white label. They didn't want DJs pre-judging the single based on the Bee Gees commercially poisonous name." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Nights on Broadway Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Distributor Change (1976)** : In early 1976, RSO Records switched its distribution from Atlantic Records to Polygram, which impacted the Bee Gees' collaboration with producer Arif Mardin. "At the start of 1976, Robert Stigwood's RSO Records switched distributors from Atlantic to Polygram." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Nights on Broadway Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Unprecedented Chart Run (1977-1978)** : From December 1977 through May 1978, RSO Records achieved an unprecedented chart run, holding the number one spot on Billboard's Hot 100 for five solid months with hits from the Bee Gees, Andy Gibb, and Player. "Thus began an unprecedented chart run, not only for the Brothers Ghib, but for their record label, Robert Stigwood's RSO Records." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Nights on Broadway Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Sergeant Pepper's Soundtrack Shipment (1978)** : RSO Records shipped the double LP soundtrack for "Sergeant Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band" to music retailers in massive quantities at the height of "Bee Gees Mania." Despite initial chart success, thousands of unsold sets were returned, leading to the coining of the term "shipped gold-returned platinum." **Bee Gees' Manager and Record Company (1979)** : During their 1979 Grammy acceptance speech for "Saturday Night Fever," the Bee Gees thanked "RSO Records" as their record company and Robert Stigwood, who owned the label, as their manager. **Lawsuit for Release (Early 1980s)** : The Bee Gees spent much of the first half of the 1980s suing their former manager Robert Stigwood and demanding to be released from his RSO Records label due to unpaid royalties. They eventually settled and won their freedom from the label.
