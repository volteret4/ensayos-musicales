# label - VJ Records

## curiosities
**Founding and Early Success** : VJ Records was a Chicago independent label founded and run by the African American husband and wife team of Vivian Carter and James Bracken. The "V" and "J" in the label's name represented their initials. VJ established its reputation with smash R&B records, such as Gene Chandler's "Duke of Earl," and scored hits in 1962 with the Jersey Boy Doo-wop group The Four Seasons. **First US Label for The Beatles** : VJ Records made history by becoming the first label to issue a Beatles single in America, releasing "Please Please Me" in February 1963. This occurred after Atlantic Records and other major US labels passed on the opportunity. The Beatles' name was even misspelled "Beattles" on this first single's label, indicating their unknown status in the US at the time. "Think of it. **Financial Troubles and Mismanagement** : In 1963, VJ Records experienced significant financial difficulties, including a royalty dispute with The Four Seasons who sought a better deal, and a gambling debt incurred by VJ's president in Las Vegas, which "decimated the company's 1963 operating budget." These issues severely hindered VJ's ability to effectively promote and distribute Beatles singles, causing the label to miss opportunities despite holding the rights. **Exploiting Loopholes During Beatlemania** : As Beatlemania exploded in America, VJ Records, despite its earlier financial woes, aggressively exploited "every possible loophole in their agreement with EMI" to release as much Beatles product as possible. This included issuing "Twist and Shout," an album cut in England, as a single on their newly founded Tali subsidiary, and bringing older singles like "From Me to You" to the Hot 100.
