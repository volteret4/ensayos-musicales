# label - LaFace Records

## curiosities
**Foundation and Success** : LaFace Records was co-founded by producer L.A. Reed and his songwriting partner Babyface. In the mid-1990s, the label was "riding high" with successful R&B acts like OutKast and the girl group TLC. The label played a pivotal role in Pink's early career, signing her group Choice and later Pink as a solo artist. "L.A. Reed, the producer turned president of LaFace Records. **L.A. Reed's Role** : L.A. Reed was the producer turned president of LaFace Records. He signed Pink's first group, Choice, and later gave Pink an ultimatum to go solo, which launched her career. He was initially skeptical of Pink's rock-oriented direction for her second album, "Missundaztood," believing it would be "commercial suicide," but eventually relented, giving her the "opportunity to fail." **Founding and Mission (Late 80s - Early 90s)** : Founded by Antonio L.A. Reed and Kenneth Babyface Edmonds, LaFace Records was envisioned as a "Motown-like label" at the turn of the 1990s. Its primary focus was on generating crossover R&B hits that appealed equally to pop and Black radio, evolving into a "rap conversant label" rather than a hardcore rap label. "So it made sense that Reed and Edmonds would form their own Motown-like label. **Early Roster and Success (Early 90s)** : By the early 1990s, LaFace Records was generating smashes with rising stars, including Pebbles and, most notably, TLC, who became their biggest seller of the decade with their blend of sultry R&B and a dash of rapping. The label's success with artists defining Atlanta's sound burnished its reputation as a "red hot center of black music in the 90s." **First Rap Act: Outcast (1992)** : Outcast became LaFace Records' first-ever rap act, having auditioned for L.A. Reed in 1992. This move marked a departure for a label better known for urban pop and crossover R&B, and ultimately helped redefine its artistic scope. **Christmas Compilation (1993)** : In 1993, LaFace released "A LaFace Family Christmas," a compilation album that included Outcast's debut single "Players Ball," along with more overtly jolly material from TLC and Toni Braxton. The album was considered a "lark" for the label and was not a big hit, peaking at number 186 on the Billboard album chart.
