# label - Jive Records

## curiosities
**High Priority Solo Debut (2002)** : In the summer of 2002, Jive Records invested heavily in launching the solo career of Justin Timberlake, a high-priority artist emerging from a blockbuster boy band, with the aim of crafting a unique adult sound for his debut album. **Strategy for Pure Pop** : Although known at the time for its R&B and hip hop acts, Jive Records viewed signing Britney Spears as their strategic entry point into the "pure pop sweepstakes," seeing her as a more cost-effective talent than established "power divas." "Jive founder Clive Calder thought Spears might be their ticket into the pure pop sweepstakes." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Give Me a Sign Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Max Martin Collaboration** : Jive contacted Max Martin to explore his potential involvement with their new signing, Britney Spears. They also had a demo already prepared for her consideration. "Jive contacted him to see if he could do anything with their new signing and he flew to New York to meet her." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Give Me a Sign Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Title Change for "...Baby One More Time"** : Jive President Barry Weiss proposed replacing the phrase "Hit Me" with ellipses in the title of Britney Spears' debut single to avoid misinterpretation of the phrase as advocating physical violence. Consequently, the song was officially released in fall 1998 as "...Baby One More Time." "It was finally Jive President Barry Weiss, who had the idea to replace the phrase Hit Me in the title with ellipses." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Give Me a Sign Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Strategy for CD Sales** : Jive Records, Backstreet Boys' label, actively sought to sell as many full-length CDs as possible, sometimes by withholding singles for retail release, as was the case with "I Want It That Way." "As we discussed in our Great War against the single episode of Hit Parade, Backstreet's label, Give Records, was looking to sell as many full length CDs as possible." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > We Want It That Way Edition Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**"Everybody (Backstreet's Back)" Release Controversy** : Jive Records President Barry Weiss initially opposed releasing "Everybody (Backstreet's Back)" as a single and kept it off the US album due to its incongruity, but eventually added it to a 1998 reissue after Canadian and US radio stations began playing it.
