# label - MGM Records

## curiosities
**Million-Dollar Advance (1965)** : MGM Records offered Roy Orbison a million-dollar advance to sign with them, a deal brokered by his manager Wesley Rose who was in conflict with Monument's Fred Foster. However, immediately after Orbison signed with MGM, his string of hit records dried up. **National Release of "Old Home Filler-Up"** : Following the significant local success of CW McCall's single "Old Home Filler-Up an' Keep on a-Truckin' Cafe," which sold 30,000 copies in the Midwest, MGM Records acquired the rights for a national release. This move propelled the song onto the Billboard Country Charts, where it reached number 13. "It sold 30,000 copies in the Midwest, prompting MGM records to release it to a national audience." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Decoder Ring - We Got Ourselves a Convoy | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Compelled Release of "Mrs. Brown"** : MGM Records initially had no intention of issuing Herman's Hermits' cover of "Mrs. Brown, You've Got a Lovely Daughter" as a single from their album "Introducing Herman's Hermits." However, due to overwhelmingly strong pre-release airplay from US radio stations, the label was compelled to release it as a single in April 1965. "The Hermits label, MGM, had no intention to issue the Hermits cover of Mrs. Brown as a single from their album introducing Herman's Hermits.
