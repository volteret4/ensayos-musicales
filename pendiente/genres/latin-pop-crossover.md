**Mid-90s Novelty Perception** : As late as the mid-1990s, Spanish-language hits were largely perceived as novelties in the American music market, primarily enjoyed for parties or dancing, and not much else. This perception began to change dramatically with a pivotal event at the 1999 Grammy Awards. **90s Club as Crossover Hub** : The most significant Latin crossovers of the 1990s predominantly emerged from the club scene rather than "the street corner." This environment produced sleeper hits and decade-defining pop hits, such as The Blackout All-Stars' "I Like It Like That" and Los del Río's "Macarena." **Increased Bilingualism in Pop (Post-1987)** : Following the chart-topping success of Los Lobos' all-Spanish "La Bamba" in 1987, it became more plausible for mainstream pop records to incorporate lyrics in Spanish or Portuguese. This opened the door for globally phenomena like Kaoma's "Lambada" at the turn of the 90s.
