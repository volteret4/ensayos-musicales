## curiosities
**Mainstream Presence in Early 2000s** : In the early 2000s, the pop-punk genre maintained a significant presence on the music charts, largely dominated by popular bands like Glint 182 (Blink-182) or Green Day. This commercial success was characteristic of the rock landscape before the rise of the garage and post-punk revival movements. **Mid-90s Galvanization** : PopPunk, a genre dating back to the 70s, experienced significant revitalization and commercial "galvanization" in the mid-1990s, largely propelled by the success of bands like Green Day and The Offspring. This period saw the genre rise in popularity, paving the way for its later fusion with emo. **Dominance in Early 2000s** : From 2000 through 2002, pop punk continued to "run the table," producing massive rock radio hits, including Green Day's "Shivery Minority" and Sum 41's "Beastie Boys' Esk Fatlet." Despite these songs being modern rock number ones and sounding like pop records, they achieved only minor success on the Hot 100, indicating a ceiling for pure pop punk on mainstream pop radio. **Classic Triumph in 2004** : Classic pop punk experienced one final major triumph in 2004, spearheaded by Green Day, the band credited with popularizing the hybrid genre in the first place. Their album "American Idiot" marked a significant commercial and critical peak, demonstrating the enduring power of pop punk.
