**20th Century Crossover Hits** : In the 20th century, songs like "Juan Thadamera" and "Oye Comová" achieved standard status and resonated with Anglo audiences, partly because they were popular, catchy, and frequently featured in commercials or movies, becoming part of pop culture. They were also easily accessible to non-Latino audiences, which paved the way for later crossover efforts where songwriters integrated elements appealing to Anglo tastes. "In the 20th century, songs like Juan Thadamera and Oya Comová became standards. And they resonated with Anglo audiences. **"Version 1.0 Latin Boom" (1999-2001)** : This period saw artists like Ricky Martin, Jennifer Lopez, and Enrique Iglesias achieve significant chart success, though their music often didn't align with what many Latino communities were listening to at the time. This phase was characterized by a "tokenized movement" where artists often adapted their sound to appeal to Anglo audiences, although it retrospectively opened doors for future Latin artists to maintain their authenticity. "what I'm calling the version 1.0 Latin boom of 99 to 2001, were you excited, dismayed, confused by what Ricky Martin and JLo and Enrique all did during that period?" ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Bridge - Hits Don’t Lie | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Post-Despacito Era** : The period following "Despacito" in 2017 has led to a less compromised form of Latin music crossover, with artists like Bad Bunny, Cardi B, and J Balvin charting successfully. This shift suggests that Latin music is now so ingrained that it's considered "part of our fabric" rather than merely a crossover phenomenon, reflecting the changing demographics and listening habits of the country. However, by the late 2010s, Spanish language pop hits began crossing over "on their own terms," increasingly achieving success without necessarily requiring English verses.
