# genre - Novelty Record

## curiosities
**Early Chart Dominance** : Novelty records were "chart dominators" in the early rock era, particularly in the 1950s and 1960s, which is often referred to as their "golden era." They frequently topped the Hot 100. **Challenge to Sustain a Career** : Novelty records, by their very nature, are typically "flashes in the pan" meant to capture a specific cultural moment, making them a difficult way for artists to sustain a long-term career, especially compared to perennial holiday-related hits. "most novelty hits by their very nature are flashes in the pan, meant to capture a specific cultural moment." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The White and Nerdy Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**"Producer's Medium" in Early 60s** : In the early 1960s, novelty records were often considered a "producer's medium," where the creative vision and execution rested more with the producer, and the performing artists were often incidental to the success of the track. "The novelty records were a producer's medium, and the artists were incidental." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The White and Nerdy Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Airplay Limitation in 70s/80s** : By the 1970s and 1980s, radio airplay became a significant limiting factor for novelty records. The humor of such songs could diminish with the repetition of top 40 radio rotation. Tightening radio formats, especially with the rise of FM's "Morning Zoo" programs, often confined novelty singles to specific time slots like morning or drive time, preventing heavy rotation. "Airplay was indeed often the limiting factor with funny or novelty records. The amusement would become less amusing amid the repetition of top 40 radio rotation." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The White and Nerdy Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
