# genre - Electro-funk

## curiosities
**Dominant in Early Memphis Clubs** : In the early Memphis club scene, electro-funk from artists like Nucleus or Africa Bambaataa was the primary music for dancing before the emergence of New York hip-hop and the fusion efforts of DJ Spanish Fly. "En la escena de club de Memphis, se avellaba principalmente el electrofang de Nucleus o África Bambata." ← Memphis Rap： Cuando el rap era puro terror | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lWBpNa-xSt4
**Mainstreaming by Ronson and Mars** : Mark Ronson and Bruno Mars were instrumental in reintroducing and mainstreaming the electro-funk sound for pop audiences through their mega-hit "Uptown Funk." This particular sound had largely only charted within R&B in its heyday, especially in the wake of the early 80s disco backlash, making its mainstream pop success with "Uptown Funk" a notable achievement. "Mark Ronson and Bruno Mars then essentially reintroduced and mainstreamed this electro-funk sound for pop audiences." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Lenny on Mars Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f54d1ba84fb8f0463bf
**Ready for the World Mistaken for Prince** : Flint, Michigan's Ready for the World topped the Hot 100 in 1985 with an electro-funk jam, "Oh Sheila," that many pop fans "mistook for Prince," highlighting the powerful influence of Prince and the Revolution's imperial phase. "Ready for the World, topped the Hot 100 with an electro-funk jam, O'Sheela, that many pop fans mistook for Prince." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Material Girl in an Imperial World Edition Part 1 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4f03154465cd600c5379
**Billy Ocean's Reinvention** : Billy Ocean's collaboration with producer Keith Diamond in 1984 resulted in a "sleek, urbane electro-funks sound" for "Caribbean Queen," heavily indebted to Michael Jackson's "Thriller" era, which helped bring Ocean back from pop wilderness. "In 1984, Ocean hooked up with a new producer, Keith Diamond, who offered a sleek, urbane electro-funks sound that was indebted to thriller era Michael Jackson." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > What’s 1984 Got to Do with It Edition Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
