**Soundtrack Innovation in the 1970s** : In the 1970s, the Blaxploitation film genre became a significant user of "new soundtrack freedoms," employing non-diagetic "badass hits" to both propel on-screen action and drive album sales. These soundtracks often reflected the vision of a single major R&B artist, with *Shaft* being a prime example, leading to historical chart success and Oscar wins for Black composers. "By the 70s, the film genre that was making the best use of the new soundtrack freedoms was Blacksploitation." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Hooked to the Silver Screen Edition Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Defining the Film Movement** : The Blaxploitation film movement was significantly defined by Melvin Van Peebles' movie *Sweet Sweetback's Baadasssss Song*, released in the spring of 1971. Music was recognized as an integral component of this genre from its inception. **New Bar for Music in Film** : The success of Isaac Hayes' *Shaft* soundtrack set a new and higher bar for the quality and impact of music in blaxploitation films, influencing subsequent works like Curtis Mayfield's *Superfly* soundtrack.
