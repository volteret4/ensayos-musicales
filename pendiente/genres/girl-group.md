# genre - Girl Group

## curiosities
**Lyrical Innovation by Gerry Goffin** : With "Will You Love Me Tomorrow," Gerry Goffin found a lyrical formula that would define girl groups from that point on, introducing a kind of "female adolescent emotion" that had previously been absent from pop music. The lyrics from a woman's perspective worrying about a one-night stand resonated with millions. "Goffin's initial dislike of rock and roll music had been because of what he perceived as his lyrical vacuity, and in Will you love me tomorrow, he found a lyrical formula that would define girl groups from that point on." ← Episode 89： ＂Will You Love Me Tomorrow？＂ by the Shirelles | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Kns2HGcuSg
**New York Phenomenon in Early Years** : In their early years, girl groups were largely a phenomenon based in New York. The Shirelles, though from Pasey, New Jersey, were part of this regional trend. "girl groups in the early years were largely phenomenon based in New York and that's more or less the case with the Chirrells who didn't come from New York itself but from Pasey, New Jersey about 16 miles away." ← Episode 89： ＂Will You Love Me Tomorrow？＂ by the Shirelles | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Kns2HGcuSg
**Setting the Template for Success** : The Shirelles are credited with being the most successful of the early girl groups and, more than any other, set the template for all subsequent girl groups, influencing major acts like The Supremes, The Marvelettes, The Crystals, and The Ronettes. "Oddly the group that became the most successful of the early girl groups and the one that more than any other set the template for all those that would follow never wanted to become professional singers and almost had to be forced against their will at every stage." ← Episode 89： ＂Will You Love Me Tomorrow？＂ by the Shirelles | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=7Kns2HGcuSg
**Early 60s Heyday** : The girl group concept experienced its heyday in the early 1960s, with vocal ensembles like The Shirelles, The Ronettes, and The Shangri-La's dominating the charts. **Survival Through the 70s and 80s** : After the dominance of The Supremes in the late 1960s, the initial girl group era receded, but the genre was kept alive through the 1970s and 1980s by disco, new wave, and freestyle dance music. **90s Renaissance** : The 1990s saw a "Girl Group Renaissance," driven by a new generation of vocalists who emerged after the rise of Hip Hop, infusing the pop charts with a fresh sound. **Producer-Driven Approach in 90s** : Unlike the more organically formed groups of the 1960s, girl groups in the 1990s and beyond were typically conceived and assembled within the music industry machine by producers, similar to the creation of boy bands like New Kids on the Block and Backstreet Boys. "This producer-driven approach would become the norm for girl groups in the 90s. **21st Century "Angle" Requirement** : In the 21st century, girl groups increasingly needed a unique "angle" or brand beyond just their music to break through, as exemplified by groups like The Pussycat Dolls and Danity Kane. **American Dry Spell in 2000s** : There was an "American Girl Group Dry Spell" for the first half of the 2000s, with most significant girl group activity and number one hits occurring in the UK with groups like Sugababes and Girls Aloud. "The American Girl Group Dry Spell wasn't finally broken until halfway through the Aughts, when a troupe of burlesque dancers who called themselves the Pussycat dolls broke big on the Billboard charts." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Be My Baby-Baby-Baby Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4eed8e6dd12efb02c340
**"Girl Power" Slogan** : Although not originated by the Spice Girls themselves, the slogan "Girl Power" was heartily embraced by them and, in retrospect, was acknowledged by critics to have had a profound impact on millennial girls' formative memories, akin to the Lilith Fair. "The girls came up with neither these monikers nor their eventual slogan Girl Power, but they heartily embraced them as essential to their brand." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Be My Baby-Baby-Baby Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4eed8e6dd12efb02c340
**Solidarity and Friendship** : The underlying premise of girl groups throughout pop history, from The Shirelles to The Shangri-La's, TLC, and Destiny's Child, is that "women in harmony generate solidarity." This ethos is epitomized by the Spice Girls' debut single message, "Friendship never ends." "The premise of the Spice Girls is the premise of the girl group throughout pop history, from the sherelles to the Shangri-La's TLC to Destiny's Child. Women in harmony generate solidarity.
