# genre - Instrumentals

## curiosities
**1960s Chart Eclecticism** : In the 1960s, Top 40 radio was "more eclectic," allowing instrumental hits like The Tornados' "Telstar" and Mr. Acker Bilk's "Stranger on the Shore" to reach number one. These lyric-free hits served as a "side dish" to the vocal tracks by crooners, teen idols, and rock and roll combos. **Decline on Pop Radio** : The presence of instrumentals on the charts began to "tail off on the charts in the 1980s." This decline coincided with radio becoming "more corporate," as programmers and advertisers favored songs that "couldn't be mistaken for jingles." Consequently, instrumentals remain relatively rare on contemporary pop radio, more commonly heard in dance clubs, stadiums, or as older tracks. **Kitsch Factor** : Many 1960s instrumentals, such as Quincy Jones's "Soul Bossa Nova," were characterized as "kitschy," sometimes "even bordering on tongue in cheek."
