# genre - Doo-Wop

## curiosities
**Isley Brothers' Genre Shift** : The Isley Brothers transitioned into singing doo-wop songs at Ronald's insistence after the death of Vernon, influenced by their favorite act, Billy Ward and The Dominoes. Their first record with Richie Barrett, "Don't Be Jellis," was described as a standard doo-wop ballad. "If in turn, the group could stop singing gospel music and start singing duwop songs, like the Brothers' favourite act, Billy Ward and the Dominoes." ← Episode 81： ＂Shout＂ by the Isley Brothers | https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=USJDERCABjk
**Cross-Racial Charting (1950s)** : Doo-wop classics such as "Earth Angel" and "Why Do Fools Fall in Love" experienced black-fronted original versions charting alongside or slightly behind covers by white vocalists, illustrating a significant aspect of early cross-racial interpretation in popular music. "the duop classic, Earth Angel, was first recorded by black quartet The Penguins in 1954... **Influence on Madonna's "Cherish" (1989)** : Madonna's song "Cherish" incorporated elements of Doo-wop, alongside 60s Sunshine Pop and girl group energy, as a "sequel to True Blue." "echoing the prior song's girl group energy with elements of Dewap and 60's Sunshine Pop." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Material Girl in an Imperial World Edition Part 2 | https://shows.acast.com/hit-parade/episodes/695d4ef10c30a1408dc291b9
**Falsetto Usage** : Falsetto vocals were notably used in doo-wop music, forming an early precedent for the Bee Gees' later vocal style. "In the rock era of the 20th century, falsetto was used practically from rock and roll's birth, especially in duop and surf music." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > The Nights on Broadway Edition | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Early Boy Band Influence** : Early doo-wop harmonizing, combined with rock and roll energy, characterized the sound of groups like Frankie Lymon & The Teenagers, who are considered the first true boy band of the rock era. "Their sound was unique across of early do-wop harmonizing with rock and roll energy topped by youthful lyrics." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > We Want It That Way Edition Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Survival Post-British Invasion** : The breakthrough of The Beatles in the US in 1964 and the subsequent British Invasion made it more challenging for doo-wop groups to achieve chart success. However, this older musical style did not vanish, illustrating the common observation that new genres typically make it harder for, but do not eradicate, previous ones. "the US breakthrough of The Beatles in 1964, which spawned the British invasion in America, did make it harder for easy-listening crew-ners, girl groups, and do-up." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > These Are the Good Times, Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
