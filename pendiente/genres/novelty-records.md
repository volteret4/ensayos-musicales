# genre - Novelty records

## curiosities
**UK Christmas Number One Tradition** : Novelty records have a significant history in the UK Christmas Number One tradition, often beating more "serious or solemn" songs for the top spot. Examples include St. Winifred's School Choir, Mr. Blobby, and Bob the Builder. **Prevalence in the 1960s** : The 1960s were "positively packed with silly singles," encompassing comedy records, instructional dance crazes, British Invasion records that took on novelty characteristics, and songs inspired by comic strips or cartoons, exemplified by The Royal Guardsmen's "Snoopy vs. the Red Baron" and Shirley Ellis's "The Name Game." These "shitposting songs" were a consistent feature on the decade's charts. "The decade was positively packed with silly singles, comedy records, instructional dance crazes, British invasion records that sounded like novelties." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Singing Nuns and Green Tambourines Edition Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Modern Relegation** : Unlike the 1960s, when "purely whimsical singles" frequently made the top ten, novelty songs are now largely "relegated to YouTube" and online platforms. Contemporary examples, such as those by Kendrick Lamar or Sabrina Carpenter, are more "snarky than silly," differing from the earlier era's direct whimsical approach. "We're now long past the age where purely whimsical singles make the top ten, unless you count the funniest tracks by Kendrick Lamar or Sabrina Carpenter, and they're more snarky than silly." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Singing Nuns and Green Tambourines Edition Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
