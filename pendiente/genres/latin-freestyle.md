# genre - Latin freestyle

## curiosities
**Emergence and Defining Tracks** : John "Jellybean" Benitez's "The Mexican" featuring Jenny Han is identified as a precursor to Latin freestyle, foreshadowing the birth of this subgenre characterized by its prominent Latin percussion and fusion with freestyle elements. "Jelly Bean's The Mexican was closer to where freestyle was headed next, the birth of what came to be called Latin freestyle." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Point of No Return Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Dominant Shift by Mid-1980s** : By mid-1985, the momentum within the broader freestyle genre had decidedly shifted towards Latin freestyle, indicating its growing popularity and influence over other variations. "the momentum in freestyle had shifted by mid-85 toward Latin freestyle." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Point of No Return Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Domination of Miami Airwaves** : Latin freestyle completely took over the airwaves in Miami, where listeners favored its "uncut" sound, characterized by cheap, synthetic yet artful production designed for car stereos and dance floors. "In Miami, where Latin freestyle had utterly taken over the airwaves, they liked the uncut stuff." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Point of No Return Part 2 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Global Affirmation** : The collaboration between Pet Shop Boys and Lewis Martinee on the track "Domino Dancing" served as a significant affirmation that Latin freestyle had achieved global recognition and influence.
