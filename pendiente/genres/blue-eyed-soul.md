# genre - Blue-eyed soul

## curiosities
**Bee Gees' Pre-Disco Genre** : The Bee Gees were known as a blue-eyed soul band before disco and their involvement with "Saturday Night Fever" revived their careers and made them superstars. **The Righteous Brothers as Duo** : The Righteous Brothers are identified as a "Blue-eyed Soul Duo," known for their definitive version of "Unchained Melody." **Hall & Oates as Premiere Act** : Daryl Hall and John Oates became the premiere blue-eyed soul act of their day, topping both pop and R&B charts with their hit "I Can't Go For That." **Hall & Oates' Defining the Genre (1980s)** : Hall and Oates became central to defining "Blue Eyed Soul" in the 1980s. Their imperial run in chart history cemented their place in this genre. "Darryl Hall and John Oats define the 80s, the MTV era, and Blue Eyed Soul entering the record books in one of the greatest imperial runs in chart history." ← Hit Parade Music History and Music Trivia > Rock ’n Soul, Part 1 | https://my.slate.com/podcasts/feeds/hit-parade/
**Crossover Appeal** : This genre, exemplified by Bobby Caldwell's "What You Won't Do for Love" and Toto's "Georgie Porgy," frequently perched on the border between pop and R&B. This led to significant crossover success on both pop and black radio charts, demonstrating it was not an isolated R&B crossover by white performers.
