# genre - Album-Oriented Rock (AOR)

## curiosities
**Dominance in 70s FM Radio** : AOR was a prevalent genre that dominated FM Radio during the first half of the 1970s, recognized for its lengthy musical suites, often 15-20 minutes long, performed by elaborate rock ensembles featuring multiple guitar and keyboard solos, jazzy instrumental breakdowns, and string arrangements. **Badge of Honor for Non-Singles (1970s)** : In the 1970s, at the height of AOR, many acts considered it a "mark of respectability" not to put out 45 RPM singles and seek Top 40 airplay for certain songs. This strategy guaranteed that these songs, despite copious FM radio play (like Led Zeppelin's "Stairway to Heaven"), would be ineligible for Billboard's Hot 100, which required songs to be retail singles. **Full LP Side "Uber Songs"** : In the early to mid-70s, entire LP sides would be filled by these "uber songs" composed of movements, as demonstrated by bands such as Pink Floyd, Genesis, Jethro Tull, and Yes. This format was later adapted by Donna Summer and her producers for dance music. **Influence on Disco Album Format** : Donna Summer, her producers (Moroder and Bellotte), and record executive Neil Bogart effectively brought concepts from album-oriented rock to dance music, particularly with the *Love to Love You Baby* album, which featured a full-side suite of extended-length music. **Billy Joel's Shift to AOR** : Billy Joel re-oriented his piano playing towards an AOR "power anthem" in April 1972, with a live performance of "Captain Jack" at Philadelphia's Sigma Sound studio for WMMR. This shift helped him gain significant radio play and eventually a major label contract. **Bob Dylan's 1970s Transformation** : In the 1970s, Bob Dylan commercially transformed into an "album rocker," a staple of FM radio's Album-Oriented Rock (AOR) format. Like canonical AOR acts of the era such as Yes, Neil Young, Jethro Tull, and Led Zeppelin, Dylan often emulated their practice of not releasing many of his best-known songs as singles in America.
