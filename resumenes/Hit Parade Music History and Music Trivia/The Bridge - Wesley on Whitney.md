## albums
**Whitney Houston (1985) - Debut Album** : This album represented a gradual transition towards broader appeal, featuring singles like "Thinking About You" and "You Give Good Love" that served as "breadcrumbs" to maintain connection with her black audience, rather than an immediate push for pop crossover. "Whitney Houston, the 85 album, which is more of a gradual transition."
**Whitney (1987) - Pop Crossover Album** : This album marked a decisive shift towards pop crossover success, with the content having "nothing gospel about that album." Following its release, her career was described as "off to the races" in terms of pop chart dominance. "But once they do on the second album, it's kind of off to the races."

## songs
**Step by Step (1996)** : This song is a cover of an Annie Lennox original and was featured on the soundtrack for Houston's 1996 film, "The Preacher's Wife." It was a pop-soul track recorded for a gospel-centered film about a black Baptist church, designed to bridge her white and black fanbases. It reached number 15 on the Hot 100 and number 29 on the R&B chart in 1997. "Like so many hits in Houston's career, Step by Step aimed to bridge her white and black fanbase."
**Saving All My Love for You (Live on Silver Spoons)** : Whitney Houston performed this song live on the television show "Silver Spoons." The arrangement of her live performance was notably "saltier" and more expressive than the recorded album version, showcasing a different vocal approach. "If she had put out that version of that song, if she sang it that way in the recorded version, and not live, by the way, she sang it live on Silver Spoons."
**Where Do Broken Hearts Go? (1988)** : Despite the powerful and "blackness" of Whitney Houston's voice, this single was not considered a "black radio" song and "didn't fit with anything else on Black radio" in 1988, being described as "the lamest sounding song on the chart" for that year. Its nomination in its category was seen as "pretty generic," yet it was a testament to her vocal ability and her fun approach to "boring" songs. "But where did broken hearts go? I mean, like there is nothing black radio about that single except the power and blackness of the voice singing it."
**I Want to Dance with Somebody (1987)** : This song is considered a quintessential 80s track, perfectly matched by its music video which also embodied the era. For this presentation, Whitney Houston was perceived to have "whitened herself," adopting a "skinny, dippy blonde" or "blonde-ish" look, including her hair. "the I want to dance with somebody is a quintessential, it is a quintessential 80s sounding thing, right?"
**Greatest Love of All (1986)** : This song was the final single released from her 1985 debut album, hitting number one in mid-1986. Originally written for a Muhammad Ali biopic and first sung by George Benson, it garnered more affection from white audiences than black audiences, as if the latter received a "memo" that it wasn't for them. Its anthemic nature led to it being a frequently sung "school graduation song" and "school assembly song," characterized by generic arrangements that lacked "Black vernacular" and were only made special by Whitney Houston's voice. "And the thing that sort of makes them special is only Whitney used to singing them. There's nothing musically interesting about any of those songs."
**One Moment in Time** : This song was similar to "Greatest Love of All" in that it was often sung in schools, with arrangements described as generic and lacking a "Black vernacular" style. "We had to sing that a lot."
**Thinking About You (1985)** : Featured on her first album, this song was described as a favorite for its funkiness and as representing "the R&B of the moment." It showcased a lower vocal register with elements of yearning, grit, and sex, where Houston would "put a little stank on a song" to demonstrate her capability. "It is it's it's it's the funkiest song in the record. And it's very fun."
**Higher Love (Cover)** : This is a cover of a Steve Winwood song that Whitney Houston recorded, originally intended for release in Japan. A posthumous version of her cover was widely played on the radio, even heard in drugstores. "She recorded it. It was going to be released in Japan."

## curiosities
**Appearance on Silver Spoons** : Whitney Houston made an early television appearance on "Silver Spoons" where she sang "Saving All My Love for You" live. Her memory of her appearance, including her look, was confirmed to be accurate during preparation for a documentary. "She looked exactly as I remembered her."
**Crossover Strategy and Backlash** : Whitney Houston's career was characterized by an intentional effort to "bridge her white and black fanbase," successfully crossing over and "staying" as a pop star, unlike many who "just visited." This success, however, led to backlash from the black audience, as none of her biggest pop hits appeared in the top 10 of Billboard's Hot Black Singles chart, prompting a perception that her music was no longer "for us" and that she "owned the rest of the country" while occupying space in "black world." "And why does Whitney in particular get that backlash? That's some of what I was trying to pinpoint."
**Vocal Genius and Relatability** : Described as "the best singer ever" by Wesley Morris, Whitney Houston possessed the unique ability to "sing the blandest shit and make it sound really beautiful," drawing comparisons to Nat King Cole. She would infuse generic songs with "grit and some sex" and "throw in a little bit" of vocal embellishment to show her skill, even while some black listeners felt a questioning of her authenticity due to her immense crossover success. "She could sing, she's a little bit like Nat King Cole. She could sing the blandest shit and make it sound really beautiful."
**Live Performance Legacy** : Her greatest vocal performances were consistently delivered live in concert, where she would dynamically "rearrange these songs" and dedicate extended periods, sometimes "eight minutes on one gospel number," to showcase her powerful and improvisational singing style. "The greatest singing Whitney Houston ever did was life. All of her lives in here."
**Rock and Roll Hall of Fame Induction** : Whitney Houston was eventually inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, a move that was seen as part of a "process of rehabilitation" in the critical mind, despite the ongoing argument about the Hall's parameters. "So when Houston is finally on the rock and roll hall of fame, I've watched enough artists go through a process of rehabilitation, you know, in the critical mind."
**Rolling Stone List Ranking (2002)** : A 2002 Rolling Stone list of the greatest singers of all time controversially ranked Whitney Houston, while she was still alive, one spot below Steve Winwood. "on a rolling stone list of the greatest singers of all time, she was one spot below, wait for it. Steve Winwood, whom she's now got a hit covering on the radio right now."
**First Woman Album Chart Debut at #1** : Her 1987 album "Whitney" holds the distinction of being the first album by a woman to debut at number one on the album chart, a feat achieved before the charts were computerized by the Soundscan system. "Whitney's stunning chart records, including the first woman to debut at number one on the album chart with her Whitney album before the charts were computerized with the sound scan system."

# curiosity

**Crossover Music Phenomenon (80s-90s)** : The concept of "crossover" was critical in 80s and 90s music, with a distinction between artists who "just visited" pop charts (like Freddie Jackson and Levert) and those who "stayed" (like Michael Jackson, Prince, Lionel Richie, and Whitney Houston), enduringly bridging white and black fanbases. The phenomenon raised questions about the relationship of the black audience to artists who achieved massive mainstream success. "Right. Which begs the question about crossover and what it means."
**Black Radio in the 1980s** : Black radio during the 1980s featured a distinct sound and set of artists, such as Freddie Jackson and Bobby Womack, and operated "in completely different worlds" from mainstream pop outlets like MTV, with a noticeable "lack of overlap" in their programming. "what was also getting played on black radio during the 80s?"
**MTV and BET Divergence** : In the 1980s, MTV and BET were "in completely different worlds," demonstrating a significant "lack of overlap" in the music and artists they showcased, underscoring racial segregation in music promotion and audience targeting. "They were just in completely different worlds."
**Rock and Roll Hall of Fame Parameters** : The criteria for induction into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame are often debated, particularly regarding the definition of the "rock era." The questioning of Whitney Houston's induction, especially compared to the unquestioned entry of Aretha Franklin and the continued exclusion of artists like Roberta Flack and Dionne Warwick, highlights inconsistencies in these parameters. "I'm shocked that it's an argument like me too. What are we arguing? Like how how strict are you going to draw these lines? Right."
**Soundscan System Impact on Charts** : The Soundscan system, a computerized method for tracking album sales, revolutionized how charts were compiled. Prior to its implementation, only six albums ever debuted at number one, including Whitney Houston's "Whitney," Stevie Wonder's "Songs in the Key of Life," Bruce Springsteen's "Live," and Michael Jackson's "Bad." "before the charts were computerized with the sound scan system."
**Spanish Language #1s on Hot 100 (2000-2010)** : The Billboard Hot 100 chart did not feature any Spanish-language number one hits between 2000 and 2010, though some Spanish songs did manage to reach the top 40 during this decade, with Daddy Yankee achieving the most. "In the years 2000 to 2010, there were no Spanish language number ones on the Billboard Hot 100, but a few songs in Spanish did hit the top 40."
**"Bodacious Awesomeness of Black Women" (1982-1996)** : This phrase describes a significant era for black women in music, spanning from approximately 1982 to 1996, which is seen as a foundational period connected to contemporary artists like Lizzo, Megan Thee Stallion, and Cardi B. "we're going to spend a lot of time talking about the bodacious awesomeness of black women from like 1982 to about 1995 or 96."
**Whitney Houston Documentaries** : Two documentaries about Whitney Houston were released: "Whitney Can I Be Me" by Nick Broomefield in 2017, and "Whitney" in the summer of 2018. "the one by Nick Broomefield, Whitney Can I Be Me was out... And the second documentary, which didn't come out until the summer of 2018, simply titled Whitney, you know, that was still to come."
