## labels
Nettwerk

## concerts
Lilith Fair
Peter Gabriel tour

## albums
**Touch (1989) - Debut LP** : Sarah McLachlan's debut album was released in 1989, and it initially only reached number 132 on the American album chart. It would eventually achieve gold certification, but not for another decade after its release. "Her debut LP, Touch, released earlier that year, only got as high as number 132 on the album chart."
**Solace (1992) - Sophomore Album** : This album, released in 1992, saw McLachlan blending the driving beats and folk songwriting style of her debut with bass-driven guitar rock. It made her a star in her native Canada, reaching the top 20 and achieving double platinum status there, but charted even lower in America than her debut despite a sizeable alt-rock hit. "She got closer in 1992 when her sophomore album, Solis, blended the driving beats and folk songwriting of her debut with bass-driven guitar rock."
**Fumbling Towards Ecstasy (1993) - Third Album** : McLachlan's third album, released in 1993, became her breakthrough in the US, achieving platinum status in early 1995 (nearly a year after its release) and double platinum by summer 1996. The album never peaked higher than number 50 on the Billboard 200, indicating its success was more a "sleeper hit" driven by sustained sales rather than immediate chart dominance. "Fumbling towards ecstasy just kept quietly selling."

## songs
**Steaming (1989) - Remix** : This remix marked Sarah McLachlan's first appearance on an American singles chart in 1989, reaching number 37 on Billboard's Club Play chart. This was her initial charting success in the US, predating her later rock and pop chart presence. "It was Billboard's Club Play chart, where a remix of her song, Steaming, reached number 37."
**Into the Fire (1992)** : The lead single from her album *Solace*, "Into the Fire" brought Sarah McLachlan into the Modern Rock chart's top five, peaking at number four in March 1992. This song showcased her evolving sound, incorporating elements of bass-driven guitar rock. "Into the fire, Solis's lead single brought Sarah McLaughlin into the modern rock top five, where it peaked at number four in March of 92."
**Possession (1994)** : The first track and lead single from *Fumbling Towards Ecstasy*, "Possession" was written by McLachlan based on the ravings of a real-life stalker who sent her deranged letters. The song is notable for its passionate intensity, dark tone, aching lyrics, and "high-lonesome cavernous sound." It was a number four hit on the Modern Rock chart in 1994, with unusual staying power, as pop stations slowly picked it up and rotated it into 1995. "McLaughlin wrote possession around the ravings of a real-life stalker who'd been sending her deranged letters."
**Good Enough (1995)** : A single released by Sarah McLachlan that achieved mid-charting success on the Modern Rock chart. It contributed to the sustained sales of her album *Fumbling Towards Ecstasy* alongside other singles. "Even as McLaughlin issued new singles, like the mid-sharding modern rock hits Good Enough and Hold On."
**Hold On (1995)** : Another single released by Sarah McLachlan, which was a mid-charting hit on the Modern Rock chart. This song, like "Good Enough," helped *Fumbling Towards Ecstasy* continue to sell steadily long after its initial release. "Even as McLaughlin issued new singles, like the mid-sharding modern rock hits Good Enough and Hold On."
**Building a Mystery (1997)** : This song by Sarah McLachlan made its debut on Billboard's Modern Rock Chart the week ending July 5th, 1997. This debut coincided with the first show of her Lilith Fair festival, marking a significant moment for both her individual career and her pioneering festival. "The week ending July 5th, 1997, when building a mystery by Sarah McLaughlin made its debut on Billboard's Modern Rock Chart."

## curiosities
**Early Career as "Stealth Rock Star" (1989-1996)** : For her first five years in the music industry, McLachlan's career was characterized as a "beta test" or a series of experiments to find her eventual sound. Leading up to the idea for Lilith Fair, she was not yet a "megastar" but rather a "stealth rock star," as her albums achieved success over time through quiet sales rather than immediate chart-topping. "McLaughlin's first five years were a kind of beta test, a series of experiments toward finding her eventual sound."
**Inspiration for Lilith Fair (1996)** : In mid-1996, while experiencing writer's block for her follow-up album to *Fumbling Towards Ecstasy*, Sarah McLachlan proposed the idea of doing a few shows with other female artists she had played with before. This idea, initially a distraction, was suggested by her manager Terry McBride, and McLachlan challenged them to book an all-female lineup, leading to the genesis of Lilith Fair. "I was having writer's block. I liked the idea of doing a few shows but I didn't want to do a whole tour."
**Coined "Lilith" Moniker (1996)** : At a September 1996 gig in Vancouver, British Columbia, McLachlan coined the name "Lilith" for her multi-artist show, named after the female figure in Jewish and Babylonian mythology who spurned Adam. This event marked the first time the name "Lilith" was officially applied to her collaborative performances. "At a September gig in Vancouver, British Columbia, McLaughlin coined a new moniker for this multi-artist show. Lilith, named for the female figure in Jewish and Babylonian mythology who supposedly spurned Adam the first man."

## albums

Her consistent success during this decade cemented her influence and reputation in popular music. "She spent the rest of the 70s scoring gold and platinum albums and inspiring dozens of female and male performers."

## songs
**Woodstock (1970)** : Joni Mitchell wrote and recorded "Woodstock" in early 1970, with the song directly about the Woodstock Music and Arts Festival. She was inspired to write it by Graham Nash, who recounted his experience at the festival while they were dating, as Mitchell herself missed the event due to manager David Geffen's advice to do a television show. "Woodstock, the song, was first written and recorded by Canadian singer-songwriter, Joni Mitchell."

## curiosities
**Missed Woodstock Festival (1969)** : Joni Mitchell, despite being invited to play the Woodstock Music and Arts Festival in 1969, did not attend. Her manager, David Geffen, advised her against going to Upstate New York, citing a television show commitment the next day and concerns about travel into and out of the festival site. "However, Mitchell was writing about a concert she herself never attended. In a now infamous decision, she had been invited to play the festival, but didn't even go to Upstate New York on the advice of her manager, David Geffen."
**Influence on Other Artists** : Joni Mitchell's work and sound served as an inspiration for dozens of female and male performers in the decades after her initial success. Notable artists like Emmylou Harris, Chrissie Hynde, and Prince have all testified to her profound influence. "Everyone from Emileu Harris to Chrissy Hind to Prince would testify to Joni's influence in the decades to come."
**Spiritual Godmother of Lilith Fair** : Although Joni Mitchell did not ultimately perform at Lilith Fair in the 1990s, she was considered a "spiritual godmother" to the festival. Her influence on female artists and her position as a voice of her generation made her an important symbolic figure for the all-women event. "Though Mitchell would not ultimately play Lil' Affair in the 1990s... she served as a kind of spiritual godmother to the festival."
**"Voice of Her Generation" Missing Key Event** : Mitchell, a woman who would become a significant "voice of her generation," was pressured into missing the signature live music event of that generation, Woodstock. This situation highlights a broader issue of women performers being marginalized or excluded from major rock events. "A woman who would become the voice of her generation was pressured into missing the signature live music event of her generation."

**Main Stage Acts Certified Gold and Sold Out (1997)** : The main stage acts at Lilith Fair were all "certified gold" and their performances were consistently "sold out." This shift in sound reflected and likely contributed to the changing landscape of alternative radio, which was pivoting away from women performers. "A new generation of more bro-friendly ultrock started coming down the pike, typified by sublime's scoff-flavored dude rock anthem, What I Got, a modern rock number one in 1996."
**Decline of Women-Led Acts on Alt-Rock Radio (Late 1990s)** : Even women-fronted groups like Garbage and Hole, which continued to do "decently" on alt-rock radio in 1996 and 1997, began to see their presence diminish before the end of the decade. This indicated a broader trend of female artists falling off alt-rock radio regardless of solo or group status. "But even these women led acts started falling off at ultrock radio before the end of the decade."
**Terry McBride's Role in Lilith Fair Genesis** : Terry McBride, CEO of Nettwerk (Sarah McLachlan's Canadian label) and her manager, played a crucial role in the initial development of Lilith Fair. He suggested McLachlan do a few shows with artists she had played with before, and he was then challenged by McLachlan to book an all-female lineup. "Terry was Terry McBride, CEO of McLaughlin's Canadian label network and also her manager. As he recalls it, it was McLaughlin who challenged her manager and promoters to book all female acts to accompany this brief flurry of gigs in the summer of 96."
**All-Female Lineup Debuts (June 14, 1996)** : The first performance of the multi-artist all-female lineup, a precursor to Lilith Fair, debuted in Clarkston, Michigan, just outside Detroit, on June 14, 1996. The show was a success, playing to a packed house of over 10,000 fans. "This four woman lineup debuted at a show in Clarkston, Michigan just outside Detroit on June 14, 1996 and they played to a packed house of more than 10,000 fans."
**Mythological Origin of "Lilith" Name** : The name "Lilith" for the multi-artist show was coined by Sarah McLachlan, named after the female figure in Jewish and Babylonian mythology who is said to have spurned Adam, the first man. This mythological reference imbues the festival with symbolic meaning about female independence and defiance. "Lilith, named for the female figure in Jewish and Babylonian mythology who supposedly spurned Adam the first man."
