# artist - Elvis Presley

## genres
Rock and Roll

## songs
**Don't Be Cruel (1956) - Original A-Side** : Presley was presented with this song in the studio as a new composition, furnished by his publishers and Brooklyn songwriter Otis Blackwell. It was recorded at the same session as Hound Dog and placed on the A-side when the 45 was pressed in summer 1956. Among radio DJs it was the more popular side, topping Billboard's Jockeys chart for most of August through October 1956 and ranking several notches above Hound Dog there. Rolling Stone ranks it among the 200 greatest singles in rock and roll. "At least, Don't Be Cruel was the A-side when the single was first released."
**Hound Dog (1956) - The B-Side That Took Over** : Presley's cover is enshrined at the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as one of the 500 songs that shaped rock and roll. It was written by Jerry Leiber and Mike Stoller in 1952 and had existed for more than three years and several versions before Presley recorded it. He knew the Big Mama Thornton original, but he began performing the song live in 1956 only after seeing the goofy 1955 cover by Freddie Bell and the Bellboys. He loved that version's comic qualities and inserted it into his show as comic relief. His teen girl audience did not take it as comedy and screamed at it. "Even when Presley was trying to be funny, he had managed to put the danger back in the song."
**Hound Dog Studio Recording - Management's Insistence** : Because the song had become a live favorite, Presley's management insisted in 1956 that he capture it in the studio. Reportedly Presley never intended to record it but, perhaps reluctantly, agreed, cutting it at the same session as Don't Be Cruel. "Reportedly, Presley never intended to record Houndog"
**Don't Be Cruel / Hound Dog (1956) - Third Number One and Record Chart Run** : This was Presley's third number one single and dominated the Best Sellers in Stores chart in the week of August 18, 1956, before the Hot 100 existed. Among DJs Don't Be Cruel led, but on the jukebox and best-seller charts Hound Dog was listed first. The two-sided hit spent 11 weeks on top of Best Sellers, the flagship chart before the Hot 100 launched in 1958. That record held for 36 years until Boyz II Men's End of the Road lasted 13 weeks in 1992. "that 11 weeks on top was a record, one it held for 36 years"

## curiosities
**Hound Dog Listed Above the A-Side on the Picture Sleeve** : Hound Dog was what TV variety shows asked Elvis to perform. He sang it on The Milton Berle Show, and a month later on The Steve Allen Show he sang it to a droopy basset hound. That TV version was so popular that the single's cover featured a still of Elvis singing to the dog and listed Hound Dog more prominently than Don't Be Cruel. "Elvis literally sang to a droopy bass at Hound."
**Hound Dog as the "Viral" Song of 1956** : Although Don't Be Cruel was positioned as the A-side, it quickly became clear that Hound Dog was the more viral song. "it quickly became clear that Houndog was, to borrow a 21st century term, the more viral song."
**Cover of George Harrison's Something** : Presley is among the artists who recorded the Beatles' Something, along with Frank Sinatra and Ray Charles. "it has been recorded by everyone from Frank Sinatra to Ray Charles to Elvis Presley"

# artist - Big Mama Thornton

## genres
Blues

## labels
Peacock Records

## songs
**Hound Dog (1953) - The Original** : Blues singer Big Mama Thornton's 1953 version of Hound Dog was written by Jerry Leiber and Mike Stoller, two white Jewish songwriters. It was a raw, racy precursor to rock and roll and a national hit, spending nearly two months at number one on Billboard's rhythm and blues chart in 1953. Legend has it that Peacock Records ran three pressing plants full time to keep up with demand. The record was re-recorded several times between 1953 and 1955, so Presley's version was not a direct cover of it. "a raw, racy precursor to Rock and Roll, written by two white Jews and made famous by a black woman."

# artist - Freddie Bell and the Bellboys

## songs
**Hound Dog (1955) - The Las Vegas Lounge Version** : This white Las Vegas lounge act cut an in-between cover of Hound Dog. They turned Leiber and Stoller's thinly veiled metaphorical lyrics ("you can wag your tail but I ain't gonna feed you no more") into a complaint to a literal canine ("you ain't never caught a rabbit"). Their version even featured "arf-arf" sounds. This goofy 1955 version is the one Presley began performing live in 1956. "They're the ones who replaced the racy with the ridiculous."

## albums
**Pet Sounds (1966) - The Beach Boys' Greatest Album** : The album landed in the week ending May 28, 1966, and spun off multiple hits. Its singles were Sloop John B (number 3), Wouldn't It Be Nice (number 8) and God Only Knows (a US B-side that peaked at number 39). Lyricist Tony Asher co-wrote most of the album with Brian Wilson. Good Vibrations, though recorded in 1966, was not on Pet Sounds. "the Beach Boys greatest album landed and began spinning off multiple hits"

## songs
**God Only Knows (1966) - Underperforming US B-Side** : Rolling Stone ranks it the 25th greatest song of all time, yet in the US it was a B-side that peaked at only number 39. Many critics now regard it as Brian Wilson's greatest composition. Paul McCartney has claimed it is his favorite song of all time, and Pitchfork's survey of the greatest songs of the 1960s placed it at number 1. It was sung by Brian's younger brother Carl Wilson and arranged by Brian with French horn, sleigh bells, harpsichord and a string quartet playing in counterpoint. "No less than Paul McCartney has claimed that God only knows his favorite song of all time."
**God Only Knows (1966) - The Problem With "God" in the Title** : In America the title gave the music industry pause. Co-writer and lyricist Tony Asher said that unless you were Kate Smith singing God Bless America, nobody thought you could say God in a song. Historian Domenic Priore claims the song was banned from the radio in some parts of the US for blasphemy, though it still scraped into the top 40. "no one thought you could say God in a song then."
**God Only Knows (1966) - UK A-Side and US Chart Run** : In various countries, including the United Kingdom, God Only Knows was the A-side and Wouldn't It Be Nice the B-side. In the UK it peaked at number 2 in summer 1966. In the US it made a belated Hot 100 debut at number 80, two weeks after Wouldn't It Be Nice. It reached number 39 by late September and was gone by October, on and off the chart in just 8 weeks. "which was on and off the chart in just 8 weeks"
**Wouldn't It Be Nice (1966) - Second Pet Sounds Single** : It debuted on the Hot 100 in mid-July 1966 and reached its number 8 peak by late September. God Only Knows was on its flip side in the US, and the pair is sometimes called a double A-side given the strength of both songs. "And the second single from the album, the number eight hit, wouldn't it be nice?"
**Sloop John B (1966) - First Pet Sounds Single** : The first single from Pet Sounds reached number 3. "The first single from the album, the number three hit, Slupe John B."
**Good Vibrations (1966) - Standalone Number One** : Although recorded in 1966, Good Vibrations was not on Pet Sounds. It was issued as a standalone single later that year and was the only number one hit among these Beach Boys songs. "It was issued as a standalone single later that year."

# artist - Strawberry Alarm Clock

## genres

Baroque Rock

## songs
**Incense and Peppermints (1967) - Lyrics by an Outsider** : College student John Carter wrote lyrics as a freelance gig for an instrumental track by a new psychedelic pop band called the Sixpence. He was not part of the group, and the band resented that their producer had sent their track to an outside writer. The instrumental was originally devised by Ed King, who later became a guitarist for Lynyrd Skynyrd and co-wrote Sweet Home Alabama. The band assumed it would be a B-side and didn't mind when Carter recruited a vocalist who wasn't in the band. "A friend of the bands who was hanging around the studio and was never seen again."
**Incense and Peppermints (1967) - Flipped by Los Angeles Radio** : The song first appeared as the B-side of the Sixpence single The Birdman of Alcatraz. Los Angeles radio stations played Incense and Peppermints instead of the A-side, which changed both the promotional focus and the band's name. "In-sense and peppermints initially appeared on the B-side of the six pences single the Bird Man of Elkatrash."

## curiosities
**Name Change From the Sixpence** : In 1967 several baroque rock bands had "sixpence" in their names, so the band became Strawberry Alarm Clock, a name chosen at random from the Beatles song Strawberry Fields Forever. "Strawberry Alarm Clock was chosen at random, adapted from the Beatles song Strawberry Fields Forever."
**Short Career** : After Incense and Peppermints the band had one more top 40 hit and disbanded by 1969. "The band scored one more top 40 hit and disbanded by 1969."

**Ode to Billie Joe (1967) - Southern Gothic B-Side Turned Number One** : A massive Southern Gothic story song, with mysteries in its lyrics: what did Billie Joe and his girlfriend throw off the Tallahatchie Bridge, and why did Billie Joe jump off it the next day? DJs around the country and the world were captivated by the lyrics' soap-operatic mysteries, which flipped it to the A-side. It even charted in England. "the song took less than an hour to record even though the finished product was seven minutes long"
**Mississippi Delta (1967) - The Intended A-Side** : Capitol signed Gentry in 1966 on the strength of this song, and Ode to Billie Joe was recorded to accompany it as the B-side. "Signed to capital records in 1966 on the strength of her song Mississippi Delta."

## songs
**We Can Work It Out / Day Tripper (1965) - First Explicit Double A-Side** : This was the first 45 EMI issued explicitly as a double A-side. In England, where charts were based entirely on 45 sales, both songs went to number one together with Day Tripper listed first. In America, We Can Work It Out was treated as the A-side and reached number one, while Day Tripper effectively served as the B-side and hit number five. "In England where the pop charts were based entirely on sales of 45s, both songs went to number one together and day tripper was listed first."
**Penny Lane / Strawberry Fields Forever (1967) - Split US Chart** : In America the two sides charted separately: Penny Lane reached number one and Strawberry Fields Forever number eight. "Penny Lane reached number one while its classic flip side strawberry fields forever hit number eight."
**Hey Jude / Revolution (1968) - Split US Chart** : In America Hey Jude reached number one and Revolution, its smoldering rocker flip side, hit number 12. "Hageeud reached number one while the smoldering rocker revolution hit number 12."
**Something (1969) - Harrison Song as A-Side** : Something is George Harrison's pop standard, recorded by artists from Frank Sinatra to Ray Charles to Elvis Presley. It was an obvious enough hit that, for once, the group agreed to put a Harrison song on a double A-side, with a Lennon–McCartney song on the flip. Before the 1969 rule change it had stalled at number three. "it has been recorded by everyone from Frank Sinatra to Ray Charles to Elvis Presley"
**Come Together (1969) - Lennon's Abbey Road Opener** : This John Lennon composition opens Abbey Road and was the other side of the Something double A-side. It had climbed as high as number two before dropping back. "the John Lenin composition come together the opening track of Abbey Road"
**Come Together / Something (1969) - Number One Created by a Billboard Rule Change** : At the end of the 1969 chart year, for reasons lost to history, Billboard began combining the A and B sides of 45s into one Hot 100 position. In the very first week of the new system, the chart week ending November 29, 1969, Come Together / Something rose to number one. Fred Bronson notes that the week before, neither side looked strong enough to reach the top. It became the first double A-side to need both sides to top the chart. "billboard essentially gave the Beatles a bonus number one hit"

## curiosities
**Inventors of the Double A-Side** : By 1965 the Beatles had pioneered the double A-side single. EMI was acknowledging that for superstars like Elvis Presley in 1956, there was no point announcing in advance which track would be the bigger hit. "By 1965, the Beatles had invented the idea of the double A side at single."

# artist - Carl Douglas

## genres
Disco

## labels
Pye Records

## songs
**Kung Fu Fighting (1974) - Recorded in 10 Minutes** : Biddu, an Indian-born, England-based producer, booked several hours at a London studio and spent most of it recording Douglas on the song he thought was the A-side, I Want to Give You My Everything. With 10 minutes left, Biddu asked Douglas for something quick for the B-side. Douglas, Biddu and the session musicians made Kung Fu Fighting in two takes. Biddu said nothing was taken seriously and that they "did a lot of coos and haves." "whipped up kung-fu fighting in just two takes"
**Kung Fu Fighting (1974) - The Soho Pinball Arcade Inspiration** : Earlier in 1974, leaving a nightspot in Soho, London, Douglas passed a pinball arcade where kids were mock fighting in time to the music. He told his friend, "damn, looks like everybody's kung fu fighting." He later said he heard the whole song in his head at that moment, including the melody line, and rushed home to write it down. "I heard it all in my head melody line as well and I had to rush home and write it down"
**Kung Fu Fighting (1974) - Pye Flips It and It Tops the Charts** : Biddu called it just a fun thing, but the A&R staff at UK Pye Records heard both tracks and picked Kung Fu Fighting as the A-side. It rode the 1970s kung fu craze, then at its peak: David Carradine's TV series Kung Fu was in its third season, and Bruce Lee's death the previous year had made him a legend and helped Enter the Dragon gross tens of millions worldwide. It hit number one in England in September 1974, was picked up for US distribution, and topped the Hot 100 just before Christmas 1974. "they thought it was good enough to release as the a-side"
**I Want to Give You My Everything (1974) - The Forgotten A-Side** : Biddu considered this a much better song than Kung Fu Fighting and spent hours of studio time on it. It was a light disco song by the same songwriter who later wrote Glen Campbell's Rhinestone Cowboy. "a light disco song written by the same song writer who later penned Glenn Campbell's rhinestone cowboy"
**Dance the Kung Fu (1975) - The Follow-Up** : A quickly recorded follow-up that peaked just outside the top 40, making Douglas officially a US one-hit wonder. "making Douglas an official US one hit wonder"

## curiosities
**First Jamaican With a US Number One** : Douglas, of Jamaican descent, became the first Jamaican to have a US number one when Kung Fu Fighting topped the Hot 100. "became the first Jamaican with a US number one hit"
**Songwriting Wealth and Kung Fu Panda** : Douglas wrote Kung Fu Fighting, which sold millions and is still covered, most recently for the Kung Fu Panda films. It has likely made him quite wealthy. "has likely made him quite wealthy"

# artist - Gloria Gaynor

## labels

## songs
**Honey Bee (1974) - Debut Club Hit** : Gaynor's 1974 debut single barely registered on the pop charts because disco was still emerging, but it was a major club record. "It was too early in disco's emergence for that to happen, but it was a major club record."
**Never Can Say Goodbye (1974) - Disco Remake of the Jackson 5** : A few months after Honey Bee, Gaynor had a smash with a disco remake of the Jackson 5's 1971 hit. It was a club hit and, in early 1975, made the Hot 100 top 10, peaking at number 9. "it actually made the top 10 of the Hot 100 peaking at number 9"
**I Will Survive (1978) - The B-Side Anthem** : Co-written by veteran songwriter Freddie Perren, who was behind the Jackson 5's I Want You Back, the song was unusual even for disco: it had no background vocals and was relentlessly wordy. Polydor underestimated it and put it on the B-side of the late-1978 single Substitute. Over several months, discos and radio programmers found it. It debuted on the Hot 100 just before Christmas 1978, took three months to reach number one in late winter 1979, spent three weeks there and went platinum. "featuring no background vocalists and being so relentlessly wordy"
**I Will Survive (1979) - A Personal Declaration** : For Gaynor, who had survived a career slump and spinal surgery, the song was more than a club anthem or a defiant breakup song. It was a personal declaration, and a comeback. "For Gainer, it was a personal declaration."
**Substitute (1978) - The Forgotten A-Side** : A more traditional club track that Polydor chose as the A-side over I Will Survive. It never reached the Hot 100. "Substitute however never even breached the Hot 100."

## curiosities
**First Queen of Disco** : On the strength of Honey Bee and Never Can Say Goodbye, Gaynor was widely acclaimed the first queen of disco. In spring 1975 the National Association of Discotheque Disc Jockeys named her Queen of the Discos. Her reign lasted a year: in 1976 the title went to Donna Summer. "Gloria Gainer was by acclimation, the first queen of disco."
**Three Lean Years and Spinal Surgery** : For three years none of Gaynor's singles came near the top 40, and she had spinal surgery after collapsing on stage. "she even underwent spinal surgery after a non-stage collapse"

# curiosity

**What a B-Side Was** : On the 7-inch 45 RPM single, the A-side carried the song being pushed to radio, and the B-side was considered filler. Artists and producers often spent days or weeks on an A-side and recorded the B-side in minutes. "the B-side was supposedly filler, a throwaway, an afterthought."
**The B-Side That Becomes the Hit** : Throughout chart history, the music business repeatedly missed that the throwaway song on the B-side would be the hit. "how often a fun may be silly, but probably very catchy throwaway song, relegated to the B-side of a single, would wind up being the hit"
**Billboard's Pre-Hot 100 Charts (1956)** : In 1956 there was no Hot 100, only separate charts for best sellers in stores, most played in jukeboxes and most played by jockeys. The Hot 100 launched in 1958. "separate charts, tracking bestsellers and stores, most played in jukeboxes, and most played by Jockeys"
**Mid-1960s B-Side Rule** : In the mid-1960s, A-sides and B-sides charted separately on the Hot 100. A B-side reported by enough radio programmers got its own position. "the rule was that A sides and B sides of singles charted in tandem, but separately"
**Hot 100 Methodology and the B-Side Problem** : The Hot 100 has always combined radio airplay and singles sales. B-sides created a definitional problem: radio plays one song at a time, but a buyer gets two. Billboard changed its B-side policy roughly every decade or two. "the magazine changed its B side policy roughly every decade or two"
**1969 Policy Change - Combining Sides** : At the end of the 1969 chart year Billboard began combining A and B sides into one Hot 100 position. The Beatles' Come Together / Something went to number one in the first week, followed by a wave of two-sided hits. "billboard decided at the end of the 1969 chart year to begin combining A and B sides of 45s into a single hot 100 position"
**1967 - Bad Ears for Hits** : Three of 1967's number ones (To Sir with Love, Incense and Peppermints, Ode to Billie Joe) started as B-sides, and in each case DJs found them. "1967, which must rank as some kind of high watermark for the music business having bad ears for a hit"
**The Missing B-Sides of 1984–1987** : After Prince's Irresistible Bitch in early 1984, no B-side appeared on the Hot 100 for about three years. There was no formal policy; payola-driven pop radio simply didn't report songs labels called B-sides. That is why Erotic City, Pink Cadillac and Into the Groove, the three most famous B-sides of the decade, never charted despite heavy airplay. "payola fueled 80s pop radio stations were not reporting them to the charts"
**Charts Reflect Only What Is Reported** : Between summer 1984 and summer 1985, three major hits missed the Hot 100 entirely. "From the summer of 1984 to the summer of 1985, three of the 80s best songs missed America's premier chart barometer entirely."
**1987 Reversion to Separate Positions** : When B-sides returned to the chart around 1987, Billboard went back to charting them separately. "When B sides came back into the chart around 1987, Billboard reverted to charting them in separate chart positions again"
**1998 - One Song Per Chart Position** : At the end of 1998 Billboard added non-retail songs to the Hot 100 for the first time, turning it into a chart where only one song could hold each position. That ended combined two-sided chart runs like Jewel's. "This guaranteed that no one could pull a chart longevity feet like Jules ever again."
**The Digital Era and the End of the B-Side** : Downloads in the early 2000s made every song purchasable individually, at 99 cents each. "In the digital era, all songs are singles, and all singles are stand-alongs."
**Digital "Double A-Sides" and Launch Flotillas** : Since Billboard began counting downloads and later streams, artists including Taylor Swift, Lil Wayne, The Weeknd and Ed Sheeran have opened album campaigns with several early singles to see which catches on. Often two key tracks are released together. "Often, two key tracks will drop together, a kind of double A side on digital steroids."
**The 1970s Kung Fu Craze** : Kung Fu Fighting benefited from the craze at its peak: David Carradine's TV series Kung Fu was in its third season, and Bruce Lee's death the year before had made him a legend and helped Enter the Dragon gross tens of millions worldwide. "the song would benefit from the 70s kung-fu craze which was then at its peak"
**Fred Bronson's Billboard Book of Number One Hits** : Fred Bronson's book is the source for several of these stories, including Lulu's To Sir with Love, Bobbie Gentry's quick recording of Ode to Billie Joe, and the 1969 Beatles chart change. "according to Fred Bronson's Billboard book of number one hits"
