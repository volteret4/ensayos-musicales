# artist - Vangelis

## instruments
Synthesizer

## albums
**Chariots of Fire (1981) - Score That Ended the Post-Grease Soundtrack Drought** : After Saturday Night Fever and Grease, no soundtrack topped the Billboard album chart for several years. Vangelis, the Greek instrumentalist, ended that drought with his score to Chariots of Fire, the 1981 Academy Award winner for Best Picture. The score topped both the album and singles charts in May 1982. It combined the lush, old-school Hollywood feel of an Oscar winner's score with music composed largely on synthesizers, which pointed to where 80s movie music was going. "As music composed largely on synthesizers, it pointed the direction soundtrack music was headed in the 80s."

## curiosities
**Chart Double in May 1982** : The Chariots of Fire score topped both the Billboard album chart and the singles chart in the same month, the first soundtrack to reach number one on the album chart since the Travolta films. "topped both the album and singles charts in May of 82."

# curiosity

**Early Soundtrack History Recap** : Early movie soundtrack history ran from the Bing Crosby and Judy Garland hits of the 1940s, through Elvis's 50s and 60s hits, to show tunes from movie musicals. Most chart-topping soundtracks came from musicals until The Graduate (1967) started a wave of contemporary rock, R&B, pop, funk, and disco in film. Saturday Night Fever (1977) began the soundtrack's blockbuster era. "In fact, most chart topping soundtracks were from musicals."
**MTV Reshapes Hollywood** : Soon after MTV launched in 1981, Hollywood films began copying music-video techniques. "Quick cuts, kinetic editing, and relentless rhythms."
**Flashdance (1983) - Template for the 80s Soundtrack** : Starring Jennifer Beals as a Pittsburgh welder and exotic dancer, the film's soundtrack went platinum, spent two weeks atop the LP chart, and produced two Hot 100 number ones. "Flash Dance set the template for the 80s movie soundtrack, sleek MTV friendly, and packed with potential hit singles."
**Footloose (1984) - The Thriller of 80s Soundtracks** : Conceived by songwriter and screenwriter Dean Pitchford as a fable about a Utah town that bans dancing, with Kevin Bacon as the city kid who teaches the local kids to dance. The soundtrack spent 10 weeks at number one and produced six top 40 hits, from Kenny Loggins (two), Deniece Williams (a number one), Bonnie Tyler, and members of Loverboy, Heart, and Shalimar. Every hit appeared in the film, but mostly as non-diegetic, music-video-style sequences. "Footloose was the thriller of 80s soundtracks."
**Simpson/Bruckheimer Formula** : Don Simpson and Jerry Bruckheimer produced Flashdance, Beverly Hills Cup, and Top Gun. Each used stylized music-video sequences, topped the album chart, and produced a pair of smash hits. Top Gun, Tom Cruise's biggest box office success in 1986, is noted as possibly doubling as a military recruitment film. "Top Gun employed the same stylized music video style troops to frame the action around caffeinated pop songs."
**1985 - Record Eight Soundtrack Number Ones** : Eight number one songs in 1985 came from soundtracks, a total never matched in any other calendar year. Each had a music video built like a movie trailer, intercutting the performer with film footage. "Never again would one calendar year generate so many soundtrack number one songs."
**The Big Chill (1983) - Sleeper Hit of the 80s** : A wistful film about the aging 60s generation. Its oldies soundtrack went platinum by 1984, months after the film left theaters, double platinum by 1985, and quadruple platinum by the 90s. "a soundtrack packed with oldies that turned out to be the sleeper hit of the 80s."
**Dirty Dancing (1987) - Surprise Blockbuster** : Starring Patrick Swayze and Jennifer Grey and set at a Catskills resort in 1963. Expected to play only a week or two, it ran for months. The quickly assembled soundtrack mixed oldies with new songs by acts who had not had hits in years. It reached number one in late 1987 and held it for 18 weeks into mid-1988, went 10 times platinum by the end of 1988, and appealed across generations. "It was the big chill soundtrack model crossed with the flash dance model, and it worked with everybody."
**Rise of the Compact Disc** : The CD launched in 1983 but did not become the industry's primary format until 1992. It was extremely profitable and not easily recordable until nearly 2000. Because soundtracks were often the only way to own certain hits, CD-era soundtracks were certified at multiples of earlier sales levels. "The CD was extremely profitable in its early years."
**Disney Soundtracks in the CD Era** : The Little Mermaid (1989) and Beauty and the Beast (1991) each went double platinum in their first year, and Aladdin (1992) went triple platinum. The Lion King (1994), the first Disney soundtrack of the CD-first era, went 10 times platinum in year one and spent 10 weeks at number one. "The CD just sold more copies."
**Friday (1995) Soundtrack** : The soundtrack to the stoner comedy starring Ice Cube and Chris Tucker topped the Billboard 200 for two weeks in May 1995 and went double platinum within a year, higher than any 70s blaxploitation soundtrack. "higher than any 70s black exploitation film had ever gone."
**SoundScan Begins (1991)** : Computerized charts date to 1991, when Billboard adopted SoundScan to count album sales piece by piece for the first time. "Billboard adopted the sound scan system to count album sales piece by piece for the first time."
**Grease's Freddy My Love - Precedent for Absent Songs** : Before the inspired-by era, soundtrack songs missing from the film were usually numbers cut from a stage musical. Freddy My Love, a Broadway favorite cut from the film of Grease, still appeared on the 1978 soundtrack LP. "a favorite from the Broadway show that didn't make the cut for the movie, was nonetheless recorded and included on the movie's soundtrack LP."
**The Decade of Inspired-By Soundtracks** : By the mid-90s, multi-artist soundtracks routinely included songs that never appeared in the film. Labels and managers used them to attach artists to big-movie hype or keep them visible between albums. Batman Forever (1995) and Batman & Robin (1997) were both padded this way. "By the final years of the millennium, the inspired by soundtrack would become so commonplace you might call it uninspired."
**1998 - Commercial Peak of the Soundtrack** : A 1998 New York Times article headlined "Forget the Movie, Listen to the CD" reported that more than 50 soundtracks made the Billboard 200 that year, the most in chart history, up from 16 in 1990. Titles such as Godzilla: The Album and Armageddon: The Album avoided the word "soundtrack" and went platinum anyway. "more than 50 soundtrack albums made the Billboard 200 that year alone."
**City of Angels (1998) - Soundtrack Bigger Than the Film** : A remake of Wim Wenders's 1987 art film Wings of Desire, starring Meg Ryan and Nicolas Cage. It grossed about $55 million in spring 1998 and left theaters by June. Its soundtrack hit number one on the Billboard 200 in July, driven by radio hits, went quintuple platinum, and was reportedly more profitable than the movie. "the City of Angels soundtrack was a bigger deal than the film."
**Napster and the Soundtrack Decline** : After Titanic, City of Angels, and Armageddon topped the chart in 1998, no soundtrack reached number one for almost four years. File sharing undermined the business, labels moved toward digital singles, and padded soundtrack albums stopped being viable. "So soundtrack albums packed with random film adjacent tracks were no longer viable."
**Dreamgirls (2007) - Aughts-Era Ceiling** : The soundtrack reached number one but went only double platinum, strong for the 2000s but far below 90s figures like Waiting to Exhale's 7 million. "That was strong for the Aughts."
**Frozen (2013) - Disney Remains Reliable** : Kids' films, especially Disney's, may be the last reliable category of hit soundtrack. Frozen spent 13 weeks at number one into 2014 and was 2014's number one album. "Perhaps the last reliable category of hit soundtrack is a kids movie, especially one from Disney."
**Guardians of the Galaxy: Awesome Mix Vol. 1 (2014)** : The last successful oldies compilation soundtrack, in the line of The Big Chill, Dirty Dancing, and Pulp Fiction. It recreates the 70s AM-pop mixtape that Chris Pratt's Peter "Star-Lord" Quill plays for comfort. In summer 2014 it became the first soundtrack made up entirely of previously released songs to top the Billboard 200. It has since gone triple platinum and sells especially well on vinyl and cassette. "the first soundtrack consisting entirely of previously released songs to top the chart."
**K-Pop Demon Hunters (2025) - Record Four Simultaneous Top 10s** : A Netflix film so popular the streamer briefly put it in theaters for sing-along fans. In August 2025, four of its songs were in the Hot 100 top 10 at once, breaking the record of three shared by Saturday Night Fever and Waiting to Exhale. "four songs from the soundtrack pushed their way into the top 10 on the Hot 100."
