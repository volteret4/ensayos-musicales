## albums
**Bad Out of Hell (1977) - Blockbuster Album** : This album was a blockbuster produced in collaboration with Jim Steinman. While a big seller, it grew slowly over time and never generated a top 10 hit. Sixteen years later, fans treated *Bat Out of Hell 2* as if this album's sequel had come out only a year or two after it. "together, they produced the blockbuster album Bad Out of Hell."
**Dead Ringer (1981) - Ill-Fated Album** : This album was produced by Jim Steinman. Jim Steinman hadn't produced a full album since this one when he later worked with Bonnie Tyler. The album included the song "Rhythm and Weep," which was not released as a single and did not become a hit in Meat Loaf's version. "Steinman hadn't produced a full album since Meatloaf's ill-fated 1981 album, Dead Ringer."
**Midnight at the Lost and Found (1983) - Album with New Producers** : By 1983, Meat Loaf had moved on to different producers and songwriters for this album, indicating a professional estrangement from Jim Steinman. "by 1983, Meat had moved on to different producers and songwriters for his Midnight at the Lost and Found album."
**Bad Attitude (1984) - Album with Steinman Song** : This album featured the Jim Steinman song "Nowhere Fast," despite the ongoing "cold war" and professional separation between Meat Loaf and Steinman. "Meat Loaf's Bad Attitude."
**Bat Out of Hell II: Back into Hell (1993) - Comeback Album** : This album marked a stunning pop comeback for Meat Loaf and Jim Steinman, debuting at number 3 on the Billboard 200 album chart in October 1993, behind Garth Brooks and Mariah Carey. It leaped over CDs by Brooks and Nirvana to reach number 1 four weeks later, becoming the first chart-topping album for both Meat Loaf and Steinman. By the end of 1994, it was on its way to quintuple platinum status, a remarkable achievement for two artists in their late 40s. "Meatloafs bat out of Hell 2 back into Hell debuted on the Billboard 200 album chart all the way up at number 3."
**Bat Out of Hell 3: The Monster Is Loose (2006) - Post-Steinman Collaboration Album** : Meat Loaf recorded this album, completing it with producer Desmond Child, as Jim Steinman was recovering from a reported heart attack and could not participate. The album did reasonably well, reaching the top 10 on the album chart and going gold. It featured Meat Loaf's version of "It's All Coming Back to Me Now," a song originally made famous by Celine Dion. "Though Meeat couldn't persuade Jim to join him for the 2006 album, Steinman was then recovering from a reported heart attack, the singer managed to complete the album with producer Desmond Child, and even in Steinman's absence, Bad 3 did reasonably well, reaching the top 10 on the album chart and going gold."

## songs
**Lost in France (1976)** : This poppy, ABBA-esque song was Bonnie Tyler's first British top 10 hit, reaching number 9 in the UK by the end of 1976. "The Poppy, Abba Esque, lost in France reached number 9 in the UK."
**It's a Heartache (1978)** : This song was a worldwide blockbuster single, reaching number 4 in the UK and a remarkable number 3 in America in the summer of 1978. It established Bonnie Tyler's distinctive vocal persona: a rough-hewn, throaty, husky voice, often compared to a "gravelly female analog to Rod Stewart." Its country music overtones led her management to push her toward country songs. "It was not only a blockbuster single, reaching number 4 in the UK and a remarkable number 3 in America in the summer of 1978, it also established a vocal persona for Bonnie Tyler, a rough-hume, throatier, husky voice, a kind of gravelly female analog to Rod Stewart."
**My Guns Are Loaded (1979)** : Following "It's a Heartache," this single was part of her management's push towards country pop crossover. However, it only managed to "bubble under" the Hot 100, peaking at number 107 in America. "Tyler's 1979 single, My Guns Are Loaded, only managed to bubble under the hot 100, peaking in America at number 107."
**Total Eclipse of the Heart (1983)** : This massive hit was written and produced by Jim Steinman, who had refined and reused its irresistible melody for over a decade, including in his college musical *The Dreaming* and the 1980 film *A Small Circle of Friends*. They recorded it with many of Steinman's favorite players from the *Bat Out of Hell* sessions, including Roy Bittan (piano) and Max Weinberg (drums) from Bruce Springsteen's E Street Band, and vocalist Rory Dodd, whose falsetto was so prominent it was almost a duet. The song debuted at number 75 on the Hot 100 in July 1983 and took a dozen weeks to reach number one, knocking out Billy Joel's "Tell Her About It" in the first week of October 1983. It stayed at number one for four weeks, giving Jim Steinman his first ever number one hit. The song's lyrics and "glossy, bonkers, and much ridiculed music video" (featuring Tyler as a teacher/headmaster of a boys' prep school where the boys are possessed) are seen as the ultimate expression of the Jim Steinman aesthetic: "no logic, all fun." In 2020, Tom Bryan described it as an "extinction-level event rendered in musical form." "Debuting on the Hot 100 at number 75 in July of 1983, Bonnie Tyler's total Eclipse of the heart took a dozen weeks to reach number one."
**Holding Out for a Hero (1984)** : Co-written by Jim Steinman and Dean Pitchford, and produced by Steinman, this explosive song was featured on the soundtrack to the film *Footloose*. It served as a de facto sequel to "Total Eclipse of the Heart" and peaked at number 34 on the Hot 100 in April 1984. "Steinman co-wrote holding out for a hero with Dean Pitchford and Steinman produced the song in high Jim Steinman Steinman."
**If You Were a Woman and I Was a Man (1986)** : This anthemic single, from the Steinman-produced album *Secret Dreams and Forbidden Fire*, was written by Desmond Child at Jim Steinman's explicit instruction for a "gender crossing anthem." Despite Child's conviction that its catchiness guaranteed a hit, it only reached number 77 on the Hot 100 due to perceived lack of promotion by Tyler's label. "Steinman recruited Juryman songwriter Desmond Shild to write If You Were a Woman and I was a Man for Bonnie Tyler."
