# artist - The Dave Clark 5

## members
Dave Clark
Mike Smith

## genres
Beat Music

## songs
**Glad All Over (1964)** : This original song, co-written by Dave Clark and Mike Smith, made headlines in Britain for knocking The Beatles' "I Want to Hold Your Hand" out of the number one spot in January 1964. It debuted on the Hot 100 in mid-February 1964, and two months later, in April, it reached its peak of number six, becoming the first non-Beatle single of the British invasion to crack the U.S. Top 10. "Glad all over, made headlines in Britain for knocking the Beatles I Want to Hold Your Hand out of the number one spot in January 1964."
**Do You Love Me (1963)** : This cover of The Contours' American hit reached number 30 on the UK chart, following another successful cover of the same song by Brian Poole and The Tremeloes, who reached number one. "Like many British groups of the time, the Dave Clark V named for their drummer, Tottenham Born Dave Clark, launched their career with a cover of an American hit, The Contours Do You Love Me."
**Bits and Pieces** : This original song, part of a streak of more than a dozen top 20 U.S. hits for the band, peaked at number four on the Hot 100. "including the number four Bits and Pieces."
**Because** : This ballad, an original song, peaked at number three on the Hot 100, continuing the band's successful run of US hits. "And the number three ballad because is dry is dry to feel away I do because"
**Over and Over (1965)** : This cover of a 1950s sock hop party record by Bobby Day finally took The Dave Clark 5 all the way to number one on the Hot 100 the week of Christmas 1965. By 1966, its sound was considered a bit old school for the evolving British Invasion. "Finally took them all the way to number one the week of Christmas 1965."

## curiosities
**Tottenham Sound** : Fleet Street, a street in London historically associated with the British press, hyped The Dave Clark 5's music as the "Tottenham sound," portraying it as a rival to The Beatles and The Pacemakers' "Mersey Beat" sound. "Fleet Street even hyped up the DC-5's music as, quote, the Tottenham sound, a rejoinder to the Beatles and the pacemakers' mercy beat sound."
**Beatles Rivals** : The portrayal of The Dave Clark 5 as rivals to The Beatles (likened to *NSYNC to the Backstreet Boys three decades later) significantly helped the band's cause in America. "The portrayal of the DC-5 as Beatles rivals, imagine that three decades later they might have been the in-sync to the Beatles' backstreet boys, only helped the band's cause in America."
**Consistent US Hitmakers** : After their breakthrough, The Dave Clark 5 achieved a nearly unbroken streak of more than a dozen top 20 U.S. hits, most of which were originals co-written by Dave Clark and other band members. "And the Dave Clark 5 strung together a nearly unbroken streak of more than a dozen top 20 U.S. hits."

## albums
**Sergeant Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band (1967)** : This "totemic" LP saw The Beatles exploring the limits of the studio and adopting an alter ego of an imagined music hall group, an approach that may have been influenced by contemporary music hall-style throwback hits from bands like Herman's Hermits and The New Vaudeville Band. "It has been observed that the alter ego the fab for took on one year later on their totemic Sergeant Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club band LP was an imagined music haul group, not far from what Herman's Hermits and the new Vaudeville band were doing."

## labels
Capital Records

## songs
**Please Please Me (1963)** : This early single went to number one on two competing UK charts in early 1963, though it only reached number two on the "Record Retailer" chart, which was the predecessor to the UK's modern-day official charts company chart. "In record retailer, Please Please Me only hit number two."
**From Me To You (1963)** : This song knocked Jerry and the Pacemakers' "How Do You Do It" out of the number one spot on the "Record Retailer" chart, officially establishing a "Mersey Beat invasion" across Great Britain by the spring of 1963. "the song that knocked Jerry and the pacemakers, How Do You Do It, out of the record retailer number one spot was the Beatles own from me to you, which made it official."
**She Loves You (1963)** : This "head shaking original song" went back to number one in the UK by the fall of 1963. In the US, it replaced "I Want to Hold Your Hand" at number one for two weeks in early 1964. "And the Beatles, with their head shaking original song she loves you."
**I Want to Hold Your Hand (1964)** : Following strong independent requests on US radio, Capital Records finally released this single, which became America's number one song by the end of January 1964, a couple of weeks before the band arrived at JFK Airport. It remained at number one for seven weeks, part of a still-unbroken record of three consecutive number one hits. "I want to hold your hand was replaced at number one by She Loves You."
**Can't Buy Me Love (1964)** : This song instantly replaced "She Loves You" at number one just two weeks after "She Loves You" had replaced "I Want to Hold Your Hand," contributing to The Beatles' unbroken record of three consecutive number one hits in the US in early 1964. "Which two weeks later was instantly replaced itself by Can't Buy Me Love."
**Norwegian Wood (1965)** : This track, featuring a sitar, was part of a trend in 1966 where several British acts began writing hits influenced by the Raga Rock movement. "Sitar laid in tracks like Norwegian Wood."
**Ticket to Ride (1965)** : This jangly song was swiftly rising on the charts in May 1965, soon to become The Beatles' eighth number one hit. "rising fast with their soon-to-be eighth number one hit, the jangly ticket to ride."

## curiosities
**US Chart Conquest (1964)** : After initially passing on their singles, Capital Records released The Beatles' music due to "Beagle Mania" in England and US fan requests, leading to "I Want to Hold Your Hand" hitting number one by late January 1964, even before their arrival in the US. "capital records in the states after passing on releasing the group's singles through 1962 and almost all of 63 finally got with the program when Beagle Mania in England became too big to ignore and US fans were independently requesting I want to hold your hand on the radio."
**Unbroken Number One Streak (1964)** : The Beatles commanded the Hot 100 with three consecutive number one hits in early 1964—"I Want to Hold Your Hand," "She Loves You," and "Can't Buy Me Love"—a streak that remains an unbroken record. "And in a remarkable streak, the Beatles commanded the hot 100 through three consecutive number ones, a still unbroken record."
**Hot 100 Top 5 Sweep (April 1964)** : In April 1964, The Beatles famously held down the entire top 5 on the Hot 100, a remarkable feat that solidified their dominance and marked a significant moment in the British Invasion. "The Beatles, of course, who that week in April, 64 famously held down the entire top 5 on the hot 100."
**"Fab Four" and "Beetle Mania"** : The US media began using the word "invasion" as early as December 1963, and upon The Beatles' arrival in New York, TV anchorman Walter Cronkite declared, "the British invasion this time goes by the code name, Beetle Mania," acknowledging their immediate cultural impact. "on the day the Beatles arrived in New York, esteemed TV anchorman Walter Cronkite said, quote, the British invasion this time goes by the code name, Beetle Mania, unquote."

# genre - British Invasion

## curiosities
**April 1964 Start** : The week ending April 25, 1964, when The Dave Clark V's "Glad All Over" reached its peak of number six on Billboard's Hot 100, simultaneously with The Beatles holding 10 spots on the Hot 100, officially marked the beginning of the British Invasion. "The British invasion had begun, but it would take on many different forms in the years to come and the decades to follow from the stones to soft sell, the kinks to culture club, the Tula Clark to Annie Lennox, Herman's Hermits to Durand Iran."
**Second British Invasion (1982)** : Two decades after the 1960s invasion, in 1982, a new wave of cutting-edge, synthesizer-heavy British bands began making landfall on US shores, which the pundits eventually dubbed the "second British invasion." "Two decades later, in 1982, another wave of British bands began making landfall on US shores. As with the Beatles in 64, it had taken America a few years to catch on to this new wave of cutting edge synthesizer heavy UK acts."
**First Principle - Borrowing, Reimagining, Renewing** : A key principle of the British Invasion was that British acts borrowed, reimagined, and renewed American musical idioms, effectively selling American music back to the US in a new form. "The British acts were borrowing, reimagining and renewing American music and selling it back to us."
**Second Principle - Second-Tier Bands' Impact** : The success of "second tier" bands, such as Peter and Gordon or Spandau Ballet, which would not have charted previously, was crucial in demonstrating that the invasions were not solely dependent on "Titanic acts" like The Beatles or Duran Duran. "The second tier bands were what made it an invasion."
**Third Principle - Visual Importance** : The visual aspect of British bands, including their cultivated looks, glossy music videos, and iconography, was nearly as important to their conquest as their music, helping them stand out and appear larger than life and exotic. "The visual mattered."
**Fourth Principle - Britishness as an Asset** : The distinct "Britishness" of many acts, whether a Scouse accent, witty lyrics, or an ethereal quality, was exactly what American listeners desired in the mid-1960s and mid-1980s, often making music more appealing the more British it sounded. "The Britishness mattered."
**Fifth Principle - American Counter-Response** : Each invasion eventually wound down as American acts incorporated British Invasion tropes into their own music, and American tastes pivoted back towards sounds that the British acts couldn't perform as effectively. "America eventually struck back."
**Post-JFK Assassination Theory** : A popular theory posits that the country, in the emotional aftermath of President John F. Kennedy's assassination, transferred its youthful idealism to The Beatles, who also offered kids their first credible excuse for mania since Elvis Presley. "a popular theory holds that the country in the aftershock of President John F. Kennedy's assassination transferred to the Beatles all the youthful idealism that had been crusting under JFK."
**End of 60s Invasion (circa 1967)** : The British Invasion didn't end with a definitive declaration but dissipated around 1967, characterized by the fading idea of a "cogent movement" where "Britannia meant cool." British music then simply became part of pop's broader landscape. "After what dissipated around 1967 the first time and around 1987 the second time was the idea of a cogent movement, the sense that Britannia meant cool."
