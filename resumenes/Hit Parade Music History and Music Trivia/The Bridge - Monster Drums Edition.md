# artist - Prince

## albums
**Graffiti Bridge (1990) - Movie and Album** : Prince's 1990 film and its accompanying soundtrack served as his artistic endeavor to bridge his career from the 1980s into the 1990s. The song "Graffiti Bridge" itself explores themes of a "heaven above" or the afterworld, a concept that was a long-standing lyrical preoccupation for the late artist. "The film and soundtrack were Prince's attempt to bridge his career from the 80s into the 90s."

## curiosities
**Posthumous Influence and DIY Approach** : Prince passed away in April 2016, yet his distinctive sound and influence continue to resonate profoundly throughout contemporary pop music, with his "fingerprints" still audible on records released recently and into the 2010s. He was renowned for his DIY approach, characterized by his ability to play every instrument in the studio and his pioneering creation of genre-bending textures. "when he died in April of 2016." "his influence is just all over the sound of contemporary pop music." "that DIY approach of being in the studio playing every instrument creating these genre bending textures, like that's not goingwhere."

**Invisible Touch (1986) - First and Only Hot 100 #1** : Genesis scored their inaugural and sole number one hit on the Hot 100 chart with "Invisible Touch," which topped the charts in the summer of 1986. Over time, the group became a launching pad for multiple successful spin-off careers, most notably for members Phil Collins and Peter Gabriel. "This band that started as a prog rock band in the 70s and wound up spawning multiple successful spin-off careers, including Phil Collins and Peter Gabriel, even their guitarist, Mike Rutherford wound up being a hit maker in the 1980s and kind of defining the sound of pop and rock in the 1980s."

## songs
**In the Air Tonight (1981) - Gated Reverb Drum Break** : This iconic song is widely recognized for its "bombastic" and "most monstrous" drum outro. Uniquely, the track begins with a sparse rhythm generated by a Roland CR-78 drum machine (an early pseudo-programmable unit from 1978 and precursor to the 808), accompanied by a Prophet synthesizer and Collins' heavily reverberated voice. The dramatic transition to the powerful, natural drum solo at the end is intensified by the pioneering use of gated reverb, creating a surprising shift from a digital soundscape to an "unnatural" hybrid. "the thing that everyone of course knows about in the air tonight is the ending bombastic drum outro. Most monstrous drum breakable. It's spectacular, but we kind of forget... that the song begins with a drum machine."

## curiosities
**Pioneer of Gated Reverb Sound** : Phil Collins played a pivotal role in the accidental discovery and subsequent popularization of the gated reverb drum sound, which became a hallmark of 1980s music. This distinctive sound emerged during a recording session in a naturally reverberant drum room, where an additional talk-back microphone, equipped with a strong compressor and gate, inadvertently cut off the natural decay of the drum's reverb, creating a bigger, yet unnaturally clipped, effect. "Phil Collins is drumming in a drum room... they actually just had an extra talk back mic... And that microphone had a strong compressor and a strong gate... that would what would happen is that the sound of the drum would go through and the sound of the drum and the reverb would go through that microphone. And then the gate basically cuts off a sound if it's too quiet."
**Solo Hot 100 Success** : Before his long-time group Genesis achieved their sole number one hit "Invisible Touch" in 1986, Phil Collins had already accumulated four number one hits as a solo artist. He would go on to earn an additional three solo number one hits after Genesis's chart-topping success. "Collins had already scored four number one hits on his own. He would go on to score three more."
**Drumming Style** : Phil Collins is characterized as having a heavy drumming touch, a style contrasted with the lighter touch of drummers like Stuart Copeland of The Police, making them "polar opposites." "the other with probably the heaviest of heavy touches."

**Roland CR-78 (1978) - Early Programmable Unit** : The Roland CR-78, released in 1978, was one of the earliest pseudo-programmable drum machines. It served as a significant precursor to the more famous Roland TR-808 and was notably employed at the beginning of Phil Collins' "In the Air Tonight" to establish a sparse rhythmic foundation. "The Roland CR-78 drum machine, one of the earliest sort of pseudo programmable drum machines came out in 1978. It's the precursor to the 808."

# instrument - Gated Reverb

## curiosities
**Accidental Discovery and 1980s Dominance** : The gated reverb effect, a hallmark of 1980s drum sounds, originated by accident during a recording session. It happened when Phil Collins was drumming in a room with natural reverb, and an additional talk-back microphone, fitted with a strong compressor and gate, inadvertently captured the drum's sound and its reverb before abruptly cutting off the decaying sound. This created a distinct, large, cavernous sound that was unnaturally clipped, most notably applied to the snare drum. "The gated reverb is a mistake. It happens by accident." "It actually makes the drum sound even bigger. But it's this unnatural thing where it sounds like you're in a cavern that gets cut off, it gets clipped."
**Hybrid Sound of Traditional and Digital** : The gated drum sound represents a unique hybrid, blending traditional drumming techniques with the possibilities offered by emerging digital audio technology in the 1980s. It combines the natural acoustics of a drum with electronic processing to sculpt and abruptly shorten the reverberant tail. "But then the gated drum sound is this interesting hybrid between, to your point, traditional drumming and what digital technology as we enter the 80's is allowing you to do."
