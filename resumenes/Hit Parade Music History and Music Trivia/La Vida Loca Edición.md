## albums
**Vuelve (1998) - Grammy-Nominated Latin Pop Album** : Martin was recording *Vuelve*, the follow-up to his breakthrough, when FIFA asked him to record the official 1998 World Cup song. Later in 1998 the album earned a Grammy nomination in the Latin Pop category. Sony Music then lobbied the Grammy producers to give Martin a performing slot on the telecast the following February, which led to his career-defining 1999 Grammy performance. "Martin's album Vuelve earned a Grammy nomination in the Latin Pop category, Sony lobbied the Grammy producers to give Martin a performing slot on the telecast the following February."
**English-Language Album (1999) - Home of She's All I Ever Had** : Martin's first English-language album followed the success of "Livin' la Vida Loca." It included the ballad "She's All I Ever Had," co-written by Jon Secada, which became the official follow-up single and reached number 2 in September 1999. "in 1999 he co-wrote a ballad for Ricky Martin's English language album."
**Sound Loaded (2000) - Second English-Language LP** : Martin moved quickly in 2000 to release a second English-language album. It sold well and went double platinum, but its singles were already starting to seem like retreads, especially the lead track "She Bangs," which peaked at number 12. "Sound loaded, sold well, going double platinum, but its singles were already starting to seem like retrets."
**All-Spanish Return Album (2003) - First Spanish LP in Five Years** : As the first Latin boom wound down, Martin was the artist who stepped back from Anglo pop. In 2003 he released his first all-Spanish album in five years. Spanish-language radio welcomed him back, and he topped Hot Latin Tracks instantly. Martin said he wanted to "go back to my center to the beginning." "In 2003, Martin issued another all-spanish album, his first in five years, and he was warmly welcomed back on the Spanish language charts."

## songs
**Hold Me (Menudo era)** : One of Menudo's few English-language singles, released while Ricky Martin was in the group. It was Menudo's only single to reach the Hot 100, where it peaked at number 62. Robi Rosa sings lead. "Hold me, released during the Ricky period, was one of their few English language singles."
**María (1996) - First Global Smash** : Martin had to push against his label's advice to record up-tempo material with more forceful Latin percussion, arguing it would broaden his audience. "María" went top 10, or to number 1, in more than half a dozen countries. Although it was entirely in Spanish, it reached number 88 on the Hot 100. A Spanglish remix with a few English verse lyrics followed in 1997, but U.S. listeners still preferred the original. Its global breakthrough led to the FIFA invitation. "Ricky's instincts proved spot-off. 1996's Maria was not only Martin's first global smash."
**La Copa de la Vida / The Cup of Life (1998) - World Cup Anthem** : FIFA invited Martin to record the official theme of the 1998 World Cup, and he built on the relentlessly up-tempo, proudly Latin sound of "María." Robi Rosa and Desmond Child co-wrote it. Martin performed it at France's National Stadium at the World Cup Final on July 12, 1998, before more than a billion viewers worldwide. In the U.S. it reached number 2 on Billboard's Hot Latin Tracks chart, and Sony believed the song and Martin had greater potential. "seen by more than a billion viewers globally."
**Livin' la Vida Loca (1999) - First Hot 100 Number One** : Martin's team asked Robi Rosa and Desmond Child for another hybrid hit, this time leaning more toward English, and the blend of languages went right into the title. Martin had never come close to the top 40 before this. In four weeks the song went 54 → 32 → 8 → 1, and it stayed at number 1 for more than a month. It first hit number 1 the week ending May 8, 1999, less than three months after his Grammy performance. Child said they threw in "the kitchen sink": timbale piano stylings, Afro-Cuban and Brazilian rhythms, horns, gongs, and a rock guitar line that let Ricky strut "like a Latin Elvis." Child also modeled the melody on Frank Sinatra's Rat Pack. Jennifer Lopez's "If You Had My Love" eventually knocked it from number 1. "The song was an encyclopedia of Latin music in just four minutes."
**She's All I Ever Had (1999)** : A ballad co-written by Jon Secada for Martin's English-language album. As the official follow-up to "Livin' la Vida Loca," it reached number 2 in September 1999. "She's all I ever had, the official follow-up single to live in La Vida, Loka reached number two in September 1999."
**She Bangs (2000)** : The lead track from *Sound Loaded*. It missed the top 10 and peaked at number 12, a sign the first boom was showing strain. American Idol hopeful William Hung sang a version three years later. "Most especially its lead-off track, Shebangs, which missed the top 10, peaking at number 12."
**Tal Vez (2003)** (transcribed as "Calves") : The single from Martin's 2003 all-Spanish return album. It topped Hot Latin Tracks instantly. "topping Hot Latin Tracks instantly with Calves."

## curiosities
**Born Enrique Martín Morales in San Juan** : Martin was born and raised in San Juan, Puerto Rico. He spent five years in the late '80s in Menudo, where he became a heartthrob and breakout star. Before 1999 he had never scored a top 40 pop hit. "this dynamic young man born Enrique Marquín Morales."
**Acting Career Alongside Early Solo Music** : Martin signed with Sony Discos, Sony Music's Latin division, at the start of the '90s. He acted in sitcoms, telenovelas, and for about a year on ABC's daytime soap *General Hospital*, while releasing Spanish-language pop for the Latin market. Through the first half of the '90s he charted consistently on the Latin charts, mostly with florid ballads. "While acting in sitcoms, telenovelas, and for about a year, ABC's daytime soap opera General Hospital."
**1999 Grammy Performance - Four Minutes That Changed Latin Music** : He was not the most hyped performer that night; Madonna, Celine Dion, and Lauryn Hill, all multiple nominees, also performed. Even so, Martin was the most talked-about act. At the Shrine Auditorium in Los Angeles he performed with an army of dancers, backup singers, drummers, and circus performers, swiveling his hips in leather pants. It ranks among the most career-defining awards-show performances ever and started what became "Latin Boomita version 1.0." "Ricky Martin swiveling his hips in leather pants, changed the trajectory of both his own career and Latin music itself."
**Judged a Teenage Shakira in 1993** : At a 1993 Chilean music festival, 20-year-old former Menudo member Ricky Martin was one of the judges. He picked 16-year-old Shakira to win, but she came in third. "Ricky picked the 16-year-old Shakira to win the competition. She came in third."
**Retreat From the Anglo Spotlight** : The mainstream pop spotlight was more intense on Martin than on any other Latin crossover star, except perhaps Jennifer Lopez. He was then more than half a decade away from coming out as gay. Through the 2000s and 2010s he scored more than a dozen Latin chart top 10 hits but never returned to the pop top 40. "Martin was still more than half a decade away from coming out as gay."

## albums
**Pies Descalzos (1995) - Latin American Mega-Smash** (transcribed as "B.S. de Calzos") : One of the mega-selling albums that helped Shakira pass 10 million LPs sold across Latin America by the late '90s, while signed to Sony Colombia. "had sold more than 10 million LPs across Latin America, including her mega-smashes, B.S. de Calzos, and Donde Están Los Le Drones."
**¿Dónde Están los Ladrones? (1998) - Latin American Mega-Smash** : Her other major late-'90s Latin American blockbuster, which helped persuade Sony of her crossover potential. "including her mega-smashes, B.S. de Calzos, and Donde Están Los Le Drones."
**MTV Unplugged (2000) - Crossover Groundwork** : Sony believed enough in Shakira's crossover potential to get her a 1999 appearance on MTV's *Unplugged* showcase. The broadcast introduced MTV viewers to her magnetic, sensual, hip-swiveling live presence. The live album sold more than a quarter million copies in the U.S. in 2000 and prepared the ground for her long-planned crossover. "An album of this performance, Shakira MTV Unplugged, sold more than a quarter million copies in America in 2000."
**Laundry Service (2001) - First Primarily English Album** : Released in fall 2001, more than two and a half years after Ricky Martin's breakthrough. It was carefully calibrated not to strip away her heritage. The album debuted at number 3 on the Billboard 200 in December 2001, spent more than a year on the chart, and went triple platinum by 2002. It mixed overtly Latin tracks like "Objection (Tango)" with straight pop balladry like "Underneath Your Clothes." Looking back, it marked the unofficial end of Latin Boomita 1.0. "Shakira's was carefully calibrated not to eviscerate her heritage."
**Fijación Oral Vol. 1 (2005) - The Spanish Half** : One of two albums conceived together, an approach earlier tried by Guns N' Roses and Bruce Springsteen. Unlike them, Shakira released the two about six months apart, one in Spanish and one in English. Her team released the Spanish album first because they expected less crossover from it. Its lead single "La Tortura" reached number 23, and the album went gold and eventually platinum. "Both albums would be called oral fixation and volume one would be the Spanish album."
**Oral Fixation Vol. 2 (2005) - Rescued English Album** : Meant as the official English-language successor to *Laundry Service* and expected to be the bigger hit. Its lead single "Don't Bother" peaked at number 42, and by Christmas 2005 the album looked like a flop that might not even go gold. Epic reissued the CD in 2006 with "Hips Don't Lie," after which the album returned to the top 10 and went platinum. "by Christmas 2005 Shakira's second English album was shaping up as a flop with the potential to not even go gold."

## songs
**Whenever, Wherever (2001) - Lead Single from Laundry Service** : Co-written by Gloria Estefan. It paired mainstream pop hooks with distinctly Colombian sounds: Andean flutes and guitar lines played on a charango. "combined mainstream, pop-friendly hooks with the distinctly Colombian sounds of Andean flutes and guitar lines played on a sharangle."
**Objection (Tango) (2001)** : One of the more overtly Latin tracks on *Laundry Service*, a hybrid of new wave and tango. "the New Wave and Tango Hybrid objection tango."
**Underneath Your Clothes (2001)** : A straight pop ballad from *Laundry Service* that reached number 9. "straight-up pop balladry, like the number nine hit underneath your clothes."
**La Tortura (2005) - Reggaeton Duet with Alejandro Sanz** : The lead single from *Fijación Oral Vol. 1*, a duet about romantic torture with Spanish singer Alejandro Sanz over a thumping reggaeton beat. Its chart run lasted all summer and reached number 23 on the Hot 100. "In a chart run that lasted all summer La Tortura reached an impressive number 23 on the hot 100."
**Don't Bother (2005)** : The lead single from *Oral Fixation Vol. 2*. It missed the top 40, peaking at number 42. "Don't bother the lead single from Oral Fixation Volume 2 missed the top 40, peaking at number 42."
**Hips Don't Lie (2006) - Only U.S. Number One** : When Epic Records asked Wyclef Jean in early 2006 to help revive Shakira's album, he suggested remaking his song "Dance Like This" as "Hips Don't Lie." The new version had a hybrid salsa-reggaeton rhythm and sampled Latin trumpets that Wyclef recreated from the earlier rap hit "Deja Vu (Uptown Baby)." Epic rush-released it in spring 2006. By April it was in the top 40, and by June it was number 1 on the Hot 100, her only American pop chart-topper. "Given Shakira's own live dancing style you might say the reboot was perfectly on brand."
**She Wolf (2009)** (transcribed as "she will") : An occasional attempt at mainstream Anglo pop, a disco-fueled hit. "She would occasionally try her hand at mainstream Anglo pop like 2009's disco fueled hit she will."
**Waka Waka (This Time for Africa) (2010) - Biggest-Selling Global Single** : Like Ricky Martin twelve years earlier, Shakira was chosen to record a World Cup anthem, this time for the first final held in South Africa. She recorded it with South African group Freshlyground. It barely made the U.S. top 40 but topped charts in more than a dozen countries and sold 15 million copies. "While the song only scraped the top 40 in America it topped charts in more than a dozen countries and ultimately sold 15 million copies."

## curiosities
**Born Shakira Isabel Mebarak Ripoll in Colombia** : Like the late Selena, she ruled a clearly defined Latin subgenre, in her case rock blended with modern pop, echoing Anglo stars like Alanis Morissette. Her Latin music following was arguably more passionate than anyone's. "You might say Shakira was built for crossover."
**First Big Break Judged by Ricky Martin** : Her first big break came in 1993 at a Chilean music festival. Judge Ricky Martin, then 20, picked the 16-year-old to win, but she finished third. "In an ironic footnote, Shakira's first big break back in 1993 came at a Chilean music festival."
**Super Bowl 2020 Passing of the Torch** : She opened the 2020 Super Bowl halftime show, the first devoted entirely to Latin American performers, and was joined by Jennifer Lopez, J Balvin, and Bad Bunny. "Two women from Latin Bumita version 1.0 showcasing two men who were at the forefront of Bumita 2.0."

Rodney Jerkins produced and co-wrote it; it could just as easily have gone to Brandy, Monica, or Destiny's Child. It reached number 1 on the Hot 100 in five weeks, replacing Martin's song at the top. "Rising to number one on the Hot 100 in just five weeks, actually knocking out Martin's Livin' LaVita Loca."
**Waiting for Tonight (1999)** : A more Latin-flavored Latin house track that reached number 8 pop and number 1 on the club chart. "the Latin house track Waiting for Tonight, a number eight pop number one club hit."
**On the Floor (2011) - Lambada Sample** : Sampled Kaoma's 1990 dance hit "Lambada" and featured a rap from Pitbull. It reached number 3 on the Hot 100 in summer 2011 and number 1 on the dance club chart. "Her smash hit on the floor sampled Kaelma's 1990 dance smash Lombata."

## curiosities
**Bronx Upbringing and Dancing Career** : Growing up in the Bronx, Lopez tried acting, dancing, gymnastics, and track. In the early '90s she was a choreographer and backup dancer for New Kids on the Block and Janet Jackson, and a "Fly Girl" on the Wayans Brothers' *In Living Color*. Singing was the talent she had pursued least before her mid-20s. "Jennifer Lopez would at last become a singer after she had tried and succeeded at everything else."
**Playing Selena (1997) Launched Her Singing Career** : As a Puerto Rican actress playing Tejano music's most famous singer, she drew scorn from parts of the Mexican-American community but won acclaim. She did not sing in the film. Its reception convinced Sony Music executive Tommy Mottola, the man who discovered Mariah Carey, that she could have a full pop career. "In the 1997 biopic Selena, the late Latin superstar was played by Jennifer Lopez."
**Marc Anthony Not Yet a Couple in 1999** : Lopez and Marc Anthony both broke through on the Hot 100 in 1999 but were not dating then. They married five years later. "future paramores, Jennifer Lopez and Mark Anthony were not dating that year. They would marry five years later."
**Rechristened J.Lo** : After the first boom she moved toward hip-hop-adjacent anthems as "J.Lo." At the 2020 Super Bowl she headlined and waved a Puerto Rican flag. "At one point, the woman who is now mainly known as JLo even waved a Puerto Rican flag."

It combined a reggaeton beat with Spanish flamenco and spent 24 weeks at number 1 on Billboard's Latin Pop Airplay chart, a record at the time. Boosted by digital sales and mainstream top 40 airplay, it reached number 12 on the Hot 100. "By Lando spent 24 weeks on top of Billboard's Latin pop airplay chart then a record."

"Enrique's three Spanish language albums for the Fono Visa label had sold 14 million copies."
**Return to Spanish-Language Radio** : He kept pursuing pop hits until about 2010 with solid success. By the mid-2010s he had refocused on Spanish-language radio and scored some of the biggest hits of his career. "by the mid-tens he had refocused his energies on the Spanish language radio dial."

# artist - Ritchie Valens

## songs
**La Bamba (1959) - First Spanish-Language Rock-Era Crossover** : Reached number 22 on the Hot 100 in early 1959, the first major Spanish-language crossover hit of the rock era. Valens took songwriting credit, but it was an old instructional dance song from Veracruz, Mexico, dating to at least the early 20th century and common at weddings. It set the template for early rock-era Latin crossover: traditional songs adapted by modern performers. "In essence, an instructional dance song from the Mexican state of Veracruz."

**Dreaming of You (1995) - Posthumous Number One** : Her first partly English album, meant to launch her as a multilingual, multi-genre crossover artist. "Dreaming of You became the first predominantly Spanish language album to debut at number one on the Billboard 200 album chart."

## curiosities
**The Ultimate What-If of Latin Crossover** : Selena Quintanilla-Pérez signed with EMI's Latin division in 1989 under a long-term plan to break her in the Anglo market, and became known as the Queen of Tejano music. In March 1995, at 23, she was shot and killed by her fan club president, Yolanda Saldívar. She became Latin music's martyr and its near miss. "Selena became not only Latin music's martyr, but its near miss."

# artist - Luis Fonsi

## songs
**Nada Es Para Siempre** : A Latin tracks chart-topper from his guitar-based love song phase in the mid-2000s. "his Latin tracks chart topper, nada espada siempre or nothing is forever."
**Despacito (2017) - Biggest Latin Crossover Single Ever** : Around 2015 Fonsi wanted to fuse his guitar ballads with Latin dance rhythms. He and co-writer Erika Ender first imagined a Colombian-style cumbia, then settled on the dembow beat of reggaeton. Daddy Yankee recorded the duet in fall 2016. Released at the start of 2017, it topped charts in about a dozen Latin American countries and led Billboard's Hot Latin Songs by mid-February. Justin Bieber's remix took it from number 48 to number 9 to number 1 in three weeks, the first primarily Spanish number 1 since "Macarena" 21 years earlier. It spent 16 weeks at number 1, tying the record held by Mariah Carey and Boyz II Men, while making almost no compromise with American pop radio. "Indeed, it was Bieber who had to learn a bit of Spanish and adapt to the sound of the song."

## curiosities
**Reliable Hitmaker From San Juan** : A Latin pop veteran since the late '90s, never especially hip, and so constant on Spanish-language radio that a generation of Hispanic Americans probably couldn't remember a time without him. "an entire generation of Hispanic Americans probably couldn't remember a time he wasn't somewhere on the airwaves."

# genre - Latin Music

## curiosities
**More Than a Genre** : Latin music spans sounds developed over centuries by Afro-Caribbean and Iberian descendants: salsa, merengue, bachata, Mexican ranchera, Brazilian bossa nova and samba, and Cuban son. Together they form a parallel hit parade with millions in sales. "All of these Latin genres have sold millions and form a kind of parallel hit parade hidden in plain sight."
**Influence on Mainstream Pop** : Mariachi brass and the piano clave rhythm, derived from Latin jazz and salsa, have shaped English-language pop and dance music. "the irresistible rhythm of a piano clave derived from Latin jazz and salsa."
**Treated as Novelties Until the Mid-'90s** : Until the mid-'90s, Spanish-language hits were seen as party or dance novelties, and legendary Latin songs often never appeared on U.S. flagship charts. Through the '70s and '80s a Spanish song usually needed a "meta-narrative" or backstory to cross over. "Spanish language hits were basically treated as novelties, fun for parties or dancing, but not much more."

# genre - Reggaeton

## curiosities
**Origins and Sound** : A mix of Jamaican dancehall, Spanish-language street tales, and American hip-hop that exploded in Puerto Rico in the late '90s. Its core is the dembow rhythm from reggae, plus a rap-derived snare and kick. 2004 was its mainstream breakthrough year, and in the 2010s it fused with Latin ballads, culminating in "Despacito." "The core element of the genre was the Dembo rhythm taken from reggae, augmented by a rap-derived snare and kick drum."

# curiosity

**Latin Crossover Canon** : "La Bamba," "Guantanamera," "The Girl from Ipanema," "I Like It Like That," and "Oye Como Va" became touchstones even non-Latin Americans recognize. They gave later performers both cultural credibility and instant familiarity, and several came back in later hits by Los Lobos, the Blackout All-Stars, Cardi B, and Bad Bunny. "songs that offered both cultural credibility and instant familiarity."
**Latin Boomita Version 1.0 (1999-2001)** : Started by Ricky Martin's number 1 the week ending May 8, 1999. In 1999 the most consistently high-charting Hot 100 acts were Latin stars, ahead of teen pop acts like Britney Spears and the Backstreet Boys. "on the hot 100, the more consistently high charting performers were all Latin stars."
**Backlash: "Inauthentic and Watered Down"** : By 2000 every hit of the wave was in English, and many were written or produced by non-Latin collaborators aiming at Anglophone crossover. Critics and longtime Latin pop fans called it inauthentic. "critics, and many longtime fans of Latin Pop called the wave inauthentic and watered down."
**Latin Boomita Version 2.0** : Started by "Despacito" in 2017. Stars like J Balvin and Bad Bunny compromised far less on language and sound than Ricky Martin, Enrique Iglesias, or J.Lo had. "this time, the Latin stars are compromising far less than Ricky and Ricky or J-Level."
