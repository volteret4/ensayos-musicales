## songs
**Old Town Road (2019)** : This song became the longest-lasting number one hit of all time on America's flagship pop chart, the Billboard Hot 100, holding the top position for 19 weeks. It was a singular phenomenon, achieving rapid and broad cultural penetration, connecting with diverse audiences across over 40 countries and consumed by fans of multiple genres including pop, R&B, rap, dance, rock, and country. Remarkably, it was Lil Nas X's first-ever charting single, contrasting with the long careers of superstars like The Beatles, Michael Jackson, and Madonna whose biggest hits spent fewer weeks at number one. The song is described as an "apotheosis," culminating trends in genre, technology, consumption, and identity, simultaneously lionizing and satirizing genre tropes and troubling the very idea of genre. It's a country song built out of an alternative rock sample (from Nine Inch Nails), a hip hop song in which nobody truly raps, and a comical song that transcends novelty. It achieved virality organically through profoundly inorganic technologies like YouTube, Spotify, and TikTok. Lil Nas X intentionally used a "cornpone accent" and lines like "I got the horses in the back" to make the song funny and go viral. It is an efficient song, with the original version clocking in at 1 minute 53 seconds, featuring a very enveloping hook from the banjo sample, catchy melody, and a good beat. The song is also considered a queer anthem, as Montero Hill came out during Pride Month 2019, in the middle of its chart-topping run. "In at least one key statistical sense, no song has done what Old Town Road did."
**Old Town Road (Remix) (2019)** : The remix of "Old Town Road" features Billy Ray Cyrus and extends the song from 1 minute 53 seconds to 2 minutes 37 seconds, while still remaining one of the shorter recent number one songs. Billy Ray Cyrus's verse, "hat down, cross-town, living like a rock star," adds to the song's catchiness and enhances its inherent country elements. Lil Nas X sought Billy Ray Cyrus for the remix because he was the only country star he knew, primarily through Billy Ray's role as Miley Cyrus's father on the TV show Hannah Montana, not due to an affinity for his music like "Achy Breaky Heart." "And it takes what already kind of had country bones in the first place and makes it more country."
**Montero (Call Me By Your Name) (2021)** : This song is one of Lil Nas X's multiple number one hits achieved in different genres, demonstrating his ability to capture the zeitgeist and defy the "one-hit wonder" label. "He said multiple number one hits in different genres."

**Music Charts - Pre-Soundscan Era (Pre-1991)** : In the era before Soundscan, chart data was susceptible to manipulation and "fudging" by record companies. It was believed that records typically rose, crested, and fell within 12 to 15 weeks. This period is contrasted with the more accurate and dynamic post-Soundscan era. "the music business of 1968 and that of 2019 were very different."
**Soundscan (Introduced 1991)** : Soundscan revolutionized music chart tracking by providing accurate sales data from barcodes in record stores, replacing manual reporting. This marked a "BC AD moment of chart following" that made it harder for record companies to manipulate chart positions. Within a month of its online launch, N.W.A. achieved a number one album, demonstrating its immediate impact on revealing true popularity. "Soundscan moment literally at billboard, they talk about history in terms of pre sound scan and post sound scan."
**Billboard Hot 100 - Chart Formula Evolution** : Since its inception in 1958, the Hot 100 has measured sales of songs and radio airplay. However, the introduction of streaming as a third factor has fundamentally changed its "metabolism." Streaming data is now larger than sales and airplay combined, functioning as a blend of both (user selection like buying, but ephemeral like radio). This enables songs to debut at number one more easily and hold on for much longer periods, leading to frequent resetting of longevity records. "Now remember, for all of its history, the hot 100 has existed since 1958. It has always measured at least two things. Sales of songs and radio air play. And it's an average of blend of those two things."
**BDS (Broadcast Data Systems) (Early 1990s)** : BDS revolutionized radio airplay tracking by digitally fingerprinting songs played on the radio, akin to an "early version of Shazam." This accurate measurement of airplay, combined with Soundscan's sales data, fundamentally "upended our understanding of how records rise and fall on the charts." "the hot 100 gets converted thanks to the airplay you're talking about, it was called BDS or broadcast data systems. And it completely upended our understanding of how records rise and fall on the charts."
**Post-Soundscan/BDS Chart Behavior (Post-1991)** : After the implementation of Soundscan and BDS, the understanding of chart dynamics changed significantly. Albums were observed to behave more like movies, typically debuting at their highest position. Songs also began to break much faster, debut higher, and exhibit much greater longevity on the Hot 100, leading to rapid successive breaking of "most weeks at number one" records by artists like Boys II Men, Whitney Houston, and Mariah Carey & Boys II Men. "What we learned after we had accurate data was that isn't really, it certainly isn't the way albums behave."
**Streaming Impact on Chart Longevity** : Streaming has become the largest factor in chart performance, enabling songs to debut at number one more easily and maintain their positions for extended periods. This has led to the resetting of "most weeks on the whole chart" records by artists such as The Weeknd ("Blinding Lights") and Glass Animals ("Heat Waves"), as well as the annual return of Christmas classics. "The record for most weeks in hot 100 history, like most weeks on the whole chart has been reset several times in the last few years."
**Christmas Music Chart Resurgence** : Modern streaming data has allowed classic Christmas songs like Mariah Carey's holiday hits, Brenda Lee's "Rocking Around the Christmas Tree," Andy Williams' "It's the Most Wonderful Time of the Year," and Wham!'s "Last Christmas" to return to the charts annually. **Old Town Road (2019) - Chart Controversy & Coming Out** : Lil Nas X uploaded this song to streaming services, tagging it as pop, hip hop, and country. It debuted at number 19 on Billboard's Hot Country Songs chart, but was subsequently removed by Billboard after intervention from what was called the "Nashville industrial complex," which argued it was not a country record.
