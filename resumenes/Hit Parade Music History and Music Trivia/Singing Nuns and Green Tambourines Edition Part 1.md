"San Francisco, the song, was a sizable national hit, reaching number four on Billboard's Hot 100 in that summer of 1967."

# genre - Easy Listening

## curiosities
**Billboard Chart History** : Billboard magazine initiated its first easy listening chart in July 1961, designed to track a popular radio format that catered to older listeners or those disaffected by rock and roll. This "mellow music chart" went through various names, including "Middle Road singles" in the mid-1960s, and was a "chart juggernaut" in the early 60s. "In July of 1961, Billboard magazine launched its first easy listening chart to track a popular radio format that modified older listeners and those of any age who were just alienated by rock and roll."
**Evolution to Adult Contemporary** : The easy listening radio format evolved from its 1960s prominence, undergoing name changes such as "Middle Road singles" in the mid-60s, before eventually converting to "adult contemporary" in 1979. This is the name by which "soft radio music designed to waft in the background" is commonly known today. "Until finally in 1979, the chart converted to the name by which we know this radio format today, adult contemporary."
**Modern Perception** : Despite its significant chart presence and popularity in the 1960s, the "60s version of easy listening was overtaken in our collective memory by more youthful rock, pop and soul hits." This has led to its underrepresentation in modern cultural recollections of the decade. "But the 60s version of easy listening was overtaken in our collective memory by more youthful rock, pop and soul hits."

"It was written by the mamas and the poppas, John Phillips, at the behest of producer, Lou Adler, to help promote the 1967 concert they were organizing, the Monterey Pop Festival."
