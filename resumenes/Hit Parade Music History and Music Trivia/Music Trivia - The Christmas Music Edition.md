# artist - Ace of Base

## genres
Pop
Teen Pop

## albums
**The Bridge (1995) - Second Studio Album** : The Swedish pop group's second album, appropriately titled The Bridge, which contained the 1995 hit single "Beautiful Life." "taken from their second album, called Appropriately Enough, The Bridge."

## songs
**Beautiful Life (1995) - Max Martin's First US Top 40 Hit** : A 1995 hit single taken from The Bridge. It was the first American Top 40 hit for producer and songwriter Max Martin, who went on to score dozens more. The song is framed as a bridge between the early-90s era of grunge and gangsta rap and the late-90s era of boy bands and bling. "Beautiful life was the first American top 40 hit for producer, songwriter, and pop mastermind Max Martin."
**All That She Wants - Non-Idiomatic English Hook** : Cited as an example of Swedish pop songwriting whose unusual English phrasing makes for a stronger pop hook. Nobody fluent in idiomatic English would phrase the line that way, but it works better as a pop song. "all that she wants is another baby like nobody would say it that way who you know knows idiomatic English but it makes for a better pop song."

## curiosities
**Precursor to the Late-90s Teen Pop Wave** : Ace of Base came before the late-90s teen pop boom led by Swedish craftsmen behind Britney Spears, Backstreet Boys, NSYNC and Robyn, the hitmakers of the MTV TRL era. "Ace of Base were a precursor to that whole teen pop wave."

# artist - Max Martin

## genres
Pop
Teen Pop

## songs
**Beautiful Life (1995) - First US Top 40 Hit** : Ace of Base's 1995 single was Max Martin's first American Top 40 hit. He went on to score dozens more. "He would go on to score dozens more."
**...Baby One More Time (1998) - First Hot 100 Number One** : The first Hot 100 number one for both Britney Spears and writer-producer Max Martin. Martin's English was "still a bit of a work in progress," so he did not foresee how people would read the phrase "hit me baby one more time." He didn't understand why anyone objected to it and refused to change it. Martin had originally thought of it as an R&B song, and others had to persuade him it was really maximalist pop suited to a newly signed teenager. "he did not foresee how people would interpret the phrase hit me baby one more time he didn't know why anybody would have an issue with it and then he refused to change it."

## curiosities
**Never Gives Interviews** : Max Martin no longer gives interviews, and Jessica Goldstein could not speak with him for her 2018 oral history of "...Baby One More Time." His air of mystery feeds speculation about how he works. "he doesn't give interviews he doesn't talk to anyone it makes the whole process like that much more intriguing like how does he do it what are the secrets what is the algorithm inside of his brain."
**Rivaling Lennon–McCartney for Hot 100 Number Ones** : Twenty years after "...Baby One More Time," Martin rivals Lennon–McCartney for the most number one hits on the Hot 100. In 1998 he was seen as very talented and a "worthy gamble." Now his excellence is treated as inevitable and he is considered one of the greats. "now the perception is that his excellence is inevitable and that he's it's a given that he's one of the greats."
**Thought He Was Writing an R&B Song** : Martin believed "...Baby One More Time" was an R&B song. Others had to convince him it was maximalist pop fit for a newly signed teenager who had herself wanted to sing like an R&B diva. "it took some people saying to him well this is not really an R&B song we know you think it's an R&B song."
**Cheiron Team in Stockholm** : Martin worked out of the Cheiron team in Stockholm, which produced both "...Baby One More Time" and its follow-up single "Sometimes." "also produced by Martin's Sharon team in Stockholm."

# artist - Britney Spears

## genres
Pop
Teen Pop

## albums
**...Baby One More Time - Debut Album** : Britney Spears' debut album, produced largely by Max Martin's Cheiron team in Stockholm. Its singles included the title track (her first Hot 100 number one), "Sometimes" (number 21), and "(You Drive Me) Crazy," which returned her to the Top 10 in summer 1999. "Born to Make You Happy" and "From the Bottom of My Broken Heart" are also associated with the album. "the second top 40 hit from the baby one more time album also produced by Martin's Sharon team in Stockholm."

## songs
**...Baby One More Time (1998) - First Hot 100 Number One** : Her first Hot 100 number one and Max Martin's first. Much of the song's alchemy rests on a cultural misunderstanding: Martin's imperfect English produced the line "hit me baby one more time," which he refused to change. The video, directed by Nigel Dick, followed Britney's own treatment, including the Catholic schoolgirl uniforms. Its 20th anniversary in 2018 was treated as a cultural event. "if he had spoken English like in American he never would have written such and such a great and like electric lyric for this song."
**Sometimes (1999) - Second Single** : A mid-tempo ballad produced by Max Martin's Cheiron team in Stockholm. It was the immediate follow-up to "...Baby One More Time" and the second Top 40 hit from the album, peaking at number 21 on the Hot 100. "a mid tempo ballad was the immediate follow up to the baby one more time single and it peaked on the hot 100 at number 21."
**(You Drive Me) Crazy (1999) - Third Single** : The album's third single, which returned Britney and Max Martin to the Top 10 in summer 1999. "the album's third single you drive me crazy returned Britney and Max to the top 10 in the summer of 99."
**Oops!... I Did It Again - Video Concept Recycled** : Nigel Dick's original treatment for the "...Baby One More Time" video had Britney on Mars. She rejected it in favour of her own idea, and the Mars concept later became the "Oops!... I Did It Again" video. "his original idea was what ended up being the video for oops I did it again it was Britney on Mars."

## curiosities
**Britney Drove Her Own Image** : Contrary to the perception that she was created by middle-aged male record executives, everyone Jessica Goldstein interviewed for her oral history, including director Nigel Dick and label president Barry Weiss, insisted the vision was hers. The video treatment, the outfit and the attitude all came from her. "everyone I talked to who worked with her was adamant that it was her. It was her choice that it was her vision."
**Catholic Schoolgirl Video Concept** : Britney came up with the Catholic schoolgirl uniforms and the whole video treatment. Newly signed and with little life experience, she demanded to speak to director Nigel Dick because she had a better idea. Dick recalled being a grown man taking orders from a 16-year-old girl, then deciding she knew better and going along with her. "I'm a grown man taking orders from a 16 year old girl. But then you realize you know what she knows this better than I do."
**Wanted to Be an R&B Diva** : When she was signed, the teenage Britney wanted to sing like an R&B diva. "this teenager we just signed who. You know actually kind of wanted to sing like an R&B Diva to begin with."
**Undercredited as Architect of Her Career** : Many sources, particularly men, insisted Britney architected her own career and has not received credit for things usually credited to men: creative vision, reading the marketplace, and connecting with fans. "so many people particularly men insist that Britney is the architect of her career and that she has not been given the credit that she is due for."
**The Label Tried to Make More Britneys** : Joe Levy of Rolling Stone remarked in the oral history that the record company would have created more than one Britney if it could, and that it tried. Mandy Moore was cited as an example. "if the record company could have created more than one Britney they would have done it and they tried."
**Entertainment Weekly Oral History (2018)** : Jessica Goldstein's October 2018 Entertainment Weekly piece, "Britney Spears Wanted to Be a Star," was an oral history of "...Baby One More Time," written for the song's 20th anniversary in response to a call for pop-anniversary pitches. "I had seen this call out for ideas about pop anniversaries and this was the first thing that came to my mind."
**A Child Viewer's Perspective, Revisited** : Goldstein, younger than Britney, watched MTV every day as a child and was transfixed by the video. She did not understand why adults found it "problematic," because Britney seemed very grown up. As an adult she now sees Britney as the very young girl she was. "I really did not understand why adults thought anything about it was to use a 2018 word problematic. Because I thought she seemed very grown up."

# genre - Teen Pop

## curiosities
**Swedish Craftsmen Behind Late-90s Teen Pop** : The late-90s teen pop boom of the MTV TRL era (Britney Spears, Backstreet Boys, NSYNC, Robyn) was crafted largely by Swedish producers and songwriters, part of a line of Swedish chart dominance going back to ABBA. "the Swedish craftsmen who were behind the Britney Spears, Backstreet Boys, NSYNC and Robin Hitz."
**Nostalgia for the TRL Era** : Revisiting the era as adults, its former child fans still feel nostalgic but now see its young stars differently. "To have gone from just consuming that culture kind of thoughtlessly as children to now thinking about it, still enjoying it, still feeling like all of those all that nostalgia."

# curiosity

**Swedish Songwriters and the "Happy Accident" of English** : Swedish songwriters and producers have dominated the charts since ABBA. Their non-native English often produces odd phrasing that makes for stronger pop hooks, as in Roxette's "Joyride," Ace of Base's "All That She Wants" and Max Martin's "...Baby One More Time." "so much of it hinges on some cultural misunderstanding."
**The UK Christmas Number One Tradition** : Only Britain has institutionalised the race for the Christmas number one. Every year Britons obsessively discuss it, and bookies take bets on which song will top the UK chart in Christmas week. "every year the British obsessively talk about bet on literally their bookies who take bets on this on what will be number one on the UK charts the week of Christmas."
**Novelty Records Win Christmas Number One** : In nearly 50 years of following and betting on the Christmas number one, novelty records have often beaten more serious or solemn songs to the title, among them St. Winifred's School Choir (1980), Mr Blobby (1993) and Bob the Builder (2000). "a number of novelty records have taken the title often beating more serious or solemn fair for the number one spot."
**The 1987 Christmas Number One Race** : The 1987 contenders included Rick Astley's "When I Fall in Love," Mel & Kim's "Rockin' Around the Christmas Tree," T'Pau's "China in Your Hand" and the Pogues and Kirsty MacColl's "Fairytale of New York." The Pet Shop Boys' "Always on My Mind" won. "what 1987 number one hit held off the pokes and McCall and went down as that years Christmas topper."
**Love Actually and "Christmas Is All Around"** : Most Americans who know the Christmas number one tradition know it from Love Actually. In one plot, an ageing rock star played by Bill Nighy records what he admits is a terrible cover of "Love Is All Around," rewritten as "Christmas Is All Around," in a naked bid for the Christmas number one. "he's recording a terrible by his own admission terrible cover of love is all around that he has turned into Christmas is all around in a naked bid to score a Christmas number one."
**Billboard/Nielsen Holiday Download Rankings (2016)** : In late 2016 Billboard published a list of the most downloaded US holiday songs based on Nielsen data. Nielsen began tracking digital downloads in 2003. "billboard published a list of the most downloaded holiday songs in the US based on data from the Nielsen company which began tracking digital downloads in two thousand three."
