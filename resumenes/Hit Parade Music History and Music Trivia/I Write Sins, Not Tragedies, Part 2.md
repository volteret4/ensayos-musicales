## genres

## curiosities
**Heart-on-Sleeve Lyrics in Early-80s DC Hardcore** : Minor Threat were a seminal band of the Washington DC early-80s hardcore scene. Frontman Ian MacKaye's lyrics carried open, vulnerable sentiments not normally associated with punk, which made the band an early precursor of emo, a genre that at first was defined more by lyrics than by music. "a closer listen to frontman Ian McCye's lyrics revealed heart-on-sleeve sentiments, not normally associated with punk."
**Inspiration for Rites of Spring** : Minor Threat inspired the formation of the DC band Rites of Spring, whose Guy Picciotto matched MacKaye's open-hearted lyrical approach. "Minor Threat, in turn, inspired the formation of DC band rights of spring."

## albums
**Weezer (1994) - The Blue Album, Self-Titled Debut** : This Los Angeles band broke wide open in 1994 and 1995 with their self-titled debut, better known as The Blue Album. It was produced by The Cars frontman and new wave icon Ric Ocasek. The record went double platinum and rode the Billboard album chart for a year and a half. Gen X fans saw it as clever nerd rock, while millennial fans connected with Rivers Cuomo laying bare his insecurities over crashing pseudo-punk. "If it wasn't quite Emo, it was certainly emotional."
**Pinkerton (1996) - Commercial Misfire Turned Emo Totem** : Rivers Cuomo's follow-up was full of even more showily self-pitying and self-flagellating lyrics, with the opening track literally titled "Tired of Sex." Gen X critics gave it poor reviews, and the music business saw it as a colossal misfire. It produced no top 10 modern rock hits, dropped off the charts within three months, and did not go gold that year. Over the next five years it became a word-of-mouth hit, quietly selling to thousands of teenagers who came to regard it as an unlikely emo totem. By Cuomo's return in 2001 it had gone gold. "Pinkerton spent the next five years as a word of mouth hit, quietly selling to thousands of teenagers."

## songs
**Buddy Holly (1994)** : This kitschy single paired Boomer-era pop culture with Generation X slang and was a smash across alt rock, album rock, and even Top 40 radio. For Weezer's initial Gen X fans, it defined the band as the kings of too-clever nerd rock. "Buddy Holly, Weezer's kitschy single-pairing Boomer-era pop culture with Generation X slang was a smash across Alt Rock, Album Rock and even Top 40 Radio."
**My Name Is Jonas (1994)** : Teen fans heard this Blue Album track as Rivers Cuomo laying bare his insecurities over crashing pseudo-punk. "Teen fans keyed into Rivers Cuomo laying bare his insecurities over crashing pseudo punk."
**Undone – The Sweater Song (1994)** : This Blue Album hit was cited alongside "My Name Is Jonas" as a song where millennial fans keyed into Cuomo's vulnerability rather than nerd-rock cleverness. "On tracks like My Name is Jonas and The Hit, Undone, the Sweater Song."
**Say It Ain't So (1995)** : The Blue Album's final and most acclaimed single reached No. 7 on the modern rock chart in summer 1995. It combined cryptic but very vulnerable lyrics with metallic power chords. Pitchfork later ranked it among the 10 best songs of the 1990s and called Weezer an influential touchstone for young bands. "a bizarrely influential touchstone for so many young bands."
**Pinkerton Lead Single (1996)** : The lead single from *Pinkerton* opened with a notoriously blunt line, typical of the album's self-flagellating tone. "Goddamn you half Japanese girls, do it to me every time."
**Tired of Sex (1996)** : *Pinkerton*'s opening track, notable for its literal-minded title. "its opening track sported the literal-minded title, Tired of Sex."
**Pink Triangle (1996)** : Rivers Cuomo's lament about a possibly gay girl he was crushing on from afar. It connected with lovelorn Gen Y fans who embraced *Pinkerton* as an emo totem. "Rivers Cuomo's lament about a possibly gay girl he was crushing on from afar."

## curiosities
**Emo-Adjacent Pivot to the Millennial Generation** : Weezer were hard to classify: a little grungy, a little punk, a bit metal, and very poppy. Still, they proved oddly pivotal to emo's pop crossover. Gen X fans and millennial fans heard the band in very different ways. "they would prove oddly pivotal to Emo's pop crosser."
**Self-Imposed Seclusion and 2001 Return** : Rivers Cuomo went into self-imposed seclusion after *Pinkerton*. When he began releasing music again in 2001, *Pinkerton* had gone gold and his fanbase had been rebooted with a new army of young fans. "his fanbase had been rebooted with a new army of young fans."
**Hella Mega Tour 2021** : Weezer joined Green Day and Fall Out Boy on the Hella Mega tour after concerts reopened following the first wave of the COVID-19 pandemic. "fallout boy joined green day as well as weezer on the touring hella mega festival."

(The transcript renders the title "Bleed America.") "the middle was the lead single from Jimmy Eat World's third album Bleed America."

## songs
**The Middle (2001)** : Released in fall 2001 as the lead single from *Bleed American*, it disguised its misfit sympathies beneath a shiny pop exterior. On the surface it played like a perky party jam. The video showed its real theme: an introvert at a house party of popular kids stripping to their underwear finds an equally shy girl. Its encouraging lyrics ("just try your best, try everything you can. Be yourself, it doesn't matter if it's good enough") made emo broadly relatable. It debuted on the modern rock chart in November 2001 and took nearly six months to reach No. 1 there in April 2002. It then crossed to the Hot 100 and reached No. 5 on the pop chart by June 2002. "The middle made emo broadly relatable and musically irresistible."
**Work (2004)** : This anthemic 2004 single gave the band another modern rock No. 1. "Jimmy Eat World, who scored another modern rock number one with 2004's Anthemic work."

## curiosities
**Arizona Buzz Band of the Late 90s** : The band was among the late-90s groups closest to the emo sound defined by Jawbreaker and Sunny Day Real Estate. They infused their punk-pop with brisk melodies. "Arizona band Jimmy Eat World, who infused their punk pop with brisk melodies."

## albums
**Dookie (1994) - Peaked at No. 2** : Green Day's major-label breakthrough, which helped galvanize pop punk in the mid-90s. It peaked at No. 2, one of several Green Day albums to miss No. 1. "both 1994's Duky and its 1995 follow-up Insomniac had peaked at number two."
**Insomniac (1995) - Peaked at No. 2** : The follow-up to *Dookie* also stalled at No. 2. "its 1995 follow-up Insomniac had peaked at number two."
**Warning (2000) - Lowest Seller Since Indie Days** : This album only went gold, Green Day's lowest seller since their indie days. It suggested the eternally youthful band was getting long in the tooth. "Green Day's 2000 album, Warning, only went gold, their lowest seller since their indie days."
**American Idiot (2004) - First No. 1 Album** : Billie Joe Armstrong conceived it as a Tommy-style concept album with interconnected songs about suburbia and American politics. It was released just weeks before the 2004 US presidential election. Green Day's seventh studio album debuted at No. 1, their first chart-topper after more than a decade on a major label. It became their biggest seller since *Dookie* and their longest-charting album at nearly three years, and it produced their biggest pop hits. It won the Grammy for Best Rock Album and was nominated for Album of the Year. "Green Day had become Downrights Respectable."

## songs
**Minority (2000)** : A modern rock No. 1 that was only a minor Hot 100 hit, even though it sounded like a straight-up pop record. It showed that even Green Day was not guaranteed Top 40 success. "Even the mighty Green Day was not guaranteed to hit every time at top 40 radio."
**Boulevard of Broken Dreams (2004)** : A stately ballad-slash-anthem whose tune echoed Oasis's "Wonderwall." It peaked at No. 2 on the Hot 100 in early 2005, held from No. 1 only by 50 Cent's "Candy Shop." It won the Grammy for Record of the Year and Video of the Year at the 2005 MTV Video Music Awards. "only 50 cents candy shop prevented it from reaching number one."
**Wake Me Up When September Ends (2005)** : The final top 10 single from *American Idiot*. In October 2005 it sat in the top 10 just a couple of places from Fall Out Boy's "Sugar, We're Goin Down." This was a coincidence that nobody at the time saw as a passing of the pop-punk torch. "No one at the time would have regarded it as a passing of the pop punk torch."

## curiosities
**Arrival at Adulthood in Their Mid-30s** : By the mid-2000s Green Day had reached something like adulthood. They kept scoring hit albums and rock radio hits, but teenagers turned to a younger band that worshipped Green Day. "Green Day's unwitting arrival at something like adulthood in their mid-30s was in a way well-timed."
**Rock and Roll Hall of Fame Induction 2015** : Green Day were inducted in 2015 and presented by Fall Out Boy, the band that had in essence succeeded them in punk-to-pop crossover. Pete Wentz joked that fans often mistake Fall Out Boy for Green Day. "holy shit I just got a picture with fucking green day."
**Hella Mega Tour 2021** : Green Day toured with Fall Out Boy and Weezer on the Hella Mega tour after concerts reopened in 2021. "fallout boy joined green day as well as weezer on the touring hella mega festival."

Its songs included "Switchblades and Infidelity," "The World's Not Waiting (For Five Tired Boys in a Broken Down Van)," "Parker Lewis Can't Lose (But I'm Gonna Give It My Best Shot)," and the comparatively simple but self-deprecating "Honorable Mention." "sounding like hot topic, but feeling like CBGB."
**From Under the Cork Tree (2005) - Major-Label Debut** : Recorded over the winter of 2004–2005. The title comes from the children's book *The Story of Ferdinand*, about a bull who preferred lying under a cork tree to bullfighting. Patrick Stump wrote all the music and Pete Wentz all the lyrics, which produced soaring melodies with random, lyrically unrelated titles like "Sophomore Slump or Comeback of the Year." "Patrick Stump wrote all of the albums music and Pete Wentz, all of the lyrics."
**Infinity on High (2007) - Eagerly Anticipated Follow-Up** : The album opened with a flex: a track titled "Thriller," after Michael Jackson's blockbuster album, featuring then-label boss Jay-Z as a hype man. ## songs
**Sugar, We're Goin Down (2005)** : One of the last tracks written for *From Under the Cork Tree*, it became the first single. The label saw its potential but hated the chorus and nearly made Wentz and Stump change it, as Wentz later told Spin ("our label told us the chorus was too wordy and the guitars were too heavy, and that the radio wasn't going to play it"). The crunching guitars and inscrutable wordiness made it emo pop's signature hit and a showcase for Stump's voice. It took all of summer 2005 to climb, cracking the modern rock top 10 by late August and the Hot 100 top 10 about a month later. By October it sat near Green Day's "Wake Me Up When September Ends." "rocking but also aching, punky but also poppy."
**Dance, Dance (2005)** : Its video, shot in a high school gym with formation dancing, was a TRL favorite. It reached No. 9 on the Hot 100 by January 2006 and peaked at No. 2 on the modern rock chart, higher than "Sugar." It confirmed Fall Out Boy as a millennial favorite and kicked off an epic year for emo. "It also kicked off an epic year for EMA, filled with frothy peaks and wordy song titles."
**Thriller (2007)** : The opening track of *Infinity on High*, named after Michael Jackson's album and featuring an intro by Jay-Z, then the band's label boss. "they titled the opening track thriller after the blockbuster michael jackson album."
**This Ain't a Scene, It's an Arms Race (2007)** : The first single from *Infinity on High*, structured around a crowd-chant hook. It debuted at No. 2 on the Hot 100, fueled by more than 160,000 first-week downloads on iTunes. Only Beyoncé's "Irreplaceable" kept it from No. 1. "fallout boy would have debuted at number one if it hadn't been for the boss man's fiance."
**Thnks fr th Mmrs (2007)** : The follow-up single spent about two months longer on the Hot 100 than "Arms Race," despite peaking only at No. 11. "arms race was a flashy hit but not as enduring as fallout boys follow up thanks for the memories."
**My Songs Know What You Did in the Dark (Light Em Up) (2013)** : A big radio hit with a notably long title. "2013's impressively titled my songs know what you did in the dark light them up."
**Centuries (2014)** : A top 10 hit built on an interpolation of Suzanne Vega's "Tom's Diner." It remains Fall Out Boy's most popular song on Spotify. "it remains fallout boys most popular song on spotify."

# genre - Emo

## curiosities
**No Band Admits to Being Emo** : Andy Greenwald wrote in the introduction to *Nothing Feels Good: Punk Rock, Teenagers, and Emo* that no major band has ever admitted to being emo. "there is not now, nor has there ever been a single major band that admits to being Emo, not one."
**A Genre Defined by Fans** : Greenwald argued emo isn't really a genre. What links the 80s–90s underground bands and the 2000s platinum bands is the fans. "Emo is in the eye and ear of the beholder."
**Lyrics Before Sound** : Early emo was about lyrics more than music. By the early 90s it was taking shape as a sound, codified by Jawbreaker and Sunny Day Real Estate. "Early on, Emo was more about the lyrics than the music."
**Emo's Imperial Year - 2006** : The modern rock chart became a playground for emo bands, and Panic's VMA win showed emo defining trends musically, culturally, and in fashion. "by 2006 the modern rock chart had become a playground for emo bands."
**Emo Fashion Takeover** : In 2006 Hot Topic sales hit a new high. Men swapped the Strokes look (shaggy bedhead, ragged jeans) for skinny jeans (sometimes girls' jeans), angular floppy fringes, guyliner, and nail polish. "skinny jeans sometimes girls jeans on boys angular hair with floppy fringes guy liner and nail polish."
**Pop Punk and Emo Fully Blurred** : By 2007 emo was the established term for poppy punk, and the two genres had completely blurred. "the lines between pop punk and emo had completely blurred."
**End of the Imperial Phase - 2008** : Emo's imperial phase closed around 2008, and the energy gradually shifted away by 2009. "emo's imperial phase began to draw to a close by 2008."
**Gradual Fizzle, Not Backlash** : Emo never had a backlash so much as a gradual fizzle, so its bands never really went away. "which was fortunate because it meant the bands emo made famous never really went away."
**Gen Z Renaissance** : Gen Z listeners who were children in the 2000s kept their fondness for pop punk and emo, which have seen a chart renaissance. "those genres have experienced a renaissance on the charts just in the last couple of years."

**Rock Radio Dominance 2000–2002** : Pop punk generated massive rock radio hits like "Minority" and "Fat Lip," which were only minor Hot 100 hits. "From 2000 through 2002, pop punk was still running the table."
