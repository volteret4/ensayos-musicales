## albums
**Girl You Know It's True (1989) - US Debut and Chart-Topping Album** : The American album that produced all five of Milli Vanilli's US hits. Driven by "Blame It on the Rain," it reached a stunning 6 million US sales by January 1990, and it spent seven weeks at No. 1 on the album chart between 1989 and 1990. By the night of the Grammys on February 21, 1990, it had outsold the combined albums of all four other Best New Artist nominees, about 6 million against roughly 4 million. It was not nominated in any Grammy category, including the pop, R&B and dance genre categories. Only the American edition's liner notes credited Rob Pilatus and Fabrice Morvan as vocalists, and that made Arista liable in the later class action. "Millie Vanilley's album was packed with hits that had kept radio and retail afloat for more than a year."
**All or Nothing (1988) - Original European Version of the Album** : The original European Milli Vanilli album. Its title track also made the American album. Unlike the American edition, its liner notes did not list Rob and Fab as vocalists. Several European tracks that didn't make the US LP later appeared on the 1990 remix album. "only the liner notes of the American edition of the album, not the European All or Nothing, listed Rob Polatus and Fabrice Morvon as vocalists, which made Arista liable."
**Milli Vanilli: The Remix Album (1990) - Farian's Stall-for-Time Release** : Frank Farian released this US remix album over the summer of 1990 to stall for time. It contained marginally different versions of all five American hits, plus tracks from the original European album that hadn't made the American LP, such as "Boy in the Tree." Critic Jimmy Guterman mocked it in his book *The Worst Rock and Roll Records of All Time*. "imagine if you can, the existence of songs not good enough to make it onto a Millie Vanilley record."

## songs
**Girl You Know It's True (1989) - First Platinum Single** : The title hit of the US album. It was the first of the group's two platinum singles, with "Blame It on the Rain" the second. "their second to go platinum after Girl You Know It's True."
**Girl I'm Gonna Miss You (1989) - September 1989 No. 1** : It was at No. 1 on the Hot 100 in September 1989, the same week Cher's Diane Warren–penned "If I Could Turn Back Time" sat at No. 3. "the same week Millie Vanillee were at number one with Girl I'm Gonna Miss You."
**Blame It on the Rain (1989) - Diane Warren–Penned Third Straight No. 1** : Diane Warren originally wrote the song for the family pop group The Jets, who declined to record it. She gave it to Arista president Clive Davis, who passed it on to Frank Farian. The main verse vocal appears to be by Brad Howell, joined on the chorus by John Davis, Farian's other ghost vocalist. In late 1989 it followed Bad English's Warren-penned "When I See You Smile" at No. 1 and held the top spot for two weeks itself, giving Warren four straight weeks as writer of America's top song. It was the group's third consecutive No. 1 and second platinum single, and it pushed the album to 6 million US sales by January 1990. It is described as the most enduring Milli Vanilli hit, still heard on radio decades later. Its link to one of the industry's most celebrated songwriters gave the group industry legitimacy even after the Bristol whispers, and it was their hit right around Grammy voting time. Warren later told Rolling Stone, "whoever sang that song, I love it." "Blame It On The Rain is arguably the most enduring Millie Vanilley hit of all."
**All or Nothing (1990) - Final Single and Copyright Dispute** : This was the title track of the original European album and also appeared on the American album. It peaked at No. 4 on the Hot 100 the same week the group won the Grammy. Later in 1990, David Clayton-Thomas of Blood, Sweat & Tears realized its chorus melody was identical, note for note, to BST's 1969 No. 2 hit "Spinning Wheel." He sued Frank Farian, and the matter was settled with Clayton-Thomas belatedly paid for the infringement. It became the last single from the album and the final Milli Vanilli single ever. "the chorus melody of both songs was identical. Note for note."
**Baby Don't Forget My Number (1989) - Hit Translated for the Stage** : One of the five US hits. It was later featured in the jukebox musical *Daddy Cool* and translated into several languages, including German and Dutch, as the show toured Europe. "Millie Vanilley hits like Baby Don't Forget My Number were translated into several languages including German and Dutch."
**Boy in the Tree (1990) - European Album Leftover** : A track from the original European album that didn't make the US LP and was later included on the 1990 remix album. "Songs like Boy in the Tree."

## curiosities
**Bristol, Connecticut Lip-Sync Glitch - July 21, 1989** : At a Club MTV tour date in Bristol, Connecticut, in front of thousands of screaming fans, the backing track supplying Rob and Fab's vocals got stuck in a loop. The footage is the most damning evidence in the Milli Vanilli story, and it was captured in an era before camera phones or even light camcorders. Rob Pilatus reportedly ran offstage mortified, convinced the game was up. Club MTV host Downtown Julie Brown coaxed him back. She recalled that the audience didn't care and that people laughed at them more behind the scenes. The incident was not their Waterloo, and the revelation and fallout came more than a year later. "This piece of footage is the most damning evidence in the Millie Vanilley story."
**Ghost Vocalists Behind the Duo** : The real voices were Brad Howell and John Davis, Frank Farian's ghost vocalists, with Charles Shaw as the studio rapper. Unlike Technotronic or Black Box, whose models were used mostly for videos, Rob and Fab toured, made TV appearances and gave interviews, and they sold far more records. "Rob and Fab were more than faces for Millie Vanillee. They toured, made TV appearances, and gave interviews."
**Time Magazine Boast - "I'm the New Elvis"** : In an infamous Time magazine article, Rob Pilatus told interviewer Jay Cocks: "musically we're more talented than any Bob Dylan or Paul McCartney. Mick Jagger, his lines are not clear. He don't know how he should produce a sound. I'm the new modern rock and roll. I'm the new Elvis." "the fame was going to their heads."
**American Music Award - Favorite Pop/Rock New Artist 1990** : At the 1990 AMAs, where prizes were then determined by an industry cabal, Milli Vanilli won Favorite Pop/Rock New Artist. They beat hard rock band Living Colour and the George Harrison–fronted supergroup the Traveling Wilburys. "Millie Vanilley took home the favorite pop rock new artist award, defeating Hard Rock Band Living Color, and the George Harrison fronted Supergroup the traveling wheelbarrows."
**Grammy Best New Artist Win - February 21, 1990** : This was the only Grammy category they were nominated in. They beat the Indigo Girls, Tone Lōc, Soul II Soul and Neneh Cherry. By Grammy night they had outsold those four acts combined (about 6 million albums versus roughly 4 million). They had also scored three No. 1s and a No. 2 on the Hot 100, while the other four acts had no No. 1 hits among them. "Millie Vanilley had moved more albums by February 21st, 1990, the night of the Grammys, than the other four acts combined."
**Five Singles, Five Top Fives** : Milli Vanilli issued only five US singles in their career, and all five reached the Top 5, an exceptional hit ratio. By comparison, Ugly Kid Joe had just two charting singles, both Top 10, and Jesus Jones had two, both Top 5. The streak was likened to actor John Cazale, who died of cancer at 42 in 1978 and acted in only five feature films, all Best Picture Oscar nominees. "For an act to issue only five songs ever in a career and have all five reached the top five is some kind of record."
**Internal Revolt and Charles Shaw's Retracted Confession** : Between 1989 and 1990, Rob, Fab and studio rapper Charles Shaw all threatened at various times to expose the scheme. Shaw gave an interview claiming he was the real vocalist, then rescinded it under pressure from Farian. Rob and Fab told Farian they couldn't keep up the ruse and wanted to sing on the next album. "Rob and Fab were finally pushing back."
**Farian's Unmasking - November 1990** : In November 1990, Frank Farian decided to unmask the group before anyone else could. He told the press: "the record company never knew. I never told them anything. Later on, after the record was out, there were some people who raised some questions." "he decided he would unmask Millie Vanilley before anyone else could do so."
**Grammy Revoked and Vacated** : Within days, Recording Academy president Michael Greene declared the Best New Artist Grammy would be taken back. He said the Awards and Nominations Subcommittee might give it to the runner-up or to no one. The committee vacated the 1990 prize entirely, so the runner-up remains unknown. "They just may say best new artist, no Grammy given."
**Los Angeles Press Conference - November 20, 1990** : Less than a week after Farian's revelation, Rob and Fab held a press conference in Los Angeles, accompanied by a lawyer, amid a feeding frenzy of reporters and photographers. Even MTV's Kurt Loder was taken aback. They claimed they ended it themselves: they had told Farian to go to the press because they couldn't break their contract. They also credited Brad Howell and John Davis as the real singers. "Rob and Fab ended Millie Vanilley not Frank Farron."
**Class Action Refunds** : Arista Records settled a class action lawsuit from buyers. An estimated 10 million US buyers of Milli Vanilli albums and singles were eligible to claim a refund of a few dollars by 1992, but reportedly few took advantage. "An estimated 10 million buyers of Millie Vanilley albums and singles in the United States were eligible to claim a refund of a few dollars."
**Boom to Bust in Two Years** : The group's rise and fall took barely two years. Their success came from combining Farian's ear for pop hooks, Rob and Fab's stage presence and the studio musicians' chops. "The whole was far greater than the sum of its parts."
**Lasting Influence on Early-90s Pop and Rap** : Their rap-informed dance pop style shaped early-90s charts, including C+C Music Factory, Snap! and Real McCoy. They made the "Ashley's Roachclip" beat safe for pop crossover. They arguably opened Top 40 radio to more melodic rap, such as Naughty by Nature's "O.P.P." Almost everything Farian conceived for them later became commonplace: phony front persons, lip-sync scandals and club rap. "the Millie Vanilley approach, that rap-informed dance pop style, that proved far more enduring."

# artist - Frank Farian

## member of
Boney M

## curiosities
**Non-Singing Frontmen Since the 70s** : As far back as the 70s, Farian experimented with non-singing frontmen in his Europop group Boney M. That disco act scored no big US hits, but his 80s project Milli Vanilli scored a string of massive US hits. "Group Creator Frank Ferrien was already experimenting with non-singing frontman as in his Europop group, Boni M."
**Self-Unmasking - November 1990** : Fed up with the duo's threats, Farian revealed the deception himself and claimed the record company never knew. "the record company never knew. I never told them anything."
**Spinning Wheel Lawsuit** : David Clayton-Thomas sued Farian because "All or Nothing" copied the chorus of "Spinning Wheel," and the case was settled with payment. "Clayton Thomas would sue Frank Ferry over the copyright violation."
**Industry Comeback - La Bouche, Le Click, No Mercy** : The industry seemed to admire Farian's hustle rather than shame him. Within a few years he was back in the Top 40 with hits produced for La Bouche and their sister project Le Click. In 1996, anticipating pop radio's shift from hip-hop back to pure pop, he assembled No Mercy, a trio of Latin boy-band-style American singers. "the music industry seemed to admire his hustle."
**Daddy Cool The Musical (2006)** : Like Jim Steinman before him, Farian saw his hits reach the stage. The jukebox musical *Daddy Cool* debuted in London's West End in 2006, featuring mostly Boney M songs plus several Milli Vanilli hits, and toured Europe for years. "A jukebox musical called Daddy Cool The Musical made its debut in London's West End in 2006."
**Institutionalizing Club Rap** : Farian is credited with institutionalizing the club rapper subgenre: acts that rap but aim for the dance floor rather than the street. "Farion even institutionalized the subgenre of hip-hop now commonly called the club rapper."

# concert - Grammy Awards

## curiosities
**Best New Artist - The Oddball Category** : One of the Big Four Grammys alongside Album, Record and Song of the Year, it is the only Grammy given to an artist rather than a specific work. It works like a most-promising-newcomer prize. "it's the only Grammy presented not for a specific work, a song, an album, but rather to the artist themselves."
**History of Questionable Best New Artist Winners** : In 1963, Robert Goulet beat the Four Seasons and Peter, Paul and Mary. In 1981, Christopher Cross beat The Pretenders. In 1992, Marc Cohn, known for his only Top 40 hit "Walking in Memphis," beat Boyz II Men and Seal. In 2014, Macklemore & Ryan Lewis beat Kendrick Lamar, Kacey Musgraves, James Blake and Ed Sheeran. "the prize tends to go to the schlockiest, most middle of the road act in the category."
**Great Winners, Uncertain Careers** : Carly Simon (1972) had a roughly 10-year run as a reliable hitmaker, Cyndi Lauper's (1985) platinum phase lasted under half a decade, and Lauryn Hill (1999) never made a second studio album. "even in these cases, the award is no guarantee of career trajectory."
**1990 Best New Artist Vacated** : After Milli Vanilli's fraud was revealed, Recording Academy president Michael Greene took back the award. The committee chose to give it to no one rather than to Neneh Cherry, the Indigo Girls, Tone Lōc or Soul II Soul. "We'll never know who that runner up was."

**Hidden Vocalists Commonplace in 1989–90 Dance Music** : Hit dance records with selectively hidden vocalists, especially from European acts, were becoming commonplace in 1989–90. This helps explain why the Bristol incident didn't sink Milli Vanilli right away. "hit dance records with selectively hidden vocalists were becoming commonplace, particularly among acts that originated in Europe."
**The Ashley's Roachclip Beat Goes Pop** : Milli Vanilli made the "Ashley's Roachclip" drum beat safe for pop crossover, and P.M. Dawn later used it on a No. 1 hit. "they made the Ashley's Roach clip beat, safe for pop cross-over."
**Daddy Cool The Musical - Multilingual European Tour** : The 2006 West End jukebox musical of Farian's Boney M and Milli Vanilli hits toured Europe for years, with songs translated into German and Dutch. "The show wound up touring Europe for years."
**John Cazale Comparison** : Milli Vanilli's five-for-five Top 5 singles record was compared to actor John Cazale, who died of cancer at 42 in 1978. He acted in only five feature films, and all five were Best Picture Oscar nominees. "had only ever acted in five feature films total and all five were best picture Oscar nominees."
