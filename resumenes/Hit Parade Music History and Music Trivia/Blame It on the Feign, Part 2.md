## albums
**Girl You Know It's True (1989)** : This US album was pushed to a stunning 6 million in US sales by January of 1990 and spent seven weeks at number one on the album chart between 1989 and 1990. Its liner notes, specifically those of the American edition, listed Rob Pilatus and Fabrice Morvan as vocalists, which made Arista Records liable in a class action lawsuit. "Millie Vanilley's album was packed with hits that had kept radio and retail afloat for more than a year."
**The Remix Album (1990)** : Released over the summer of 1990, this US remix album contained marginally different versions of all five of Millie Vanilley's American hits, plus tracks from the original European Millie Vanilley album that had not made the cut for the American LP, such as "Boy in the Tree." Critic Jimmy Gooterman later questioned the quality of tracks not good enough for a Millie Vanilley record. "It contained marginally different versions of all five of their American hits. Plus tracks from the original European Millie Vanilley album that hadn't made the cut for the American LP."

## songs
**Girl I'm Gonna Miss You (1989)** : This song was a number one hit on the Hot 100 in September 1989, making it one of two Diane Warren compositions charting highly that week, alongside Cher's "If I Could Turn Back Time" at number three. "Millie Vanilley were at number one with Girl I'm Gonna Miss You."
**Blame It On The Rain (1989)** : Originally intended for the family pop group The Jets by songwriter Diane Warren, the song was given to Frank Farian by Arista Records President Clive Davis after The Jets declined it. It became Millie Vanilley's third consecutive number one hit and their second to go platinum, spending two weeks at the top of the Hot 100 in November 1989. The main verse vocal was performed by Brad Howe, joined by John Davis on the chorus. This song is arguably the most enduring Millie Vanilley hit and gave them a stamp of industry legitimacy. "It spent its own fortnight at number one."
**Baby Don't Forget My Number** : This hit was one of several Millie Vanilley songs featured in the 2006 jukebox musical "Daddy Cool The Musical." The song's lyrics were translated into multiple languages, including German and Dutch, for the musical's European tour. "Millie Vanilley hits like Baby Don't Forget My Number were translated into several languages including German and Dutch."
**All or Nothing (1990)** : This track, originally the title track from the European version of the album and also included on the American LP, reached number four on the Hot 100 the same week Millie Vanilley won their Grammy. It was the last single issued from "Girl You Know It's True" and the final Millie Vanilley single overall. The chorus melody was found to be identical to Blood, Sweat and Tears' 1969 hit "Spinning Wheel," leading to a copyright lawsuit by David Clayton Thomas, which was settled. "It reached number four on the Hot 100."
**Girl You Know It's True (1989)** : This song was one of the group's early hits and went platinum, preceding "Blame It On The Rain" in achieving this sales milestone. "their second to go platinum after Girl You Know It's True."

## curiosities
**Founding and Deception** : Millie Vanilley was Frank Farian's 1980s project, building on his earlier experiments with non-singing frontmen in his 1970s Europop group, Boni M. Rob Pilatus and Fabrice Morvan were hired as the non-singing frontmen, initially scoring a string of massive US hits while keeping the vocal deception a secret. "Ferrien's 80s project, fronted by non-singing frontman Rob Palatus and Fabrice Morvon, was scoring a string of massive US hits."
**Bristol Connecticut Incident (July 21, 1989)** : At a Club MTV tour date in Bristol, Connecticut, in front of thousands of fans, the backing track supplying Rob Pilatus and Fabrice Morvan's vocals got stuck in a loop. Rob Pilatus, mortified, ran offstage but was coaxed back by host Downtown Julie Brown. Although the incident was captured on tape and became "damning evidence," it was not their immediate downfall, as the public revelation of their deception wouldn't occur for over a year. "On July 21st, 1989, at a date on the club MTV tour in Bristol, in front of thousands of screaming fans, the backing track, supplying Rob Palatus and Fabrice Morvon's vocals got stuck in a loop."
**Fame Going to Their Heads** : The growing celebrity of Rob Pilatus and Fabrice Morvan led to hubristic statements. In an infamous Time magazine article, Rob Pilatus claimed musical superiority over legends like Bob Dylan, Paul McCartney, and Mick Jagger, declaring himself "the new modern rock and roll" and "the new Elvis." "In one infamous time magazine article, a heubristic Rob Palatus told interviewer Jay Cox, quote, musically we're more talented than any Bob Dylan or Paul McCartney."
**American Music Awards Win (1990)** : Millie Vanilley won the Favorite Pop/Rock New Artist award at the American Music Awards, a prize determined by an industry cabal at the time. They defeated Hard Rock band Living Color and the George Harrison-fronted supergroup The Traveling Wilburys. "Millie Vanilley took home the favorite pop rock new artist award, defeating Hard Rock Band Living Color, and the George Harrison fronted Supergroup the traveling wheelbarrows."
**Grammy Award Win (1990)** : The group famously won the Best New Artist Grammy, an award that ultimately brought about their downfall. This was their only Grammy nomination that night, as none of their hit songs or their smash album were nominated in any genre categories. Their win was largely attributed to their significant sales momentum (6 million albums compared to 4 million combined for other nominees) and multiple number one hits. "The fact that Millie Vanilley were in the category at all probably had something to do with the hit they had right around voting time. The Diane Warren Penn blame it on the ring."
**Pushback from Rob and Fab (1989-1990)** : Behind the scenes, Rob Pilatus and Fabrice Morvan began pushing back against Frank Farian, stating they could not maintain the charade and desired to sing on the next Millie Vanilley album. Along with studio rapper Charles Shaw, they periodically threatened to expose the deception, though Shaw later recanted his claims under Farian's pressure. "Rob and Fab were finally pushing back. They were telling Fary and they couldn't keep up the ruse. And more important, that they wanted to actually sing on the next Millie Vanilley album."
**Frank Farian's Revelation (November 1990)** : Having had enough of the internal struggles, Frank Farian decided to unmask Millie Vanilley himself before anyone else could. He issued a press statement revealing the deception, claiming the record company was unaware and he had never informed them. "Finally, in November of 1990, Fary and himself had had enough. And he decided he would unmask Millie Vanilley before anyone else could do so."
**Grammy Revocation (November 1990)** : Following Frank Farian's revelation, Michael Greene, president of the Recording Academy, announced that the Best New Artist Grammy would be taken back. The Grammy committee ultimately decided to vacate the prize entirely for 1990, not awarding it to any of the other nominees: Neneh Cherry, Indigo Girls, Tone Loc, or Soul II Soul. "Within days, Michael Greene, the president of the Recording Academy, declared the best new artist Grammy would be taken back."
**Press Conference and Grammy Return (November 20, 1990)** : Rob Pilatus and Fabrice Morvan, accompanied by a lawyer, held a highly publicized press conference in Los Angeles to confront reporters. They asserted that they, not Frank Farian, ended Millie Vanilley because they wanted to sing on their records and were unable to break their contract. They named Brad Howe and John Davis as the real singers and publicly returned the Grammy. "Rob Polatus and Fabrice Morvon accompanied by a lawyer held a press conference that was a feeding frenzy of reporters and photographers."
**Class Action Lawsuit** : Arista Records settled a class action lawsuit filed by buyers of the Millie Vanilley album in the United States. This liability stemmed from the fact that only the American edition's liner notes, unlike the European "All or Nothing" album, explicitly listed Rob Pilatus and Fabrice Morvan as vocalists. Approximately 10 million US buyers were eligible for a refund, though few took advantage of the offer. "Arista Records wound up settling a class action lawsuit among buyers of the Millie Vanilley album."
**Career Comparison to John Cazale** : Millie Vanilley had an abnormally short career, lasting barely two years from boom to bust, but an unusually consistent chart streak with all five of their singles reaching the US Top 5. This unusual ratio is compared to actor John Cazale, who only appeared in five feature films, all of which were Best Picture Oscar nominees. "Millie Vanilley had a similarly abnormally short career and an unusually consistent chart streak."
**Post-Scandal Influence** : The rap-informed dance pop style pioneered by Millie Vanilley proved enduring, influencing chart-topping music in the early 90s, particularly hip-hop permutations. They notably made the "Ashley's Roachclip" beat safe for pop crossover and arguably opened Top 40 radio playlists to more melodic rap, like Naughty by Nature's "O.P.P." "But the Millie Vanilley approach, that rap-informed dance pop style, that proved far more enduring."
**Jukebox Musical - Daddy Cool The Musical (2006)** : Frank Farian's array of pop hits, including several made famous by Millie Vanilley and Boney M, were adapted into "Daddy Cool The Musical," which debuted in London's West End in 2006 and subsequently toured Europe for years. "A jukebox musical called Daddy Cool The Musical made its debut in London's West End in 2006, featuring mostly songs from Farion's Boney M years, but also several hits made famous by Millie Vanilley."

# artist - Frank Farian

## curiosities
**Early Experimentation with Non-Singing Frontmen** : Frank Farian, a group creator, was already experimenting with non-singing frontmen in his 1970s Europop group, Boni M, before his 1980s project, Millie Vanilley, became a massive US success. "Group Creator Frank Ferrien was already experimenting with non-singing frontman as in his Europop group, Boni M."
**Role in "Blame It On The Rain"** : Frank Farian received the song "Blame It On The Rain" from Arista Records President Clive Davis, who in turn had received it from songwriter Diane Warren, eventually bestowing it upon Millie Vanilley. "Remember that Diane Warren track I mentioned earlier, the one she gave to Arista Records President Clive Davis, who then bequeathed it to Frank Farion?"
**Lawsuit for "All or Nothing" (1990)** : Frank Farian was sued by David Clayton Thomas, lead singer of Blood, Sweat and Tears, for copyright violation, as Millie Vanilley's "All or Nothing" shared an identical chorus melody with BST's 1969 hit "Spinning Wheel." The matter was settled, and Clayton Thomas was belatedly paid for the infringement. "So Clayton Thomas would sue Frank Ferry over the copyright violation. The matter was settled with Clayton Thomas belatedly paid for the infringement."
**Unmasking Millie Vanilley (November 1990)** : After internal pressure from Rob Pilatus and Fabrice Morvan, Frank Farian decided to unmask Millie Vanilley himself in November 1990, preempting anyone else from doing so. He issued a statement to the press, claiming the record company was unaware of the deception. "Finally, in November of 1990, Fary and himself had had enough. And he decided he would unmask Millie Vanilley before anyone else could do so."
**Post-Scandal Career Resilience** : Rather than being shamed out of the music business after the Millie Vanilley scandal, Frank Farian's "hustle" seemed to be admired. Within a few years, he was back in the Top 40, producing hits for LaBouche and their sister project LeClick, and later assembling No Mercy in 1996 for a Top 10 hit. "Rather than shaming him out of the business, the music industry seemed to admire his hustle. Within a few years, he was back in the top 40, with hits he'd produced for LaBouche, and their sister project LeClick."
**Institutionalized Pop Concepts** : Virtually everything Frank Farian conceived for Millie Vanilley became more commonplace on the charts in subsequent decades, including pop acts with deliberately phony front persons (like Gorillaz), acts caught lip-syncing in live settings (like Ashlee Simpson), and the institutionalization of the "club rapper" subgenre (like Flo Rida or The Black Eyed Peas). "Virtually everything Frank Farion conceived for Millie Vanilley became more commonplace on the charts in the decades to come."
**Guilt and Attempted Comeback (1997)** : Around 1997, Frank Farian, feeling "guilt-ridden" over Rob Pilatus's condition, bailed Pilatus out of jail after an attempted robbery. He then began working with Rob and Fab again on a potential Millie Vanilley comeback, though this effort was short-lived. "Around 1997, Frank Farion, guilt-ridden over Rob's condition, bailed Pellatus out of jail after an attempted robbery."
**Jukebox Musical - Daddy Cool The Musical (2006)** : Frank Farian's extensive array of pop hits eventually made it to the stage in a jukebox musical titled "Daddy Cool The Musical," which debuted in London's West End in 2006. The musical primarily featured songs from his Boney M years, but also included several hits made famous by Millie Vanilley. "A jukebox musical called Daddy Cool The Musical made its debut in London's West End in 2006, featuring mostly songs from Farion's Boney M years, but also several hits made famous by Millie Vanilley."

## curiosities
**Mystery Title** : The very title of the Italian House Music group, Black Box, suggested a kind of mystery regarding the identities of the actual performers. "Italian House Music Group Black Box, an act whose very title suggested a kind of mystery about who exactly was performing."
**Vocalist Deception** : Black Box engaged in a layered deception regarding its vocalists for "Ride On Time," starting with a sample of Loleatta Holloway, then having Heather Small re-record the part, and finally employing model Catrin Cranall to lip-sync in the video, without either of the actual singers performing for the cameras. This pattern continued with "Everybody, Everybody," where Martha Wash sang but Quenall was the group's public face. "So a vocal that had started as Loliada Holloway's and had been re-recorded by Heather Small was performed without either of them for the cameras."
**Martha Wash Lawsuit** : Famed vocalist Martha Wash, who sang on "Everybody, Everybody" and two subsequent Top 40 hits for Black Box, ended up suing the producers and the label due to her uncredited contributions. She won a large settlement, even as the group continued to score hits with her "invisible vocals." "Wasch would end up suing the producers and the label and won a large settlement, even as Black Box scored two more top 40 hits with her invisible vocals on the tracks."

# artist - Diane Warren

## curiosities
**Prolific Songwriter** : Diane Warren is described as a "journeywoman songwriter" renowned for her uncanny ability to transform "hallmark sentiments into songs," consistently authoring numerous hit records throughout her career. "But the journey woman songwriter, the author of So Many Hits with the uncanny ability to turn hallmark sentiments into songs, would not be deterred."
**Highly Successful Year (1989)** : The year 1989 ranked as one of Warren's most successful, with her late 1988 hit "Look Away" with Chicago becoming Billboard's number one song of 1989. In September 1989, she had two songs in the Top 3 of the Hot 100: Millie Vanilley's "Girl I'm Gonna Miss You" at number one and Cher's "If I Could Turn Back Time" at number three. "Indeed 1989 ranks as one of Warren's most successful years."
**Biggest Chart Coup (November 1989)** : Diane Warren achieved her biggest chart coup in November 1989 by having two consecutive number one hits on the Hot 100. First, Bad English's power ballad "When I See You Smile" held the top spot for two weeks, followed immediately by Millie Vanilley's "Blame It On The Rain" for another two weeks, totaling four straight weeks with a Warren-penned song at number one. "Two months later, Diane Warren pulled off her biggest chart coup, two consecutive number ones on the Hot 100."
**Quote on "Blame It On The Rain"** : Two decades after its release, when interviewed by Rolling Stone about her songwriting career, Diane Warren expressed a simple but heartfelt sentiment regarding "Blame It On The Rain," saying, "whoever sang that song, I love it." "Two decades later, in an interview about her songwriting career with Rolling Stone, Diane Warren said simply, quote, whoever sang that song, I love it."

# curiosity

**Best New Artist Grammy Category** : This Grammy category is considered one of the "big four" prizes, alongside Album, Record, and Song of the Year, but it's noted as the "weirdest, most oddball category" because it's the only one awarded to the artist themselves rather than a specific work. The Recording Academy uses it as a "most promising newcomer" prize, attempting to predict a long career, which often results in selections that later appear foolish, such as 70s One Hit Wonders the Starland Vocal Band or Robert Goulet over Peter, Paul and Mary. "To this day, best new artist is considered one of the big four prizes at the Grammys, alongside album, record, and song of the year."
**1989 Pop Chart Domination by Diane Warren** : The year 1989 was a peak for songwriter Diane Warren, whose songs occupied top positions on the Billboard charts. Her composition "Look Away" for Chicago was Billboard's #1 song of the year, and in November 1989, she achieved a chart coup with four straight weeks where her songs were America's #1 hit, with Bad English's "When I See You Smile" followed by Millie Vanilley's "Blame It On The Rain." "Indeed 1989 ranks as one of Warren's most successful years."
**Exceptional Hitmaking Ratio of Millie Vanilley** : Millie Vanilley achieved an "exceptional hitmaking ratio" by issuing only five singles in their entire career, all of which reached the US Top 5, an unusually consistent chart streak compared to most acts. "For an act to issue only five songs ever in a career and have all five reached the top five is some kind of record."
**Pop Acts with Phony Front Persons** : The concept of pop acts featuring deliberately phony front persons, where models or animated characters represent the musical talent, became more commonplace on the charts in decades following Frank Farian's work with Millie Vanilley, exemplified by acts like Gorillaz in the early 2000s. "Pop acts with deliberately phony front persons."
**Acts Caught Lip-Syncing in Live Settings** : The phenomenon of musical acts using backing tracks and being caught lip-syncing in live performances, such as Ashlee Simpson's incident on Saturday Night Live in 2004, became a more common occurrence on the charts, a trend that Frank Farian's approach arguably institutionalized. "Or acts that use backing tracks and get caught lip-syncing in a live setting, ask Ashley Simpson how that worked out for her on Saturday Night Live in 2004."
**Club Rapper Subgenre** : Frank Farian is credited with institutionalizing the subgenre of hip-hop now commonly known as the "club rapper," which features acts whose primary aim is the dance floor rather than the street. 21st-century examples include artists like Flo Rida, The Black Eyed Peas, and LMFAO. "Farion even institutionalized the subgenre of hip-hop now commonly called the club rapper."
**"Ashley's Roachclip" Beat in Pop Crossover** : Millie Vanilley played a role in making the "Ashley's Roachclip" beat, a prominent drum break, safe for pop crossover. This paved the way for groups like PM Dawn, who built their 1991 number one smash "Set Adrift on Memory Bliss" partly using that irresistible beat. "For one thing, they made the Ashley's Roach clip beat, safe for pop cross-over."
