## albums
**She's So Unusual (1983)** : This quadruple platinum album formed the basis of Lopper's early success, launching a string of hits in the mid-1980s. Its sales were later less than half of her *True Colors* album. "Lopper had scored a string of hits off her quadruple platinum album."
**True Colors (1986)** : Lopper's second album, which cracked the top 10 on its way to number four, matching the peak of *She's So Unusual*. It featured a hodgepodge of material including covers of songs by Marvin Gaye and the New Orleans standard "Iko Iko," as well as a remake of a Blue Angel song. The album eventually went double platinum. "The True Colors album cracked the top 10 on its way to number four, matching the peak of she's so unusual."
**A Night to Remember (1989)** : This album peaked at number 37 and failed to go gold, a significant drop from her previous platinum successes. It contained the hit single "I Drove All Night." "A Night to Remember, which peaked at number 37 and failed to go gold."
**Hat Full of Stars (1993)** : This album found Lopper searching for a sound suitable for the 90s. It was largely met with indifference from both the public and Epic Records, resulting in a dismal peak at number 112. "But it found her still adrift, casting about for a sound for the 90s."
**A Decade of Remixes (1994)** : This greatest hits album achieved gold sales, significantly boosted by the rebooted single "Hey Now (Girls Just Want to Have Fun)." "It propelled a 1994 greatest hits album to gold sales."
**Sisters of Avalon (1996)** : This album saw Lopper attempting alternative rock, particularly on its title track, though this direction did not yield significant commercial success compared to her achievements on the dance charts. "Though Lauper did try her hand at Alternative Rock, most especially the title track of her 1996 album, Sisters of Avalon."

## songs
**Girls Just Want to Have Fun (1983)** : The music video for this song featured pro wrestler Captain Louis Elbano playing Lopper's father, showcasing her early co-promotion of the World Wrestling Federation. "In her girls just want to have fun video, her father was played by pro wrestler Captain Louis Elbano."
**Money Changes Everything (1985)** : This single was a brief exception to Lopper's string of top 10 hits, peaking at number 27 in the winter of 1985. "With the brief exception of Money Changes Everything, which was only a number 27 hit in the winter of 1985."
**The Goonies 'R' Good Enough (1985)** : The music video for this song included appearances by several wrestlers, and its MTV premiere devolved into playful WWF-style trash talk with Rowdy Roddy Piper, further highlighting Lopper's association with wrestling. "Several more wrestlers made appearances in her Goonies are good enough video."
**Maybe He'll Know (1986)** : Originally a modest retro-pop song from her Blue Angel days, Lopper remade this track for her *True Colors* album, featuring Billy Joel singing backup as a "payback" for her earlier collaboration on his album. "Lopper remade this song with Billy Joel singing back up."
**Code of Silence (1986)** : Lopper contributed vocals to this deep cut on Billy Joel's album *The Bridge* in early 1986, leading to Joel returning the favor by singing on her own album later that year. "Lopper to do it with him on Code of Silence, a deep cut on his album The Bridge."
**True Colors (1986)** : Written by pop songsmiths Billy Steinberg and Tom Kelly, Lopper's arrangement of this song, featuring a "childlike vocal" that swelled briefly to her full voice, redefined it. She pictured a friend who died of AIDS while singing it, and it emerged as a perennial anthem for the LGBTQ community, becoming her first number one hit since "Time After Time" in 1984 after nine weeks on the Hot 100. "Though she had no hand in writing it, Cindy's arrangement of True Colors defined the song and made it a standard."
**Change of Heart (1986)** : The follow-up single to "True Colors," this track reached number three on the charts and featured The Bangles providing backup vocals. "The Bangles were back in the top three as backup singers. Supporting their pal Cindy Loppers on her true colors follow up the number three hit Change of Heart."
**What's Going On (1987)** : Lopper's synth-pop cover of Marvin Gaye's legendary song, which peaked at number 11. This marked her first single in two years to miss the top 10, signaling a potential shift in her chart performance. "Cindy's synth pop cover of Marvin Gaye's legendary song What's Going On also reached number 11."
**Boy Blue (1987)** : This peppy torch song missed the top 40 entirely, peaking at a lowly number 71, indicating a significant decline in chart performance for Lopper. "Her next single, the peppy torch song Boy Blue, didn't miss the top 40 entirely, peaking at a lowly number 71."
**I Drove All Night (1989)** : Written by Billy Steinberg and Tom Kelly, this vintage torch ballad featured Lopper's powerful voice delivering "big glory notes" in its pre-chorus and chorus. It brought her back into the top 10, peaking at number 6 by the summer of 1989, but its impact on her album sales was questionable, and it currently generates fewer Spotify streams than versions by Roy Orbison and Celine Dion. "Cindy gave the song her all, reminding pop audiences of the power of her voice."
**Who Let in the Rain (1993)** : The first single from *Hat Full of Stars*, this soulful pop song only managed to scrape the lower reaches of the adult contemporary chart. "The first single who led in the rain was a soulful pop song that scraped the lower reaches of the adult's contemporary chart."
**That's What I Think (1993)** : A deep cut from *Hat Full of Stars*, this track marked Lopper's return to Billboard's Club Play chart for the first time in seven years, where it reached number 14. "A deep cut on Hatfull of Stars called, that's what I think, brought Lauper back to Billboard's Club Play chart for the first time in seven years, where she reached number 14."
**Hey Now (Girls Just Want to Have Fun) (1994)** : A reboot of Lopper's very first hit, built around an interpolation of Redbone's 1974 hit "Come and Get Your Love." This version achieved top five status in England, Japan, and New Zealand, and helped propel a 1994 greatest hits album to gold sales in America. "It went top five in England, Japan, and New Zealand, and in America, it propelled a 1994 greatest hits album to gold sales."

## curiosities
**Grammy Award - Best New Artist (1985)** : Lopper won this major award at the 1985 Grammy Awards, famously accepting it with pro wrestlers Hulk Hogan and Captain Louis Elbano, and Katrina Dominique, highlighting her strong association with the World Wrestling Federation. "When she went up to accept the prize, she took along a muscle bound man named Terry Balea, better known as Hulk Hogan."
**World Wrestling Federation Co-promotion (1980s)** : In her initial flush of stardom, Lopper actively co-promoted the World Wrestling Federation, featuring wrestlers in her music videos and participating in events like the first Wrestlemania in 1985 as manager for Women's Champion Wendy Richter. "In her first flush of stardom, Lopper was co-promoting the world wrestling federation at every turn."
**Imperial Period (1984-1986)** : For approximately two years, Lopper experienced a period of immense public acceptance where she could be her "most flamboyantly outrageous version" of herself, and the public embraced her as her hits continued to flow. "For about two years, Cindy Lopper could be the most flamboyantly outrageous version of herself, and the public ate it up."
**Acting Debut in Vibes (1988)** : Lopper ventured into acting, appearing in the flop 1988 film *Vibes* alongside Jeff Goldblum. Despite her "fairly charming" performance, the movie earned "savage reviews." "Cindy tried acting, appearing in a flop 1988 film with Jeff Goldblum... called Vibes."
**Pee-wee's Playhouse Theme Song (Late 1980s)** : Lopper recorded the theme song for her friend Pee-wee Herman's Saturday morning kids show, "Pee-wee's Playhouse," as a favor. "Lopper did a favor for her friend, Peewee Herman, recording the theme to his Saturday morning kids show Peewee's Playhouse."
**Emmy Award - Mad About You (1995)** : Lopper won a 1995 Emmy Award for Outstanding Guest Actress in a Comedy Series for her series of cameos as the character Marianne Lugasso on the hit NBC sitcom *Mad About You*. "Her series of cameos on the hit NBC sitcom, Matt About You, as the character, Marianne Lugasso, won Lauper a 1995 Emmy Award for Outstanding Guest Actress in a Comedy Series."
**Continued Success on Dance Charts (1990s)** : In her forties and fifties, Lopper found significant success on the dance charts, where she remained popular, especially within the gay community. "She had far greater success in her forties and fifties on the dance charts, where she was still welcome, especially by the gay community."

## albums
**Voices Carry (1985)** : The debut album, which cracked the top 20, peaking at number 18, and went gold. It established their ethereal, moody pop sound and earned a VMA for its captivating music video. "Still, the Voices' carry album did crack the top 20, peaking at number 18 and going gold."
**Welcome Home (1986)** : The band's second album, recorded throughout 1986, continued the ethereal moody pop sound of their debut. Its lead single, "What About Love," returned them to the top 40. "Till Tuesday would spend much of 1986 recording their all-important follow-up album."

Despite critical acclaim for Mann's songwriting growth, Epic Records did not heavily promote it, leading to a dismal peak at number 124 on the album chart and a failure to go gold. "The third and final Till Tuesday album, everything's different now, wound up a turning point for Amy Mann."

It was a minor hit, reaching number 95 on the Hot 100, and made the top 40 on both the Adult Contemporary Chart and Billboard's new Modern Rock Chart. "Believed you were lucky, the single Mann co-wrote with her ex was a minor hit, reaching number 95 on the Hot 100."

## curiosities
**Prog Rock Collaboration with Rush (1987)** : Mann took an unexpected gig singing backup for the Canadian power trio Rush, demonstrating her versatility and willingness to not be "pinned down." "Mann and Bryan formed a friendship and an ongoing collaboration that would shift from Boston to Los Angeles, and that would eventually reboot Amy Mann's career."

**Largo Club Residency (1990s)** : Mann and her husband Michael Penn were regulars at LA Club Largo, often sitting in on stage with John Bryan during his weekly Friday night musical residency, connecting with other rising LA artists. ## albums
**All Over the Place (1984)** : The band's debut album, which only reached number 80 on the album chart. This relatively modest performance meant they "likely had nowhere to go but up" for their next release. "The Bangles 1984 album all over the place had only reached number 80 on the album chart."
**Different Light (1986)** : This album was a determined effort by producer David Kahne and Columbia Records to make it a pop breakthrough. It contained their first Hot 100 hit "Manic Monday" and their biggest hit "Walk Like an Egyptian," which propelled the album to number two on the charts and double platinum sales. "They hoped to capture that energy on their next album, which they would title different light."
**Everything (1988)** : The band's third album, issued in the closing weeks of 1988. It quickly generated the hit "In Your Room" and their final chart-topper "Eternal Flame." However, internal tensions regarding Susanna Hoffs's spotlighting created strain, leading to the band's breakup during its supporting tour. "The Bangles issued their third album Everything in the closing weeks of 1988."

## songs
**Hero Takes a Fall (1984)** : This song gained significant praise from Prince, who, after catching their LA gig at the Palace Theatre, later told Susanna Hoffs that the song was "number one in my car," considered very high praise from him. "He caught their LA gig at the Palace Theatre and later told Hoffs that their song Hero Takes a Fall was, quote, number one in my car."
**Let It Go (1986)** : A track written and sung by all four members for the *Different Light* album, which best exemplified the band's core sound of "Sixties-style California harmonies juxtaposed with 80s garage-style crash and burn." While all vocals were by The Bangles, its 60s-style backwards guitar solo was performed by a session musician, a decision made by producer David Kahne. "For example, on their cover of the big star power pop classic September Girls, the vocals are all the Bangles, but its 60s style backwards guitar solo is a session musician."

Producer David Kahne recorded it in just two takes, contrasting with the numerous takes often required for instrumental tracks on the album. "The bassist Michael Steele later said that her moody composition following one of the album's best songs was recorded by Khan in just two takes."
**Manic Monday (1986)** : Written and demoed by Prince in 1984, this song was given to Susanna Hoffs by Prince via a mutual connection. Issued as the first single from *Different Light* in the winter of 1986, it became their first single to crack the Billboard Hot 100. Despite Prince's request for a pseudonym (Christopher) for the writing credit, the public soon learned of his authorship. It rose to number two in April 1986, prevented from reaching number one by Prince's own song, "Kiss." "Manic Monday was unsurprisingly issued as the first single from the Bangles' different light LP in the winter of 1986."
**If She Knew What She Wants (1986)** : A cover song written by Jules Sheer. Originally penned from a frustrated man's point of view, it was transformed when sung by Susanna Hoffs with The Bangles' harmonies, taking on a new, sad, and poignant meaning about a couple's doomed relationship. It reached number 29 in July 1986, marking Sheer's second-ever top 40 hit as a songwriter. "Sheer's Sheer, who knows what she wants was a little like Girls Just Want to Have Fun, originally written from a man's point of view, then transformed when she sang it."
**Walk Like an Egyptian (1986)** : The band's biggest hit, written by Liam Sternberg. It featured "near nonsensical lyrics" and a "shimmying dance beat," inspiring an easy-to-do imitation hieroglyphic dance. Three of the four Bangles sang a verse each, and the video's release on MTV led kids to imitate the dance. The song ruled the Hot 100 for four weeks into January 1987 and became the number one song of that year, sending *Different Light* to double platinum status. "Walk Like an Egyptian, a collection of near nonsensical lyrics into shimmying dance beat was written by songwriter and producer Liam Sternberg from Akrono Hio."
**Walking Down Your Street (1987)** : The group's follow-up to their number one hit, "Walk Like an Egyptian." This harmony-laden rocker peaked at number 11 in April 1987. "The Harmony, Late in Rocker walking down your street peaked at number 11 in April 1987."
**A Hazy Shade of Winter (1987)** : A cover of Simon and Garfunkel's 60s hit, reinvented as "stomping hard rock" for the *Less Than Zero* soundtrack by producer Rick Rubin. By early 1988, it reached number 2, easily surpassing the Simon and Garfunkel original's peak of number 13 in 1966. "The group recorded a fierce cover of Simon and Garfunkel's 60s hit A Hazy Shade of Winter."
**In Your Room (1989)** : A rocking, slightly psychedelic track from the *Everything* album, featuring a lead vocal by Susanna Hoffs. It reached number 5 on both the Hot 100 and the Modern Rock Chart by early 1989. Hoffs co-wrote the song with Billy Steinberg and Tom Kelly. "The Rocking Slightly Psychedelic, in your room, which had reached number 5 on both the Hot 100 and the Modern Rock Chart by the start of 1989."
**Eternal Flame (1989)** : Named by Susanna Hoffs after the perpetual fire at Elvis Presley's gravesite in Memphis, this gentle ballad was primarily a showcase for Hoffs's prominent lead vocal, likened to The Beatles' "Yesterday" as a solo member's project disguised as a group effort. The rest of the group bristled at its inclusion on the *Everything* album due to its orchestration and Hoffs-spotlighting arrangement, but the label pushed for it and chose it as the second single. It went all the way to number one by April 1989. "In essence, Eternal Flame was to the Bangles what yesterday had been to the Beatles, a showcase for a single member disguised as a group project."
**Be With You (1989)** : Co-written and sung by drummer Debbie Peterson, this song was chosen as the *Everything* album's third single by the band to provide a showcase for someone other than Susanna Hoffs. However, it stalled at number 30 on the Hot 100, which the label used as ammunition for their argument to make Hoffs a solo star. "But when Be With You stalled at number 30 on the Hot 100, it gave the label ammunition for their long-running argument that Susanna Hoffs should be the star or go solo."

## curiosities
**Early Sound Description (1980s)** : The band described their sound as combining "Sixties-style California harmonies" with "80s garage-style crash and burn," a style best exemplified by their song "Let It Go." "To the four women of the Bangles, their sound was about both the singing and the playing."
**Producer David Kahne's Approach (1986)** : For the *Different Light* album, producer David Kahne, at the behest of Columbia Records, prioritized making it a pop breakthrough. He focused heavily on vocals, often requiring numerous takes for instrumental tracks or bringing in studio sidemen, and seemed primarily interested in songs featuring Susanna Hoffs's lead vocals. "David Khan, a producer who'd made his bones producing punk acts and had produced the Bangles debut LP, he went into the second album with other ideas."
**Susanna Hoffs's Prominence & Internal Tension (1986-1989)** : Columbia Records and producer David Kahne increasingly pushed Susanna Hoffs as the group's "standout star" and "de facto front person and sex symbol." This focus on her lead vocals and image created growing tension within the four-piece band, particularly with tracks like "In Your Room" and "Eternal Flame." "Khan also seemed uninterested in anything not featuring lead vocals by Susanna Hoffs, who was being pushed by the label as the Bangles standout star."
**Prince's Fandom and "Act of Courtship" (Mid-1980s)** : Prince became a serious fan of The Bangles after seeing them at the Palace Theatre and praised "Hero Takes a Fall." He developed a particular interest in Susanna Hoffs, and though dating was unconfirmed, he passed her a cassette with "Manic Monday" as an "act of courtship." "But Prince liked Hoffs in particular. It has never been confirmed that the two dated. But what he did next has been widely regarded as an act of courtship."
**Dance Craze for Walk Like an Egyptian (1986-1987)** : The song "Walk Like an Egyptian" sparked a dance craze with its easy-to-do imitation hieroglyphic pose, contributing significantly to its smash success as its video hit MTV and "kids began imitating the dance." "There was a little dance to go with Walk Like an Egyptian, an imitation hieroglyphic pose with your hands stabbing the air sideways."
**Band Breakup (1989)** : Following a summer 1989 tour where members were "barely speaking to each other" due to internal tensions and label pressure to spotlight Susanna Hoffs, Michael Steele quit the band in September, and the Peterson Sisters were told by the label they were "no longer needed," leading to the Bangles' dissolution before the end of 1989. "Before 1989 was even over, the bangles were done."
**Influence on 90s Pop (Early 1990s)** : The band missed out on the next wave of alternative music, but elements of their sound, particularly their "updated 60-style harmonies and the title of the bangles blend of voices," were borrowed by early 90s pop groups like Wilson Phillips. "Even the pop music of the early 90s borrowed moves from the bangles."
