## albums
**She's So Unusual - Quadruple-Platinum Debut** : Lauper's debut album went quadruple platinum and produced a string of hits by 1985. It peaked at No. 4 on the album chart, a peak later matched by True Colors. Its sales were more than double those of True Colors. "By 1985, Lopper had scored a string of hits off her quadruple platinum album."
**True Colors (1986) - Sophomore Album Packed with Guests** : Lauper's long-awaited second album was a hodgepodge of material with many guests. It included covers of Marvin Gaye, the New Orleans standard "Iko Iko" (made famous by the Dixie Cups and the Belle Stars), and "The Faraway Nearby," a calypso-flavored techno-pop ditty with backing vocals from labelmate Aimee Mann. With a bigger budget, Lauper also remade "Maybe He'll Know," a modest retro doo-wop song from her Blue Angel days, with Billy Joel singing backup. The album reached the top 10 the week after its title single hit No. 1, peaked at No. 4 (matching She's So Unusual), and went double platinum, so for the moment Lauper seemed to have dodged the sophomore jinx. "It was a hodgepodge of material."
**A Night to Remember (1989) - Post-Comeback Album** : Released three years after True Colors, the album was barely helped by its hit "I Drove All Night." It peaked at No. 37 and failed to go gold, never mind platinum. "Never mind, Platinum."
**Hat Full of Stars (1993) - Adrift in the 90s** : This album came four years after A Night to Remember and found Lauper still casting about for a sound for the 90s. The public and her label, Epic Records, largely met it with indifference, and it peaked at a dismal No. 112. "It found her still adrift, casting about for a sound for the 90s."
**Greatest Hits (1994) - Gold Compilation** : The "Hey Now (Girls Just Want to Have Fun)" remake propelled this 1994 greatest hits album to gold sales in America. "It propelled a 1994 greatest hits album to gold sales."
**Sisters of Avalon (1996) - Alternative Rock Attempt** : Lauper tried her hand at alternative rock on this album, most especially on its title track. She had far greater success on the dance charts in her forties and fifties. "Lauper did try her hand at Alternative Rock, most especially the title track of her 1996 album, Sisters of Avalon."
**Bring Ya to the Brink (2008) - Two Club No. 1s** : With this album Lauper went deeper into club music. It produced two No. 1 singles on Billboard's Club Play chart, "Same Ol' Story" and "Into the Nightlife," her first chart-toppers there since "Girls Just Want to Have Fun" in 1984. "Lopper went deeper into club music."
**Kinky Boots (2013) - Tony-Winning Broadway Score** : Harvey Fierstein was adapting the 2005 British film about a family shoe factory that saves its business by making men's fetish footwear with a drag queen, and he recruited Lauper to write the music. Lauper had never written a musical, though she identified with drag queens and was a lifelong Broadway fan. Fierstein taught her the principles of writing for the stage but left the music to her. The show opened on Broadway in April 2013 with future Tony winner Billy Porter. It won six Tonys, including Best Musical, Best Actor for Porter, and Best Score, which went solely to Lauper, making her the first unaccompanied solo female composer to win that prize. "She became the first ever unaccompanied solo female composer to win the prize."

## songs
**Girls Just Want to Have Fun (1983/1984) - Debut Hit** : Pro wrestler Captain Lou Albano played Lauper's father in the video. Like "If She Knew What She Wants," the song was originally written from a man's point of view and transformed when a woman sang it. It was her last Club Play chart-topper until 2008. "In her girls just want to have fun video, her father was played by pro wrestler Captain Louis Elbano."
**The Goonies 'R' Good Enough (1985)** : Several wrestlers appeared in the video. At its MTV premiere, Captain Lou Albano was with her, and the broadcast devolved into WWF-style trash talk when Rowdy Roddy Piper theatrically barged in. "The broadcasts devolved into playful WWF-style trash talk when Rowdy Roddy Piper theatrically barged in."
**Money Changes Everything (1985) - Lone Misstep** : This single reached only No. 27 in the winter of 1985. It was the one exception in a run of seven top 10 hits between 1984 and 1986. "Money Changes Everything, which was only a number 27 hit in the winter of 1985."
**All Through the Night - Jules Shear Composition** : This Lauper hit was written by songwriter Jules Shear. "The songwriter behind Cindy Loppers hit all through the night."
**Time After Time (1984) - First No. 1** : This was Lauper's first chart-topper. It was co-written by Rob Hyman of the Hooters, who later wrote Joan Osborne's "One of Us." "Her first chart topper since time after time in 1984."
**True Colors (1986) - No. 1 and LGBTQ Anthem** : Billy Steinberg and Tom Kelly wrote the song two years after their No. 1 "Like a Virgin" for Madonna, and imagined it as gospel-flavored like Simon & Garfunkel's "Bridge Over Troubled Water." Lauper felt it needed to get smaller. She sang it in a meek, childlike voice, unleashing her full voice only briefly at the chorus. While singing she pictured a friend who had died of AIDS, at a very early moment in public acknowledgment of the disease's toll on the gay community. It hit No. 1 after nine weeks on the Hot 100 and stayed two weeks. It became a perennial LGBTQ anthem as the rainbow flag rose as a pride icon, and she often performs it at Pride marches. "Cindy's arrangement of True Colors defined the song and made it a standard."
**Code of Silence (1986) - Billy Joel Collaboration** : Billy Joel invited Lauper to sing on this deep cut from his album The Bridge. She repaid him by having him sing backup on "Maybe He'll Know." "Joel, who earlier in 86 had invited Lopper to do it with him on Code of Silence."
**Change of Heart (1987) - Bangles on Backing Vocals** : The follow-up to "True Colors" reached No. 3. Her friends the Bangles sang backup, two weeks after Different Light peaked. "The bangles were back in the top three as backup singers."
**What's Going On (1987) - Marvin Gaye Cover** : Lauper's synth-pop cover of Marvin Gaye's song reached No. 11 in May 1987. It was her first single to miss the top 10 in two years. "What's going on became her first single to miss the top ten in two years."
**Boy Blue (1987) - Missed the Top 40** : This peppy torch song missed the top 40 entirely, peaking at No. 71. "Peaking at a lowly number 71."
**Pee-wee's Playhouse Theme - Favor for a Friend** : Between albums, Lauper recorded the theme to her friend Pee-wee Herman's Saturday morning kids' show. "Lopper did a favor for her friend, Peewee Herman."
**I Drove All Night (1989) - Comeback Torch Ballad** : Billy Steinberg and Tom Kelly wrote this torch ballad, studded with big glory notes, originally for Roy Orbison, who recorded it before his death in 1988. Lauper's version reached No. 6 in summer 1989 and No. 7 in the UK. Orbison's posthumous version charted worldwide in 1992, also hitting No. 7 in the UK. Celine Dion covered it in 2003, missing the Hot 100 top 40 but reaching the AC top 10, with a remix hitting No. 2 on the dance chart. Lauper's remains the highest-charting version, yet three decades later Orbison's and Dion's versions get more Spotify streams. "An artist is always just one song away from a comeback."
**Who Let in the Rain (1993)** : This soulful pop song, the first single from Hat Full of Stars, scraped the lower reaches of the adult contemporary chart. "Scraped the lower reaches of the adult's contemporary chart."
**That's What I Think (1993) - Club Return** : This deep cut from Hat Full of Stars returned Lauper to Billboard's Club Play chart for the first time in seven years, peaking at No. 14. "Brought Lauper back to Billboard's Club Play chart for the first time in seven years."
**Hey Now (Girls Just Want to Have Fun) (1994) - Proto-Mashup** : This remake of her first hit was built around an interpolation of Redbone's 1974 hit "Come and Get Your Love." It went top five in England, Japan, and New Zealand. "The song was a mashup before the age of mashups."
**Same Ol' Story / Into the Nightlife (2008) - Club No. 1s** : Both singles from Bring Ya to the Brink topped Billboard's Club Play chart. "Her first toppers on that chart since girls just want to have fun in 1984."
**Sex Is in the Heel (2012) - Kinky Boots Club Hit** : Lauper released her own version of this Kinky Boots song, and it became a top 10 club hit. "Scoring another top 10 club hit in 2012 with Sex in the Heal."

## curiosities
**1985 Grammy Best New Artist - Accepted with Hulk Hogan** : Lauper won Best New Artist and brought Hulk Hogan (Terry Bollea), who flexed behind the podium. In her acceptance she thanked Captain Lou Albano and Katrina Dominique. "That's Hulk Hogan flexing behind the podium next to Cindy."
**WWF Co-Promotion and WrestleMania 1985** : In her early stardom, Lauper co-promoted the World Wrestling Federation at every turn. At the first WrestleMania in 1985 she played the manager of Women's Champion Wendy Richter. When she got into a tussle at ringside, sportscasters covered it blow by blow. "Lopper was co-promoting the world wrestling federation at every turn."
**Imperial Period - Seven Top 10 Hits** : For about two years, Lauper could be her most flamboyantly outrageous self, and she strung together seven top 10 hits between 1984 and 1986. "Cindy Lopper could be the most flamboyantly outrageous version of herself, and the public ate it up."
**Rush's First Choice for Time Stand Still** : Rush first wanted Lauper for the female counterpoint vocal on "Time Stand Still," but she was unattainable. The part eventually went to Aimee Mann. "Their first choice for that vocal was No Kidding Cindy Lopper."
**Vibes (1988) - Flop Film with Jeff Goldblum** : Lauper tried acting in the film Vibes with Jeff Goldblum. She was fairly charming, but the movie earned savage reviews. "The movie earned savage reviews."
**Mad About You - 1995 Emmy Win** : Lauper's cameos as Marianne Lugasso on the NBC sitcom Mad About You won her the 1995 Emmy for Outstanding Guest Actress in a Comedy Series. "This time to much greater acclaim."
**Influence on 90s Rock** : Much of 90s rock echoed Lauper's work from a decade earlier, including Juliana Hatfield's post-punk with girlish vocals and Joan Osborne's alt-pop. "So much of 90s rock echoed what Lauper had been doing a decade prior."
**Embraced as Gay Icon** : In the 2000s, Lauper embraced and doubled down on her status as an LGBTQ icon. The dance charts and gay community kept welcoming her in her forties and fifties. "She not only embraced her status as an icon to the LGBTQ community, she doubled down."
**Fierstein's Reason for Choosing Her** : Harvey Fierstein explained that he wanted a composer who could write both club music and show tunes. "I saw an opportunity to work with someone with a big musical range, somebody who could write club music, along with Showtunes."
**Tony Speech - Learned to Sing from Cast Albums** : Lauper was shocked and deliriously happy at the 2013 Tonys. She thanked her mother for the Broadway cast albums she wore out as a child. "That's how I learned how to sing."
**One Oscar Away from EGOT** : Lauper has a Grammy for singing, an Emmy for acting, and a Tony for composing. "She's one Oscar away from an E-GOT."

## albums
**All Over the Place (1984) - Debut LP** : Produced by David Kahn, the debut reached only No. 80, so the band likely had nowhere to go but up. "They likely had nowhere to go but up."
**Different Light (1986) - Pop Breakthrough** : The band saw its sound as both singing and playing: 60s California harmonies against 80s garage crash-and-burn. At Columbia's behest, producer David Kahn, who had made his name producing punk acts, set out to make it their pop breakthrough. He recorded their harmonies beautifully but had them redo instrumental takes repeatedly or brought in session players. He focused on Susanna Hoffs's vocals. Three of four singles were covers. After "Walk Like an Egyptian," the album climbed back to No. 2 and went double platinum. "To Khan, the Bangles were mostly about the vocals."
**Everything (1988) - Final Album Before Breakup** : Released in late 1988, the album produced "In Your Room" and the No. 1 "Eternal Flame." The label used it to further position Susanna Hoffs as frontwoman and sex symbol, deepening tension in the band. "Creating tension within the forcing."
**Doll Revolution (2003) - Independent Reunion Album** : After four years of touring post-reunion, the band recorded this independent album. It was named after an Elvis Costello song they covered, and the rest was written by the four members, some remaking songs from their 90s projects. "Named after an Elvis Costello song that they covered for the album."
**3 × 4 (2018) - Paisley Underground Covers Album** : The Bangles joined Rain Parade, the Dream Syndicate, and the Three O'Clock on a joint album covering each other's 60s-inspired material. "They joined the other paisley bands, Rain Parade, the Dream Syndicate, and the Three o'clock on a joint album."

## songs
**Hero Takes a Fall (1984) - Prince's Favorite** : After seeing the band at the Palace Theatre in LA, Prince told Hoffs this song was number one in his car. "Number one in my car."
**Let It Go (1986)** : This Different Light track, written and sung by all four members, best represented the band's harmonies-plus-garage approach. "Written and sung by all four members."
**September Gurls (1986) - Big Star Cover** : The Bangles covered Big Star's power pop classic. The vocals are all theirs, but the 60s-style backwards guitar solo was played by a session musician. "Its 60s style backwards guitar solo is a session musician."
**Following (1986) - Michael Steele Composition** : Steele said producer David Kahn recorded her moody song, one of the album's best, in just two takes. "Recorded by Khan in just two takes."
**Manic Monday (1986) - Written by Prince** : Prince wrote it in 1984 and demoed it as a duet with Apollonia Kotero for Apollonia 6, then kept it off that album. Smitten with Hoffs, he passed her a cassette of two unreleased songs through a mutual friend, widely seen as courtship. It became the Bangles' first Hot 100 entry, credited to the pseudonym "Christopher." It reached No. 2 in April 1986, blocked by Prince's own "Kiss." "Recording a Prince song in 1986 was all by itself, a hit making insurance policy."
**If She Knew What She Wants (1986) - Jules Shear Cover** : Jules Shear's original was about a frustrated man who can't please his girlfriend. Sung by Hoffs with Bangles harmonies, it became mutual friends musing about a doomed couple. Michael Steele had briefly played in Jules Shear and the Polar Bears. It peaked at No. 29 in July 1986, Shear's second top 40 hit as a songwriter. "It's sad, poignant, and beautiful."
**Walk Like an Egyptian (1986) - Biggest Hit and No. 1 Song of 1987** : The song was written by Liam Sternberg of Akron, Ohio, a contemporary of Devo and the Waitresses. The band picked the demo from a pile of cassettes from David Kahn. Every member but Debbi Peterson sings a verse, and Hoffs's sideways glance at the video's end stole teenage hearts. Its hieroglyphic hand dance was widely imitated. It was No. 1 for four weeks into January 1987 and was the year's top song. "It was silly, but it was easy to do."
**Walking Down Your Street (1987)** : This harmony-laden rocker peaked at No. 11 in April 1987. "Peaked at number 11 in April 1987."
**A Hazy Shade of Winter (1987) - Less Than Zero Soundtrack** : Rick Rubin invited the band onto the Less Than Zero soundtrack. Their hard-rock cover of Simon & Garfunkel reached No. 2 in early 1988, beating the original's No. 13 peak from 1966. "Reinvented the groovy folk song as stomping hard rock."
**In Your Room (1988)** : Hoffs co-wrote this slightly psychedelic, gently risqué song with Billy Steinberg and Tom Kelly. It hit No. 5 on the Hot 100 and Modern Rock chart by early 1989. "The gently risqué song furthered the label's aims to position her as the group's de facto front person and sex symbol."
**Eternal Flame (1989) - The Bangles' Waterloo** : Hoffs, Steinberg, and Kelly co-wrote this ballad, which Hoffs named after the perpetual flame at Elvis Presley's grave in Memphis. Hoffs's vocal was more prominent than on any earlier Bangles song, and the others only sang backup. The band bristled at including it, but the label pushed it as the second single, and it hit No. 1 in April 1989. "Eternal Flame was to the Bangles what yesterday had been to the Beatles."
**Be With You (1989) - Debbi Peterson Showcase** : The band insisted the third single feature someone other than Hoffs and chose this song co-written and sung by drummer Debbi Peterson. It stalled at No. 30, giving the label ammunition to argue that Hoffs should be the star or go solo. "It gave the label ammunition for their long-running argument."
**Get the Girl (1999) - Austin Powers Reunion** : Hoffs persuaded her old bandmates to reform for Austin Powers: The Spy Who Shagged Me. The song was a loving throwback to their Paisley Underground origins. "It was a loving throwback to their paisley underground origins."

## curiosities
**Touring with Cyndi Lauper** : In 1985 the Bangles appeared in Lauper's videos and on her tour while still seeking a hit. "The Bangles were appearing in Cindy's videos and on her tour."
**1989 Breakup** : The band toured in summer 1989 barely speaking. At a September meeting after the tour, Michael Steele quit, and the label told the Peterson sisters they were no longer needed. "Before 1989 was even over, the bangles were done."
**Influence on Wilson Phillips** : Early-90s pop borrowed from the Bangles, notably the sisterly trio Wilson Phillips, who topped the charts with updated 60s-style harmonies. "Even the pop music of the early 90s borrowed moves from the bangles."
**90s Side Projects** : Debbi Peterson formed Kindred Spirit, and Michael Steele formed Crash Wisdom. The most enduring was Vicki Peterson's Continental Drifters with Susan Cowsill. "Other former bangles joined their own 90s projects."
**Joy of Reuniting** : In a 2003 interview, Vicki Peterson marveled at working with the band again. "We would look at each other and go, but we're not suffering. Is it art? Nobody's suffering."
**Later Years as a Trio** : Michael Steele left again in the mid-2000s. The trio reconnected with their garage rock roots, played a 2013 Paisley Underground reunion, and played their last gig to date about six months before the COVID-19 pandemic. "The Peter Sincisters and Susanna Hoffs have reconnected with their garage rock roots."

## songs
**My Side of the Bed (1991)** : This slick radio pop single leaned on the label's image management for Hoffs. It peaked at No. 30 in March 1991, her only solo pop top 40 hit. "It would be Hoffs only solo song to crack the pop top 40."
**BBC (1997) - Ming Tea in Austin Powers** : Ming Tea performed this would-be British Invasion hit in Austin Powers: International Man of Mystery. "A shaggedellic would-be British invasion hit called BBC."

## curiosities
**Prince's Courtship** : Prince was especially fond of Hoffs. They were never confirmed to have dated, but his gift of "Manic Monday" was widely seen as courtship. "It has never been confirmed that the two dated."
**Label's Star Pick** : Columbia pushed Hoffs as the Bangles' standout star. Her former bandmates knew she wasn't scheming to push them out; she had only gone along with the star-making machinery. "Hoffs had merely been compliant with the star-making machinery. Maybe too compliant."
**The Allnighter (1987)** : Hoffs let her filmmaker mother, Tamar Simon Hoffs, direct her in this college romp. The poster showed her in a bikini, and the film was neither a hit nor a cinematic gem. "The movie's poster featured a picture of Susanna in a bikini."
**Ming Tea and Austin Powers** : In the mid-90s Hoffs, Matthew Sweet, and Mike Myers formed Ming Tea, a fake British 60s band, for fun. It helped inspire Austin Powers, directed by Hoffs's husband Jay Roach, who teamed with Myers again for the 1999 sequel. "A faux British 60s band they called Ming-T."

## albums
**Voices Carry (1985) - Gold Debut** : The album reached the top 20, peaked at No. 18, and went gold. It was their only top 40 album until Aimee Mann's Lost in Space. "The Voices' carry album did crack the top 20, peaking at number 18 and going gold."
**Welcome Home (1986) - Second Album** : The band spent much of 1986 recording it, trying to avoid the sophomore jinx. It continued the ethereal, moody pop of the debut. "Till Tuesday would spend much of 1986 recording their all-important follow-up album."
**Everything's Different Now (1988) - Final Album and Turning Point** : Much of the album was about Mann's ex, Jules Shear, who co-wrote two songs, and one song was titled "J for Jules." Critics praised Mann's growth as a songwriter, but Epic wasn't interested in promoting it. It peaked at No. 124 and was their only album not to go gold. "In a Romana-cliff fashion later made famous by Taylor Swift, one heart-rending song was even titled J for Jules."

## songs
**Voices Carry (1985) - Best New Artist Video** : Fueled by Mann's cinematic video performance, it was a huge single and won Best New Artist in a Video at the second MTV VMAs. "Till Tuesday had a huge single fueled by Mann's cinematic music video performance."
**Looking Over My Shoulder (1985)** : The follow-up single had peaked at No. 61 by the VMAs. "Had already peaked at number 61."
**Love in a Vacuum (1985)** : The third single missed the Hot 100 entirely by the end of the year. "Missed the Hot 100 entirely."
**What About Love (1986)** : The lead single from Welcome Home echoed "Voices Carry" with lyrics like "Living on silence, living by the book." It returned the band to the top 40 at No. 26 in fall 1986, but it wasn't Mann's first choice. "What about love was not Amy Mann's first choice for a single."
**Coming Up Close (1986/1987)** : Mann felt closest to this folky, guitar-driven story song, the album's second track. It peaked at No. 59 in early 1987. "Amy Mann's songwriting was evolving."
**(Believed You Were) Lucky (1988)** : Co-written by Mann and Jules Shear, it reached only No. 95 on the Hot 100 but made the top 40 on the Adult Contemporary and new Modern Rock charts. "The single Mann co-wrote with her ex was a minor hit."

## curiosities
**1985 VMA Best New Artist** : At the acceptance, Mann thanked MTV for breaking the record and joked that she forgot they meant her band. The prize proved a curse, implying more hits than came. "Yeah, I forgot that they were talking about us for a minute."
**Jon Brion Joins on Letterman** : Promoting the final album on NBC's Late Night with David Letterman, Mann was backed on guitar by new member Jon Brion, a 25-year-old multi-instrumentalist. Their collaboration moved from Boston to LA and later rebooted her career. "Would eventually reboot Amy Mann's career."

## albums
**Whatever (1993) - Solo Debut** : After years waiting out contracts (leaving Epic, signing elsewhere, then being reabsorbed by Epic), Mann released her debut on indie label Imago. It peaked at No. 127, produced or co-produced by Jon Brion. "Amy Mann spent nearly half a decade waiting out her recording contract."
**I'm With Stupid (1995) - Geffen Album** : Imago went under and Geffen's DGC took over Mann's contract. Geffen, focused on Weezer and Garbage, barely promoted this album, which peaked at No. 82 in 1996. Jon Brion produced it, with sparkling sonics and smart hooks, and brought in Michael Penn. "Geffen, distracted by bigger bands like Weezer and Garbage, did little to promote Mann's album."
**Bachelor No. 2 or, the Last Remains of the Dodo (2000) - Independent Release** : After the 1998 Universal–PolyGram merger moved her to Interscope, Jimmy Iovine refused to release the album without more material. Mann bought it back and released it in spring 2000 on her own SuperEgo label. The extinct bird in the title was a sly jab at the record industry. It sold nearly a quarter million copies, far more profitable for her than a major-label release. "The extinct bird in the title was a sly commentary on the traditional recording industry."
**Lost in Space (2002)** : This independent album matched Bachelor No. 2's sales. At No. 35, it was her first top 40 album since the 'Til Tuesday debut. "Lost in Space peaked at No. 35."
**Queens of the Summer Hotel (2021)** : This album doubles as a stage adaptation of Susanna Kaysen's 1993 memoir Girl, Interrupted, about her time in a mental institution. "An album that doubles as a stage adaptation of Girl Interrupted."

## songs
**The Faraway Nearby (1986) - Backing Vocal for Lauper** : Mann sang backup on this calypso-flavored techno-pop track on Lauper's True Colors album. "As unlikely a song to feature an Amy Mann vocal as a prog rock song from Rush."
**Time Stand Still (1987) - With Rush** : Rush wanted a female counterpoint vocal and tried Cyndi Lauper and Chrissie Hynde before Mann. She sang the title phrase and appeared in the video by Zbigniew Rybczyński, pushing a giant studio camera. It reached No. 3 on the Album Rock chart in 1987. "Time Stand Still became one of Rush's biggest radio hits."
**J for Jules (1988)** : A heartbreaking song about Jules Shear on Everything's Different Now. "One heart-rending song was even titled J for Jules."
**I Should've Known (1993)** : Imago took it to No. 16 on the Modern Rock chart. "The label even got it single, I should have known, to number 16."
**That's Just What You Are (1994)** : Featured on the Melrose Place soundtrack, it reached No. 24 on Modern Rock and was included on I'm With Stupid. "Which was on the soundtrack to the TV Primetime soap opera Melrose Place."
**Long Shot (1995)** : A catchy I'm With Stupid track. "Packed with catchy tracks like Longshot."
**Wise Up (1996/1999)** : Cameron Crowe took it for the Jerry Maguire soundtrack but left it out of the film. Paul Thomas Anderson built a key Magnolia scene around it, with the cast singing it. "A forgotten ballot buried deep on the soundtrack to the 1996 film Jerry McGuire."
**Deathly (1999) - Inspiration for Magnolia** : Anderson credited its opening line as the inspiration for Magnolia. "Anderson later claimed that the inspiration for his 1999 film Magnolia came from the opening line in one of Mann's songs, Deathly."
**Save Me (1999) - Oscar Nominee** : Written for the Magnolia soundtrack, it was nominated for Best Original Song. Mann performed it at the 2000 Oscars but lost to Phil Collins's "You'll Be in My Heart." "The song that lost an Oscar to Phil Collins and his cartoon Monkey Love Song."
**Nothing Is Good Enough (2000)** : Mann wrote this about frustration with label executives saying they didn't hear a single. "Mann, pen to song, nothing is good enough about how fed up she was with label executives."
**Red Vines (2000)** : A Bachelor No. 2 tribute to Paul Thomas Anderson. "An homage to her friend Paul Thomas Anderson."

## curiosities
**Relationship with Jules Shear** : Mann and Jules Shear dated for about two years starting 1986, and he influenced her music. The breakup shaped Everything's Different Now. "Sheer would wind up having an impact on her music as well."
**Marriage to Michael Penn** : Michael Penn, a musician who scored No. 13 with "No Myth," played on I'm With Stupid; they married in 1997. "They went and are still married to this day."
**Largo Scene in LA** : After moving to LA in 1995, Mann and Penn joined Jon Brion's Friday shows at Largo with artists like Fiona Apple and Elliott Smith. "Their friend John Bryan was the nexus of that scene."
**Paul Thomas Anderson** : A Largo regular, Anderson built Magnolia around Mann's songs and helped establish her as a hip LA artist. "Anderson was evangelical about Mann's music."
**Live Performances** : Mann performs songs from her whole catalog live, often with Michael Penn, Jon Brion, Ted Leo, or Jonathan Coulton, and still plays "Voices Carry." "It was in widescreen from the moment Amy Mann dreamed it up."
