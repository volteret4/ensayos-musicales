## albums
**High Voltage (1975) - International Debut** : This was AC/DC's very first internationally distributed album, composed of songs from two prior Australian albums, and it set the template for their sound. It led off with the anthem "It's a Long Way to the Top If You Want to Rock and Roll," and its cover featured their iconic logo with a lightning bolt and an artist's rendering of guitarist Angus Young in a schoolboy uniform. Atlantic Records got this album onto the Billboard Top LP chart for the first time in the summer of 1981, five years after its initial release. "ACDC's very first internationally distributed album, High Voltage, composed of songs from two prior Australian albums, set the template."
**Dirty Deeds Done Dirt Cheap (1976 / US 1981) - Atlantic Refusal & US Reissue** : Atlantic Records initially refused to issue this 1976 album in the United States entirely. It was finally released stateside in the Spring of 1981, five years after its original release, and shot into the Billboard LP chart, peaking at number 3. This release was confusing for US fans and frustrating for the band because the vocals were by the now-deceased frontman Bon Scott, just months after hits featuring new singer Brian Johnson. Songs from "Dirty Deeds" were prominent on rock radio in the summer of 1981. "In fact, Atlantic Records refused to issue one of their 1976 albums in the United States entirely."
**Highway to Hell (1979)** : This album marked a turning point for AC/DC as producer Robert John "Mutt" Lang, working with the band for the first time, condensed their sound to its essence and showcased Bon Scott's piercing vocals. "Highway to Hell" broke into the top 20 on the album chart and became the band's first US platinum album. "Highway to Hell broke into the top 20 on the album chart and was the band's first US platinum album."
**Back in Black (1980) - Post-Tragedy Masterpiece** : Released six months after Bon Scott's death, this album served as a tribute to him and became the band's biggest LP ever. It sported an all-black cover and opened with the funereal chimes of "Hell's Bells." Producer Mutt Lang honed AC/DC's sound to a diamond-hard core, and it is still considered a reference standard for hard rock acoustics by producers today. Despite peaking at number 4 on the Billboard album chart in December 1980, it has spent nearly 450 weeks on the chart and was certified for sales of 22 million copies in 2016. After counting streaming into tallies, it was re-certified in 2019 for 25 million total sales plus streams, continuing to sell well into 2021. "Back in black by ACDC was a monster success and a career triumph."
**For Those About to Rock We Salute You (1981) - First Number One Album** : This album, recorded with producer Mutt Lang, arrived like a major sequel and became AC/DC's first-ever number one album on the Billboard 200 album chart in December 1981, reaching the top in just three weeks and staying there for three weeks. Despite its chart-topping performance, it is clearly overshadowed by its predecessor, "Back in Black." Critics generally regard it as a "3-star follow-up to a 5-star classic," spending only 30 weeks on the charts compared to "Back in Black's" epic run. It is certified for 4 million copies (quadruple platinum) but has not been re-certified in over two decades and does not rank among AC/DC's top five sellers. "For those about to rock we salute you was the album that finally made ACDC chart toppers."
**Who Made Who (1986) - Film Soundtrack** : This album served as the soundtrack for Stephen King's 1986 horror film "Maximum Overdrive." The band produced another anthem that sounded distinctly like AC/DC for the film, and the album sold especially well. "When suspense author and filmmaker Stephen King asked ACDC to do the soundtrack for his 1986 horror film Maximum Overdrive, ACDC produced another anthem that sounded like ACDC. Who made who? That album sold especially well."
**The Razors Edge (1990)** : Released at the turn of the 90s, this LP maintained AC/DC's consistent formula even as alternative rock was taking over the radio and grunge was emerging. It spent more than a year on the charts and generated hard rock classics like "Thunderstruck," along with their biggest Top 40 pop hit, "Money Talks." "At the turn of the 90s, as alternative rock was taking over the radio, ACDC changed their formula not a bit."

## songs
**It's a Long Way to the Top If You Want to Rock and Roll (1975)** : This anthem was the lead-off track on AC/DC's first internationally distributed album, "High Voltage." It was described as riffy, fist-pumping, and unsubtle, setting the tone for their brand of no-frills hard rock. "Leading off with the anthem, it's a long way to the top if you want to rock and roll."
**Jailbreak** : Mentioned as an example of AC/DC's "delinquent lyrics," reflecting their proud "maladjustment" in their music. "This proud maladjustment was reflected in ACDC's music from jailbreak to big balls, which is about pretty much what it sounds like."
**Big Balls** : Cited as an example of the band's "delinquent lyrics" and crude double-entendre style. The song is described as being "about pretty much what it sounds like." "This proud maladjustment was reflected in ACDC's music from jailbreak to big balls, which is about pretty much what it sounds like."
**Whole Lotta Rosie (1977)** : This track from 1977 is highlighted as an example of AC/DC's musicianship, demonstrating their ability to operate as an "efficient, unsparing riff machine." "On tracks like 1977's Whole Lotta Rosie, ACDC were an efficient, unsparing riff machine."
**Hell's Bells (1980)** : This track opened AC/DC's "Back in Black" album with "funereal chimes of doom," setting a somber tone for the album dedicated to the late Bon Scott. The album was ostensibly about death, though it was a very lively album overall. "Hell's Bells was the lead-off track to Back in Black."
**Let Me Put My Love Into You (1980)** : Featured on "Back in Black," this song exemplifies AC/DC's use of "double entendres"—or more accurately, "single entendres." It is humorously suggested as the inspiration for Nigel Tuffmo's Spinal Tap anthem, "Lick My Love Pump." "Let Me Put My Love Into You, Babe. Let Me Catch A Chain With My Babe."
**You Shook Me All Night Long (1980)** : One of AC/DC's two most iconic songs from "Back in Black," described as a "down and dirty, raunchy dance anthem." It became a rock radio staple and even cracked the American Top 40, reaching number 35 on the Hot 100 in November 1980. It is often heard at weddings. "You Shook Me All Night Long, which you probably heard the last time you attended a wedding."
**Back in Black (1980)** : The killer, riff-tastic title track from the album of the same name, described as "strutting and funky." This song became so influential that it was later sampled in more than 50 hip hop songs. It also became a rock radio staple and reached number 37 on the Hot 100. "And the album's absolutely killer, riff-tastic title track, a song so strutting and funky it was later sampled in more than 50 hip hop songs."
**Let's Get It Up (1981)** : A quintessentially-lewd follow-up single from the album "For Those About to Rock We Salute You." This song just missed the pop Top 40, peaking at number 44. "ACDC's quintessentially-lewed follow-up single Let's Get It Up, which just missed the pop top 40, peaking at number 44."
**Sink the Pink** : A "ribbled sex anthem" from the post-Mutt Lang era of AC/DC, demonstrating their continued adherence to their established formula. "They kept recording ribbled sex anthems like Sink the Pink."
**Heat Seeker** : A "ball-busting anthem" from the post-Mutt Lang era, illustrating AC/DC's consistent sonic identity and lyrical themes. "and ball-busting anthems like Heat Seeker."
**Thunderstruck (1990)** : A "hard rock classic" from "The Razors Edge" LP. This song contributed to the album's success, which spent over a year on the charts even at the dawn of grunge. "generated hard rock classics like Thunderstruck."
**Money Talks (1991)** : AC/DC's biggest Top 40 success, reaching number 23 on the pop charts in 1991. It came from "The Razors Edge" album. "And their biggest top 40 success, the 1991 number 23 pop hit Money Talks."

## curiosities
**Band Foundation and Origins - 1973** : AC/DC was founded in Sydney, Australia, in 1973 by brothers Malcolm and Angus Young, about a decade after their family had moved from Scotland. "Started in Sydney in 1973 by brothers Malcolm and Angus Young about a decade after the young family had moved from Scotland to Australia."
**Angus Young's Schoolboy Uniform** : Guitarist Angus Young famously wore a schoolboy's uniform while shredding his guitar on stage, a visual element that became iconic for the band. As recently as the mid-2010s, he was still wearing a version of the uniform. "And an artist's rendering of guitarist Angus Young in a schoolboy uniform."
**Socially Maladjusted Image** : Music critic Stephen Thomas Urlewine notes AC/DC's "socially maladjusted" persona, favoring crude double-entendres and violent imagery with a mischievous sense of fun. Lead singer Bon Scott was literally labeled "socially maladjusted" by the Australian Army and had minor criminal convictions. "Throughout their career, they favored crude double-entongers and violent imagery, all spiked with a mischievous sense of fun."
**Early Industry Rejection and Relentless Touring** : For their first half-decade, AC/DC faced rejection or indifference from various music industry factions, including radio programmers, critics at Rolling Stone magazine, and even their own record label, Atlantic Records. They built their American fanbase primarily through relentless touring. "By and large, ACDC won their American fan base by touring relentlessly."
**Producer George Young & Harry Vanda** : Malcolm and Angus Young's older brother, George Young, along with his partner Harry Vanda, produced all of AC/DC's albums for their first half-decade. They were co-founders of the 1960s Australian band The Easy Beats, known for the international hit "Friday on My Mind." "To this point, all of ACDC's albums were produced by Malcolm and Angus's older brother, George Young, and his partner, Harry Vanda."
**Reluctance to Change Producers** : The band was initially reluctant to ditch their brother George as producer when Atlantic Records pushed for a more radio-friendly producer. They hated the first producer Atlantic proposed before eventually sending material to Robert John "Mutt" Lang. "He was reluctant to ditch their brother, George, and hating the first producer the label proposed."
**Bon Scott's Tragic Death - February 1980** : Just six months after the release of the "Highway to Hell" album in February 1980, lead singer Bon Scott was found dead of alcohol poisoning at the age of 33. The band considered disbanding as Scott was a celebrated frontman critical to their image. "Just six months after the release of The Highway to Hell album in February of 1980, Bond Scott was found dead of alcohol poisoning at just 33 years of age."
**Brian Johnson's Vocal Style** : Upon joining AC/DC after Bon Scott's death, Brian Johnson, formerly of the band Jordy, brought an "even higher pitched shriek" than his predecessor to the band's sound. "He possessed if this is even possible, and even higher pitched shriek than Bond Scott."
**Four Albums Charting Simultaneously (Summer 1981)** : In the summer of 1981, AC/DC had four LPs charting simultaneously: the then-newly reissued "Dirty Deeds Done Dirt Cheap" (peaked at #3), the initially charting "High Voltage," and their still charting "Highway to Hell." Three of these were fronted by the late Bon Scott, effectively charting from "beyond the grave," alongside hits featuring new singer Brian Johnson. "ACDC now had 4 LP's charting simultaneously, and 3 of them were fronted by late vocalist Bond Scott from Beyond the Grave."
**Sonic Consistency** : AC/DC is noted for its unfailing sonic consistency throughout its nearly 50-year career. They do not record ballads, acoustic numbers, or anything that relies on electronic beats, never altering their formula much even after changing producers or in response to musical trends like alternative rock or grunge. "ACDC don't record ballots, acoustic numbers, or anything that leans on electronic beats."
**Platinum Album Achievements** : Over the course of their nearly 50-year career, AC/DC has scored 20 platinum albums in America, with a dozen of them achieving multi-platinum status. "Over the course of a nearly 50 year career, they have scored 20 platinum albums in America, a dozen of which are multi-platform."
**"It Ain't Broke So They Don't Fix It" Philosophy** : This phrase encapsulates AC/DC's approach to their music, highlighting their dedication to a consistent sound and formula over five decades. "It ain't broke so they don't fix it."

# artist - Adele

## albums
**19 (2008) - Debut Album** : Adele's debut album, named after her age when she finished recording it, took the better part of a year to reach number 10 on the US album chart. It sold its first million copies in the US slowly over the course of three years before being certified platinum approximately a month before her follow-up album arrived. "A Del's 2008 debut album, 19, was named after her age when she finished recording it."
**21 (2011) - Breakthrough Blockbuster** : Named for her age when she composed its songs, this album entered the Billboard 200 album chart at number 1 in March 2011, a significant leap considering her debut "19" had not risen higher than number 10. "21" became a mega blockbuster, selling more copies than any album in 2011 or 2012, going diamond, and spinning off multiple smash hits. It opened big with about 350,000 copies sold in its first week, largely due to fans' love for "19." It is widely regarded as her best album. "A Del's 2011 album, 21, named again for her age when she composed its songs, entered the Billboard 200 album chart at number 1 in March of 2011."
**25 (2015) - Record-Breaking Opening** : This "Uber Blockbuster" achieved the biggest opening week for any album ever, selling 3.38 million copies in the US in its first week alone. Its massive initial sales were a direct reflection of the public's profound love for its predecessor, "21." "Her third album, the 2015 Uber Blockbuster 25, opened even bigger. 3.38 million sold in the US in its first week alone, the biggest opening week for any album ever."

## songs
**Rolling in the Deep (2010)** : This smash hit from the album "21" was rising on the Hot 100 Songs chart when the album dropped, though it had not yet reached the top 10. "But how did the album come in so hot and heavy? The week 21 dropped, rolling in the deep was rising on the Hot 100 Songs chart, but it wasn't even in the top 10 yet."
**Someone Like You (2011)** : One of the multiple smash hits spun off from Adele's mega blockbuster album "21." "spinning off multiple smashes like someone like you."
**Set Fire to the Rain (2011)** : Another one of the hugely successful singles originating from Adele's "21" album. "spinning off multiple smashes like someone like you, set fire to the rain."

## curiosities
**Album Naming Convention** : Adele names her albums after her age when she finished recording or composed the songs, such as "19," "21," and "25," creating a "kind of like a movie sequel" numbering system. "A Del's 2008 debut album, 19, was named after her age when she finished recording it."
**Not the Best "AC/DC Rule" Example** : While Adele's albums like "21" and "25" demonstrated huge opening sales based on love for their predecessors, she is not considered the best example of the "AC/DC Rule" because her follow-up albums (especially "21") not only opened bigger but also ultimately sold the best, spun off the most hits, and are widely regarded as her best work, thus not being overshadowed by their predecessors. "The reason my rule isn't named for Adele is because Adele is honestly not the best example of the rule."

# curiosity

**The "AC/DC Rule" - Album Chart Idiosyncrasy** : The "AC/DC Rule" is a term coined by chart analyst Chris Mulanfee to describe an album chart idiosyncrasy where initial sales of an album are a referendum on the public's feelings about the act's *prior* album, not the current one. This phenomenon explains why a hit act might chart better or open bigger with a follow-up album than they do with a classic or better-selling predecessor, but then the follow-up ultimately falls short in long-term sales, chart longevity, hits generated, and critical/fan reception. AC/DC exemplifies this rule with "For Those About to Rock We Salute You" (a #1 album) being overshadowed by "Back in Black" (a #4 album with much higher sales and impact). "initial sales of an album are a referendum on the public's feelings about the act's prior album, not the current one."
**Sequel Phenomenon in Pop Culture** : This describes a pattern observed across various forms of entertainment, including movies, TV shows, and book series, where a sequel or subsequent installment generates significantly larger initial audience engagement (e.g., box office opening weekends, TV ratings, book sales) compared to its predecessor. This surge in initial interest is often a "referendum" on the audience's love for the *first* installment, not necessarily the quality of the new one, which can sometimes receive lower critical reviews. Examples include "Austin Powers: The Spy Who Shagged Me," "The Matrix Reloaded," "Pirates of the Caribbean: Dead Man's Chest," "The Hangover Part 2," "The Sopranos," "Harry Potter," and "The Hunger Games." "The instant smash opening of these sequels was a referendum on how much audiences loved their predecessors, not the new film."
**Box Office Record - The Spy Who Shagged Me (1999)** : The sequel "Austin Powers: The Spy Who Shagged Me" reportedly holds the unique distinction of being the first film ever to outgross its predecessor ("Austin Powers International Man of Mystery" from 1997) in a single opening weekend. It took in nearly $55 million in its first three days, surpassing the first film's entire run of $53 million, on its way to a final US gross of over $200 million. "Reportedly, at least as long as box office tallies have been kept, the spy who shagged me stands as the first film ever to outgrow its predecessor in a single weekend."
