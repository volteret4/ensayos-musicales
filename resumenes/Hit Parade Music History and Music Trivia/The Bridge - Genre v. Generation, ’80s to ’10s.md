## albums
**Japanese Whispers (1983)** : This album, which was an EP compiled from earlier tracks, marked The Cure's first record to ever chart in America. It includes the deep cut "Lament," a dark and foreboding track emblematic of the band's recording style during that period. "An EP called from that LP was the Cure's first album to chart in America."
**Pornography (unknown year)** : Mentioned as a notable album by The Cure during a discussion about the difficulty of categorizing various bands. "no pun on the word pornography right now because of that of course is a cure album."
**Kiss Me Kiss Me Kiss Me (1987)** : The album from which "Just Like Heaven" was released as its second single. The single went on to become The Cure's first top 40 hit in the US. "The second single from Kiss Me Kiss Me Kiss Me scraped the top 40 the first week of January 1988."

## songs
**Lament (1983)** : A deep cut from the album "Japanese Whispers," this song is characterized as one of the many dark and foreboding tracks The Cure recorded in the early 1980s. In it, singer and songwriter Robert Smith delivers a mournful vocal performance recounting a tragedy he observed under a bridge. "In the song, Cure Singer and Songwriter Robert Smith sings mournfully about a tragedy he witnessed underneath a bridge."
**Let's Go to Bed (1983)** : This track "bubbled under" Billboard's Hot 100 in 1983, reaching position number 109, indicating early but limited chart presence in the US. "For the record, let's go to bed, bubbled under the hot 100 at number 109 in 1983."
**In Between Days (1986)** : In early 1986, this song spent a single week on the US charts, peaking at number 99 on Billboard's Hot 100. "In between days spent a single week on the chart at number 99 in early 86."
**Just Like Heaven (1988)** : The second single from "Kiss Me Kiss Me Kiss Me," this song "scraped the top 40" on Billboard's Hot 100 during the first week of January 1988. It notably became The Cure's first top 40 hit in America. "The second single from Kiss Me Kiss Me Kiss Me scraped the top 40 the first week of January 1988."
**Love Song (1989)** : This track achieved significant commercial success for The Cure in America, becoming a number two smash hit in 1989. "And of course, love song was a number two smash in 1989."

## curiosities
**British Cultural Cachet (1984)** : By 1984, The Cure held a recognizable place in British popular culture, as illustrated by a casual reference to the band in an episode of the early 80s BBC comedy "The Young Ones," which was understood by the show's younger audience. This level of recognition was significantly higher in the UK compared to America at the same time, where a similar reference would have likely prompted indifference. "So the idea that in a, in Italy, you know, more Coltie, but still BBC comedy show, you could just throw out a casual reference to the character in this episode of some 84. And it have to be generally understood at least by the younger audience that was watching."
**Rock and Roll Hall of Fame Induction (2019)** : The Cure was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2019, on what was reportedly their second time on the ballot. Their induction was met with pleasant surprise and delight, marking a generational shift in recognition for their influence and legacy. "I believe it was their second time on the ballot this year and just so delighted that they got in."
**Great Survivors of the Era** : The Cure are considered one of the "great survivors" from their era of music, maintaining the ability to play large venues and organize special events and tours decades after their formation. They also share a strong "mutual appreciation society" with Depeche Mode, which has been evident almost from the start. "I think it's telling that those two are you could say the great survivors, the ones who could play the big venues have the special events and tours, things like that."
**Influence on My Chemical Romance's Aesthetic** : The visual and musical legacy of The Cure, particularly Robert Smith's distinctive look, is recognized as an influence on later bands. Gerard Way of My Chemical Romance, especially in his mid-2000s appearance with fully teased hair, is cited as an example of this influence, which Robert Smith himself acknowledges. "It's like, yeah, Robert Smith, come on. And he'll cop to it."

# curiosity

**Ned Raggett's Fandom Journey (Late 1980s)** : Music critic Ned Raggett's deep appreciation for UK post-punk and goth rock bands like Joy Division, New Order, The Cure, and Love and Rockets fully developed in 1988-1989. His initial exposure came through their pop hits and MTV airplay, such as Love and Rockets' "No New Tale to Tell," leading him to explore their history after attending UCLA. "It really wasn't until 88 89 that it fully sunk in with me and I had only encountered them beforehand because they started scoring pop hits."
**Categorization of 80s UK Bands** : The act of lumping diverse 80s UK bands (like The Cure, Bauhaus, New Order, The Smiths, Depeche Mode) under a single genre term like "goth" is noted as reductive and potentially limiting. Despite a shared emphasis on melancholia, these bands had little musical or geographical connection, with origins ranging from London suburbs to Northampton and Manchester. This phenomenon is compared to the first and second British invasions, where success at the same time often led to broad categorization. "But again musically there's very little connection. There's very little connection in terms of where they're from in the country."
**Changing Perceptions of Synthesizer-Based Music** : There has been a notable shift in public perception regarding music heavily reliant on synthesizers. Previously, some viewed it as "not real music," but this sentiment is diminishing. This changing attitude is seen as a factor that will eventually lead to greater recognition, such as induction into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, for bands like Depeche Mode, who extensively use synthesizers. "I think there are less people now who basically look at someone like to Peshmo go like, synthesizers, that's not real music."
