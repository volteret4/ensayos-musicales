## albums
**Nursery Cryme (1971)** : The album featured "Return of the Giant Hogweed," which was not the band's first album but demonstrated their ambitious, long-winded rock style from a time when they were building their following as an ornate and uncompromising live act. "That's the return of the Giant Hogweed. Let me repeat that, return of the Giant Hogweed."
**The Lamb Lies Down on Broadway (1974)** : This double LP concept album was Genesis's most ambitious work, a song cycle about a Latin American New York City character named Ryle, with its narrative, lyrics, and concept largely driven by Peter Gabriel. It was the band's first album to approach the top 40 in America and became their most acclaimed with critics, making it seem poised for a cultural breakthrough just before Peter Gabriel's departure. "The Lamb Lies Down on Broadway was Genesis first album to approach the top 40 in America, and it became their most acclaimed with critics."
**A Trick of the Tail (1976)** : This album was recorded after Peter Gabriel's departure, with the band initially having no permanent singer. Phil Collins, the drummer, sang on the tracks assuming his vocals would be replaced, but ultimately, these temporary vocals became permanent, leading to Collins becoming the frontman by default. "The group placed an open ad in the British Music Weekly melody maker, reportedly some 400 singers responded."
**Wind and Wuthering (1976)** : Released in late 1976, this album bridged the ornate progressive sound of the Peter Gabriel era with the more pop-friendly hooks introduced by Phil Collins. It helped break the band on radio, reaching the top 30 on the Billboard album chart in April 1977, marking Genesis's first album to achieve such high placings. This was also the last Genesis album to feature ace guitarist Steve Hackett. "Wind and Weathering reached the top 30 on the Billboard album chart, the first Genesis album to reach such lofty heights."
**And Then There Were Three (1978)** : Appropriately titled, this was Genesis's first album as a trio after Steve Hackett's departure, featuring Mike Rutherford, Tony Banks, and Phil Collins. Its lead single, "Follow You Follow Me," was even more romantic and catchy than "Your Own Special Way." "Follow You Follow Me was the lead single from Genesis first album as a trio, 1978's appropriately titled, and then there were three."
**Duke (1980)** : This album further elevated Genesis's chart profile and radio success, becoming their first number one album in the UK and peaking just outside the Billboard Top 10 in the US in July 1980. The album successfully balanced longer progressive rock tracks with more concise pop songs. "The album Duke continued to ratchet up the chart profile and radio success of Genesis."
**Abacab (1981)** : Released after Phil Collins's initial solo success, this album demonstrated that Collins's solo career provided "rocket fuel" for the band, becoming their second straight UK number one album and their first to break into the top 10 in America, matching the peak of Collins's solo album "Face Value" at number seven in November 1981. The album's title track highlighted the band's shift towards a poppier, synth-laden sound. "Abacab became the band's second straight UK number one album and their first to break into the top 10 in America, matching face values peak of number seven in November 1981."
**Genesis (1983)** : This self-titled LP, released in the fall of 1983, reflected the band's increasingly uncluttered and pop-friendly direction, moving away from their more elaborate earlier album titles. It became Genesis's fastest breaking album to date in the US, entering the top 10 within a month. "Released in the fall of 1983, the self-titled LP was in the US top 10 within a month, Genesis' fastest breaking album to date."

Its deeply romantic lyrics and melody would later become bitterly ironic to Phil Collins due to his personal upheaval. "At number 35, second week on the chart, Genesis, their first top 40 hit is Follow You Follow Me."

"Misunderstanding became Genesis' biggest US hit to date, peaking at number 14 in August of 1980."

The song exemplified the band's increasingly poppy and synth-laden sound, further blurring the sonic line between Genesis and Phil Collins's solo work. "The album's title track, Abacab, was named for the common verse chorus and bridge lettering technique of pop songwriting, A-B-A-C-A-B."
**No Reply at All (1981)** : Another significant single from the "Abacab" album, this track featured the Phoenix Horns from Earth Wind and Fire, whom Phil Collins had invited to join Genesis. It achieved a number two hit on Billboard's Rock Tracks chart and reached number 29 on the Hot 100, showcasing the band's evolving R&B influences. "Collins invited back the Phoenix horns from Earth Wind and Fire to join Genesis on No Reply at All, which gave Genesis a number two hit on Billboards Rock Tracks chart and a number 29 hit on the Hot 100."

It shot into the Top 5 in the UK and became a Top 5 rock hit in the US, though its unusual nature made it a bit strange for American Top 40 radio. "In the UK, the album's lead single, Shot Into The Top 5, Mama, a sinister blend of synthesized rock and, as we told you in Hit Parade's Death Jam's edition, a spooky laugh, which was inspired by Hip Hop's Grandmaster Flash and Millie Mel."
**That's All (1984)** : A wistful mid-tempo love song with multiple layers of "BDLS hooks," this track from the 1983 album "Genesis" was explicitly "built for radio ubiquity" and served as Genesis's most straightforward love song since "Misunderstanding" in 1980. It featured key contributions from Tony Banks (keyboard solo) and Mike Rutherford (guitar solo) but was a showcase for Phil Collins's open-hearted therapeutic lyrics, peaking at number 6 in February 1984 and becoming the band's first-ever Top 10 American hit, as well as the first such hit written by Collins. "When it peaked at number 6 in February 1984, it became not only Genesis' first ever top 10 American hit, it was also the first such hit written by Collins, his prior solo top 10 hit You Can't Hurry Love, having been a Motown cover."
**Invisible Touch (1986)** : This song topped the Billboard Hot 100 in July 1986, just one week before Peter Gabriel's "Sledgehammer" achieved the same feat, marking a unique period of head-to-head competition between former and current Genesis members at the top of the charts. "Just one week after his former bandmates found themselves on top with Invisible Touch."

## curiosities
**Formation and Early Years** : The band started in the mid-1960s, formed by teenage schoolmates Tony Banks, Mike Rutherford, and Peter Gabriel, along with two other attendees of England's Charterhouse School, originally under the name Garden Wall. They were renamed Genesis in 1967 by their manager, Jonathan King. "The band had been together since the mid 1960s, when teenage schoolmates Tony Banks, Mike Rutherford and Peter Gabriel, formed a band with two other attendees of England's Charterhouse School called Garden Wall."
**Initial Lack of Hit Singles** : Genesis spent nearly a full decade recording largely devoid of hit singles, partly because their early songs were often too sprawling and conceptual to be confined to one side of a 45 RPM record. "Maybe that was because Genesis started out with songs that were hard to confine on one side of a 45."
**Progressive Rock Pioneers** : The first version of Genesis, fronted by Peter Gabriel, was a leading group in the progressive or prog rock movement of the early 1970s, characterized by sprawling, quirky, and highly conceptual music, and was a major concert draw. They pushed the boundaries of prog rock further than virtually any band had to that time. "Fronted by Art Rocker Peter Gabriel, the first version of Genesis, sprawling, quirky, highly conceptual, was a leading group in the progressive or prog rock movement and a major early 70s concert draw."
**Peter Gabriel's Departure (1975)** : After Genesis issued their most ambitious and acclaimed album, The Lamb Lies Down on Broadway, Peter Gabriel stunned the rock press by announcing his departure in 1975. This move was widely believed by the media and the band itself to spell the end of Genesis, as Gabriel was seen as the focal point and driving force. "To this point, Gabriel was the focal point, and it was believed the driving force of the group."
**Phil Collins Becomes Frontman by Default** : Following Gabriel's departure, Genesis sought a new singer, even placing an open ad in British Music Weekly Melody Maker, to which some 400 singers reportedly responded. Phil Collins, a devoted drummer who had only occasionally sung lead, recorded temporary vocals for the new album, A Trick of the Tail, never expecting them to be permanent. Unsatisfied with the auditions, the band ultimately gave Collins the frontman position "essentially by default." "Unsatisifed with any of the singers auditioning for the group, Genesis wound up giving Collins the frontman position essentially by default."
**Strength from Solo Success** : Contrary to the usual trend of solo careers weakening a band, Phil Collins's monstrous and unexpected solo success, which included two US Top 10 albums in a single year (Face Value and Abacab in 1981), seemed to provide "rocket fuel" for Genesis, bringing the band to new commercial heights. Tony Banks, a bandmate, admitted that "Phil became so big, but I think the two careers helped each other." "Rather than taking away from Genesis, Collins' solo success seemed to provide rocket fuel."

## albums
**Peter Gabriel (1977) - Debut Album** : Gabriel's first solo album, released two years after leaving Genesis. True to his iconoclastic nature, he named this and his subsequent three LPs simply "Peter Gabriel." While the first track, "Moribund the Burgermeister," retained a 70s prog rock sound, the second track, "Solsbury Hill," clearly signaled his intention to move beyond his Genesis roots. "Peter Gabriel's debut album was called simply Peter Gabriel."
**Peter Gabriel (1978) - Second Eponymous Album** : This was Gabriel's second self-titled album, on which he continued to explore a range of new musical modes, attempting strutting rock on "Modern Love," power balladry on "Here Comes the Flood," and nodding to punk and post-punk with "DIY." He was seen as "alternative" before the New Wave movement had a name. "Over the course of his first two albums, again, both titled Peter Gabriel, the former Genesis frontman tried on a range of new musical modes."
**Peter Gabriel (1980) - "Melt"** : Gabriel's third consecutive self-titled album and his most acclaimed to date, featuring a distinctive black and white polaroid cover with smeared chemicals, leading fans to unofficially call it "Melt." The album included cutting-edge singles like "Games Without Frontiers" and featured his former bandmate Phil Collins on drums, contributing to pivotal tracks like "Biko" and "Intruder." "In the spring of 1980, Peter Gabriel issued his third straight Peter Gabriel album, and his most acclaimed to date."
**Security (1982) - Fourth Solo Album** : Gabriel initially intended to continue his tradition of eponymous album titles, but his US label, Geffen Records, insisted on a more promotable title, resulting in "Security." This album marked Gabriel's emergence as a music video star with a Top 40 hit, "Shock The Monkey," demonstrating his material becoming more commercially successful even without Phil Collins's drumming. "His US label, Geffen Records, finally pushed back and insisted the album have its own title, they called it security."

## songs
**Solsbury Hill (1977)** : The second track from his debut solo album, this song clearly articulated Gabriel's intention to leave Genesis far behind. With lyrics that could be seen as his personal "Why I Left Genesis," such as "I walked right out of the machinery," it was Gabriel's purest pop song to date, sounding more wistful and self-actualizing than spiteful. It peaked at number 13 in the UK and number 68 in the US, but has since become a radio staple, ranking as his second most played song on US terrestrial radio with over 400,000 spins since the 1990s. "But by track two, it was clear Gabriel had every intention of leaving Genesis far behind. Saul's Barry Hill was like a press release turned into poetry."
**Games Without Frontiers (1980)** : The cutting-edge lead single from his 1980 album (Melt), which became a Top 5 UK hit. It featured distinctive backing vocals by his friend Kate Bush, who sang the song's French refrain, "Zhu Song Frontier." "On the radio, Gabriel led off the album release with the cutting-edge single Games Without Frontiers, a top 5 UK hit that featured backing vocals by Gabriel's friend Kate Bush singing the song's French refrain, Zhu Song Frontier."
**Biko (1980)** : A powerful eulogy for South African anti-apartheid activist Stephen Biko, featured as the closing track on Gabriel's 1980 album (Melt). Phil Collins contributed significantly to the track, playing the heavy Brazilian drum known as the Suddho. "Biko remains a standard in both Gabriel's live shows and in protest music worldwide."
**Intruder (1980)** : The sinister opening track of Peter Gabriel's 1980 album (Melt), this song fundamentally changed the sound of rock and pop drumming. The heavy, unique drum sound, achieved accidentally by Phil Collins and producer Hugh Padgham using a heavily compressed talk-back microphone, became known as the Gated Drum or Gated Reverb. "In Trudor, the sinister song that opens 1980's Peter Gabriel album changed the sound of rock and pop drumming."
**Shock The Monkey (1982)** : One of the earliest hits on MTV, this single from the "Security" album (1982) featured a visually striking music video with two versions of Peter Gabriel, one a businessman transforming into madness and the other a ghost-like figure reminiscent of his Genesis persona. With its most danceable beat yet for a Gabriel single, it crossed over onto various Billboard charts in early 1983, hitting number one on Rock Tracks, and breaking into the Top 30 on both the Hot 100 and the Dance and Disco chart, peaking on the Hot 100 the same week Phil Collins had a Top 10 hit with "You Can't Hurry Love." "Shock The Monkey was one of the earliest hits on the then new music video channel MTV."
**Sledgehammer (1986)** : This song topped the Billboard Hot 100 on July 26, 1986, just one week after his former bandmates in Genesis reached number one with "Invisible Touch," marking a rare instance of former and current band members competing at the top of the charts. "Billboards Hot 100 was topped by Peter Gabriel's Sledgehammer."

## curiosities
**Flamboyant Early Stage Persona** : As the lead singer of early 70s Genesis, Peter Gabriel was known for his flamboyant stage antics and outrageous outfits. His conservative look involved a mask of white makeup and severely parted, hippy-length hair, which served as a "blank slate" for ever more elaborate costumes, including a Jesus-like crown of thorns, a rooster's cockscomb, a grotesque amoeba-like alien suit, and a giant flower head for "Suppers Ready." "Gabriel's most conservative onstage outfit was a mask of white makeup, his hippy-length hair severely parted like a mad scientist."
**Leaving Genesis** : In 1975, after Genesis released their most acclaimed album, "The Lamb Lies Down on Broadway," Peter Gabriel announced he was leaving the band, a move that stunned the rock press and was widely perceived as the end of the group, given his central role. His song "Solsbury Hill" later provided a poetic explanation for his departure, expressing a need for self-actualization. "Peter Gabriel announced he was leaving Genesis. As far as the media of 1975 was concerned, this spelled the end of Genesis, just as they were hitting their stride."
**Iconoclastic Album Titles** : Demonstrating his unique approach as a solo artist, Peter Gabriel named his first four studio LPs simply "Peter Gabriel," a self-titling convention common for debut albums but unusual to repeat four times. This practice was only broken when his US label, Geffen Records, insisted on a distinct title for his fourth album, which became "Security." "Eponymous debuts are common in rock, but Gabriel, ever the iconoclast, wound up naming his first four LPs Peter Gabriel."
**Early "Alternative" Artist** : Peter Gabriel was described as "alternative" before that musical movement had a name, experimenting with punk and post-punk sounds on his 1978 album "DIY" at a time when terms like New Wave were just emerging. His solo work, while more accessible than his Genesis output, remained restless and experimental. "At a time when terms like New Wave were only starting to come into being, Gabriel was alternative before that musical movement had a name."

## albums
**Face Value (1981)** : This album, which Collins "never realized he was making," was born from cathartic musical journal entries during his 1979 divorce. Implored by Ahmet Ertegun of Atlantic Records, Collins finished it with guest musicians like the Phoenix Horns of Earth, Wind and Fire. It topped the UK album chart in February 1981 and broke into the US top 10 by May, peaking at number seven, becoming the first US top 10 album by any current or former member of Genesis. "As for the album Collins never realized he was making Face Value top the UK album chart in February of 1981 and by May broke into the top 10 in America, peaking at number seven."
**Hello, I Must Be Going! (1982)** : Collins's second solo album, recorded soon after Genesis's "Abacab" began slipping down the charts, deepened his connection to classic R&B. Its lead single, a cover of The Supremes' "You Can't Hurry Love," became his biggest hit to date, reaching number one in the UK and number 10 in the US. The album also featured the track "I Don't Care Anymore," which reinvented the spooky sound of "In The Air Tonight." "Abacab had barely slipped down the charts when Collins began recording, hello, I must be going, his second solo album."

## curiosities
**Early Career as Child Actor and Drummer** : Phil Collins was already a busy former child actor turned drummer from Greater London when he was hired by Genesis in 1970, alongside Steve Hackett, transforming the band into a five-piece. "And an already busy former child actor turned drummer from Greater London named Philip Collins."
**Reluctance to Become Frontman** : A self-effacing drummer, Collins initially enjoyed his side-man status and by 1975 had already played behind luminaries like Brian Eno and John Cale. He even tried to convince Genesis to continue as an instrumental combo after Peter Gabriel's departure. He reluctantly took on the frontman role, perceiving the singer's job as "cheap, about looking good and wiggling your bum." "Self-effacing and a devoted drummer, Collins enjoyed his side-man status, and by 1975 he had already played behind such luminaries as Brian Eno and John Cale."
**Divorce and Creative Surge (1978-1979)** : In late 1978, Collins's wife, Andrea Berterelli, filed for divorce, citing his workaholism and long tours, and admitting to an affair. By 1979, living alone and brooding, Collins began experimenting with a prototype Roland CR-78 drum machine in his empty house. This period, initially for cathartic musical journal entries, led to a string of heartbreak songs that would later form the basis of his solo career. "Collins's workaholism and long months on the road had left her bereft caring alone for their children, and she even admitted to an affair."
**Invention of Gated Reverb (1980)** : While playing drums on Peter Gabriel's 1980 album "Melt," Collins and producer Hugh Padgham accidentally discovered the "Gated Drum" or "Gated Reverb" sound. A talk-back microphone, intended only for communication, was placed near Collins's drum kit, and the heavily compressed, feeding-back drum sound was clipped, creating a sound that was both "sharply contained and yet massive." Collins later called this "life-changing," as it became his ultimate signature drum sound. "The heavy sound Collins and his producer Hugh Pageham achieved on the track was a total serendipitous accident."
**Myths Around "In The Air Tonight"** : Numerous urban legends surround the song "In The Air Tonight," falsely claiming Collins witnessed a drowning or murder and later confronted the perpetrator at a concert. Collins clarified in his memoir, "Not Dead Yet," that the spiteful lyrics indirectly reflected his wounded feelings in the wake of his divorce. "No, in real life, Phil Collins never saw someone drowning. The lyrics are not a true story about him witnessing a murder and no, he did not call out the supposed perpetrator later at a concert, contrary to urban legend."
**Solo Career by Insistence** : Collins had no intention of releasing "In The Air Tonight" or any of the tracks he had been tinkering with at home. It was legendary record impresario Ahmet Ertegun, founder of Collins's US label Atlantic Records, who implored and virtually insisted that the music must be released as a solo album, even agreeing to provide resources and backing musicians. "It was only after he played the demos for legendary record in Pricario Ahmet Erdogan, founder of Collins' US label Atlantic Records, that Erdogan implored Collins virtually insisted that the music must come out as a solo album."
**Collaborator and Producer for Other Stars** : Collins became highly in demand as both a side musician and a producer due to his "magic touch" and the widespread influence of his gated reverb drum sound. He played on Robert Plant's 1982 solo debut, "Pictures at Eleven"; produced drums on Adam Ant's 1983 hits "Strip" and "Puss in Boots"; and produced the entirety of former ABBA singer Frida's 1982 album, "Something's Going On," which became the best-selling solo album by any former ABBA member. "Collins was suddenly in demand, as not only a side man, but a producer for other songs, from current pop stars to longtime rock legends."
**Competition and Strengthening Genesis** : Collins's solo career was not only thriving but also uniquely made his original band, Genesis, stronger, a phenomenon that puzzled even his bandmates. Tony Banks joked, "he was our friend, and we wanted him to be successful, just not that successful," but conceded that "Phil became so big, but I think the two careers helped each other." "Collins' bandmates had mixed feelings. Tony Banks would later joke in interviews, quote, he was our friend, and we wanted him to be successful, just not that successful."

## curiosities
**Joined Genesis in 1970** : An accomplished young, up-and-coming guitarist, Steve Hackett was hired by Genesis in 1970 along with Phil Collins, completing the five-piece lineup that would build the band's following as an ornate and uncompromising live act. # instrument - Gated Reverb

## curiosities
**Accidental Signature Drum Sound** : The Gated Reverb drum sound was accidentally discovered by Phil Collins and producer Hugh Padgham during the recording of Peter Gabriel's 1980 album. It originated from a talk-back microphone placed near Collins's drum kit, which, when heavily compressed, created a loud, reverberant sound that was then abruptly clipped, resulting in a sound that was both "sharply contained and yet massive." This innovation became Collins's ultimate signature drum sound, profoundly influencing his own material and infecting that of other pop stars. "It came to be called the Gated Drum or Gated Reverb. It was a loud, reverberant drum sound that was then clipped, making it sound both sharply contained and yet massive."
