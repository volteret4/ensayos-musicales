## albums
**Cracked Rear View (1994) - Debut Album, Top-Selling Band of 1995** : Hootie & the Blowfish's debut sold more than 12 million copies and made them the top-selling band of 1995, at a moment when alternative rock was turning toward jangly pop. It spent eight weeks at No. 1 on the album chart. By the time of the follow-up, the band had been performing its songs for half a decade, with some dating back to 1990. Atlantic would have been happy to keep releasing singles from it. "after selling more than 12 million copies of their debut album, Cracked Rear View, Hoody now had the awesome challenge of following it up."
**Fairweather Johnson (1996) - Second Album, Fast Start and Quick Fade** : Released just two months after the band's February 1996 Grammy wins, because the band insisted to the label and management that they wanted to put out their follow-up. It sold 411,000 copies in its first week, more than twice as much as any single week of Cracked Rear View's eight weeks at No. 1. It then peaked quickly, fell out of the album chart's top 10 by July, and was certified double platinum right away. It eventually, belatedly, went triple platinum. It is presented as a prime example of a follow-up album whose chart performance is a referendum on the previous bestseller: it opens big and then fails to match its slower-growing predecessor. The music kept the same mix of jangly rock and soulful, broadly socially conscious lyrics, and the marketplace appeared saturated with Hootie. "It seemed the marketplace was finally saturated with Hoody."
**Musical Chairs (1998) - Third Album, Broadening Sound** : The band's sound broadened on this album. Michelle Post brought banjo-inflected Americana, Only Lonely was cinematic balladry, and Desert Mountain Showdown was bluegrass. The album debuted and peaked at No. 4, sold a million copies to the most loyal fans, and was the last Hootie album to go platinum or chart that high. It pointed toward Darius Rucker's future direction. "Musical Chairs was pointing toward the future. If not of Hoody, then certainly Darius Rucker."
**Final Atlantic Studio Album (mid-2000s) - Exit from the Major Label** : In the mid-2000s Hootie delivered one last studio album to Atlantic Records. In exchange for a greatest hits compilation, they negotiated their release from the label. Tim Sommer had long since left Atlantic, and with no one guiding them through the corporate process, the label's interest in promoting or keeping them was near zero. "In just four albums, Hoody and the blowfish had gone from the top band on the charts to a casualty of the major label system."
**Looking for Lucky (2005) - First Nashville Recording** : Released on the longtime independent label Vanguard Records. Their regular producer Don Gehman had moved to Nashville, so the band joined him and recorded in the country music capital for the first time. They were not trying to make a country record, but on tracks like Autumn Jones the influence was hard to miss. "recorded their next album, 2005's Looking for Lucky, in the country music capital for the first time."
**Live Album (2006) - Failed to Chart** : This 2006 live album failed to chart at all. It was one of the factors that led to the band's 2008 hiatus. "a 2006 live album had failed to chart entirely."
**Imperfect Circle (2019) - Comeback Album on Capitol Nashville** : Recorded after the whole band signed to Capitol Records Nashville, the same label that issued Darius Rucker's solo country work. It contained more overtly country material than anything else in the band's career. It produced no big radio hits, but it reached No. 26 on the Billboard 200, their highest since Musical Chairs, and No. 3 on the country albums chart. "Hoody recorded more overtly country material than ever in their career."

## songs
**I Go Blind (1995) - Friends Soundtrack Hit** : A cover of an obscure Canadian alt-rock song that Hootie had performed in concert for years. It was left over from the Cracked Rear View sessions and given to NBC's Friends after the band declined to appear on the show. Chandler, Ross and Monica jam to it at the Hootie show in the episode. It appeared on the Friends soundtrack CD in fall 1995, which went platinum, and reached No. 13 on Billboard's Radio Songs chart in 1996. "their leftover tracks were becoming hits."
**Let Her Cry (1995) - Grammy Winner** : Won Best Pop Performance by a Duo or Group with Vocal at the February 1996 Grammy Awards. "And let her cry took Best Pop Vocal Performance by Aduo or Group."
**I'm Going Home - Grammy Telecast Deep Cut** : Performed live on the 1996 Grammy telecast even though it had never been released as a single or video. The band wrote it after Darius Rucker lost a close family member. "They wrote it after lead singer Darius Rucker lost a close member of his family."
**Old Man & Me (When I Get to Heaven) (1996) - Fairweather Johnson Lead Single** : A road-tested song from the band's early days that had already appeared on the independent Kootchypop EP. Radio stations picked it up immediately, and it reached the top 20 at pop radio within two weeks. It debuted on the Hot 100 at No. 28, the fastest opening for any Hootie single, but topped out at No. 13 by May. "it was already in the top 20 at pop radio."
**Tucker's Town (1996) - First Major Label Single to Miss the Top 20** : The second single from Fairweather Johnson. It is a story song about a man seeking refuge in the predominantly Black community of Tucker's Town, Bermuda. It barely reached the top 40, peaking at No. 38 in August 1996. "Hoody's first major label single to miss the top 20."
**I Will Wait (1998) - Musical Chairs Lead Single** : Promoted as an airplay-only track, it reached No. 18 on the Radio Songs chart. It blended rock structure, R&B-style vocals and a slightly twangier melody. "an interesting blend of rock bones, R&B style vocals, and the slightly twangier melody."
**Only Lonely (1998) - Adult Contemporary Hit** : A cinematic ballad from Musical Chairs that reached No. 25 on the adult contemporary chart. "Poised cinematic balladry on only lonely, a number 25 adult contemporary hit."
**Michelle Post (1998) - Banjo Americana** : A Musical Chairs track built on banjo-inflected Americana. "the band tried Banjo-inflected Americana on Michelle Post."
**Desert Mountain Showdown (1998) - Bluegrass Experiment** : A bluegrass track on Musical Chairs. "And even bluegrass on Desert Mountain Showdown."
**Autumn Jones (2005) - Country-Tinged Nashville Recording** : A Looking for Lucky track where the Nashville and country influence is clearly audible. "Even though they weren't trying to record a country record on tracks like Autumn Jones, the influence was hard to miss."
**Hold My Hand (1994) - Letterman Breakthrough and Farewell** : The song that made the band famous through their 1994 TV debut on David Letterman's stage. Darius Rucker's Southern drawl was already audible on it. The band reunited to perform it there again in 2015 when Letterman retired, 21 years later. University of South Carolina football fans still sing along to it. "the song that had catapulted them to fame in that same venue 21 years earlier."

## curiosities
**Friends Episode - "The One with Five Steaks and an Eggplant"** : In its second season, NBC's Friends, already one of the three biggest shows on TV, built much of this episode around three of the friends wanting to see Hootie in concert and going backstage. The band members declined to appear because they were too busy, so they gave the show the I Go Blind cover instead. "The band members politely declined to appear in this Friends episode."
**Best New Artist Grammy (February 1996)** : Darius Rucker, Mark Bryan, Dean Felber and Jim "Soni" Sonefeld won Best New Artist over Brandy, Alanis Morissette, Joan Osborne and Shania Twain. In one year they had gone from bar band to stadium band. "No one was surprised when they took home the Best New Artist Award."
**Restlessness at the Peak** : After half a decade of playing the Cracked Rear View songs, the band pushed Atlantic and their management to release new material instead of continuing to pull singles from the debut. "For a band seemingly at the top of their game, however, Hoody were restless."
**Backlash - "Death to Hootie"** : As multimillionaires, the band became a target for critics, cool kids and other rock stars, who saw them as the symbol of middle-of-the-road pablum. Trent Reznor of Nine Inch Nails declared "death to Hootie" in Rolling Stone, and his protégé Marilyn Manson said the band would make your life completely boring. "Hoody were the symbol of middle of the road Pablo."
**A Band Without a Country (Late 1990s)** : By the late 1990s pop fans had moved on, rock fans were getting their fix from other bands, and Hootie's music was still too rock-based for R&B or country audiences. "Hoody and the blowfish were a band, so to speak, without a country."
**Out of Step in the Nickelback Era** : In the early 2000s, the era of Nickelback, Incubus and System of a Down, Hootie were unlikely to place on rock charts, and they were no longer really trying to be rock stars. "the band weren't really trying to be rock stars anymore."
**2008 Hiatus** : With the members entering their 40s, the band went on an amicable hiatus in 2008. Years on the road had taken a toll, and drummer Soni Sonefeld told the band he no longer wanted to tour. Darius Rucker decided on his next move during that band meeting. "Drummer Sony Sonafeld in particular told the band he didn't want to tour anymore."
**Cracked Rear View 25th Anniversary Tour (2019)** : The tour hit 44 cities and drew more than 650,000 people, a bigger audience than in their 1990s heyday. The band said it was more lucrative than any previous tour, and not only because of higher ticket prices. Nostalgia combined with Rucker's country stardom made it a near sellout. "They played to more than 650,000 people, a larger crowd than in their chart topping 90s heyday."
**Back on a Major Label (2019)** : The whole band signed to Capitol Records Nashville in 2019. "Hoodie and the Blowfish were on a major label again."
**Country Before Its Time** : Genre boundaries were stricter in the mid-1990s. Much of Cracked Rear View could qualify for country radio today with minimal changes, although the band's music was always fundamentally pop, born from 1980s college rock and Rucker's tape collection. The biggest hits were inspired by R.E.M. and Bob Dylan. "Were cracked rearview released today, much of its material would with minimal tweaking qualify for country radio."
**Signed by Tim Sommer** : Former A&R man Tim Sommer signed Hootie & the Blowfish and helped them navigate the major label system. "Tim Summer about how he signed Hoodie and the Blowfish and helped them navigate the major label system."

## albums
**Back to Then (2002) - Shelved R&B Solo Debut** : Rucker started this contemporary R&B album in 1999 as a side project, in his spare time and with encouragement from a senior Atlantic executive. He turned it in around 2000, but Atlantic refused to commit its R&B promotion team to it, even though the label was having hits with Brandy, Aaliyah and Timbaland. Rucker told Tim Sommer the label said it had no hits, and when he pointed to Exodus and Wild One, someone replied "yes, they're hits, but not for you." It was an adult R&B record, closer to neo-soul like D'Angelo, Erykah Badu and Jill Scott than to New Jack Swing or Timbaland-style R&B. To make amends, Atlantic returned the rights. Jill Scott's neo-soul label Hidden Beach, distributed by Epic/Sony, released it in summer 2002, nearly three years after recording began, with a duet with Scott herself. It debuted and peaked at No. 127 on the Billboard 200, left the chart after two weeks, and none of its singles charted at Black radio or on the R&B chart. By then R&B had moved closer to hip-hop with Ja Rule, Ashanti and Nelly. "I made this little hippie R&B record and they didn't have any idea what to do with it."
**Learn to Live (2008) - Country Debut** : Produced within the Nashville system by Frank Rogers, who had worked with Brad Paisley, Trace Adkins and Josh Turner. Rucker co-wrote nearly every track with songwriters including Clay Mills, Chris DuBois and Rivers Rutherford, and with a then relatively unknown Chris Stapleton on the opener Forever Road. Singing in his natural South Carolina accent felt freeing to him. It debuted at No. 1 on Top Country Albums on the chart dated October 4, 2008. It produced four big country hits in a row: three No. 1s and a No. 3. It went platinum by the time it peaked in March 2010. "That same week in 2008 on the top country albums chart, Rucker's Learn to Live Album debuted at number one."

## songs
**Exodus and Wild One (c. 2000) - The Disputed "Hits"** : Two songs from Back to Then that Rucker cited when Atlantic said the album had no hits. "These songs are hits, Rucker continue. And someone at the label said yes, they're hits, but not for you."
**Forever Road (2008) - Chris Stapleton Co-Write** : The opening track of Learn to Live, co-written with a then relatively unknown Chris Stapleton. "on the album opener Forever Road, a still relatively unknown Chris Stapleton."
**Don't Think I Don't Think About It (2008) - First Country No. 1** : An aching mid-tempo story song with lyrics about whiskey, tail lights and dusty roads, released in spring 2008 as the lead single. It debuted on Hot Country Songs at No. 51 and climbed steadily for five months. In its 23rd week, on the chart dated October 4, 2008, it reached No. 1. It was the first country No. 1 by a Black solo artist since Charley Pride's Night Games in 1983. The same week, Learn to Live debuted at No. 1, a month before Barack Obama was elected the first Black president. "the first country number one by a black soloist since Charlie Pride's final chart topper Night Games in 1983, a full quarter century earlier."
**It Won't Be Like This for Long (2009) - Second Country No. 1** : This follow-up single also reached No. 1 in spring 2009, showing that his first country success was not a fluke. "The follow-up single, it won't be like this for long, also rose to number one, in the spring of 2009."
**Alright (2009) - Third Learn to Live No. 1** : A celebratory up-tempo single that also reached No. 1 on the country chart. "the celebratory up tempo all right also reached number one."
**History in My Making (2010) - Top 3 Country Hit** : The fourth single from Learn to Live, which reached No. 3. "And history in the making reached number three."
**Wagon Wheel (2013) - Crossover Smash** : The song began as a Bob Dylan fragment from 1973 and was completed by Ketch Secor of Old Crow Medicine Show. Rucker's 2013 version topped both Country Airplay and Hot Country Songs, which by then included digital data. It reached No. 15 on the Hot 100, his highest solo pop chart peak. It was certified for 3 million downloads by 2014, recertified for more than 10 million once streams counted, and was 11 times platinum as of 2022. "Rucker's Wagon Wheel was an across-the-board smash."

## curiosities
**Radney Foster Inspiration** : Rucker's country move was inspired by his love of Radney Foster, a Del Rio, Texas singer and 1990s country hitmaker. He had originally planned to make the record in his basement or a small South Carolina studio with friends. "Ever since I heard Radney Foster back in the day, Rucker told Tim Summer, I would say, I'm going to make a country record someday. It was like a mantra in my head."
**Signing to Capitol Nashville** : Hootie manager Doc McGhee heard Rucker talking about a country record and called Mike Dungan, president of Capitol Records Nashville, who agreed to sign him. Rucker said the risk was twofold. "it was the race thing and the pop thing."
**Atlantic Thwarting His Range** : Rucker accused Atlantic of wanting him to stay close to the Hootie sound instead of branching out stylistically. "My record label who had paid for the whole thing didn't want to put it out. They wouldn't even try."
**Radio-Only Chart Success** : At the time, Hot Country Songs was based entirely on country radio data, so Rucker's early peaks reflected heavy airplay, not purchases by old Hootie fans. Downloads and streams were added a few years later. "These were songs that country radio listeners across America were hearing in heavy rotation every day."
**Country's Top Tier** : In his early 40s he joined the top tier of country hitmakers alongside Kenny Chesney, Toby Keith and Rascal Flatts. In his first decade as a country singer he scored seven No. 1 country hits out of a dozen top 10s. "to score seven number one country hits out of a dozen top tens."
**Recording with Hootie Again (2003)** : After Back to Then, Rucker resumed recording with Hootie & the Blowfish in 2003, though the band's commercial prospects were fading. "By 2003, he had resumed recording with Hoody and the blowfish."

## songs
**Rock Me Mama / Wagon Wheel (1973) - Unfinished Fragment** : Dylan wrote only a fragment of the song in 1973 as a demo for his soundtrack to the film Pat Garrett and Billy the Kid. He left it unfinished, and it circulated widely as a bootleg called Rock Me Mama. When Ketch Secor tried to publish his completed version, Dylan agreed to a 50-50 publishing split. "Dylan agreed to a 50-50 publishing split, making Catch Seacord one of a handful of songwriters to receive co-writing credit with Bob Dylan."

# genre - Country

## curiosities
**Pop "Carpetbaggers"** : Artists who start in country, like Dolly Parton or Taylor Swift, generally stay welcome on the format. Pop acts moving to country get a colder reception. In the 2000s Jewel, Kid Rock and Bon Jovi all struggled, and a country duet version of a pop hit became a common crossover tactic. "so-called carpet baggers who go from pop to country are given generally a chillier greeting."
**Race and the Country Establishment** : The country establishment's acceptance of Black performers was poor. By 2008, no Black solo artist since Charley Pride had topped the country chart, not counting Ray Charles's duet with Willie Nelson. "The country establishments embrace of black performers was to say the least, not great."
**Slow Climbs** : Slow chart climbs are common in country, and even established superstars are expected to earn radio listeners' acceptance. "Slow climbs are commonplace on the country charts."
**Post-Eric Church Rock Guitars** : By 2019, country music with rock guitars had become so common after Eric Church that Hootie's country album did not sound drastically different from their earlier work. "in a post-Eric church world, country music with rock guitars was now so commonplace."

# curiosity

**Billboard Hot Country Songs Methodology** : Around 2008–2010 the chart used only country radio data. Downloads and streams were added a few years later, and by 2013 the chart included digital data. "Billboard's hot country songs chart was composed entirely of country radio data, not single sales."
**Wagon Wheel's RIAA Journey** : Streams were added to the RIAA's certification formula later in the 2010s, and Rucker's Wagon Wheel was recertified for more than 10 million units, reaching 11 times platinum by 2022. "As of 2022, Wagon Wheel was 11 times platinum."
