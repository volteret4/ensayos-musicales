## albums
**Cracked Rear View (1994) - Debut Album** : This debut album sold over 12 million copies, establishing Hootie & the Blowfish as the top-selling band of 1995. The band had been performing some of its songs for half a decade, with some material dating as far back as 1990. "after selling more than 12 million copies of their debut album, Cracked Rear View, Hoody now had the awesome challenge of following it up."
**Fair Weather Johnson (1996) - Second Album** : Released just two months after the 1996 Grammys, this album debuted with sales of 411,000 copies in its first week, which was more than twice as big as any sales week "Cracked Rear View" had pulled in any of its eight weeks at number 1. However, both the album and its lead single peaked quickly, with the album out of the top 10 by July. It was instantly certified double platinum but stalled, eventually, very belatedly, going triple platinum, significantly underperforming its 12-million-selling predecessor. "411,000 copies in its first week."
**Musical Chairs (1998) - Third Album** : This album debuted and peaked at number 4 on the album chart in 1998, selling a million copies to Hootie's most loyal fanbase. It showcased a broadening of the band's sound, incorporating banjo-inflected Americana on "Michelle Post," cinematic balladry on "Only Lonely," and bluegrass on "Desert Mountain Showdown." It would be the last Hootie & the Blowfish album to go platinum or chart that high, but its diverse stylistic leanings foreshadowed Darius Rucker's future musical directions. "Musical Chairs debuted and peaked at number 4 on the album chart in 98 and sold a million copies to the most loyal of Hoody's fan base."
**Looking for Lucky (2005) - Vanguard Records Release** : Recorded in Nashville for the first time with their regular producer Don Gehman (mis-transcribed as Don Gamer), this album was released on the independent imprint Vanguard Records after the band left Atlantic. Tracks like "Autumn Jones" demonstrated country influences, even though the band wasn't explicitly trying to record a country record. "recorded their next album, 2005's Looking for Lucky, in the country music capital for the first time."
**Live Album (2006) - Chart Failure** : Released in 2006, this live album failed to chart entirely, indicating the declining commercial prospects for Hootie & the Blowfish leading up to their hiatus. "a 2006 live album had failed to chart entirely."
**Imperfect Circle (2019) - Comeback Album** : Released after the band signed to Capitol Nashville, this comeback album featured more overtly country material than ever before in their career. It reached number 26 on the Billboard 200, making it their highest-charting album since "Musical Chairs," and climbed to number 3 on the country albums chart. "for their 2019 comeback album Imperfect Circle, Hoodie recorded more overtly country material than ever in their career."

## songs
**I Go Blind (1995) - Canadian Alt-Rock Cover** : This was a leftover track from the "Cracked Rear View" sessions, an obscure Canadian alt-rock song that Hootie & the Blowfish had been performing in concert for years. The band gave it to the NBC sitcom "Friends" to use in an episode in Fall 1995 when they declined to appear on the show. It appeared on the "Friends" soundtrack CD, which went platinum, and the song itself reached number 13 on Billboard's Radio Songs Chart in 1996. "they did give the show a leftover track from the cracked rearview sessions, a cover of an obscure Canadian alt-rock song called I Go Blind, that Hoody had been performing in concert for years."
**Let Her Cry (1996) - Grammy Award Winner** : This song earned Hootie & the Blowfish the Grammy Award for Best Pop Vocal Performance by a Duo or Group in February 1996. "And let her cry took Best Pop Vocal Performance by Aduo or Group."
**I'm Going Home (1996) - Grammy Telecast Deep Cut** : Hootie & the Blowfish performed this song live on the Grammy telecast in February 1996. It was a deep cut that had never been released as a single or video, written after lead singer Darius Rucker lost a close family member. "They wrote it after lead singer Darius Rucker lost a close member of his family."
**The Old Man and Me (When I Get to Heaven) (1996) - First Single from Fair Weather Johnson** : This road-tested track from Hootie's early days had previously appeared on the independent "Kootchypop EP." It was immediately embraced by radio stations, entering the pop radio top 20 within two weeks. When released as a single, it debuted on the Hot 100 at number 28, marking the fastest opening for a Hootie single ever, but it topped out at number 13 by May. "The old man and me was embraced by radio stations right away."
**Tuckers Town (1996) - Second Single from Fair Weather Johnson** : This was a story song about a man seeking refuge in Tuckers Town, Bermuda, a predominantly black community. Released in August 1996, it barely scraped the top 40 at number 38, becoming Hootie & the Blowfish's first major label single to miss the top 20. "Its second single, Tuckers Town, for example, was another story song about a man seeking refuge in the predominantly black community of Tuckers Town, Bermuda."
**I Will Wait (1998) - Lead Single from Musical Chairs** : This was the lead single from Hootie & the Blowfish's third album, "Musical Chairs." Promoted as an airplay-only track, it still managed to reach number 18 on the radio songs chart. The song featured an interesting blend of rock bones, R&B-style vocals, and a slightly twangier melody. "I will wait was the lead single of Hoody and the blowfish's third album, Musical Chairs, in 1998."
**Michelle Post (1998)** : A track from the "Musical Chairs" album that showcased the band's banjo-inflected Americana sound. "Elsewhere on the Musical Chairs album, the band tried Banjo-inflected Americana on Michelle Post."
**Only Lonely (1998)** : A track from the "Musical Chairs" album, described as poised cinematic balladry, which became a number 25 adult contemporary hit. "Poised cinematic balladry on only lonely, a number 25 adult contemporary hit."
**Desert Mountain Showdown (1998)** : A track from the "Musical Chairs" album that featured a bluegrass sound, demonstrating the band's broadening scope. "And even bluegrass on Desert Mountain Showdown."
**Hold My Hand (1994) - Breakthrough Single** : This song catapulted Hootie & the Blowfish to fame. Darius Rucker's natural Southern drawl was apparent on this track from the start, and the band performed it on David Letterman's soundstage in 2015 for his retirement, 21 years after their television debut in the same venue. "Hold My Hand, the song that had catapulted them to fame in that same venue 21 years earlier."

## curiosities
**Top Selling Band of 1995** : Hootie & the Blowfish arrived as the top-selling band in 1995, fueled by their lead singer Darius Rucker's potent baritone, at a time when alternative rock was evolving into jangly pop. "His potent baritone was key to the band's sound, and their arrival as the top selling band of 1995."
**Friends TV Show Episode (1995)** : The NBC sitcom Friends, one of the three biggest shows on TV in its second season, devoted large stretches of the plot of "The One With Five Stakes And An Eggplant" to three of the friends (Chandler, Ross, Monica) yearning to see Hootie & the Blowfish in concert and their backstage experience. The band members politely declined to appear due to busy schedules but provided a leftover track, "I Go Blind." "In its second season, NBC's Friends, already one of the three biggest shows on TV, devoted large stretches of the plot of the one with five stakes and an eggplant, to three of the friends yearning to see Hoody in concert, and what happened when they went backstage."
**Grammy Award Wins (1996)** : At the Grammy Awards in February 1996, Hootie & the Blowfish won the Best New Artist Award, beating Brandy, Alanis Morissette, Joan Osborne, and Shania Twain. Their song "Let Her Cry" also took home the award for Best Pop Vocal Performance by a Duo or Group. "No one was surprised when they took home the Best New Artist Award."
**Rapid Ascent to Stardom** : Within just one year, from 1995 to 1996, Hootie & the Blowfish rapidly transformed from a bar band into a stadium band, indicative of their massive success following "Cracked Rear View." "and just one year they've gone from bar band to stadium band."
**Band Restlessness and Label Conflict (1996)** : Despite being at the top of their game, the band was restless, having performed songs from "Cracked Rear View" for half a decade (some dating back to 1990). They insisted to Atlantic Records and management on finishing their follow-up album, while Atlantic would have preferred to continue releasing singles from "Cracked Rear View." "They had been performing the songs from Cracked Rear View for half a decade."
**Market Saturation and Backlash (1996)** : After the massive success of "Cracked Rear View," the marketplace seemed saturated with Hootie & the Blowfish's sound, leading to quick peaks for their subsequent album "Fair Weather Johnson" and its singles. Critics, "cool kids," and other rock stars like Trent Reznor and Marilyn Manson began to brand the band as "middle of the road pabulum." "It seemed the marketplace was finally saturated with Hoody."
**Genre Challenges in the Late 90s** : By the late 1990s, Hootie & the Blowfish found themselves without a clear audience, as pop and rock fans moved on to other bands. Their music was considered too rock-based to appeal to R&B or country audiences, despite the band broadening its sound. "Pop fans had moved on."
**Departure from Atlantic Records (Mid-2000s)** : In the mid-2000s, Hootie & the Blowfish delivered one final studio album to Atlantic Records and, in exchange for a greatest hits compilation, negotiated their release from the label. This occurred after Tim Summer, who had signed them, had left Atlantic, leading to near-zero label interest in promoting or retaining them. "Hoody and the blowfish had gone from the top band on the charts to a casualty of the major label system."
**Recording in Nashville (2005)** : For their 2005 album "Looking for Lucky," Hootie & the Blowfish recorded in Nashville, the country music capital, for the first time. This decision was influenced by their regular producer, Don Gehman (transcribed as Don Gamer), having relocated there. "Interestingly, their regular producer, Don Gamer, had relocated to Nashville, so the band joined him and recorded their next album, 2005's Looking for Lucky, in the country music capital for the first time."
**Amicable Hiatus (2008)** : In 2008, with band members entering their 40s, Hootie & the Blowfish went on an amicable hiatus. Years of touring had taken a toll, and drummer Jim "Sony" Sonofeld specifically expressed his desire not to tour anymore. "Finally, in 2008, with the band members entering their 40s, Hoody and the blowfish went on an amicable hiatus."
**David Letterman Reunion Performance (2015)** : The band reconvened in 2015 for a final performance of "Hold My Hand" on David Letterman's soundstage, commemorating his retirement from late-night television. This was significant as they had made their television debut in the same venue 21 years earlier. "When David Letterman announced his retirement from late-night television in 2015, the band reconvened for a final performance on Dave's sound stage of Hold My Hand, the song that had catapulted them to fame in that same venue 21 years earlier."
**25th Anniversary Tour Success (2019)** : In 2019, Hootie & the Blowfish embarked on a 44-city tour to commemorate the 25th anniversary of "Cracked Rear View." The tour was more lucrative than any they had played before, drawing over 650,000 people—a larger crowd than in their 90s heyday, attributed to nostalgia and Darius Rucker's country stardom. "The tour, which hit 44 cities in 2019, was, according to the band, more lucrative than any they had played before and not just because of higher ticket prices."
**Re-signing with a Major Label (2019)** : In 2019, the entire band signed to Capitol Nashville, becoming a major label act again. This was the same label that had issued Darius Rucker's successful solo country work. "That same year, the whole band signed to the capital Nashville label."
**Early Music Qualifies as Modern Country** : The genre boundaries of the mid-90s were more rigid, but if "Cracked Rear View" were released today, much of its material would, with minimal tweaking, qualify for country radio. "Were cracked rearview released today, much of its material would with minimal tweaking qualify for country radio."
**Influences and Broad Appeal** : Hootie & the Blowfish's music was fundamentally pop, rooted in 80s college rock and Darius Rucker's diverse musical influences. Their biggest hits were inspired by "hipsters" like R.E.M. and Bob Dylan, but the end product was not "hip," contributing to its broad appeal as "music for everyone." "Hoodies and Ruckers biggest hits were inspired by hipsters like REM and Bob Dylan, but the end product isn't and never was hip."

## albums
**Back to Then (2002) - Solo R&B Album** : Darius Rucker began recording this contemporary R&B album in 1999. Initially rejected by Atlantic Records, who considered it to have "no hits" and not fitting their R&B promotion strategy, Rucker eventually released it in Summer 2002 on Hidden Beach, Jill Scott's neo-soul imprint, distributed by Epic Records. The album debuted and peaked at number 127 on the Billboard 200 and was off the chart in two weeks, with none of its singles charting on Black Radio or the R&B chart. It featured a duet with Jill Scott and is described as a "charming anomaly" in his catalog, a "little hippie R&B record" that struggled due to its genre ambiguity and changing R&B market trends. "Back to then, debuted and peaked on the Billboard 200 at number 127 and it was off the album chart in just two weeks."
**Learn to Live (2008) - Country Debut Album** : Produced by Frank Rogers, this album marked Rucker's official entry into country music and was his debut on Capitol Nashville. Rucker co-wrote nearly every track, collaborating with songwriters like Clay Mills, Chris DuBois (transcribed as DuWa), Rivers Rutherford, and an "still relatively unknown" Chris Stapleton (on "Forever Road"). It debuted at number one on the Top Country Albums chart on October 4th, 2008, and went platinum by March 2010. The album generated four massive country hits, including three consecutive number ones: "Don't Think I Don't Think About It," "It Won't Be Like This For Long," and "Alright," plus a number three hit, "History in the Making." "Rucker worked solidly within the Nashville system, producing his country debut album Learn to Live with established producer Frank Rogers, who'd worked with country hit makers like Brad Paisley, Trace Adkins, and Josh Turner."

**Don't Think I Don't Think About It (2008) - Debut Country Single** : The lead single from "Learn to Live," this mid-tempo story song featured lyrics about "whiskey, tail lights, and dusty roads." Released in spring 2008, it debuted at number 51 on Hot Country Songs and steadily climbed over five months, reaching number one on October 4th, 2008. It marked a historical achievement as the first country number one by a Black soloist since Charley Pride in 1983. "Released in the spring of 2008, Don't Think I Don't Think About It debuted on Hot Country Songs at number 51 and began a steady march upward for the next five months."
**It Won't Be Like This For Long (2009)** : The second single from the "Learn to Live" album, this song also rose to number one on the country charts in the spring of 2009, demonstrating Darius Rucker's sustained success in the country genre. "The follow-up single, it won't be like this for long, also rose to number one, in the spring of 2009."
**Alright (2009)** : A celebratory up-tempo track from "Learn to Live" that reached number one on the country charts, making it one of four massive hits from the album. "the celebratory up tempo all right also reached number one."
**History in the Making (2010)** : A single from "Learn to Live" that reached number three on the country charts, peaking in March 2010. "And history in the making reached number three."
**Wagon Wheel (2013) - Cover Song** : This 2013 number one hit for Darius Rucker has an interesting backstory: Bob Dylan wrote an unfinished fragment called "Rock Me Mama" in 1973; Ketch Secor of Old Crow Medicine Show added new verses, gave it a two-step tempo, and released it as "Wagon Wheel" in 2004, sharing 50-50 publishing with Dylan. Rucker's version was an "across-the-board smash," topping Billboard's Country Airplay Chart and Hot Country Songs (which by then included digital data), and reaching number 15 on the Hot 100, his highest solo pop chart position. It was certified for 3 million downloads by 2014 and 11 times platinum (sales and streams) by 2022. "The Rucker chart topper that pop crossover fans are likely to know was his 2013 number one, which has an interesting backstory."

## curiosities
**Solo Album Attempt with Atlantic (1999-2000)** : In 1999, Darius Rucker began recording a solo contemporary R&B album, "Back to Then," with the encouragement of an Atlantic senior executive. However, when he turned in the finished album around 2000, Atlantic balked, refusing to commit their R&B promotional team to an R&B singer they perceived as not having "hits" in that genre, leading Rucker to accuse them of wanting him to "stick closer to the Hootie lane." "In 1999, in his spare time and with the encouragement of a senior executive at Atlantic, Darius Rupper began recording a solo album as a side project."
**"Neither Fish Nor Foul" R&B Record (2000-2002)** : Darius Rucker's R&B album "Back to Then" was described as "neither fish nor foul" and not fitting neatly into the R&B marketplace of the time, being neither New Jack Swing/Timbaland style nor strictly neo-soul like D'Angelo. This lack of categorizability, combined with label "shenanigans," foiled the project. "Darius had delivered a neither fish nor foul record."
**Release on Jill Scott's Label (2002)** : To make amends after refusing to release his R&B album, Atlantic Records returned the rights to Darius Rucker, who then released "Back to Then" in Summer 2002 on Hidden Beach, Jill Scott's neo-soul imprint distributed by Epic Records (a Sony family label and rival to Atlantic). "Atlantic gave him back the rights to his debut solo LP and Rucker wound up releasing it on Jill Scott's label."
**Inspiration for Country Music Shift (2008)** : Darius Rucker was inspired to make a country record by his fandom for 90s country hitmaker Radney Foster, stating it was "like a mantra" in his head. His initial plan was to record it in a small studio in South Carolina with friends for fun, but his manager, Doc McGee, had bigger plans. "Ever since I heard Radney Foster back in the day, Rucker told Tim Summer, I would say, I'm going to make a country record someday."
**Doc McGee's Role in Country Career** : Doc McGee, Hootie's manager, upon hearing Rucker's desire to make a country record, called Mike Dungan, president of Capitol Records Nashville, who surprisingly agreed to sign Rucker to a country recording contract. This was a pivotal moment in launching Rucker's successful country career. "Doc McGee, Hoody's manager, had other ideas."
**Overcoming "Pop Thing" and "Race Thing" in Country** : Darius Rucker's move to country music was considered risky for two reasons: the "pop thing" (pop acts traditionally receiving a chilly greeting in country) and the "race thing" (the historical lack of embrace for Black performers in country music). His subsequent chart-topping success defied these entrenched biases. "As Darius told Tim Summer, quote, it was the race thing and the pop thing."
**Singing in Natural Accent** : One of the most freeing aspects for Darius Rucker in his country career was singing fully in his natural South Carolina accent, which had always been present in his Hootie & the Blowfish material, notably on "Hold My Hand," but was given full voice on his country album. "What was most freeing for Darius Rucker was singing in his natural South Carolina accent."
**Historical Country Chart Double Triumph (2008)** : On October 4th, 2008, Darius Rucker achieved a double triumph on the Billboard charts: his single "Don't Think I Don't Think About It" rose to number one on Hot Country Songs, becoming the first country number one by a Black soloist since Charley Pride in 1983 (a 25-year gap). In the same week, his album "Learn to Live" debuted at number one on the Top Country Albums chart. This occurred just one month before Barack Obama was elected as the first Black president in American history, adding to its cultural significance. "On the Billboard charts dated October 4th, 2008, Darius Rucker pulled off a double triumph."
**Sustained Country Success** : Darius Rucker's country chart conquest was not a fluke; his debut album "Learn to Live" generated four massive country hits, including three consecutive number ones. Over his first decade as an official country singer, he scored seven number one country hits out of a dozen top tens, establishing himself among the top tier of country hitmakers alongside Kenny Chesney, Toby Keith, and Rascal Flatts. "Anyone who expected Rucker's country chart conquest to be a fluke were disabused of that notion right quick."
**Broad Appeal and Genre Blending** : Darius Rucker's career, particularly through a song like "Wagon Wheel," is seen as a metaphor for his ability to supersede genre. His music, originally based in folk and rock, transformed into Americana, appealing to both country and pop listeners, mirroring his own multi-genre identity. "It is of many genres and superseeds genre, like Rucker himself."
