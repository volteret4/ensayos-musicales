# artist - Curtis Mayfield

## member of
The Impressions

## genres
R&B
Soul
Funk
Psychedelic Soul

## labels
Curtom

## albums
**Superfly (1972) - Soundtrack LP** : This album, from the Gordon Parks Jr. movie of the same name, was a watershed for Mayfield and for popular Black music in the 70s. It debuted on the album chart in late August 1972, a few weeks after the movie arrived, and took eight weeks to reach number one on Billboard's top LP's chart, riding the chart for nearly a year. It was exceptional for a soundtrack, featuring mostly vocal tracks that stood apart from the film, telling a story all its own. The album was more hit-packed than any funk album before it. "Superfly was an amalgam of funk and soul, rhythm and melody, story and groove."
**Back to the World (Album Name Mentioned) - Number One Soul Chart Album** : This album produced the R&B chart hit "Future Shock," and reached number one on the Soul Chart.

## songs
**Don't Worry If There's a Hell Below, We're All Going to Go (1971)** : This early 1971 hit found Mayfield adapting his socially conscious sound for the era of P-Funk and Sly Stone, reaching number three on the R&B chart and number 29 on the Hot 100. It laid the groundwork for the soundtrack he would write and record a year later for "Superfly." "It laid the groundwork for the soundtrack he would write and record a year later, not long after Isaac Hayes topped the charts with Shaft."
**Freddy's Dead (1972) - Superfly Lead Single** : This lead single from the *Superfly* soundtrack broke into the top 10 on Billboard's Hot 100, reaching its number 2 peak on the Soul Singles chart the week ending October 28th, 1972. It also reached number four on the Hot 100. "Freddy's Dead. The lead single from Curtis Mayfield's Superfly soundtrack broke into the top 10 on Billboard's Hot 100 and reached its number 2 peak on the Soul Singles chart."
**Pusherman (1972) - Superfly Album Track** : This track set the tone for the *Superfly* album. "From Pusher Man, which set the tone for the album to the lead single, Freddy's Dead."
**Superfly (1972) - Title Track** : The title track from the *Superfly* soundtrack reached number eight on the Hot 100. "the title track reaching number eight."
**Future Shock (Year Not Specified) - R&B Chart Hit** : This song was a hit on the R&B chart, taken from the number one Soul Chart album *Back to the World*. "scoring hits on the R&B chart, like Future Shock from the number one Soul Chart album Back to the World."
**Kung Fu (Year Not Specified) - Trend-Hopping Hit** : This song was a trend-hopping Top 40 Pop hit and reached number three on the R&B chart. "And the trend-hopping Top 40 Pop number three R&B hit Kung Fu."

## curiosities
**Musical Shift from 60s to 70s** : Mayfield made a fairly radical musical shift from the 60s into the 70s, similar to James Brown, Sly Stone, and George Clinton. "Mayfield, like James Brown, Sly Stone, and George Clinton, had made a fairly radical musical shift from the 60s into the 70s."
**60s Blackpop Icon and Budding Mogul** : Already an iconic voice in 60s Blackpop, Mayfield became a budding mogul by the end of the decade, founding his own Curtom record label. "Already an iconic voice in 60s Blackpop, by the end of the decade, Curtis Mayfield became a budding mogul, founding his own Curtin label."
**Pivotal Role in Blaxploitation Soundtracks** : A year after Isaac Hayes's *Shaft* soundtrack topped the charts, Mayfield affirmed the commercial viability of Blaxploitation soundtracks with his own smash LP, *Superfly*. He helped open the floodgates to a new wave of deep funk. "Mayfield had helped open the floodgates to a new wave of deep funk."

# artist - James Brown

## genres
Soul
Funk
R&B

## songs
**Papa's Got a Brand New Bag (1965)** : This song was James Brown's first top 10 pop hit, reaching number 10 in the summer of 1965. It is considered a dividing line in soul music rhythm, effectively introducing "the one" concept and becoming the bedrock of funk and other rhythmic music. Critic Dave Marsh stated no record before it sounded like it, and none since have been unmarked by it. "Papa's got a brand new back. James Brown's first top 10 pop hit and the dividing line between how rhythm was understood in soul music before and after."
**Cold Sweat (1967)** : This 1967 hit reached number seven and demonstrated how James Brown had distilled "the one" concept down to its essence, refining the rhythmic approach introduced in "Papa's Got a Brand New Bag." "Brown refined this approach on later tracks on his 1967 number seven hit Cold Sweat, for example."
**Sex Machine (1970)** : This seminal 1970 single was a number two R&B and number 15 pop hit. Its groove is described as the blueprint for much of 70s funk, showcasing Brown's highly syncopated approach where his band functioned as a pure funk rhythm section. "Listen to his seminal 1970 single Sex Machine, a number two R&B number 15 pop hit. This groove is the blueprint for much of 70s funk."
**The Payback (1974/1975) - Rejected Soundtrack Anthem** : This blacksploitation-style anthem was originally intended for the soundtrack of the film *Hell Up in Harlem*. When the filmmakers rejected it, Brown released it himself as a "shivery funk murder ballad." It returned him to number one on the R&B chart, cracked the pop top 30, and was his first gold single in two years. "When the filmmakers rejected the song, Brown released it himself as a kind of shivery funk murder ballad."

## curiosities
**"Soul Brother No.1" and "Hardest Working Man in Show Business"** : James Brown is known by these self-proclaimed titles. "The man known as Soul Brother No.1. The self-proclaimed hardest working man in show business, James Brown."
**"Godfather of Soul"** : This title is used to refer to James Brown. "Godfather of Soul James Brown recorded his own black exploitation style anthem, The Payback."
**Codifier of "The One" Concept** : James Brown codified the concept of "the one," which involves placing rhythmic emphasis on the first beat in a measure, in contrast to the second and fourth beats typical in rock and traditional pop. "James Brown, who codified the very concept of the one."
**Pure Funk Rhythm Section** : By the dawn of the 70s, Brown had evolved his highly syncopated approach to the point where his band essentially operated as a pure funk rhythm section. "By the dawn of the 70s, Brown had boiled down this highly syncopated approach to the point where his band was essentially a pure funk rhythm section."

**70s Dominance and Versatility** : The 1970s was "positively awash in funk in all its forms," including Latin fusion funk, romantic Budwara funk, and strutting funk. It was commercially viable with a massive crossover audience, defining what pop sounded like and dominating the charts as reliably as rock and disco. "The 70s was positively awash in funk in all of its forms."
**Consumption on LPs** : Funk in the 70s was perhaps best appreciated on long playing vinyl, where its grooves seemed to stretch into outer space. Many acts scored number one or platinum LPs. "funk in the 70s was perhaps best appreciated on long playing vinyl, where its grooves seemed to stretch into outer space."
**Evolution and Adaptation** : As funk competed with rock and disco in the 70s, it adapted, with bands incorporating smoother grooves and some funk craftsmen transitioning into 80s pop songsmiths. This evolution laid the foundation for entirely new genres. "As funk in the 70s competed with not only rock, but disco, it found itself having to adapt to keep up with the relentless demand for dance rhythms."
**"The One" as Bedrock** : James Brown's innovation of "the one" – emphasizing the first beat in a measure – became the bedrock of funk and other forms of rhythmic music. **Pop Music Status** : In its day, funk was not just a blueprint for later music or for DJs and producers, but was mainstream pop music, dominating the charts in the 1970s. "In its day, funk was pop music. Dominating the charts, nearly as reliably that decade as Rock and Disco."

# curiosity

**The One (Rhythmic Concept)** : This rhythmic innovation, codified by James Brown, involves placing the primary rhythmic emphasis on the first beat in a measure, as opposed to the more common second and fourth beats found in rock and traditional pop songs. It became a foundational element for funk and other rhythmic music forms. "Brown's innovation was to lead his band toward what he called the one. That is placing the rhythmic emphasis on the first beat in a measure rather than the second and fourth beats, which is more typical in Rock and other traditional pop songs."
**Dave Marsh's "The Heart of Rock and Soul"** : Music critic Dave Marsh, in his book *The Heart of Rock and Soul*, described James Brown's "Papa's Got a Brand New Bag" as a groundbreaking record that no prior record sounded like, and no subsequent record has been unmarked by its influence. "In his book The Heart of Rock and Soul, critic Dave Marsh writes quote, no record before Papa's got a brand new bag sounded anything like it. No record since has been unmarked by it. Unquote."
**Funk Influence on 2022 Charts** : Even decades past the G funk era, the charts of 2022 continue to reflect the world that funk created, as evidenced by contemporary chart-topping singles like Silk Sonic's "Love's Train" being a remake of an older funk hit. "even the charts of 2022 are living in a world funk created."
**Quentin Tarantino's "Pulp Fiction" and Funk Revival** : Quentin Tarantino's film *Pulp Fiction* revived Kool & the Gang's 1973 hit "Jungle Boogie" decades after its initial release, demonstrating the enduring cultural impact of funk music. "The number four hit Jungle Boogie, which decades later would be revived in Quentin Tarantino's film Pulp Fiction."
