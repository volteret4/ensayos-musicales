## albums
**Superfly (1972) - Soundtrack to Gordon Parks Jr.'s Film** : Mayfield's soundtrack for Gordon Parks Jr.'s Blaxploitation film *Superfly*, starring Ron O'Neal, was the number one album in America 50 years before this October 2022 episode. It debuted on the album chart in late August 1972, a few weeks after the movie came out. It took eight weeks to reach number one on Billboard's Top LPs chart and stayed on the chart for nearly a year. During the week ending October 28, 1972, it settled into a month-long run at number one. Isaac Hayes's *Shaft* album had been mostly film score with only a couple of vocal tracks, but *Superfly* was a funk album made up mostly of vocal tracks that stood apart from the film and told a story of its own. It opens with "Pusherman," which set the tone. It was more hit-packed than any funk album before it. "Freddie's Dead" reached number four on the Hot 100 and the title track reached number eight. The movie was the highest-grossing Blaxploitation film to that date, but the album developed a life of its own. It confirmed that R&B and soul had been transformed by funk, that funk could sell to a huge crossover audience, and that Blaxploitation soundtracks were commercially viable after Hayes's *Shaft* had topped the album chart for only one week. "Superfly was an amalgam of funk and soul, rhythm and melody, story and groove."

**Back to the World - Number One Soul Album** : This later Mayfield album reached number one on the Soul chart and produced the R&B hit "Future Shock." "Future Shock from the number one Soul Chart album Back to the World."

## curiosities
**From Impressions Leader to Solo Funk Mogul** : In the 1960s Mayfield was a member of The Impressions, which began as a gospel-inflected doo-wop group showcasing Jerry Butler's vocals. When Butler left, Mayfield became the group's de facto leader and main songwriter. By the end of the decade he had become a budding mogul and founded his own Curtom label. He first used it to release his final albums with The Impressions, then moved into a solo career with a much funkier sound. "By the end of the decade, Curtis Mayfield became a budding mogul, founding his own Curtin label."

**A Radical Shift from the 60s to the 70s** : Like James Brown, Sly Stone and George Clinton, Mayfield made a fairly radical musical shift from the 1960s into the 1970s. He was the soul legend who established that funk would fuse with psychedelic soul and shape the decade. "Mayfield had helped open the floodgates to a new wave of deep funk."

**Curtom Signed Lakeside** : Lakeside, the Dayton, Ohio funk group whose 1980 "Fantastic Voyage" was the basis for Coolio's 1994 hit, first signed with Mayfield's Curtom label in the early 70s. "So many roads lead back to Curtis Mayfield."

# artist - James Brown

## genres
Funk
Soul
R&B

## songs
**Papa's Got a Brand New Bag (1965)** : This was James Brown's first top 10 pop hit, peaking at number 10 in summer 1965. It marks the dividing line between how rhythm was understood in soul music before and after it. It effectively introduced Brown's concept of "the one" and became the bedrock of funk and other rhythmic music. In his book *The Heart of Rock and Soul*, critic Dave Marsh wrote: "no record before Papa's got a brand new bag sounded anything like it. No record since has been unmarked by it."

**Cold Sweat (1967)** : A number seven hit on which Brown refined his "one" approach. On it Brown distills "the one" to its essence. "You can really hear how James has distilled the one down to its essence."

**Sex Machine (1970)** : A seminal single that reached number two R&B and number 15 pop. By the dawn of the 70s, Brown had boiled his highly syncopated approach down so far that his band was essentially a pure funk rhythm section. This groove is the blueprint for much of 70s funk. "This groove is the blueprint for much of 70s funk."

**The Payback (1973-74)** : Brown's own Blaxploitation-style anthem, originally intended for the soundtrack of *Hell Up in Harlem*. When the filmmakers rejected it, Brown released it himself as a kind of shivery funk murder ballad. It returned him to number one on the R&B chart, cracked the pop top 30, and was his first gold single in two years. "When the filmmakers rejected the song, Brown released it himself as a kind of shivery funk murder ballad."

## curiosities
**Inventor of "The One"** : Known as Soul Brother No. 1, the Godfather of Soul and the self-proclaimed hardest working man in show business, Brown codified the concept of "the one." This meant placing the rhythmic emphasis on the first beat of a measure rather than on the second and fourth beats, which is more typical of rock and traditional pop. "Brown's innovation was to lead his band toward what he called the one."

**Sampled by Hip-Hop** : Old James Brown records later became the building blocks of rap and pop songs. "Two pop songs that are built out of old James Brown records."

## curiosities
**Origins and Nicknames** : Clinton was born in North Carolina and raised in New Jersey. He worked in parallel with James Brown's funk revolution as another innovator who "seemed to hear things differently." He was later known as the Prime Minister of Funk, Uncle Jam and Dr. Funkenstein. "Clinton and his funky adventures couldn't be contained within just one band."

**Two Bands Born from a Trademark Lawsuit** : Clinton formed Funkadelic while his original band, The Parliaments, was fighting a trademark lawsuit over its name. After winning the suit, he renamed them Parliament, and they also moved in a much funkier direction. Both bands recorded for more than a decade, with Clinton switching names and sets of players from album to album. The lines blurred enough that fans called his output Parliament-Funkadelic, or simply P-Funk. "Most fans referred to Clinton's exploits as Parliament Funkadelic or simply P funk."

# genre - Funk

## curiosities
**Funk Was Pop Music in the 70s** : Funk dominated the 70s charts nearly as reliably as rock and disco, and it was the lingua franca of the decade for both dancing and extended listening. Its forms included War's Latin fusion funk, Marvin Gaye's romantic boudoir funk, the Ohio Players' strutting funk, and P-Funk. All of these acts scored number one or platinum LPs. "In its day, funk was pop music."

**Best Appreciated on Long-Playing Vinyl** : 70s funk acts sold truckloads of albums, and the music was arguably best appreciated on LP. "Funk in the 70s was perhaps best appreciated on long playing vinyl, where its grooves seemed to stretch into outer space."

**Adapting to Disco** : Competing with rock and disco, funk adapted to the relentless demand for dance rhythms. Once-pure funk bands adopted smoother grooves, and some 70s funk craftsmen turned into 80s pop songsmiths. "As funk in the 70s competed with not only rock, but disco, it found itself having to adapt to keep up with the relentless demand for dance rhythms."

**Foundation for Hip-Hop** : Funk laid the foundation for hip-hop and G-funk. Examples include a De La Soul hit sampling Funkadelic and the Ohio Players, songs built from James Brown records, and a Big Punisher hit interpolating Earth, Wind & Fire. "OG funk underpins so much of what we take for granted in pop to this day."

**Funk Fusing with Everything (1974-75)** : By 1974 and 1975, funk was fusing with Latin, jazz, rock and other genres, and it spread beyond America. "Funk was fusing with everything, and everything it touched turned to gold."

**Motown Veterans as Early Crossover Hitmakers** : Many of the first big funk crossover hits came from 60s Motown veterans, including the Temptations, Marvin Gaye and Stevie Wonder. "At first, many of the big funk crossover hits came from Motown Veterans of the 60s."
