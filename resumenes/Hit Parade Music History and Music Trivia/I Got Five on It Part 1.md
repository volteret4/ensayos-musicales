## songs
**Red Red Wine (1983/1988) - Neil Diamond Cover Turned Transatlantic Number One** : The song began as a Neil Diamond flop that peaked at number 62 in 1968. # curiosity

**Billboard Hot 100 Methodology** : Since its inception in 1958, the Hot 100 has tracked at least two things: song sales and radio airplay. Since 2012, it has also measured streaming, such as Spotify. As a result, it captures both passive fandom (radio, public spaces) and active fandom (music fans seek out). "Since its inception in 1958, the Hot 100 has always tracked at least two things, sales of songs and radio airplay."

**SoundScan Chart Revolution** : The launch of SoundScan brought about a chart revolution and enabled rap to top the Hot 100. "the chart revolution brought about by the launch of sound scan."

**One Hit Wonder Rules** : Hit Parade devised formal rules for one-hit wonders. Under them, an act qualifies if it had only one top 40 hit on either the Hot 100 or the R&B chart. "The rules I devised for One Hit Wonders."

**Hear 'n Aid (1985)** : In 1985, after "We Are the World," a group of snubbed metalheads got together to record their own charity single, "Hear 'n Aid," with stars. "a bunch of snubbed metalheads got together to record their own single here in AID with stars."

**UK Christmas Number One Competition** : The race for the UK Christmas number one is a recognized chart phenomenon. "The UK Christmas number one competition is a thing."

**Early-80s Medley Craze** : The early 1980s saw a medley craze on the charts that nobody feels nostalgic for. "The Early 80s saw a medley craze that nobody feels nostalgic for."

**The Doobie Bounce** : The "Doobie Bounce" was among listener Jarelle McAllister's favorite facts. "The Doobie Bounce is a thing."

**Clint Eastwood Movie Makes a Number One** : A striving nightclub pianist and singer scored a number one hit thanks to a Clint Eastwood movie. The song came from the folk tradition, and its chart-topping version opened the Hit Parade to other Black female singers making number ones out of funkier, left-field songs by white creators. "a striving nightclub pianist and singer scored a number one hit thanks to a Clint Eastwood movie."

**1971's Two Versions of "You've Got a Friend"** : 1971 sounded so strong in part because the year encompassed two different versions of "You've Got a Friend." "it encompassed both this version of You've Got A Friend. And this version of You've Got A Friend."

**1984's Crossover Year** : 1984 is lionized by music critics and is the subject of a whole book, because Black acts were rocking out while white acts were getting down. "the black acts were rocking out and the white acts were getting down."

**Late-90s and Early-Aughts Revival on Radio** : In the early 2020s, radio programmers reported a revival of interest in late-90s and early-aughts music. This is partly nostalgia and partly because turn-of-the-millennium pop was so stylistically broad. "radio programmers are reporting a revival of interest in late 90s and early Aughts music."

**Science of Teenage Music Memory** : Psychology Today has published articles on why the music people hear around ages 13 to 16 sticks with them. "Psychology today has devoted articles to why the music we hear as teenagers sticks to us."
