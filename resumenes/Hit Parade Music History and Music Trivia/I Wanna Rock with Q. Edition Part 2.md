## albums
**The Dude (1981) - Platinum Blockbuster and Qwest's Best Seller** : The best-selling LP in Qwest Records' first year was by Quincy Jones himself. It fused pop jazz, soul and Latin music and produced three Top 40 pop hits, the most of any LP credited to Quincy himself: "Ai No Corrida" (#28), "Just Once" (#17) and "One Hundred Ways" (#14). It went platinum and introduced James Ingram as a featured vocalist. "A fusion of pop jazz, soul and Latin music, The Dude spawned three top 40 pop hits, the most of any LP credited to Quincy himself."
**Back on the Block (1989) - Bebop to Hip Hop, Album of the Year** : Released in fall 1989, it was his first album under his own name in eight years, since The Dude. The goal was to span all of his interests and influences "from bebop to hip hop". One track mixed jazz greats Miles Davis and Sarah Vaughan with rappers Kool Moe Dee and Big Daddy Kane, and friends from across the industry sang on it. Three straight singles hit #1 on the R&B chart: "I'll Be Good to You", "The Secret Garden" and "Tomorrow". It went platinum. At the 1991 Grammy Awards it won Album of the Year over LPs by Mariah Carey, Phil Collins, MC Hammer and Wilson Phillips. That made Jones the only artist in Grammy history to win Album of the Year twice, once as producer of Thriller and once for his own LP. Its eclecticism and Quincy's deep respect across the industry are credited with the win. "One track found him mixing jazz grates like Miles Davis and Sarah Vaughn with rappers Cool Modee and Big Daddy Kane."
**Q's Jook Joint (1995) - Last Platinum Album** : His last platinum album, a similarly diverse record. Collaborators included rising star Tamia and veteran hitmaker Babyface. "His last platinum album was 1995's Cue's Juke Joint, a similarly diverse album on which he collaborated with Rising Star Tamiya."

## songs
**Ai No Corrida (1981)** : The lead single from The Dude, a cover of a Latin funk club track. Patti Austin and Charles May sang the vocals. It reached #28 on the Hot 100. "The lead single, a cover of the Latin funk club track I Know Corita with vocals by Patty Austin and Charles May reached number 28 on the Hot 100."
**Just Once (1981)** : The first of two ballads from The Dude sung by James Ingram, whom Quincy had basically discovered and cajoled into singing. It was a #17 hit and jump-started Ingram's career. "Jones jump started Ingram's career with featured vocals on just once a number 17 hit from The Dude."
**One Hundred Ways (1981)** : The follow-up ballad from The Dude, again sung by James Ingram. It reached #14 on the Hot 100. "And the number 14 follow up 100 ways."
**I'll Be Good to You (1989)** : A remake of the Brothers Johnson hit for Back on the Block, with new vocals from Ray Charles and Chaka Khan. It topped the R&B chart and reached #18 on the Hot 100. "A remake of the brother's Johnson hit I'll Be Good to You featuring new vocals from Ray Charles and Shaka Khan topped the R&B chart and hit number eighteen on the hot 100."
**The Secret Garden (1989)** : A slow jam from Back on the Block that brought together several generations of soul singers: Barry White, James Ingram, Al B. Sure! and El DeBarge. It topped the R&B chart and reached #31 on the pop chart. "A slow jam called The Secret Garden, teaming Quincy with multiple generations of soul singers, Barry White, James Ingram, Al B. Shore and El De Barge also topped the R&B chart."
**Tomorrow (1990)** : Quincy's single with teen singer Tevin Campbell. In June 1990 it became the third straight single from Back on the Block to reach #1 on the R&B chart. "By the time tomorrow Quincy's single with teen singer Tevin Campbell became the third straight single from back on the block to hit number one on the R&B chart in June 1990."
**Blue Monday 88 (1988) - New Order Remix** : Quincy worked with fellow producer John Potoker to remix New Order's classic "Blue Monday" while promoting the band's 1987 compilation Substance. The remix took the song onto the Hot 100 for the first time and topped Billboard's Club Play chart. "The result, Blue Monday 88, brought the pioneering song to the hot 100 for the first time."

## curiosities
**Q - Arranger, Conductor, Composer, Producer** : The music industry fondly calls him Q. Before he met Michael Jackson he was already the force behind hits by Ray Charles, Lesley Gore, Frank Sinatra, Aretha Franklin, the Brothers Johnson and Chaka Khan. "the man whom the music industry fondly calls Q"
**Signing On to Off the Wall** : Jones was impressed with Michael Jackson's drive and agreed to produce his first adult solo album. "Shake Your Body" already had elements of the smooth, polished funk sound Q had been developing with the Brothers Johnson and Chaka Khan, so the two pushed further in that direction. "Quincy Jones, impressed with Jackson's drive, signed on to the project."
**First Hot 100 #1 as Producer Since It's My Party** : "Don't Stop 'Til You Get Enough" topped the Hot 100 in October 1979. It was Quincy's first #1 as a producer since Lesley Gore's "It's My Party", more than 16 years earlier. "The producer since Leslie Goors' It's My Party more than 16 years earlier, and the album was just getting started."
**Rod Temperton - Q's Secret Weapon** : Jones brought in Heatwave's Rod Temperton, whose refined grooves suited the Quincy Jones sound. Temperton wrote three songs for Off the Wall, the title tracks of Give Me the Night and Thriller, and co-wrote "Stomp!". "Temperton's refined grooves were ideal for the Quincy Jones sound, and he became Q's secret weapon."
**The Quincy Dream Team of Session Players** : Jones built a stellar lineup of songwriters and players for Off the Wall and reused them on other projects, giving those records his polished sonic stamp. The team included keyboardist Greg Phillinganes, percussionist Paulinho da Costa, horn arranger Jerry Hey and Toto synth programmer Steve Porcaro. "Quincy Jones had surrounded him with a stellar lineup of songwriters and instrumentalists."
**Founding Qwest Records** : George Benson's Give Me the Night was the first release on Qwest Records, Quincy's new label. He started it partly to release music by artists in his stable, such as his frequent session singer Patti Austin. "Jones started the label in part to issue material by artists in his stable like his frequent session singer Patty Austin."
**Discovering James Ingram** : Before The Dude, James Ingram mostly worked as a keyboardist and wasn't convinced he should sing. Quincy loved his gritty, plain-spoken voice and talked him into it. "He was not convinced he should sing, but Q had a way of changing others destiny."
**Selective, but Unable to Resist Superstars** : Jones had become more selective about outside production work but could rarely turn down a superstar, which is how he came to produce an album for Donna Summer. "But he generally couldn't resist the lore of a superstar."
**Donna Summer LP as Dress Rehearsal for Thriller** : Many fans and critics later saw his 1982 Donna Summer album as Quincy's dress rehearsal for Thriller. "Quincy's dress rehearsal for his other major 1982 production."
**Consecutive Hot 100 #1s as Producer** : In 1983, Patti Austin and James Ingram's "Baby, Come to Me", which Jones had produced back in 1981, reached #1 directly after "Billie Jean". That gave him back-to-back Hot 100 chart-toppers as a producer. "Baby hit number one back to back with Billie Jean, giving Quincy Jones consecutive hot 100 chart toppers as a producer."
**1984 Grammy Producer of the Year Speech** : At the 1984 Grammys, which Michael Jackson and Thriller all but swept, Jones received Producer of the Year from his friends in Toto. In his speech he thanked Paul McCartney, Eddie Van Halen, Vincent Price, Bruce Swedien, "62 musicians" and "22 of the best singers in the world". He also thanked songwriters Rod Temperton, James Ingram, Steve Porcaro and John Bettis, and Michael Jackson for co-producing three sides of the album, whom he called one of the greatest entertainers of the 20th century. "The main job of a producer is to produce and see that everything works, and we'd like to thank the people that make this work."
**Pop's Svengali King** : His clout in 1983–84 produced many hits outside of Michael Jackson. The biggest sign of his status came in early 1985, when he was asked to oversee Jackson and Lionel Richie's charity single. "the biggest mark of Quincy's status as pop's Svengali king would come in early 1985"
**Benevolent Dictator of We Are the World** : Jones connected Michael Jackson with Lionel Richie to write the song. He produced, arranged and conducted the session, waving a conductor's baton, and kept the stars in line. Footage that has circulated since 1985 shows him as charming, funny and self-deprecating, but in command. "Q comes across as a charming, funny, self-deprecating but commanding benevolent dictator."
**"Check Your Egos at the Door" Sign** : The best-known anecdote from the We Are the World session is the sign at the entrance to A&M Studios in Hollywood that read "check your egos at the door". Quincy Jones put up that sign. "For the record, Quincy Jones posted that sign."
**1986 Grammy Record of the Year Speech** : Accepting Record of the Year for We Are the World, Jones thanked the performers. He apologized that the slogan had ever gotten out of hand, saying it was never necessary because the cause itself commanded the teamwork. "apologize that the slogan, keep your eagles at the door, ever got out of hand because it was never necessary."
**The Color Purple Score (1985)** : Jones composed the score for the original 1985 film of Alice Walker's novel The Color Purple, directed by Steven Spielberg. "He composed the score to the original 1985 film adaptation of Alice Walker's novel The Color Purple from director Steven Spielberg."
**Only Two-Time Album of the Year Winner Across Roles** : With Back on the Block, Jones became the only artist in Grammy history to win Album of the Year twice: as producer of Thriller and for his own LP. "Jones's win made him the only artist in Grammy history to take album of the year twice"
**Honorary Oscar** : The Motion Picture Academy announced that Jones would receive an honorary Oscar at the Governors Awards. He has never won an Oscar competitively. "the Motion Picture Academy announced that Jones will be receiving an honorary Oscar at the Governor's Awards later this year. He has never won competitively."
**Collaborations with Travis Scott and The Weeknd** : Contemporary pop and hip-hop artists from Travis Scott to The Weeknd have worked with Jones. Some of those contributions were non-musical, such as video cameos and album monologues, including one on a 2022 Weeknd album. "everyone from Travis Scott to the Weekend has collaborated with Quincy Jones, albeit sometimes on non-musical contributions like video cameos and album monologues"
**75th Birthday at Montreux** : Jones has marked several milestone birthdays with music. For his 75th, Chaka Khan performed "Stuff Like That", Quincy's first-ever chart-topping R&B single, at the Montreux Jazz Festival with a big band and dozens of fellow admirers. "That was when Shaka Khan performed stuff like that. Quincy's first ever chart-topping R&B single, at the Montro Jazz Festival"

## albums
**Off the Wall (1979) - First Adult Solo Album and First Post-Disco Album** : This was Jackson's first solo LP for Epic, and he wanted to separate his solo work from the Jacksons once and for all. Quincy Jones produced it. It was recorded at the height of disco just as the backlash was taking hold, which made it effectively the first post-disco album. Rod Temperton wrote three songs, including the title track. It came out in late summer 1979, weeks after Disco Demolition Night in Chicago, and won praise from critics who were otherwise tired of disco. It launched Michael as a solo superstar. It sat in the album chart Top 10 for most of six months and topped Billboard's R&B album chart for months, but peaked at only #3 on the all-genre pop chart. It was the first album by a solo act to produce four Top 10 hits. At the 1980 Grammys it won a single award, in the R&B categories, and its hits weren't nominated for Record of the Year, which went to the Doobie Brothers. Jackson felt he had hit a racial glass ceiling. "Quincy and Michael were recording the first post disco album."
**Thriller (1982) - Best-Selling Album of All Time** : The follow-up to Off the Wall, produced by Quincy Jones. Michael co-produced three sides. It was launched with the Paul McCartney duet "The Girl Is Mine", and "Billie Jean" sent it to #1 on the pop album chart for 37 weeks across most of 1983 and early 1984. It produced a record seven Top 10 singles, a Hot 100 record that stood for more than three decades until the streaming era of Drake and Taylor Swift. It is certified 34 times platinum in the US, with global sales estimated at 70 to 100 million copies, and made Jackson the biggest pop star in the world. Jackson and Thriller all but swept the 1984 Grammys. "Now, the best-selling album of all time, Certified 34 times Platinum in the US, and with global sales estimated at anywhere from 70 to 100 million copies."
**Bad (1987) - Final Collaboration with Quincy Jones** : The long-awaited, heavily hyped follow-up to Thriller. It couldn't match Thriller's heights but set its own chart records. It was the first album by either Jackson or Jones to debut at #1 on the album chart, which was extremely rare before SoundScan. It was also the first album to produce five Hot 100 #1 hits: "I Just Can't Stop Loving You", "Bad", "The Way You Make Me Feel", "Man in the Mirror" and "Dirty Diana". It was Jackson's last collaboration with Jones. "bad was the first album to generate five hot 100 number one hits."

## songs
**Ben (1972)** : Michael's first Hot 100 #1 as a solo artist, scored at age 14 with a tender song addressed to a pet rodent. It came out while his solo records were still a brand extension of the Jackson 5. "Michael had even scored a hot 100 number one way back in 1972 at age 14, with his tender ode to a pet rodent named Ben."
**Don't Stop 'Til You Get Enough (1979)** : The lead single from Off the Wall, a dance-floor track Jackson wrote himself and Quincy Jones polished. It topped the Hot 100 in October 1979. It was Jackson's first #1 since "Ben" seven years earlier and Jones's first #1 as producer since "It's My Party". "The album led off with a dance floor single, Jackson wrote himself, which Quincy Jones polished into a diamond."
**Rock with You (1979)** : The second Off the Wall single, written by Rod Temperton. It topped the Hot 100 in January 1980, Jackson's first #1 of the '80s. It has disco strings and Jackson sings about riding the boogie, but its driving guitars and synthesizers sounded more like the new decade. It became a template for multiracial '80s pop and R&B. "a template for multi-racial 80s pop and B."
**Off the Wall (1979)** : The third single and title track, another Rod Temperton song. It peaked at #10 in April 1980, giving the album three Top 10 hits. "It peaked at number 10 in April 1980."
**She's Out of My Life (1979)** : A melodramatic ballad and the album's fourth single. It peaked at #10 in June 1980, making Jackson the first solo act to pull four Top 10s from one LP. The only earlier album with four Top 10 hits was Fleetwood Mac's Rumours. "becoming the first solo act to pull four top 10s from an LP."
**Girlfriend (1979)** : A light pop song written by Paul McCartney, then with Wings. Jackson covered it on Off the Wall on Quincy Jones's advice as part of his pop crossover strategy. "He'd even taken Quincy Jones's advice, and covered a light pop track for Off the Wall, called Girlfriend"
**The Girl Is Mine (1982)** : A duet with Paul McCartney recorded in the hope of repeating the success of McCartney and Stevie Wonder's "Ebony and Ivory". It turned out to be no one's favorite track on Thriller, but Quincy and Michael made it the first single, and it was treated as an event. Released in fall 1982, it topped the R&B chart in January 1983 and reached #2 on the Hot 100. That pushed Thriller to #1 on the R&B album chart and into the pop album Top 5. "Jackson's duet with McCartney would turn out to be no one's favorite track on the new album, but Quincy and Michael knew what they were doing."
**Billie Jean (1982)** : The "undeniable" second Thriller single, widely considered one of the best pop recordings ever. Its driving groove was played by Brothers Johnson bassist Louis Johnson. Its haunted lyrics drew on Michael's paranoia about his fame. Its video broke barriers for Black artists on the then-new MTV, and Jackson debuted his moonwalk performing it on the 1983 Motown TV special. Quincy Jones later accused Jackson of lifting the throbbing synth bass line from Donna Summer's "State of Independence". Jackson never admitted this and said instead that it came from Hall & Oates' "I Can't Go for That". It hit #1 in March 1983, stayed there for seven weeks, and sent Thriller to #1 on the pop album chart. "It went to number one in March 1983 and stayed there for seven weeks."
**Beat It (1983)** : A rock song Quincy ordered Michael to write. Once it was done, Jones had the idea, and the connections, to bring in Eddie Van Halen, who added a face-melting guitar solo to this tough-sounding song about not trying to be tough. It also topped the Hot 100, just over a week after "Billie Jean" left #1. "the next hit was a rock song that Quincy ordered Michael to write."
**Wanna Be Startin' Somethin' (1983)** : It borrows the chant "Mama-ko mama-ssa mama-ko-ssa" from "Soul Makossa" by Manu Dibango, a friend of Quincy's. Jackson settled out of court with Dibango over the blatant interpolation. It reached #5 in summer 1983. "Jackson wound up settling out of court with DeBango for the blatant interpolation."
**Human Nature (1983)** : Quincy invited Toto to play on and write for Thriller. When he heard an appealing demo by Steve Porcaro that needed lyrics, he hired lyricist John Bettis to finish it. Four members of Toto played on the track with Michael singing lead. It was a #7 hit and Thriller's ultimate yacht soul moment. "The result on which four members of Toto played with Michael singing lead was human nature, a number seven hit and thrillers ultimate Yacht Soulside."
**P.Y.T. (Pretty Young Thing) (1983)** : The sixth single, written by Quincy's protégé James Ingram. Quincy came up with the title "Pretty Young Thing" and, as he often did with the Brothers Johnson, asked Ingram to write a song around it. It was a #10 hit in fall 1983. "Q himself came up with the title, Pretty Young Thing."
**Thriller (1984)** : Rod Temperton's demo was originally called "Starlight". Jones disliked the title and asked for a better one. Temperton rewrote the lyric around horror movies and renamed it "Thriller", and Q invited his wife's friend Vincent Price to do a spooky rap. It became a cinematic music video that played around the clock on MTV. Released in 1984 as the seventh and final single, it reached #4, giving Thriller a record seventh Top 10 hit. "The original demo of the song was called Starlight, but Jones didn't like that title and asked Temperton to come up with a better one."

## curiosities
**Famous Since Pre-Adolescence** : By the time he met Quincy Jones, Jackson had been famous for most of a decade. His solo records had been released as a brand extension of the Jackson 5, and after each solo success he went back to the group under his father Joe Jackson. "Michael always dutifully returned to the fold, rejoining his Jackson 5 brothers under the command of their imperious patriarch Joe Jackson."
**Racial Glass Ceiling After Off the Wall** : Even after four Top 10 hits, Jackson resented how Off the Wall was received. The industry treated it as an R&B album that had overperformed: it got one Grammy in the R&B categories and peaked at only #3 on the pop album chart. "Jackson felt he had played the pop crossover game, but hit a racial glass ceiling."
**Backing Vocals on State of Independence** : Quincy invited Jackson to sing backup on Donna Summer's "State of Independence". Jones later said Jackson took its bass line for "Billie Jean". "Among the vocalists on the track was Michael Jackson, whom Q had invited to sing back up on the track."
**Moonwalk Debut on the 1983 Motown TV Special** : Jackson introduced his version of the backward-gliding dance while performing "Billie Jean" on the 1983 Motown TV special. "Michael's performance of the song on the 1983 Motown TV special at which he introduced his version of the backwards gliding dance, The Moonwalk."
**Breaking MTV's Color Barrier** : The "Billie Jean" video broke barriers for Black artists on MTV. "It's music video which broke barriers for black artists on the then new video channel MTV."
**Co-Writing We Are the World** : Quincy Jones connected Jackson with Lionel Richie to write the USA for Africa charity single in early 1985. "In addition to connecting Michael Jackson with Lionel Richie to write the song"

**Ideal Quincy Artist** : With jazz chops, smooth vocals and pop ambitions, Benson was a natural fit for Jones before they ever worked together. "George Benson was basically an ideal artist for Quincy Jones to work with, even before they actually did."

# artist - USA for Africa

## members
Michael Jackson
Lionel Richie

## songs
**We Are the World (1985)** : The American answer to Bob Geldof's British Ethiopian famine-relief single "Do They Know It's Christmas". Written by Michael Jackson and Lionel Richie, with Quincy Jones producing, arranging and conducting. On the night of the 1985 American Music Awards, more than five dozen US pop stars recorded it at A&M Studios in Los Angeles. It spent four weeks at #1 on the Hot 100, sold four million copies in the US, was the first multi-platinum single, topped charts around the world, raised millions for famine relief, and won Record of the Year at the 1986 Grammys. Critics still disagree about its artistic value. "Four weeks at number one on the Hot 100 and four million copies sold in the US alone."

## curiosities
**The Greatest Night in Pop Documentary** : Netflix's hit documentary tells the story of the session. It has no new interviews with Jones, who is over 90, but he comes across as its quiet hero. "Jones emerges in the story as a quiet hero of the song's creation."
**A Superhuman Feat** : The recording was described as unprecedented in complexity and vocal firepower. "more than 5 dozen US Pop superstars gathered in a cavernous LA studio for a recording of unprecedented complexity and vocal firepower."
