# artist - Quincy Jones

## labels
Quest Records

## albums
**The Dude (1981) - Platinum Blockbuster** : This album was Quincy Jones's best-selling LP on Quest Records in its first year, achieving platinum status. It represented a fusion of pop jazz, soul, and Latin music, and remarkably, spawned three top 40 pop hits, the most for any LP credited to Quincy himself. "the best selling LP in quests first year was by Quincy Jones himself, his platinum blockbuster The Dude."
**L.A. Is My Lady (1984) - Frank Sinatra's Final Album** : Quincy Jones produced this album for his long-time friend Frank Sinatra, encouraging him to sing about a location other than New York. This album, Sinatra's final solo studio effort, featured a title track that, though described as "schlocky," briefly put Sinatra on MTV thanks to a music video showcasing cameos from Quincy's friends, including Donna Summer and Eddie Van Halen. "L is my lady, would be Sinatra's final solo studio album, and its title track, however, Schlocky, actually got Frank briefly on MTV, thanks to a music video with cameos by friends of Quincy. Quincy, Donna Summer, Eddie Van Halen, David Lee Roth, and Michael McDonald."
**Back on the Block (1989) - Genre-Spanning Masterpiece** : This was Quincy Jones's first album under his own name in eight years since "The Dude." His artistic vision for the album was to encompass all his diverse interests and influences, from bebop to hip hop. The album featured an eclectic mix, including jazz greats like Miles Davis and Sarah Vaughan alongside rappers such as Kool Moe Dee and Big Daddy Kane. It achieved platinum status and won the Grammy for Album of the Year in 1991, making Jones the sole artist in Grammy history to win this award twice (once as a producer for "Thriller" and once for his own LP). "In the fall of 1989, Quincy Jones issued back on the block. His first album under his own name in eight years since the dude. Q's goal on back on the block was to span all of his interests and influences from bebop to hip hop."
**Q's Jook Joint (1995) - Diverse Collaboration Album** : Quincy Jones's last platinum album, released in 1995, featured a diverse array of collaborations. On this project, he worked with a rising star, Tamia, and the veteran hitmaker Babyface, showcasing his continued ability to connect different generations and styles. "His last platinum album was 1995's Cue's Juke Joint, a similarly diverse album on which he collaborated with Rising Star Tamiya. And veteran hitmaker Babyface."

## curiosities
**Industry Nickname "Q"** : The music industry fondly refers to Quincy Jones as "Q," recognizing him as a key figure in pop. "the man whom the music industry fondly calls Q"
**Vast Career Contributions** : Quincy Jones has had a rich, varied, and exceptionally busy career as an arranger, conductor, composer, and producer, acting as the "secret sauce" behind hits for diverse artists from Ray Charles to Chaka Khan, even before his work with Michael Jackson. "We talked about the rich, varied, and exceptionally busy career of Quincy Jones, a ranger, conductor, composer, producer, and the secret sauce behind hits from Ray Charles to Leslie Gore, Frank Sinatra to Aretha Franklin, the brother's Johnson to Shaka Khan."
**Impressed by Michael Jackson's Drive** : Jones was highly impressed by Michael Jackson's drive, leading him to sign on to the project that would become Jackson's first adult solo album. "Quincy Jones, impressed with Jackson's drive, signed on to the project."
**First Hot 100 Number One as Producer (1979)** : Quincy Jones achieved his first Hot 100 number one as a producer with Michael Jackson's "Don't Stop 'Til You Get Enough" in 1979, a significant milestone given his prior producing work since Leslie Gore's "It's My Party" more than 16 years earlier. "Quincy Jones's first Hot 100 number one as a solo superstar. The producer since Leslie Goors' It's My Party more than 16 years earlier"
**"Master Catalyst" for George Benson's Album** : All Music recognized Quincy Jones as the "master catalyst" for George Benson's "Give Me the Night" LP, highlighting his regular team's control and how Benson's voice was beautifully captured in the album's rich, floating sound. "All music says of the Give Me the Night LP quote, Quincy Jones is the master catalyst and his regular team is in control."
**Founding Quest Records (1980)** : Quincy Jones established Quest Records as his new record label in 1980, with George Benson's "Give Me the Night" serving as its inaugural release. The label was partly founded to issue material by artists within his stable, such as Patti Austin. "Also significant about the Give Me the Night LP was that it was the first release on quest records Quincy's new record label."
**Discovery and Mentorship of James Ingram** : Quincy Jones played a pivotal role in James Ingram's career, essentially "discovering" him and persuading him to transition from a keyboardist to a singer. Jones greatly admired Ingram's "gritty, plain-spoken voice," thereby jump-starting his singing career. "Prior to The Dude, James Ingram worked mostly as a keyboardist. He was not convinced he should sing, but Q had a way of changing others destiny. He loved Ingram's gritty plain spoken voice."
**Selective Projects and "Superstar Lure"** : Despite becoming more selective with external production work, Quincy Jones found it hard to resist "the lure of a superstar," which explains his continued involvement in high-profile projects. "Quincy Jones was being more selective about outside production projects. But he generally couldn't resist the lore of a superstar."
**"Dress Rehearsal" for Thriller** : Many fans and critics retrospectively viewed Donna Summer's self-titled 1982 album, which Quincy Jones produced, as not only a valuable addition to her discography but also as a "dress rehearsal" for his subsequent major production, Michael Jackson's "Thriller." "many fans and critics later came to regard the Donna Summer LP as not only a worthy entry in her catalog, but also Quincy's dress rehearsal for his other major 1982 production."
**Advice for Michael Jackson's Pop Crossover** : Quincy Jones advised Michael Jackson to include a light pop track, "Girlfriend" written by Paul McCartney, on "Off the Wall" to ensure its pop crossover appeal, addressing Jackson's ambition to break racial barriers in the charts. "Jackson felt he had played the pop crossover game, but hit a racial glass ceiling. He'd even taken Quincy Jones's advice, and covered a light pop track for Off the Wall, called Girlfriend, that was written by former Beatles and now Wings superstar Paul McCartney."
**Aiming to Recreate "Ebony and Ivory" Success** : Quincy Jones and Michael Jackson intentionally aimed to replicate the chart success of Paul McCartney's duet with Stevie Wonder, "Ebony and Ivory," with their own collaboration "The Girl Is Mine." "They were unabashedly hoping to recreate the chart's success of Ebony and Ivory."
**Accusation of Bassline "Biting" (Michael Jackson)** : Decades later, Quincy Jones openly accused Michael Jackson of "biting" the distinctive throbbing synthesized bassline from Donna Summer's "State of Independence" for one of Jackson's own compositions. "Decades later, Jones accused Jackson of biting the track's throbbing synthesized bass line for a song of his own."
**Initiating Eddie Van Halen's Solo on "Beat It"** : After Quincy Jones instructed Michael Jackson to write a rock song for the "Thriller" album, Jones took the initiative to connect with Eddie Van Halen, leveraging his ideas and connections to secure a guitar solo from the legendary musician for "Beat It." "Once he did, it was Jones who had the idea and the connections to call this man to add a guitar solo."
**Inviting Toto Members for "Thriller"** : Quincy Jones extended an invitation to the members of Toto, who were experiencing their own string of hits, to both play on and contribute songs to Michael Jackson's "Thriller" album. "For the next single, Quincy had invited the members of Toto who were coming off their own string of hits to play and contribute songs to thriller."
**Commissioning "Human Nature" Lyrics** : Quincy Jones discovered an alluring demo by Toto's Steve Porcaro that lacked lyrics for "Thriller." He then commissioned lyricist John Bettis to complete the track, which ultimately became "Human Nature." "When Quincy heard an alluring demo by Toto's Steve Porcaro that needed some lyrics, Q commissioned lyricist John Betis to finish the track."
**Title Concept for "P.Y.T. (Pretty Young Thing)"** : Quincy Jones conceptualized the title "Pretty Young Thing" (P.Y.T.) and, in a manner similar to his work with the Brothers Johnson, encouraged his protégé James Ingram to compose a song around that specific title for "Thriller." "Q himself came up with the title, Pretty Young Thing. And as he had so often with the brothers Johnson, Jones invited Ingram to write a song around that title."
**Request for "Thriller" Title Change** : Quincy Jones expressed his disapproval of the original demo title, "Starlight," for what would become the "Thriller" song. He consequently requested Rod Temperton to devise a more fitting title, leading to the iconic horror-themed name. "The original demo of the song was called Starlight, but Jones didn't like that title and asked Temperton to come up with a better one."
**Endorsing "Thriller" Title and Vincent Price Rap** : When Rod Temperton revised the lyrics to focus on horror movies and proposed "Thriller" as the new title, Quincy Jones enthusiastically endorsed the change. He then famously invited his wife's friend, Vincent Price, to contribute a "spooky rap" to the track. "When Temperton redirected the lyric around horror movies and called it Thriller, Q endorsed the change and invited his wife's friend Vincent Price to do a spooky rap on the song."
**1984 Grammy Producer of the Year Acceptance Speech** : At the 1984 Grammy Awards, where Michael Jackson and "Thriller" dominated the prizes, Quincy Jones accepted the Producer of the Year award, using the opportunity to acknowledge his "singular skill set" and thank the vast network of collaborators, including notable figures like Paul McCartney, Eddie Van Halen, Vincent Price, and numerous musicians and songwriters. "At the 1984 Grammy Awards, where Michael Jackson and Thriller all but swept the prizes, Quincy alluded to his singular skill set when he went up to the podium to accept the producer of the year prize from his friends in Toto."
**Consecutive Hot 100 Chart-Toppers (1983)** : In 1983, Quincy Jones achieved a remarkable feat by having consecutive Hot 100 chart-toppers as a producer, with Patti Austin and James Ingram's "Baby, Come to Me" hitting number one immediately after Michael Jackson's "Billie Jean." "Baby hit number one back to back with Billie Jean, giving Quincy Jones consecutive hot 100 chart toppers as a producer."
**"Pop's Svengali King" Status (1985)** : In early 1985, Quincy Jones's unparalleled influence and status as "pop's Svengali king" were underscored when he was tasked with overseeing the collaborative charity project "We Are the World," initiated by Michael Jackson and Lionel Richie. "but the biggest mark of Quincy's status as pop's Svengali king would come in early 1985, when Jones was asked to oversee a special project that Jackson was working on with Lionel Richie."
**Lionel Richie's Rationale for Jones's Involvement** : Lionel Richie specifically sought Quincy Jones's involvement for "We Are the World," recognizing him as "the master orchestrator" and the most influential producer of that era, commanding universal respect among musicians. "But one thing for sure I knew I wanted Quincy involved because he's the master orchestrator. Mr. Quincy Jones. At that particular time you could not be a producer, be any hotter. We had the respect of every musician on the planet."
**Leadership of "We Are the World" Session** : Beyond connecting Michael Jackson and Lionel Richie for songwriting, Quincy Jones took charge of producing, arranging, and conducting the "We Are the World" session. He was famously responsible for managing the large ensemble of pop superstars, described as "herding the cats." "In addition to connecting Michael Jackson with Lionel Richie to write the song, Quincy was in charge of not just producing, arranging and conducting the session. In the footage you can see him waving a conductor's baton. But also you might say, hurting the cats."
**Posting the "Check Your Egos At The Door" Sign** : It was Quincy Jones who posted the iconic sign at the entrance to A&M Studios in Hollywood during the "We Are the World" session, famously advising participants to "check your egos at the door." "For the record, Quincy Jones posted that sign."
**"Benevolent Dictator" Role in "We Are the World"** : Footage of the "We Are the World" session, widely available since 1985, portrays Quincy Jones as a "charming, funny, self-deprecating but commanding benevolent dictator," effectively guiding the many artists involved. "In footage of the session, which has circulated on video since 1985 and is now widely available online, Q comes across as a charming, funny, self-deprecating but commanding benevolent dictator."
**Composing "The Color Purple" Score (1985)** : Quincy Jones composed the musical score for Steven Spielberg's 1985 film adaptation of Alice Walker's acclaimed novel, "The Color Purple." "He composed the score to the original 1985 film adaptation of Alice Walker's novel The Color Purple from director Steven Spielberg."
**Promoting New Order's "Substance" (1987)** : Quincy Jones personally contributed to promoting New Order's best-selling 1987 compilation "Substance." He collaborated with producer John Potaker to remix the band's classic single "Blue Monday," resulting in "Blue Monday 88." "Quincy Jones himself even got involved with promoting the band's best selling 1987 compilation substance. Q teamed with fellow producer John Podaker to remix new order's classic single Blue Monday."
**Grammy Album of the Year Twice (1991)** : Quincy Jones's 1991 Grammy win for "Back on the Block" solidified his unique place in history, making him the only artist to win Album of the Year twice—once as the producer for "Thriller" and once for his own album. The award reflected the album's broad eclecticism and Jones's deep respect across the music industry. "Jones's win made him the only artist in Grammy history to take album of the year twice as both the producer on a full-winning LP thriller and his own LP back on the block."
**Revered Figure and Honorary Oscar (21st Century)** : In the 21st century, Quincy Jones is recognized as a revered figure within the entertainment industry. He was honored with an honorary Oscar at the Governor's Awards in 2024, acknowledging his contributions despite never winning a competitive Oscar. "In the 21st century, Quincy remains a revered figure in the entertainment industry. Just this week, as we were putting together this episode, the Motion Picture Academy announced that Jones will be receiving an honorary Oscar at the Governor's Awards later this year. He has never won competitively."
**Contemporary Hip-Hop/Pop Collaborations** : Quincy Jones has influenced contemporary pop and hip-hop, with artists like Travis Scott and The Weeknd collaborating with him in the 21st century, often through non-musical contributions such as video cameos or album monologues. "In the world of contemporary pop and hip-hop, everyone from Travis Scott to the Weekend has collaborated with Quincy Jones, albeit sometimes on non-musical contributions like video cameos and album monologues, like say this 2022 Weekend internally."

He has celebrated several milestone birthdays, including his 75th at the Montreux Jazz Festival, where Chaka Khan performed "Stuff Like That"—his first chart-topping R&B single—accompanied by a big band and numerous admirers. "when it comes to the music, Cue seems content these days to let others do the performing for him. He has celebrated several milestone birthdays with music. My favorite came on his 75th birthday, now many moons ago. That was when Shaka Khan performed stuff like that. Quincy's first ever chart-topping R&B single, at the Montro Jazz Festival, along with a big band and dozens of fellow admirers."
**The "Center of the Party"** : Quincy Jones is depicted as the "center of the party" and the "binding force" in musical collaborations, consistently orchestrating, conducting, convening, or simply listening, always surrounding himself with dedicated music lovers. ## albums
**Off the Wall (1979) - Solo Superstar Debut** : Released in late summer 1979, merely weeks after the infamous Disco Demolition Night in Chicago, this album garnered critical acclaim from a disco-weary audience. It served as Michael Jackson's powerful emergence as a solo superstar. Recorded by Quincy Jones and Jackson amidst the disco era's peak and its nascent backlash, it is considered the first post-disco album. The LP notably remained in the album charts' top 10 for most of six months and produced four top 10 hits. "Released in the late summer of 1979, just weeks after the infamous disco demolition night in Chicago, off the wall won a claim from the critics who were otherwise burned out on disco music, and it served as a coming out for Michael Jackson as a solo superstar."
**Thriller (1982) - Legendary Global Phenomenon** : This album was Michael Jackson's highly anticipated and legendary follow-up to "Off the Wall." Kicking off its campaign with the duet "The Girl Is Mine" featuring Paul McCartney, the album was propelled to the top of the R&B chart and the top five on the pop album chart. It eventually dominated the pop album chart for an astonishing 37 weeks, from 1983 into early 1984. "Thriller" is certified 34 times Platinum in the US and boasts estimated global sales of 70 to 100 million copies, solidifying its status as the best-selling album of all time. "The Girl is Mine, led off the campaign for Thriller, Michael Jackson's now legendary album."
**Bad (1987) - Record-Breaking Follow-Up** : Michael Jackson's highly anticipated and extensively hyped follow-up to "Thriller," released in 1987. Although it couldn't surpass "Thriller"'s colossal success, "Bad" established its own chart benchmarks: it was the first album by either Jackson or Jones to debut at number one on the album chart (a rare feat in the pre-Soundscan era) and the first album ever to generate five Hot 100 number one hits. "Bad could never live up to the heights hit by Thriller, but it did earn several chart benchmarks of its own. The first album by either Jackson or Jones to debut at number one on the album chart, an extraordinarily rare feat in the pre-Soundscan era. And bad was the first album to generate five hot 100 number one hits."

**Early Fame from Jackson 5** : Michael Jackson had already been famous for nearly a decade, dating back to his pre-adolescence with the Jackson 5, before he even met Quincy Jones. **"Chip on His Shoulder" about "Off the Wall" Reception** : While proud of "Off the Wall," Michael Jackson felt a "chip on his shoulder" regarding its industry reception. He believed it was pigeonholed as an overperforming R&B album, rather than a pop crossover success, which was reinforced by its single Grammy win in R&B categories and the absence of Record of the Year nominations. "While Jackson was proud of Off the Wall, he had a chip on his shoulder about how that first Quincy produced LP had been received, yes, even after four Top 10 hits. To the industry, Off the Wall was an R&B album that had overperformed, and they treated it like an R&B album."
**Experiencing a "Racial Glass Ceiling" (1980)** : At the 1980 Grammy Awards, "Off the Wall" received only one award in the R&B categories, and none of its hits were nominated for Record of the Year (which went to The Doobie Brothers). Despite topping Billboard's R&B album chart for months, it only peaked at number three on the all-genre Pop album chart, leading Jackson to feel he had encountered a "racial glass ceiling." "At the 1980 Grammy Awards, Off the Wall only won a single prize in the R&B categories, and its hits weren't even nominated for Record of the Year, which was taken instead by the Dubie Brothers. Off the Wall had also topped Billboard's R&B album chart for months, but peaked on the All-Genre Pop album chart only at number three. Jackson felt he had played the pop crossover game, but hit a racial glass ceiling."
**Backup Vocals on Donna Summer's "State of Independence"** : Michael Jackson contributed backup vocals to "State of Independence," a single from Donna Summer's 1982 album, which was produced by Quincy Jones. "Among the vocalists on the track was Michael Jackson, whom Q had invited to sing back up on the track."
**Claimed Bassline Origin for "Billie Jean"** : Michael Jackson refuted Quincy Jones's accusation of borrowing the bassline for "Billie Jean" from Donna Summer's "State of Independence." Instead, Jackson asserted that he derived the rumbling bassline from Darryl Hall and John Oates' 1981 chart-topping hit, "I Can't Go for That." "Michael never copped to this. Instead, Jackson claimed he poached the song's rumbling bass line not from the Donna Summers single, but from the 1981 chart topping Smash by Darryl Hall and John Oates. I can't go for that."
**Global Impact of "Thriller"** : "Thriller" not only propelled Michael Jackson to become the biggest pop star in the world but also served as a powerful testament to Quincy Jones's exceptional and unique production skills. "Thriller not only made Michael Jackson the biggest pop star in the world, it also stood as a testament to Quincy Jones' unique set of skills."
**Co-Producing Tracks on "Thriller"** : At the 1984 Grammy Awards, where Michael Jackson and "Thriller" all but swept the prizes, Quincy Jones accepted the Producer of the Year award, and specifically credited Michael Jackson for co-producing three of the album's tracks. "At the 1984 Grammy Awards, where Michael Jackson and Thriller all but swept the prizes, Quincy alluded to his singular skill set when he went up to the podium to accept the producer of the year prize from his friends in Toto."
