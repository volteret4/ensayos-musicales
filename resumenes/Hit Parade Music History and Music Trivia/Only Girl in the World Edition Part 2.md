## albums
**Good Girl Gone Bad (Original)** : This was the original album, which was later re-released in a "reloaded" version. It was released more than two years before Rihanna's 2009 album "Rated R." "It had been more than two years since the original Good Girl Gone Bad album."
**Good Girl Gone Bad: Reloaded (Reboot)** : This was a re-release or reboot of the original "Good Girl Gone Bad" album. It was released about a year before Rihanna's 2009 album "Rated R." "It had been more than two years since the original Good Girl Gone Bad album and about a year since the reloaded reboot."
**Rated R (2009) - Reflecting Trauma and Strength** : Released in the fall of 2009, this album was a deliberate artistic response to the Chris Brown incident, with Rihanna aiming for the music to reflect the dark experience without becoming solely defined by it. Thematically, it predominantly focused on themes of strength amidst adversity and the resolve to overcome confrontation and violence. Although critically acclaimed, it wasn't a place either Rihanna or her audience wished to linger. "She knew the music had to reflect the darkness of the Chris Brown incident and yet not let it weigh down her art."
**Loud (2010) - Upbeat Pop Pivot** : Released in November 2010, less than a year after "Rated R," this album marked a significant shift in Rihanna's musical direction, indicated by her new cherry red hairstyle on the cover. Responding to the charts' pivot towards Euro dance, electro, and EDM, Rihanna aimed for "happy and up-tempo records," as revealed by Stargate's Tor Eric Hermenson. The album successfully generated three number one hits and was packed with additional singles. "Rihanna came to us before we started recording and said, I feel great about myself. I want to go back to having fun. I want to make happy and up-tempo records."
**Talk That Talk (2011) - Overshadowed by Lead Single** : Arriving in mid-November 2011, this album followed a relentless release schedule. It was described as less "hit-packed" than its predecessor, "Loud," with its lead single, "We Found Love," significantly overshadowing all other tracks on the disc. "We Found Love overshadowed everything else on the disc."
**Unapologetic (2012) - First Number One Album** : Released in the fall of 2012, this marked Rihanna's seventh album and her first to reach number one. The title was an oblique reference to her personal life, particularly her controversial re-involvement with Chris Brown, which upset some fans. The album's success was boosted by Rihanna's pervasive radio presence, her fanbase's intense engagement, and a dramatic album cover featuring Rihanna naked, adorned with tattooed words like "Fearless" and "Victory" for social media impact. It debuted at number one on the Billboard 200 with the highest first-week sales of her career, fulfilling her original 2005 contract with Def Jam. "Unapologetic debuted at number 1 on the Billboard 200 with the highest first week sales of Rihanna's career, the culmination of years of her relentless musical output."
**Anti (2016) - Critically Acclaimed Streaming Success** : Announced in the fall of 2015, the album's cryptic title, "Anti," was defined by Rihanna as "doing the very antithesis of what the public expects." The cover, an artist's rendering of a childhood photo of Rihanna with a black balloon and a gold crown covering her eyes, echoed her roots. After nearly four years in the making, it became an album both fans, critics, and Rihanna herself consider her best, climbing to number one on the Billboard 200 by mid-February 2016. "Anti" was consumed as an album more than any prior Rihanna release, with deep cuts like "Kiss It Better" receiving as much acclaim as the singles. Built for the streaming era, it remained on the Billboard 200 for over 450 weeks as of 2025, and is Rihanna's only album to feature on Rolling Stone's ranking of the top 500 albums of all time. "On Anti, Rihanna recast pop as her own hazy playground."

## songs

"Rihanna's vocal helped push Live Your Life to number 1 in October 2008."

The song notably climaxed with the sound of gunshots, and despite its dark nature, it peaked at number nine, a remarkably high position for such a track, though low for Rihanna's usual chart performance. "The song, which even climaxed with the sound of gunshots, peaked at number 9, low for Rihanna but remarkably high for such a chilling radio unfriendly track."
**Hard (2009)** : As the second single from "Rated R," this track was a "banger" with an edge, co-written by the team behind "Umbrella" and featuring rapper Jeezy, then known as the King of Trap music. "Hard, a song she wrote with the umbrella team featuring rapper GZ, then the King of Trap music. Hard reached number 8 and got Rihanna back into major radio rotation."
**Rude Boy (2010)** : This "percolating banger" emerged as the biggest hit on the "Rated R" album, surprising many given the album's darker themes. It was an atypical track, blending Jamaican Patois, dancehall iconography, and shimmering electropop. Promoted as the album's third US single in February 2010, almost exactly a year after the Chris Brown incident, "Rude Boy" soared to number one and held that position for five weeks, marking Rihanna's return to upbeat success. "Rude Boy was the album's most atypical track, a mix of Jamaican Patswa, dance hall iconography and shimmering electropop."
**Love the Way You Lie (2010)** : This ballad-plus-banger by rapper Eminem, from his acclaimed album "Recovery," featured a very prominent hook sung by Rihanna. While not directly autobiographical, the song's themes resonated with both artists' public struggles—Eminem's rehabilitation from painkiller addiction and anger management issues, and Rihanna's recent experience with Chris Brown. It became a smash, spending seven weeks at number one in late summer 2010, and was the last time Rihanna addressed her abuse, even obliquely. "Perhaps it was cathartic to hear them both confront their demons because love the way you lie turned into a smash, spending 7 weeks at number 1 in the late summer of 2010."
**Only Girl (In the World) (2010)** : This euphoric dance-pop track, which kicked off the "Loud" album, showcased Rihanna's signature style with fluttering synths, flirty lyrics, a house beat, and an explosive chorus. It quickly jumped to number three and seemed destined for the top, eventually following "What's My Name" to the number one spot two weeks later. "The album release kicked off with only girl in the world, which was euphoric dance pop done the Rihanna way, fluttering synths, flirty lyrics, a house beat and an explosive chorus."
**What's My Name (2010)** : This Caribbean-flavored electropop track featured guest vocals from rising Toronto rapper-singer Aubrey Graham, known as Drake. The collaboration, arriving amidst years of dating rumors between Rihanna and Drake, was received as a significant event due to their unmistakable "rapport on wax." It quickly shot to the top spot in just three weeks, marking Drake's first credit on a Hot 100 number one hit. "The single was received as an event, a meeting of a would be couple with chemistry, and it shot to the top spot in just three weeks."
**S&M (2011)** : Issued as a single in early 2011, this "freaky titillating" and sex-positive track was perceived as life-affirming and "devil-may care" coming just a couple of years after the Chris Brown incident. Initially stuck at number two behind Katy Perry's "ET," Team Rihanna called in a favor and released a remix featuring "fellow pop deity" Britney Spears. The remixed version, credited to "Rihanna and Britney," hit number one for one week, interrupting Perry's five-week chart reign, and was celebrated by fans during the peak of the iTunes era. "S&M credited to Rihanna and Britney, hit the top for one week, actually interrupting Perry's five week run with ET."
**Cheers (Drink to That) (2011)** : This boozy anthem, a lower-charting single from the "Loud" album, was distinctive for being built out of an Avril Lavigne sample. It successfully rose to number seven on the charts by October 2011. "The biggest was Cheers Drink to That, a boozy anthem built out of an Avril Lavigne sample. It rose all the way to number seven by October 2011."
**We Found Love (2011)** : The lead single from her 2011 album "Talk That Talk," this exultant club banger was written by Calvin Harris, who was Rihanna's opening act on tour at the time. Credited to "Rihanna featuring Calvin Harris," the song became an anthem at the peak of EDM, channeling both joy and pain. Its lyric "We Found Love in a Hopeless Place" resonated as a rallying cry for the millennial generation emerging from the Great Recession. It became a chart juggernaut, spending 10 weeks at number one on the Hot 100 from November 2011 through January 2012—Rihanna's longest-running chart topper ever—and hitting number one in over two dozen countries globally. The surreal music video, directed by frequent collaborator Molina Matsukus, depicted a toxic but exhilarating relationship fueled by drugs, fighting, and sex, which many fans interpreted as an allusion to Rihanna's time with Chris Brown, and won Video of the Year at the 2012 MTV VMAs. Though initially given a poor review by Rolling Stone, the magazine later ranked it as Rihanna's greatest single, recognizing it as her masterpiece. "Eventually, We Found Love was recognized as Rihanna's masterpiece, Rolling Stone, which gave the single a poor review in 2011, seven years later ranked it as Rihanna's greatest single."
**Where Have You Been (2012)** : This song was the second single produced by Calvin Harris for Rihanna's "Talk That Talk" album. The collaboration sparked intense speculation among fans, who "went wild" with rumors that Rihanna and Drake were romantically involved again. "When Rhee duetted again with Drake, this time on the title track of his 2011 album Take Care, the fans went wild with speculation that they were an item again."
**Diamonds (2012)** : A massive chart-topping single from the "Unapologetic" album, this song was described by one critic as "Rihanna's version of a James Bond theme" due to its cinematic quality, despite not being part of a soundtrack. With lyrics written by Australian pop star Sia and production from Rihanna's longtime collaborators Stargate, it combined the romance of a torch song with the grandeur of a hymn. It hit number one in over a dozen countries, including America, where it topped the Hot 100 for three weeks. "One critic called Diamonds quote Rihanna's version of a James Bond theme, and even though it was not in fact part of a soundtrack, it was indeed cinematic."
**Stay (2013)** : The second single from "Unapologetic," this was Rihanna's quietest ballad ever released, contributing to a trend of stark ballads on the charts following Adele's "Someone Like You." The song, a duet with new singer Mikky Ekko and written by Justin Parker (who had worked with Lana Del Rey), showcased Rihanna's vulnerable yet commanding vocal performance as it explored a couple struggling to maintain their relationship. It reached number three on the Hot 100 and remains one of her most consumed songs, with lifetime streams surpassing several of her number one hits. "She was both vulnerable and commanding. Featuring production and organ playing by Dave Longstreet of the indie band Dirty Projectors, the track sounded unlike anything in the previous catalogs of any of the three artists. It was treated as a significant event and became a hit, reaching number four on the Hot 100, which gave Paul McCartney his first US top 10 hit in nearly 30 years. "McCartney played acoustic guitar but did not sing. Yet, all three artists were equally build on the one off track."
**Bitch Better Have My Money (2015)** : Released in the spring of 2015, this was Rihanna's first completely solo new single in over two years, notable for its explicit title and provocative music video that featured graphic violence and nudity, much of it involving Rihanna herself. Pitchfork, which later ranked it among the top 100 songs of the decade, described the video as "the music video that launched a thousand pearl clutching critiques, along with about as many think pieces about its radical significance" for Rihanna's nonchalant wielding of a phrase often associated with men. While a modest pop hit at number 15 on the Hot 100 (driven by sales, streams, and video play), it topped Billboard's Dance Club Play Chart and R&B Hip Hop Airplay Chart, showing its edgy experiment found its audience. This song, which did not appear on her subsequent album "Anti," served as a "trial balloon" for the experimental sounds and imagery she would explore. "With the explicit title, Bitch Better Have My Money. That was the least of its provocations. Its music video featured graphic violence and nudity, much of it involving Rihanna herself."

She chanted and slurred the song title in a syncopated island patois over a "burbling and bumping" track. Rihanna interpreted "work" with a double meaning: the effort a lover must put in, and her own commitment to her career. For the second time in the 2010s, she collaborated with Drake on vocals, a combination that generated a number one hit, reaching the top of the Hot 100 in under a month. This gave Drake his second chart-topper, while for Rihanna, it marked her record 14th number one, surpassing Michael Jackson's 13. "Work" spent nine weeks atop the Hot 100, making it her second-longest run at the top after "We Found Love," and remains her last number one hit to date. "As with what's my name six years earlier, the Rihanna Drake combination generated a number one hit. Drake shot to the top of the hot 100 in under a month, giving Drake his second chart topper."

## curiosities
**Chris Brown Assault Incident (February 2009)** : The night before the Grammy Awards in 2009, Rihanna and then-boyfriend Chris Brown had a violent altercation after attending Clive Davis's annual pre-Grammies party. According to police reports, Rihanna confronted Brown about a text message from another woman, leading to Brown assaulting her, including choking her when she attempted to call the police. Brown fled the scene but later turned himself in, was charged with assault, and eventually sentenced to probation and community service. The public learned of the incident through a leaked graphic police photo of a bloodied Rihanna, a rare public display of celebrity abuse. Initially, Rihanna expressed profound embarrassment and humiliation over the leak, telling Diane Sawyer, "I get angry all over again every time I see it." Years later, she would forgive Brown and even record with him again. "According to police reports, Rihanna confronted Brown about a text message from another woman on his phone. Brown assaulted her, and when she tried to call the police, he choked her among other acts of violence."
**Taking Time Off Between Albums (2009)** : Following the Chris Brown incident, Rihanna took more time off between albums than was typical for her, with more than two years since the original "Good Girl Gone Bad" album and about a year since its "reloaded" version. Despite this, she was determined to release new music before 2009 ended, ensuring it reflected the darkness of her experience without being overwhelmed by it. "As for her music, Rihanna took more time off between albums than usual. It had been more than two years since the original Good Girl Gone Bad album and about a year since the reloaded reboot."
**Forgiveness and Collaboration with Chris Brown** : Years after the 2009 assault, Rihanna forgave Chris Brown and, seemingly in defiance of public opinion surrounding his transgressions, even recorded with him again. "Years later, Rihanna would forgive Brown and seemingly in defiance of the popular narrative around his transgressions, even recorded with him again."
**Post-Def Jam Break (2013-2014)** : After fulfilling her original seven-album contract with Def Jam, Rihanna took a significant break from music in 2013 and 2014, with no new album releases and minimal recording. This marked the first time in her career that she allowed herself the time and space to deliberate her next steps without any timetable, reflecting a shift in her career priorities. "For the first time in her career, Rihanna had earned the time and space to consider exactly what she wanted to do, with no timetable."
**The Navy Fandom** : Rihanna's fanbase, which emerged around the turn of the 2010s, adopted the name "the Navy" after a lyric in one of her deep cuts. This large and devoted following developed a "parasocial fandom," leading to in-depth interpretations of her lyrics and intense speculation about her relationships with other stars, particularly Drake. "By 2012, Rihanna's fanbase, which since the turn of the tens had called themselves the Navy after Aliric in a deep cut, had become large and devoted enough to make her album releases into events."
**Long Music Hiatus and Business Ventures (2017-2022)** : Following guest appearances on tracks in 2017, Rihanna's music career largely paused for about half a decade, with almost no new recordings until 2022, save for brief features with NERD and PartyNextDoor. During this extended break, she successfully launched a significant fashion and cosmetics empire. "And then the music from Rihanna largely stopped.
