# artist - Joni Mitchell

## albums
**Untitled (Pre-1969) - Early Catalog** : By the summer of 1969, Joni Mitchell had only released "a couple of albums" and had not yet achieved a "big breakout hit." This indicates her career was still in its relatively early stages at the time of Woodstock. "By summer of 1969, she's only got a couple of albums in her catalog thus far."

## songs
**Woodstock** : This "chilling and super beautiful" song was written by Joni Mitchell and subsequently appeared on the first Crosby, Stills, Nash & Young album. Notably, Mitchell wrote the song about the festival without having attended it herself, reportedly gathering details from her boyfriend, Graham Nash. "She wrote the song Woodstock that wound up on the first Crosby Still's Nash and Young album."
**Big Yellow Taxi (1970s)** : A song by Joni Mitchell that was released in the 1970s, indicating it came after her early career period around Woodstock. "Songs like Big Yellow Taxi or Help Me. Those are coming in the 70s."
**Help Me (1970s)** : A song by Joni Mitchell released in the 1970s, following her initial albums and before her "big breakout hit" period. "Songs like Big Yellow Taxi or Help Me. Those are coming in the 70s."
**Both Sides Now** : This song was written by Joni Mitchell and became a hit for Judy Collins in 1968, predating Mitchell's own major breakthrough as an artist. "There are songs by her like both sides now, which had been a hit for Judy Collins in 1968."

## curiosities
**Missed Woodstock Due to Traffic Fears** : Joni Mitchell was advised by her manager, David Geffen, not to attend the Woodstock Festival in August 1969. The concern was that she wouldn't be able to return from upstate New York in time for her scheduled taping of The Dick Cavett Show on the following Monday, due to widespread reports of severe traffic congestion. "They were afraid that if Joni Mitchell went to upstate New York, she wouldn't make it back."
**Dick Cavett Show Appearance (1969)** : Joni Mitchell appeared on The Dick Cavett Show in August 1969. Ironically, other performers who had attended Woodstock, such as Jefferson Airplane and members of Crosby, Stills, Nash & Young, managed to make it back to New York City for the same taping. "We have two, these are the Jeffs and Airplane, should you be so foolish as to have just joined us and Joni Mitchell."
**"Missed Opportunity" of Woodstock** : Despite being an artist whose career was still in its early stages in 1969, Joni Mitchell did not attend Woodstock. Her absence was considered a "missed opportunity" for a potential "real breakthrough moment" in her career, given the festival's capacity to launch artists. "So she really is kind of the missed opportunity of Woodstock."
**Writing "Woodstock" Without Attending** : Joni Mitchell wrote the "chilling and super beautiful" song "Woodstock" without actually attending the festival herself. She reportedly gathered details and impressions from her boyfriend, Graham Nash, who did attend. "I felt a little betrayed to learn that she hadn't been and just sort of got the details from her boyfriend."
**Relationship with Graham Nash** : At the time of the Woodstock Festival in August 1969, Joni Mitchell was dating Graham Nash, a member of Crosby, Stills, Nash & Young. This personal connection was significant as Nash's experiences at the festival reportedly informed Mitchell's songwriting for her song "Woodstock." "She was dating Graham Nash at the time."

## albums
**Rhythm Nation 1814 (1989) - Unprecedented Chart Performance** : Released in September 1989, this is a "seminal album" lauded by artists and critics for its innovative blend of pop, R&B, and hip-hop production styles. It achieved an unprecedented chart record by producing seven Top 5 hits on Billboard's Hot 100, a feat unmatched by any other album. It was the highest-selling album of 1990 but notably received no major Grammy nominations that year, though it did win "Best Music Video, Long Form." Artists like Beyoncé and TLC have cited it as a significant influence and predecessor to their work. "it's the biggest hit generating album of all time. It scored seven top 10 hits and they were all top five hits, which no other album has equaled."

## curiosities
**Grammy Nomination for Producer of the Year** : At the 1990 Grammys, Janet Jackson made history by becoming the first woman ever nominated for "Producer of the Year, Non-Classical," for her work on "Rhythm Nation 1814." Although she did not win, this nomination was a significant milestone. "It was the first time ever that a woman was nominated in that category."
**"Rhythm Nation 1814" Unsnubbed by Grammys** : Despite "Rhythm Nation 1814" being the highest-selling album of 1990, it notably received no nominations in major categories at the Grammy Awards that year, only earning four nominations in minor categories, with one win for "Best Music Video, Long Form." "Despite selling more copies than any other album that year, Rhythm Nation 1814 was not nominated in any major category at the Grammys in 1990."

# concert - Woodstock Festival (1969)

## curiosities
**Artist Breakthrough Potential** : In contrast to modern festivals where established artists headline, Woodstock 1969 had the unique capacity to launch an artist's career and "put an artist on the map." "when we think about festivals now, it's so rare for a festival to put an artist on the map."
**Performer Payment Hierarchy** : The Woodstock Festival featured a distinct hierarchy among its performing artists, which was reflected in their payment. While headliners like The Who or Blood, Sweat & Tears received higher compensation, newer acts such as Sha Na Na were paid significantly less. "there was absolutely a hierarchy of bands at Woodstock as I point out in the episode, you can even if you want to Google it, folks. You can even find out how much everybody at Woodstock was paid."
**Traffic Congestion in Bethel, NY** : The Woodstock Festival, held in Bethel, New York, was plagued by "terrible traffic" and general disorganization throughout the weekend in August 1969. Concerns about this traffic prevented some artists, like Joni Mitchell, from attending. "the traffic was terrible and everything was a mess in Bethel, New York that weekend."
**Temporary "Second Biggest City" with No Violence** : For two nights, the Woodstock Festival site in Bethel, New York, temporarily became "the second biggest city in New York," remarkable for its complete lack of violence despite the massive gathering. "like about two nights ago, that place up there was the second biggest city in New York. And it had no violence."
**"Aquarian Exposition" and Utopian Experience** : Woodstock 1969 was officially subtitled "An Aquarian Exposition," reflecting its character as an "improbable" and "truly utopian experience." Many positive memories from the festival highlight its unique, peaceful atmosphere. "It was a truly utopian experience or to use the subtitle they gave the concert. It was an aquarium experience."
**Restored Film Screening** : A restored print of the Woodstock film was screened at the Metropolitan Museum of Art as part of its "Met Friday's series" on September 13th, concurrent with an exhibition on rock instruments. "they're even showing a restored print of the Woodstock film."
