# artist - Sarah McLaughlin

## albums
**Thumbling Towards Ecstasy (Pre-1997)** : This album was a slow-growing hit that preceded her 1997 follow-up, "Surfacing." It was released more than three years before "Surfacing." "More than three years after the release of the slow-growing hit, Thumbling Towards Ecstasy."
**Surfacing (1997) - Octupple Platinum Blockbuster** : Released in 1997, this album was her follow-up to "Thumbling Towards Ecstasy." It went on to become an octupple platinum blockbuster. It debuted on the Billboard 200 at number 2 in August 1997, only prevented from reaching number 1 by the soundtrack to Will Smith's movie "Men in Black." It was the top-selling non-soundtrack single artist album in America upon its chart debut. "Her 1997 follow-up, Surfacing, would go on to be an octopple platinum blockbuster."

## songs
**Building a Mystery (1997)** : This was the first single from her album "Surfacing," and it dropped the same week McLaughlin launched the Lilith Fair tour for which she is most famous. It debuted on the modern rock chart the week ending July 5, 1997. The song reached the top 10 on modern rock tracks in mid-August, when Lilith Fair was more than 30 dates in. It did not make its Hot 100 debut until after the first run of the tour was over, indicating its rise was organic and parallel to the tour, not necessarily because of it. "Building a Mystery debuted on the modern rock chart the week ending July 5, 1997."
**Adia (1998)** : This gentle ballad was one of Sarah McLaughlin's biggest Hot 100 hits, reaching number 3 in August 1998. It reflected her rise to superstardom after Lilith Fair. However, it received no airplay on alt-rock stations. "ADA, a number three hit in August of 98."
**Angel (1999)** : An even more delicate ballad than "Adia," this song was another of Sarah McLaughlin's biggest Hot 100 hits, reaching number 4 in March 1999. Like "Adia," it received no airplay on alt-rock stations, marking her shift away from the modern rock chart after 1997. "And the even more delicate angel, a number four hit in March of 99."

## curiosities
**Lilith Fair Founder and Co-headliner** : Sarah McLaughlin launched and co-headlined Lilith Fair, which began with a small series of gigs in 1996. She conceived of the festival, approved performers, and often personally invited them. The tour was timed with the release of her album "Surfacing" and its first single "Building a Mystery," which didn't hurt her career trajectory. "The cynic might say McLaughlin had launched the tour to promote herself. And to be sure, Lilith didn't hurt her career trajectory."
**Frustration with Radio Limitations** : McLaughlin expressed frustration with radio limitations, even after women had performed well on modern rock charts in the first half of the 1990s. She pointed out the double standards, noting that male bands were played back-to-back without comment. "I'd hear all these radio stations playing Pearl Jam and Soundgarden back-to-back. Anybody talking about how that can't happen? Black hole sound won't you come. It just seemed a bit ridiculous to me."
**Writer's Block Overcome by Lilith Beta Test** : The small series of gigs Sarah McLaughlin played in 1996 with Paula Cole, Susanne Vega, and Lisa Loeb, which served as a beta test for Lilith Fair, helped McLaughlin overcome a period of writer's block. This creative breakthrough contributed to the anticipation for her next album, "Surfacing." "The mini tour also helped get McLaughlin past her writer's block."
**Overwhelmed by Lilith Fair's Success** : The success of Lilith Fair, particularly by its second year, overwhelmed McLaughlin and her team. She described the second year as nearly "killing" them due to the expanding lineup and demands of the festival. "The second year nearly killed us, McLaughlin told Vanity Fair, success allowed us some latitude with different artists, and managers looked at the festival in a different light. But the second year there were, like, 88 artists, it was insane."
**Ending the Original Lilith Fair (1999)** : McLaughlin admitted to being both gratified by Lilith Fair's success and exhausted by its demands, leading her to end the festival on a high note in 1999 in its original incarnation. This decision came as the lineup became increasingly top-heavy and diverse. "But in any case, Sarah McLaughlin openly admitted that she was both gratified by Lilith and exhausted."
**2010 Lilith Fair Revival Struggles** : More than a decade after its original run, McLaughlin was persuaded to revive Lilith Fair in 2010. However, this revival was a struggle, with more than ten dates canceled or rebooked due to poor ticket sales, despite featuring around 80 acts on three stages across nearly two dozen dates. "While the 2010 Lilith fair featured around 80 acts on three stages and did play nearly two dozen dates, more than ten dates had to be canceled or rebooked due to poor ticket sales."
**Reflections on the 2010 Revival's Intentions** : McLaughlin admitted that the intentions behind the 2010 Lilith Fair revival were "not really pure," acknowledging that she had a new record out and hoped the festival would work as it had before. She realized they had not considered how the original audience had aged and acquired new responsibilities like children, jobs, and mortgages. "Our intentions in hindsight weren't really pure, she admitted to Rolling Stone. I have a new record out and it had worked the last time. We didn't look at how all those women who came to the shows in the 90s now have children and jobs and mortgages."
**Continued Touring Post-Pandemic** : Sarah McLaughlin continues to tour to this day. In 2020, she was forced off the road by the COVID-19 pandemic, but just days before the shutdown, she recorded an acoustic song on her tour bus to benefit the Children's Cancer Association, reflecting her ongoing commitment to music and philanthropy. "But just days before the shutdown, she was on her tour bus recording an acoustic song to benefit the Children's Cancer Association."

# concert - Lilith Fair

## curiosities
**All-Woman Festival Concert Series** : Lilith Fair was a late 90s all-women festival concert series launched by Sarah McLaughlin, named after an assertive woman icon from ancient mythology. Its premise was a challenge to conventional industry wisdom that believed multi-female concerts were commercial poison. "The late 90s All Women Festival Concert series launched by Sarah McLaughlin."
**1996 Beta Test Success** : A small series of gigs in 1996, featuring Sarah McLaughlin, Paula Cole, Susanne Vega, and Lisa Loeb, served as a successful "beta test" for the Lilith concept. Despite none of the artists being at their commercial zenith, they played enormous venues to near sell-out crowds, disproving industry skepticism. "The small series of gigs Sarah McLaughlin played in 1996 with Paul Nicole, Susanne Vega, and Lisa Loeb gave the lie to this industry premise."
**1997 Launch at The Gorge Amphitheater** : The first official Lilith Fair played the Gorge Amphitheater in Washington State on July 5, 1997, the same day Sarah McLaughlin's single "Building a Mystery" debuted. The festival quickly proved commercially successful, with practically every date selling out. "That same day, the first official Lilith Fair played the Gorge Amphitheater in Washington State."
**Lineup Strength and Sell-outs (1997)** : By August 1997, as Lilith Fair played dates across the South and Midwest, practically every date was a sellout. Sarah McLaughlin had assembled a strong lineup, including all three acts from the 1996 test gigs (Suzanne Vega, Paula Cole, Lisa Loeb) and other hitmakers like Meredith Brooks and Shawn Colvin. "And practically every date was a sellout. McLaughlin had assembled a strong lineup of artists for the festival"
**Diversity Challenges in Year One** : In its first year, Lilith Fair "fell down on diversity," being branded in the media as offering a "certain variety of white cisgendered Fokie Femrock." This exclusivity was partly due to self-selection (some acts declined or badmouthed it) and a "dearth of women of color," being light on R&B and nearly devoid of hip hop. "As inclusive as the festival organizers tried to be, however, Lilith fell down on diversity in its first year, branded in the media as offering a certain variety of white cisgendered Fokie Femrock."
**Expanding Lineup in Year Two (1998)** : Many artists who performed in the first year wanted to return for Year Two, resulting in an "ever-expanding lineup of the novel and the familiar." Lilith 98 notably served as Missy Elliott's formal concert debut and featured diverse artists like Erykah Badu and Meshell Ndegeocello, and veterans like Bonnie Raitt and Sinéad O'Connor. "Many of the artists who performed the first year of Lilith wanted to come back for Year 2. The result was an ever-expanding lineup of the novel and the familiar"
**Critically Acclaimed Artists on Second Stage** : By 1998, the Lilith lineup became so "top-heavy" that some major artists, like Lucinda Williams (whose album "Car Wheels on a Gravel Road" was critically acclaimed and won the "Paz and Joppole"), accepted second stage slots because the main stage couldn't accommodate them. This was considered "a good problem to have" for the organizers. "The Lilith lineup became so top-heavy, some major artists wound up on the second stage when the main stage lineup couldn't accommodate them."
**Most Diverse Lineup in Final Original Year (1999)** : For its 1999 installment, McLaughlin and her team "went out with a bang," putting together the most diverse lineup to date. This included the chart-topping country trio The Dixie Chicks, R&B newcomer Mýa, indie pop/hip hop group Luscious Jackson, and even pure pop artists like Christina Aguilera on side stages, alongside emerging acts like Nelly Furtado and Tegan and Sara. "For the 1999 installment, McLaughlin and her team went out with a bang, putting together the most diverse lineup to date"
**Original Run Concludes (1999)** : Perhaps inevitably, Lilith Fair lasted only one more summer in its original incarnation, concluding in 1999. Sarah McLaughlin openly admitted to being both gratified and exhausted by the demands of the festival. "Perhaps it was inevitable then that Lilith Fair would last only one more summer in its original incarnation."
**2010 Revival and Cancellations** : More than a decade later, in 2010, Lilith Fair was revived, featuring around 80 acts on three stages and playing nearly two dozen dates. However, it was largely a struggle, with more than ten dates canceled or rebooked due to poor ticket sales, in part due to the American economy and changes in the concert industry. "More than a decade later, in 2010, McLaughlin was persuaded to revive the festival. But it was mostly by Sarah's and the organizers' admission a struggle."
**Ageism and Changing Demographics (2010 Revival)** : Critic Anne Powers noted that ageism might have played a role in the struggles of the 2010 Lilith revival, as Sarah McLaughlin was in her 40s. McLaughlin herself acknowledged that the age of concert-goers had changed, with the original 90s audience now having children, jobs, and mortgages. "In Vanity Fair, critic Anne Powers noted that ageism may have played a role. Quote, Sarah was in her 40s when she revived it, so picture the blowback women at midlife always get."
**Legacy and Future Speculation** : Lilith Fair's legacy includes its original goal of challenging industry norms. Discussions persist about whether the idea of an all-woman tour is outmoded and what it would take to revive it. Many believe a new Lilith would need to be taken up by an entirely new generation of diverse female, trans, and gender non-conforming performers. "Is the idea of an all woman tour outmoded? If not, what would it take?"

Now they were ready to command the stage."
**Women "Without a Country" After Lilith Fair** : After the original Lilith Fair ended in 1999, many of its performers found themselves in a unique predicament: "Too light for rock radio, too mellow for TRL." This described a situation where their music didn't fit neatly into the increasingly segmented and "broified" rock scene or the burgeoning "Millennial Megapop" of pop radio and MTV. "Would Lilith ats find a hospitable home there? To an extent yes, but the stakes for pop had gotten so much higher during the Lilith."

This era was described as the music business's zenith before digital downloading "decimated the industry," and artists commanding MTV's Total Request Live (TRL) achieved unprecedented sales.
