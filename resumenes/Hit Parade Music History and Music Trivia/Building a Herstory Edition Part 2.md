## albums
**Fumbling Towards Ecstasy - Slow-Growing Hit Predecessor to Surfacing** : McLachlan's previous album, a slow-growing hit. More than three years passed between its release and her 1997 follow-up Surfacing, and anticipation for new music built tremendously in that gap. "More than three years after the release of the slow-growing hit, Thumbling Towards Ecstasy."
**Surfacing (1997) - Octuple-Platinum Blockbuster** : Its first single, "Building a Mystery," came out the same week McLachlan launched the first official Lilith Fair tour. In August 1997 the album debuted on the Billboard 200 at No. 2, held off No. 1 only by the hit soundtrack to Will Smith's *Men in Black*. That made it the top-selling non-soundtrack, single-artist album in America. Missy Elliott's debut *Supa Dupa Fly* entered at No. 3 the same week, one slot below. When Surfacing charted, Lilith was playing sellout dates across the South and Midwest. "Surfacing would go on to be an octopple platinum blockbuster."

## songs
**Building a Mystery (1997) - Lead Single from Surfacing** : The song debuted on the modern rock chart the week ending July 5, 1997, the same day the first official Lilith Fair played the Gorge Amphitheatre in Washington State. Cynics said McLachlan launched the tour to promote herself, but the song built organically and rose alongside the tour rather than because of it. It reached the modern rock top 10 in mid-August, when Lilith was more than 30 dates in. It did not debut on the Hot 100 until the first run of the tour was over. "It seemed to rise in parallel with the tour. Not because of it."
**Adia (1998) - No. 3 Hot 100 Ballad** : A gentle ballad that reached No. 3 in August 1998. It was one of her two biggest Hot 100 hits, a reflection of how far her star had risen after Lilith. It received no airplay at all on alt-rock stations. "ADA, a number three hit in August of 98."
**Angel (1999) - No. 4 Hot 100 Ballad** : An even more delicate ballad that reached No. 4 in March 1999. Like "Adia," it got zero airplay on alt-rock radio. After 1997 McLachlan never returned to the modern rock chart. "The even more delicate angel, a number four hit in March of 99."
**Acoustic Charity Recording (2020) - Children's Cancer Association** : Just days before the COVID-19 shutdown forced her off the road in 2020, McLachlan recorded an acoustic song on her tour bus to benefit the Children's Cancer Association. During the pandemic, quiet solo performances like this one became the norm. "She was on her tour bus recording an acoustic song to benefit the Children's Cancer Association."

## curiosities
**Frustration with Radio's No-Back-to-Back-Women Rule** : Industry wisdom held that airplay of back-to-back women artists was commercial poison, even after women had done well on the modern rock charts in the early 90s. McLachlan contrasted this with stations freely playing male rock bands consecutively. "I'd hear all these radio stations playing Pearl Jam and Soundgarden back-to-back. Anybody talking about how that can't happen?"
**1996 Beta Tour Cured Her Writer's Block** : In 1996 she played a small series of gigs with Paula Cole, Suzanne Vega and Lisa Loeb. None of the four was at her commercial peak, yet together they played enormous venues to near sellout crowds, disproving the industry premise. The mini-tour also helped McLachlan get past her writer's block. "The mini tour also helped get McLaughlin past her writer's block."
**Personally Curated the Lilith Lineups** : McLachlan conceived of, approved and often personally invited the performers. She and her fellow organizers were overwhelmed by the demands of the machine they had built, and she said so to Vanity Fair. "The second year nearly killed us."
**Lilith Made Her a Pop Star, Not a Rock Star** : Her biggest Hot 100 hits came in 1998–99, but alt-rock radio ignored them. After 1997 she never returned to the modern rock chart. "Lilith Fair made Sarah McLaughlin a superstar, but primarily a pop star."
**Ending Lilith Gratified and Exhausted** : McLachlan openly admitted she was both gratified by Lilith and exhausted by it. She ended the original festival on a high note in 1999. "Sarah McLaughlin openly admitted that she was both gratified by Lilith and exhausted."
**Candid Admission About the 2010 Revival** : Speaking to Rolling Stone, McLachlan admitted the 2010 revival was partly self-promotional. She also said organizers had failed to consider how their original 90s audience's lives had changed. "Our intentions in hindsight weren't really pure... I have a new record out and it had worked the last time. We didn't look at how all those women who came to the shows in the 90s now have children and jobs and mortgages."
**Ageism and the Revival** : Critic Ann Powers told Vanity Fair that ageism may have played a role in the 2010 revival's struggles. "Sarah was in her 40s when she revived it, so picture the blowback women at midlife always get."
**Agrees a New Lilith Needs a New Generation** : McLachlan agrees that any new all-woman tour would have to be taken up by an entirely new generation of performers. She still tours to this day. "The mantle of a new Lilith would have to be taken up by an entirely new generation."

# concert - Lilith Fair

## curiosities
**Named After a Mythic Woman** : Lilith was named after an assertive woman icon from ancient mythology. Like that figure, its whole premise was a challenge and a rebuke to the industry belief that multi-female concerts were commercial poison. "Like the mythic figure, the whole premise of Lilith was a challenge, a rebuke."
**1996 Beta Test** : Sarah McLachlan, Paula Cole, Suzanne Vega and Lisa Loeb played a small series of gigs to enormous venues and near sellout crowds. This disproved the industry premise that women together wouldn't sell. "Banded together for this Lilith Fair beta test, they played enormous venues to near sell-out crowds."
**First Official Lilith (1997) - The Gorge** : The first official Lilith Fair played the Gorge Amphitheatre in Washington State the week ending July 5, 1997, and practically every date sold out. Main-stage and touring acts included all three 1996 test-gig acts, Meredith Brooks, Shawn Colvin, newcomer Fiona Apple, Tracy Chapman, the Indigo Girls, Joan Osborne, Natalie Merchant, Emmylou Harris and Mary Chapin Carpenter. The rotating second stage featured Victoria Williams, Cassandra Wilson, Abra Moore and Dar Williams. "The first official Lilith Fair played the Gorge Amphitheater in Washington State."
**Diversity Problems in Year One** : The media branded the first year as offering a certain variety of white, cisgender folkie fem-rock. Some of this was self-selection, as several acts declined. It was light on R&B and nearly devoid of hip hop despite a wealth of platinum-level 90s women rappers: Queen Latifah, Salt-N-Pepa, Da Brat, Lisa "Left Eye" Lopes. "Lilith fell down on diversity in its first year."
**Year Two (1998) - 88 Artists** : Many 1997 artists wanted to return, producing an ever-expanding lineup. New additions included Missy Elliott, Queen Latifah, Erykah Badu, Meshell Ndegeocello, Bonnie Raitt, Sinéad O'Connor and Liz Phair. The main stage became so top-heavy that major artists like Lucinda Williams ended up on the second stage. "The second year there were, like, 88 artists, it was insane."
**Year Three (1999) - Most Diverse Lineup** : The final original edition went out with a bang. It featured the Dixie Chicks, R&B newcomer Mya, indie-pop/hip-hop group Luscious Jackson, and pure pop in Christina Aguilera. Pre-debut acts Nelly Furtado and Tegan and Sara played the side stage, showing Lilith was now helping break future stars. "Lilith was now helping to break future stock."
**Performers as "Women Without a Country"** : Most Lilith performers had gold or platinum albums but not ten-times-platinum ones. By 1999 they were too light for rock radio and too mellow for MTV's TRL. "Too light for rock radio, too mellow for TRL."
**2010 Revival Struggled** : More than a decade later McLachlan was persuaded to revive the festival. It had around 80 acts on three stages and played nearly two dozen dates, but more than ten dates were cancelled or rebooked due to poor ticket sales. Returning acts included Sheryl Crow, the Indigo Girls, Erykah Badu, Emmylou Harris and Suzanne Vega, alongside Metric, Ingrid Michaelson and Miranda Lambert. The economy and concert industry were still recovering from the Great Recession. "More than ten dates had to be canceled or rebooked due to poor ticket sales."

# genre - Alternative Rock

## curiosities
**The "Bro-ification" of Rock Radio** : The bro-ification of rock radio accelerated during Lilith's three sellout years. By 1999 Limp Bizkit, Korn, Godsmack, Kid Rock and Creed led so-called alternative music. Even less aggressive bands like Lit, Sugar Ray and Third Eye Blind filled songs with party-bro lyrics many steps removed from Nirvana and Pearl Jam. "The broification of rock radio had continued, actually accelerated during the three years Lilith was selling out across the land."

## curiosities

# curiosity

**Millennial Megapop Peak (1999–2000)** : Megapop acts commanding MTV's Total Request Live set new sales and chart benchmarks in 1999 and 2000. This was the music business's peak before downloading decimated the industry, and those acts sold at levels no Lilith headliner except the Dixie Chicks ever reached. "The peak of the music business before downloading decimated the industry."
**Men in Black Soundtrack Blocked Surfacing** : The then-hot soundtrack to Will Smith's *Men in Black* kept McLachlan's *Surfacing* from debuting at No. 1 in August 1997. "If it hadn't been for the then hot soundtrack to Will Smith's movie Men in Black, surfacing would have debuted at number 1."
**Back-to-Back Women Atop the Hot 100 (2020)** : In spring 2020, Billboard noted that in three out of four weeks the No. 1 song was a duet by two women soloists: "Say So," the "Savage" remix, and "Rain on Me." This answered the old radio complaint that led to Lilith. "Women artists are all over the radio again, teaming up with each other and literally topping the charts back to back."
**Candidates for a 2020s Lilith** : A new wave of Gen Z women were selling out arenas going into the 2020s, most notably Billie Eilish. A Lilith for the 2020s would also need to include trans and gender-nonconforming performers such as Kim Petras. "Lilith for the 20s would have to include her, as well as trans and gender non-conforming performers, such as Kim Petrus."
