## albums
**Fresh Horses (1995) - The Album That Lost to The Beatles** : After a two-year gap without a new studio album, unprecedented for him, Brooks recorded Fresh Horses in 1995. He and his label, Capitol Nashville, announced the title over the summer of 1995 and set the release for three days before Thanksgiving 1995, the same week Capitol released The Beatles' Anthology. Some at the EMI-Capitol conglomerate were reportedly leery of the timing. Brooks put a couple of million dollars of his own money into TV advertising for the album. It opened to 480,000 copies, one of his best sales weeks ever, but finished a distant No. 2 behind The Beatles' Anthology 1. That made it his first studio album to miss No. 1 in the SoundScan era. It still sold about 3 million copies in its first six months and eventually 7 million. Brooks took its underperformance and the muted fan response hard. "But it was a distant number two to The Beatles."
**Sevens (1997) - Biggest Country Album Debut to Date** : Brooks held back his seventh album for months during a dispute with his label over promotional plans, because he did not want it to meet the same tepid greeting as Fresh Horses. It reached stores in late November 1997, more than three months after the Central Park concert, and the delay appeared to stoke fans' pent-up demand. It opened to nearly 900,000 copies in its first week, the biggest debut by a country album to date. Its first single, Longneck Bottle, hit No. 1 one week later. By the time the second single, Two Piña Coladas, reached No. 1 in spring 1998, the album was well past quintuple platinum, and it eventually sold 10 million. "Sevens opened to nearly 900,000 in its first week, the biggest debut by a country album to date."
**The Limited Series (1998) - Box Set of First Six Albums** : In spring 1998 Brooks reissued his first six albums as a box set with new bonus tracks on each disc. It was part of a strategy that used the RIAA's rule of counting multi-disc sets once per disc, at a time when he was rumored to be chasing The Beatles' title as the RIAA's King of Sales. One bonus track, his cover of Bob Dylan's To Make You Feel My Love, became a No. 1 country hit. "Garth Brooks knew how this RIA math worked, and so he began putting out a lot more multi-disc sets."
**Double Live (1998) - First Live Album and Million-Seller Debut** : This was Brooks's first live album and a double-disc set, released for the 1998 holiday season. It drew on nearly a decade as a top concert draw whose shows had pyrotechnics uncommon for country and sellout crowds on a scale previously seen only among rock acts. Brooks wanted it to be the first album to sell a million copies in its first week. His team priced the two-disc set at single-CD prices, and one major account reported nine days of sales, covering two weekends instead of the usual seven days. It debuted with 1.085 million copies, the biggest week to date for any album in SoundScan history. "By hook or by crook, Double Live debuted to 1.085 million in week one, the biggest week to date of any album in sound scan history."
**Scarecrow (2001) - Final Album of the Imperial Period** : Sensing that his run as a consistent chart-topper might be ending, Brooks released this as the last studio album of his long imperial period before a self-imposed retirement from recording. It opened in late November 2001 with 466,000 copies, comparable to his openings from half a decade earlier. It eventually went quintuple platinum and produced six Top 40 country hits, including the Top 5 Wrapped Up in You. "But Scarecrow proved Garth as Garth was still a draw."
**Blame It All on My Roots (2013) - Walmart-Exclusive Box Set** : This box set marked Brooks's return in 2013. It was sold only at Walmart and briefly topped the Billboard 200. "Brooks got back in the game in 2013, releasing a box set sold exclusively at Walmart, called Blame It All on My Roots, that briefly topped the Billboard 200."
**Man Against Machine (2014) - Return to Recording and GhostTunes** : With this album Brooks returned to recording, still insisting on doing things his own way. Even at the peak of digital music he refused to sell his albums on iTunes or stream them on Spotify. To release Man Against Machine, he launched his own digital music service, GhostTunes, which lasted three years before he signed a digital exclusivity deal with Amazon. "Even at the peak of digital music, Brooks refused to sell his albums on iTunes or stream them on Spotify."

## songs
**She's Every Woman (1995) - Comeback No. 1** : This ballad in the style of James Taylor came out several months ahead of Fresh Horses and reached No. 1 on Hot Country Singles in just seven weeks. It showed that fans still wanted him after his two-year absence. "A sign Brooks had stayed away long enough to be missed and could pick up where he'd left off."
**Hard Luck Woman (1994) - Kiss Tribute Cover** : Brooks recorded this for the 1994 tribute album Kiss My Ass: Classic Kiss Regrouped. He turned the 1976 Kiss track from Rock and Roll Over into an acoustic-style rave-up, with the members of Kiss backing him. It was a modest hit that reached the lower rungs of the Adult Contemporary chart and the mainstream pop radio chart. That success encouraged him to try adapting a newer rock song. It peaked at No. 23 on Hot Country Singles, making it Brooks's first promoted single to miss the country Top 20. "It wasn't rock enough to get airplay at rock stations, but it didn't connect with country listeners either."
**The Beaches of Cheyenne (1996) - Return to No. 1** : A few months after The Fever flopped, this more traditional country ballad from Fresh Horses took Brooks back to No. 1. ## curiosities
**Two-Year Break for Family** : Before Fresh Horses, Brooks went two years without a new studio album, an unprecedented gap for him. In interviews he said he needed the time for his family and his marriage. "he said he needed the break to tend to his family and shore up his marriage."
**Love of 70s Hard Rock** : Besides his early love of acoustic soft rock, Brooks was a fan of flashy 1970s hard rock, from Aerosmith to Queen, which led him to adapt rock songs into country. "Brooks also loved his share of flashy 70s hard rock from Erasmith to Queen."
**The "No-Lose Situation" Against The Beatles** : Asked about releasing Fresh Horses in the same week as The Beatles' Anthology, Brooks said he knew what he had signed up for. As Capitol's top-selling current artist, and someone who referred to himself in the third person and the royal "we," he had the clout to make that call even against Capitol's biggest band of all time. "If we get pounded, it's by the Beatles, and everyone's expecting us to get pounded anyway."
**1996 LA Times Interview and Second Hiatus** : In spring 1996 Brooks told Robert Hilburn of the Los Angeles Times that he would quit if sales stopped showing he was making an impact. The same article noted that, just seven years into his career, his total US album sales were the highest of any solo act in RIAA history, second only to The Beatles. Discouraged, Brooks took another two-year break from recording and was mostly absent from the country singles chart for most of a year. "If the record and ticket sales don't tell me that I'm stirring things up or changing people's lives, then I think it's time for me to hang it up."
**Mentor to and Later Husband of Trisha Yearwood** : Brooks met Trisha Yearwood in Nashville at the end of the 1980s, when she sang backup on his debut album. He introduced her to a producer who helped get her signed and invited her to open his concerts. They stayed platonic friends for years. Five years after their 1997 duet, both having divorced their spouses, they began dating, and they married three years after that. They later raised his three daughters from his previous marriage during his long hiatus. "And the relationship would eventually become more than platonic."
**Chasing The Beatles' RIAA Crown** : As Brooks climbed the list of all-time album sellers, the industry rumor was that he was trying to overtake The Beatles as the RIAA's King of Sales. "he was aiming to topple the RIA's King of Sales, The Beatles."
**Never a Retail Singles Artist** : Brooks never released his radio hits as retail singles for most of his career, so none of them appeared on the Hot 100, including The Dance, Friends in Low Places and Shameless, until Billboard changed its rules in December 1998. "not a single one of them, not the dance, not friends in low places, not even shameless, had appeared on the Hot 100."
**The Chris Gaines Alter Ego and The Lamb** : Brooks had wanted to star in a movie for some time and reportedly turned down roles in films such as Twister and Saving Private Ryan because he wanted to create his first role himself. He conceived a thriller/biopic about a fictional rock star with floppy hair and a goatee named Chris Gaines. The film, The Lamb, was greenlit at Paramount Pictures, but Brooks never starred in it, and it was shelved a little over a year later. It is described as the strangest thing he has ever attempted. "A move that is still more than two decades later, the strangest thing he ever attempted, becoming someone else."
**Saturday Night Live Double Duty** : While promoting Chris Gaines, Brooks appeared on the Today Show, taped an episode of VH1's Behind the Music presenting Gaines's life story, and stayed in his dual identity as both host and musical guest on Saturday Night Live. The SNL episode was well received. "He even maintained this dual identity as a host and musical guest on Saturday Night Live."
**Only Top 40 Pop Hit** : Lost in You, released under the Chris Gaines name, remains Garth Brooks's only Top 40 pop hit. "Although Lost in You remains Garth's only top 40 pop hit to this day."
**Influence on 21st-Century Country** : Even while he was largely absent, Brooks's mix of melodic pop sensibility and unashamed country twang shaped later hitmakers who acknowledged his influence, including Kenny Chesney, Keith Urban and Carrie Underwood. Country crossover achievements of the late 1990s would have been unimaginable without his earlier breakthroughs. "he cast a long shadow over the country hitmakers of the 21st century."
**Drive-In Theater Concert During the Pandemic** : During the COVID-19 pandemic, Brooks held an online concert broadcast to 300 drive-in theaters across North America. Industry observers called it one of the most ingenious ways around the limits on live performance. "He held an online concert that was broadcast to 300 Drive In theaters across North America."

**In the Life of Chris Gaines (1999) - Garth Brooks Presents** : Brooks conceived this as a "pre-soundtrack" for the film The Lamb, something like a Broadway concept album, with him performing as Chris Gaines. The music was everything except country: some light R&B, some rock, mostly pop. Because it was supposed to collect songs by a musician with a long career, Brooks wanted to call it Chris Gaines' Greatest Hits. It came out in late summer 1999 as Garth Brooks Presents... In the Life of Chris Gaines. It debuted at No. 2 on the Billboard 200 but fell to No. 16 within two weeks. Fans were confused by booklet photos of an unrecognizable Brooks posing as Gaines in different stages of his career, wearing tights or a bowler hat. Retailers were reportedly shipped more than 3 million copies, at least a million of which went unsold, though the album still went double platinum. "The SNL episode was well received. The album was not."

# concert - Central Park Concert 1997 (Garth Stock)

## curiosities
**Free Show in the North Meadow** : On August 7, 1997, Garth Brooks and his band played a free show in New York's Central Park, broadcast live on HBO. Brooks wanted to break the myth that his audience lived only in the rural American Heartland. His sales figures showed that cities like New York accounted for a significant share, and he knew fans from across the country would travel for it. His team built the largest stage the North Meadow had ever seen, and so many fans drove into the city that the show was jokingly called "Garth Stock." "so many of his fans caravaned into the city that week that the gig was jokingly dubbed Garth Stock."
**Largest Concert in Park History** : Central Park had previously hosted huge concerts by Paul Simon, Simon & Garfunkel and Diana Ross, and Brooks reportedly drew more than all of them. New York City expected about 300,000 people, but crowd estimates on the night were at least 750,000, and a later fire department estimate put the crowd at just under a million. Attendance figures for Central Park are notoriously inexact, but the show was generally agreed to be the park's largest concert ever. "A later estimate by the fire department put the crowd size at just shy of a million."
**Billy Joel Guest Appearance** : Billy Joel performed two of his own songs, including New York State of Mind, and played piano and sang on Brooks's Ain't Goin' Down ('Til the Sun Comes Up). "Joel not only performed a pair of his own songs, including New York State of Mind."

# curiosity

**RIAA Double-Album Math** : The RIAA's gold and platinum certification rules count double albums twice, which inflates the certifications of multi-disc sets. Garth Brooks used this by releasing more multi-disc sets. "What's quirky about the RIA's certification rules for gold and platinum albums is that double albums are counted twice."
**Billboard Allows Radio-Only Songs on the Hot 100 (December 1998)** : After eight years of the music industry moving away from retail singles, Billboard gave in and changed the Hot 100 rules so that radio-only songs could chart for the first time, effective December 1998. Previously invisible radio hits such as the Goo Goo Dolls' Iris suddenly appeared, as did Garth Brooks's It's Your Song. "They changed Hot 100 rules to allow radio only songs to appear on the chart for the first time."
**Double Live's Nine-Day Sales Week** : To reach a million first-week sales for Double Live, Brooks's team sold the two-disc set at single-CD prices and had one major account report nine days of sales, covering two weekends rather than the usual seven days. "getting one major account to report nine days of sales for the week, including two weekends instead of the usual seven."
