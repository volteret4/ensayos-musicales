## albums
**Fresh Horses (1995)** : This album was recorded in 1995 after a two-year hiatus, an unprecedented gap for Brooks who said he needed time for family and marriage. Capital Nashville announced its release for three days before Thanksgiving 1995, coinciding with The Beatles' *Anthology 1*. Brooks self-funded millions in TV advertising. It debuted at #2, selling 480,000 copies in its first week, a record for him but behind The Beatles. It was his first studio album not to reach number one in the Soundscan era. Despite initial underperformance and his disappointment, the album eventually sold 3 million copies in its first six months and 7 million overall, continuing to generate hits. "If we get pounded, it's by the Beatles, and everyone's expecting us to get pounded anyway."
**Sevens (1997)** : Brooks' seventh album, delayed for months due to a dispute with his label over promotional plans, as he wanted to avoid a tepid reception like *Fresh Horses*. It was released in late November 1997, more than three months after his Central Park concert. The delay appeared to stoke fan demand, as it opened to nearly 900,000 sales in its first week, marking the biggest debut by a country album to date. It quickly achieved quintuple platinum status and went on to sell ten million copies. "The Sevens album was well-passed quintuple platinum on its way to eventual sales of ten million."
**The Limited Series (1998)** : Released in the spring of 1998, this box set re-issued Brooks' first six albums. It included new bonus tracks on each disc, a strategy Brooks employed to leverage the RIA's rule of counting double albums twice towards certification, aiming to surpass The Beatles in total album sales. "Brooks knew how this RIA math worked, and so he began putting out a lot more multi-disc sets."
**Double Live (1998)** : Brooks' first live album, released as a double CD set during the 1998 holiday season. Brooks aimed for it to be the first album to debut with a million sales in its first week. To achieve this, "Team Garth pulled every trick in the book," including pricing the two-disc set at single-CD prices and arranging for one major account to report nine days of sales instead of the usual seven. It debuted with 1.085 million sales in its first week, setting a new record for the biggest week of any album in Soundscan history. "Double Live debuted to 1.085 million in week one, the biggest week to date of any album in sound scan history."
**Garth Brooks Presents... In the Life of Chris Gaines (1999)** : This album was released in late summer 1999 as a "pre-soundtrack" for Brooks' planned film *The Lamb*, in which he would star as fictional rock star Chris Gaines. The music diverged from country, incorporating light R&B, rock, and pop. Initially intended to be titled *Chris Gaines' Greatest Hits*, it aimed to portray Gaines as a musician with a long career. Despite debuting at #2 on the Billboard 200, it fell to #16 within two weeks, confusing fans with photos of an unrecognizable Brooks in the CD booklet. Over 3 million copies were shipped to retailers, but at least a million remained unsold. It eventually earned a double platinum certification. "In the life of Chris Gaines, debuted on the Billboard 200 at number two, but it was down to number 16 within two weeks."
**Scarecrow (2001)** : Released in 2001, this was the final studio album of Brooks' "long Imperial period" before his self-imposed retirement from recording. It proved that "Garth as Garth" remained a strong draw, opening in late November 2001 with 466,000 copies sold, comparable to his albums from five years prior. The album eventually achieved quintuple platinum status and generated six Top 40 country hits, including "Wrapped Up in You." "Scarecrow eventually went to Quintuple Platon and generated six top 40 country hits, including the top five hit wrapped up in you."
**Blame It All on My Roots (2013)** : A box set released in 2013, sold exclusively at Walmart, that briefly topped the Billboard 200 chart. "Brooks got back in the game in 2013, releasing a box set sold exclusively at Walmart, called Blame It All on My Roots, that briefly topped the Billboard 200."
**Man Against Machine (2014)** : Brooks' return to recording albums in 2014. As part of its release, and due to his refusal to sell albums on iTunes or stream on Spotify, Brooks launched his own digital music service, Ghost Tunes. "So as part of the release of his 2014 album, Man Against Machine, Brooks launched his own digital music service, which he called Ghost Tunes."
**Fun (upcoming)** : Brooks' upcoming album, whose first single was announced during the COVID-19 lockdown as a duet with his wife, Trisha Yearwood, covering Lady Gaga and Bradley Cooper's "Shallow." "Brooks announced that the first single from his next album, Fun, would be a duet with his wife, Trisha Yearwood, a cover of the Lady Gaga Bradley Cooper song, Shallow."

## songs
**She's Every Woman (1995)** : A James Taylor-like ballad released as a single several months before the *Fresh Horses* album. It quickly soared to number one on the Hot Country Singles chart in just seven weeks, signaling that Brooks' absence had made fans miss him and that he could resume his success. "She's every woman, a James Taylor-like ballad, was issued several months ahead of Garth's next album, and it's soared to number one on Hot Country Singles in just seven weeks."
**Hard Luck Woman (1994)** : Brooks' acoustic-style cover of a 1976 track from Kiss' *Rock and Roll Over* album, featuring the members of Kiss backing him up. It was contributed to the 1994 tribute album *Kiss My Ass, Classic Kiss Regrouped*. While only a modest hit, it managed to crack the lower rungs of both the adult contemporary and mainstream pop radio charts, encouraging Brooks to adapt more rock songs. "He turned Hard Luck Woman, a 1976 track from Kiss' rock and roll over album, into an acoustic style rave-up with the members of Kiss backing him up."
**The Fever (1995)** : Brooks' rodeo rock anthem adaptation of Aerosmith's 1993 song "Fever" from their album *Get a Grip*. Released to radio stations the week before *Fresh Horses* arrived, it began climbing the country chart but ultimately flopped. Fans were unsure how to interpret it, finding it "not rock enough" for rock stations and failing to connect with country listeners, peaking at #23 on Hot Country Singles and becoming Brooks' first promoted single to miss the Country Top 20. "Fans didn't seem to know what to make of The Fever. It wasn't rock enough to get airplay at rock stations, but it didn't connect with country listeners either."
**The Beaches of Cheyenne (1996)** : A more traditional country ballad from the *Fresh Horses* album that brought Brooks back to number one on the charts a few months after "The Fever" flopped. "The more traditional country ballad The Beaches of Shiam brought Brooks back to number one."
**Shameless (1991)** : A Billy Joel song that Brooks turned into a country smash. Billy Joel himself later performed it with Brooks at his 1997 Central Park Concert. "Among the guests, Brooks welcomed to his historic show was Native New Yorker Billy Joel, writer of the song Brooks turned into a hit, Shameless."

It reached number two on Hot Country Singles, marking Brooks' return to the charts after months of inactivity. This collaboration occurred while Brooks and Yearwood were still platonic friends, preceding their eventual marriage. "One of those was, in another's eyes, a duet with Garth Brooks, who had to date remained a platonic friend of hers."

The song has also been covered by artists like Billy Joel and Adele. "Brooks's version of the song topped Hot Country Singles in August 1998."

Notably, it was Brooks' first track to ever reach the Billboard Hot 100, peaking at number 62. Its appearance on the Hot 100 was due to Billboard's December 1998 rule change allowing radio-only songs on the chart, as Brooks had historically not released his radio hits as retail singles. "It's Your Song was also Garth's first track to ever reach the Billboard Hot 100, where it peaked at number 62."

It marked the first time Brooks issued a song as a retail single (on cassette and CD). Thanks to his existing profile, it debuted strongly at #5 on the Hot 100, but quickly fell out of the Top 10 in two weeks and the Top 40 in seven, becoming the only Top 40 pop hit for both Chris Gaines and Garth Brooks to this day. "For the first time in the career of the actual Garth Brooks, he issued the song as a retail single on both Cassette and CD."
**It Don't Matter to the Sun (1999)** : A track from the Chris Gaines album that "sounded country enough" to peak at number 24 on Hot Country Singles, making it one of the few Gaines tracks to connect with country audiences. "One track from the Gaines album It Don't Matter to the Sun sounded country enough that it peaked on Hot Country singles at a decent number 24."

## curiosities
**Two-Year Recording Hiatus (1993-1995)** : Brooks took an unprecedented two-year break from releasing new studio albums, stating in interviews that he needed the time to focus on his family and strengthen his marriage. This hiatus ended with the recording of his 1995 album *Fresh Horses*. "Garth Brooks had let two years go by without a new studio album, an unprecedented gap for him."
**Competition with The Beatles (1995)** : Brooks' album *Fresh Horses* was released the same week as The Beatles' *Anthology 1*. Despite some at EMI Capital Conglomerate being "leery" of the conflict, Brooks viewed it as a "no-lose situation," stating that being outsold by The Beatles was expected, but holding his own would make country music and himself look stronger. He reportedly invested millions of his own money into TV advertising for *Fresh Horses* to compete. "If we get pounded, it's by the Beatles, and everyone's expecting us to get pounded anyway."
**Influence at Capital Records (1995)** : As Capital's top-selling current artist, Brooks was influential enough to "call the shots" regarding his release schedule, even when it meant competing with Capital's all-time biggest band, The Beatles. He also self-funded television advertising for his album *Fresh Horses*. "Brooks was big enough to call the shots, even against Capital's all-time biggest band."
**Disappointment over Fresh Horses (1996)** : Brooks reacted strongly to the underperformance of *Fresh Horses* and its muted fan response, despite its eventual multi-million sales. By spring 1996, he told Robert Hillburn of the LA Times, "If the record and ticket sales don't tell me that I'm stirring things up or changing people's lives, then I think it's time for me to hang it up." This led to another two-year break from recording. "But Garth took the underperformance of fresh horses and the muted fan response hard."
**Highest US Album Sales for a Solo Act (1996)** : By 1996, just seven years into his career, Brooks had accumulated the highest total US album sales for any solo act in Recording Industry Association of America (RIA) history, second only to The Beatles overall. "Brooks's total US album sales were the highest for any solo act in recording industry association of America history. Second only to the Beatles."
**Mentorship of Trisha Yearwood (Late 1980s-1991)** : Brooks played a significant role in launching Trisha Yearwood's career. They met in Nashville in the late 1980s when she sang backup for his debut album. Brooks introduced her to a producer who helped her get signed, and he invited her to open his concerts. Her debut single, "She's in Love with the Boy," became a number one smash in 1991, partly due to his mentorship. "He introduced her to a producer who helped get her signed, and Garth even invited her to open his concerts."
**Marriage to Trisha Yearwood (2005)** : Five years after their duet "In Another's Eyes" in 1997, and after both had divorced their respective spouses, Brooks and Yearwood began dating. Three years later, in 2005, they married. "The relationship would eventually become more than platonic. Five years later, after Yearwood and Brooks had each divorced their respective spouses, they began dating, and three years after that, they would marry."
**Goal to Shatter Audience Demographics Myth (1997)** : Brooks aimed to disprove the myth that his audience was exclusively rural and based in the American Heartland. He recognized significant album sales in cities like New York and planned his Central Park concert to draw a national urban audience. "The biggest myth, Brooks longed to shatter about his audience, was that they were all based in rural areas of the American Heartland."
**Central Park Concert Stage & Nickname (1997)** : For his free Central Park concert on August 7, 1997, Brooks' team constructed the largest stage the North Meadow had ever seen. The influx of fans caravanning into New York City that week led the event to be jokingly dubbed "Garth Stock." "Brooks's team set up the largest stage the North Meadow had ever seen, and so many of his fans caravaned into the city that week that the gig was jokingly dubbed Garth Stock."
**Aim to Overtake The Beatles in RIA Sales (Late 1990s)** : As Brooks' album sales climbed, there was a rumor within the music industry that he was intentionally aiming to surpass The Beatles as the RIA's "King of Sales." He actively leveraged RIA's certification rules, which count double albums twice, by releasing more multi-disc sets like *The Limited Series* and *Double Live*. "The rumor within the music industry... was that he was aiming to topple the RIA's King of Sales, The Beatles."
**Digital Music Stance (2010s)** : At the peak of digital music, Brooks initially refused to sell his albums on iTunes or stream them on Spotify, preferring to control his digital distribution. In 2014, he launched his own digital music service, Ghost Tunes, for the release of his album *Man Against Machine*. Ghost Tunes lasted three years before Brooks signed an exclusive digital deal with Amazon. "Even at the peak of digital music, Brooks refused to sell his albums on iTunes or stream them on Spotify."
**COVID-19 Online Concert (2020)** : During the COVID-19 lockdown, Brooks held an online concert that was broadcast live to 300 drive-in theaters across North America. Industry observers praised this as an "ingenious" method to circumvent restrictions on live performances during a challenging year for the music business. "He held an online concert that was broadcast to 300 Drive In theaters across North America."

"The Beatles White Album, for example, their only studio double album is certified by the RIA at 24 times platinum for 12 million in sales, even though the White Album has sold only about as many copies as the 12 times platinum Abbey Road."

**Recording Industry Association of America (RIA) Certification Rules** : The RIA's rules for certifying gold and platinum albums have a quirk: double albums are counted twice towards certification totals. For example, The Beatles' *White Album* is certified 24 times platinum for 12 million sales, even though it sold about the same number of physical copies as the 12 times platinum *Abbey Road*. Garth Brooks was aware of these rules and strategically released multi-disc sets to boost his certified sales. "What's quirky about the RIA's certification rules for gold and platinum albums is that double albums are counted twice."
**Billboard Hot 100 Chart Rule Change (December 1998)** : After eight years of the music industry moving away from retail singles, Billboard changed its Hot 100 rules in December 1998 to allow radio-only songs to appear on the chart for the first time. This change meant that songs like Goo Goo Dolls' "Iris," previously "invisible" on the Hot 100 due to lack of a retail release, suddenly appeared. This also led to Garth Brooks' radio hits, which he never issued as retail singles, appearing on the chart. "Billboard finally caved.
