## albums
**Lady of the Night (1974)** : Donna Summer's debut album, entirely produced and written by Giorgio Moroder and Pete Bellotte. It was a pastiche of styles, showcasing the breadth of Summer's pop influences. The title track was an homage to 60s girl group pop, and its first single, "The Hostage," was a story song with melodramatic spoken-word segments and a hybrid of R&B and Symphonic Rock. "The Hostage" was a number two hit in the Netherlands, and "Lady of the Night" was a number four Dutch hit and minor top 40 hit in Germany. "Not a bad start for the summer Moroder Balotti team up."
**Love to Love You Baby (1975)** : Donna Summer's first American album release, issued jointly by Moroder's Oasis label and Casablanca Records in the late summer of 1975. The album featured the 17-minute retitled "Love to Love You Baby" on Side A, filling a whole side of vinyl, popularizing the concept of the extended mix and reimagining disco as album-length music, a concept similar to album-oriented rock (AOR). It sold unusually well for a disco LP, going Gold and peaking at an impressive number 11 on the Billboard album chart, alongside LPs by Electric Light Orchestra and Peter Frampton. "The album sold unusually well for a disco LP, going to the Gold and Peaking at an impressive number 11 on the Billboard album chart."
**A Love Trilogy (1976)** : Released in March 1976, while the *Love to Love You Baby* album was still in the top 40. This album continued the concept of extended length tracks, leading off with the 18-minute track "Try Me, I Know We Can Make It" taking up all of Side A. Tracks from this album reached number one on Billboard's Club Play chart, cementing Summer's status as disco's emerging queen. It went gold within weeks of its release, but did not generate a Top 40 pop hit. "Tracks from both a Love trilogy and four seasons of Love Reach Number One, cementing Summer's status as disco's emerging queen."
**Four Seasons of Love (1976)** : Released just seven months after *A Love Trilogy*, this was a full-blown concept album composed of a suite of six to eight-minute tracks named for the seasons, from "Winter Melody" to "Spring Affair." Tracks from this album also reached number one on Billboard's Club Play chart, further cementing her club queen status. It went gold within weeks of its release, but did not generate a Top 40 pop hit. "On Billboard's Club Play... tracks from both a Love trilogy and four seasons of Love Reach Number One."
**I Remember Yesterday (1977)** : A concept album devised by Pete Bellotte. Side A featured tracks recalling the 1940s, 50s, and 60s, while Side B featured current-sounding tracks and closed with a song representing the future, which turned out to be "I Feel Love." This album cemented Donna Summer as both a pop force and, with Giorgio Moroder and Pete Bellotte, an all-time innovator. "What finally cemented Donna Summer as both a pop force and, with Georgiomirotr and Pete Balotti, an all-time innovator came with her next album."
**Once Upon a Time (1977)** : Donna Summer's first double album, a fairy tale-like concept album whose lyrics retold the rags-to-riches story of Cinderella in disco form. This idea was driven by Donna herself, unlike previous single album concepts largely conceived by Pete Bellotte. It was Summer's fifth consecutive gold album, peaking at number 26 on the album chart, and arguably kicked off her "Imperial phase." "Once upon a time was Summer's fifth consecutive gold album, and although it only peaked at number 26 on the album chart, it arguably kicked off the start of Donna's Imperial phase."
**Live and More (1978)** : An ambitious double-live album capturing her eclectic live show, which was known to run the gamut from disco to jazz to showtunes. It contained three vinyl sides of concert material and a fourth side reserved for a studio recording, which featured a symphonic suite centered around "MacArthur Park." This became Donna's first number one album the same week "MacArthur Park" topped the Hot 100. It spent nearly a year and a half on the album chart and became her first platinum album. "The same week MacArthur Park topped the Hot 100, Live and More, became Donna's first number one album."
**Bad Girls (1979)** : Released in May 1979, this was Donna Summer's third consecutive double album and her second in a row to top the album chart. It was her longest studio album ever and her best seller, going double platinum, and was also her most acclaimed and sonically diverse work. It received praise from rock critics, including *Rolling Stone*, who called it "the only great disco album other than *Saturday Night Fever*." Its 15 songs, about half written by Summer, ranged from the balladry of "There Will Always Be A U" to the electrodance of "Our Love." "Bad Girls was not only Summer's longest studio album ever, it was her best seller, going double platinum, and her most acclaimed and sonically diverse work."

## songs
**If You Walk In Alone (1969)** : An early Northern Soul single for German label Phillips, released at a time when Donna Summer was releasing singles for various European labels. The title was a badly translated phrase that Donna never actually sings in the song, despite her potent young voice. "A phrase Donna never actually sings in the song, although her young voice is potent."
**Sally Go Round the Roses (1971)** : A cover of a 1963 top 10 pop and R&B hit by The Jane Nets, recorded for MCA Records' UK subsidiary in London. Donna's 1971 cover "amped up the soul, and pumped up the funk," showcasing her powerful vocals and versatility. "Donna's cover of the hit in 1971, Amped Up the Soul, and Pumped Up the Funk."
**Denver Dream (1974)** : This single for German label Lark Records was pivotal as it marked Donna Summer's first collaboration with producers Giorgio Moroder and Pete Bellotte, and was the first time her stage name appeared on a single cover spelled as "Donna Summer" (S-U-M-M-E-R), a spelling that stuck. "That single, Denver Dream in 1974, was pivotal, and not just because the single cover read Donna Sommer for the first time."
**Love to Love You Baby (1975)** : A track Donna conceived, initially thinking it might make a good demo for another artist, and which she at first regarded as a lark. It featured her voice at its smallest to date, a whispery sex kitten vocal, a deliberate choice to show versatility. Giorgio Moroder convinced her to record it, and they "camped it up" with orgasmic moans. An edited version reached number 13 in the Netherlands in early 1975. Casablanca Records founder Neil Bogart then requested an extended version, leading to a 17-minute re-recording (with a string section and additional backup singers) that filled one side of an LP, released in late summer 1975. A five-minute radio edit peaked at number two on the Hot 100 in February 1976. This song is enshrined in the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as one of its "songs that shaped rock and roll" and signaled disco's coming of age by reimagining it as album-length music and popularizing the extended mix. "Bursting with excitement, Boegart called back Moroder in the middle of the night, Germany time, to tell him not only that he wanted to distribute the track, but that he in summer should make Love to Love You longer, many times longer than a three-minute single, long enough to fill the entire side of an LP record."
**I Feel Love (1977)** : The final track and lead-off single to the *I Remember Yesterday* album, this song is generally agreed to have "single-handedly invented electronic dance music." It was the first hit single with an entirely synthesized backing track, with the only organic element being Donna Summer's voice. Summer helped Moroder and Bellotte arrange its complex vocal melody and co-wrote the mantra-like lyric with future husband Bruce Sudano. Its fluttery vocals contrasted with the driving, mechanistic backing track, proving an irresistible combination. It reached number six on the Hot 100, was her first top 10 hit and gold single since "Love to Love You Baby," and her first number one hit in England. Its "maniacal precision of its grid-like groove of sequenced synth pulses" inspired artists like Blondie, The Human League, David Bowie, and Brian Eno, and blazed the path for genres like high energy, Italo, house, techno, and trance. "I Feel Love is generally agreed to have single-handedly invented electronic dance music."
**Loves Unkind (1977)** : An "Abba-esque" song that reached number three in England during a period when Donna Summer became a more consistent hitmaker on both sides of the Atlantic. "In England, she reached number three with the Abba-esque Loves Unkind."
**I Love You (1977)** : A "lush track" from the *Once Upon a Time* album that generated another top 40 pop hit and a number one club hit in America, further cementing Summer's commercial success during her Imperial phase. "And in America Summer's sixth album, once upon a time, generated another top 40 pop and number one club hit, with the Lush Track I Love You."
**Last Dance (1978)** : Featured in the musical film *Thank God It's Friday*, where Summer also appeared in her only film acting experience. This song is considered a quintessential disco record despite its highly unusual structure, starting as a ballad for more than a third of its running time before picking up tempo after nearly a minute and a half. Co-producer Bob Esty proposed this structure, modeling it after Diana Ross's "Ain't No Mountain High Enough," knowing Summer could handle it. It reached number three on the Hot 100 in the summer of 1978 and won an Oscar for its primary writer, Paul Jabara, the following year. Summer's performance, from her dramatic balladry to the potent high note in the final minute, is described as a "vocalist's trickle." "What makes the song remarkable, what ultimately won Jabara his Oscar is Summers performance."
**MacArthur Park (1978)** : A cover of Richard Harris's 1968 baroque pop hit, written by Jimmy Webb. Giorgio Moroder transformed the "ungainly song" into a disco "thriller" for Donna Summer, incorporating a propulsive brass arrangement, ghostly synthesizers, screaming guitars, melodramatic power vocals, and a "witchy cackle." Even its four-minute radio edit went through several movements. This song became Donna Summer's first ever number one hit, topping the Hot 100 in November 1978, the same week her album *Live and More* became her first number one album. "The craziest thing of all, MacArthur Park became Donna Summers first ever number one hit, topping the Hot 100 in November 1978."
**Heaven Knows (1979)** : Issued as a standalone single, this "soaringly catchy song" was the midsection of the "MacArthur Park" suite from the *Live and More* album. Co-credited to Brooklyn Dreams, the group whose leader Bruce Sudano would later marry Summer, it became a number four hit in early 1979, contributing to Summer's platinum album success. "Co-credited to Brooklyn Dreams, the group whose leader Bruce Sudano would later marry Summers, Heaven Nose was a number four hit in early 1979."
**Hot Stuff (1979)** : The lead single from the *Bad Girls* album, described as "the ultimate hybrid single," combining "the Crunch of a Rock song [with] The Thump of a disco song." Written by Pete Bellotte, Keith Forsey, and Harold Faltermeyer, it featured a guitar solo by Jeff "Skunk" Baxter and Summer sang it with a "snarl, more Pat Benatar than Gloria Gainer." It topped the Hot 100 in June 1979 and went on to win the very first Grammy Award for Best Female Rock Vocal Performance, over songs by Bonnie Raitt, Carly Simon, and Rickie Lee Jones. "Hot Stuff was the ultimate hybrid single, the Crunch of a Rock song The Thump of a disco song."
**Bad Girls (1979)** : The title track and follow-up single to "Hot Stuff" from the *Bad Girls* album, co-written by Summer with future husband Bruce Sudano. This "chugging, chanting" song replaced "Hot Stuff" at number one on the Hot 100 two weeks after "Hot Stuff" exited the top slot, and also became Summer's first ever number one on the R&B chart. The *Bad Girls* album made her the top crossover act of the year, consolidating her popularity across different audiences. "Bad Girls was not only Summer's longest studio album ever, it was her best seller, going double platinum, and her most acclaimed and sonically diverse work."
**Dim All The Lights (1979)** : The third single from the *Bad Girls* album, written by Summer herself, who initially conceived it for Rod Stewart. Its first 30 seconds resemble a raspy rocker crooning with a "saloon-like, almost country twang" before transforming to a Latin-flavored dance record. The song features "one of the longest sustained notes in pop music history" from Summer's voice, an electro-funk breakdown, and a section for disco dancers to do the bump. It is compared to Led Zeppelin's "Over the Hills and Far Away" for encompassing all of the artist's modes in under five minutes. It reached number two in the fall of 1979, stuck behind The Eagles' "Heartache Tonight." "Dim all the lights is to Donna Summer, what over the hills and far away is to lead Zeppelin, all of the artists' modes in a single song in under five minutes."
**No More Tears (Enough is Enough) (1979)** : A feminist anthem and duet with Barbara Streisand, who invited Summer to collaborate, ratifying Summer's status as a premier vocalist on the charts. Released in the late summer of 1979, this single topped the chart in less than two months, giving Summer her third number one hit of 1979 and actually preventing her own "Dim All The Lights" from reaching the top. "Barbara Streisand, ratifying Summer's status as the premier vocalist on the charts, invited Donna to duet with her on the feminist anthem No More Tears in the late Summer of 1979."

## curiosities
**Early Church Choir Experience** : As young as eight years old, Ladonna Adrienne Gaines (Donna Summer) sang solo on hymns in her church choir in the Mission Hill neighborhood of Boston, Massachusetts. "sang in her church choir, soloing on hymns as young as eight years old."
**European Relocation and Impact** : Relocating to Germany, where she performed in the Munich production of *Hair* (October 1968), significantly changed the course of her life, both professionally and personally. This experience is compared to the impact Hamburg had on the young Beatles or Berlin on David Bowie in the 1970s. "Relocating there did for the future Donna Summer what gigs in Hamburg had done for the young Beatles at the turn of the 60s, or what Berlin would do for David Bowie in the 1970s."
**Fluency in German and Theatrical Career** : After moving to Germany, Donna Summer developed quick fluency in the language, enabling her to participate in various German theatrical productions, including *Godspell*, *Showboat*, and *The Me Nobody Knows* (*Ich Bien Ich*). In 1971, she even sang for the Viennese folk opera after moving to Vienna. "Developing quick fluency in the language, she took part in two tonic productions of God's spell, Showboat, and the rock musical The Me Nobody Knows, known in Germany as Ich Bien Ich."
**Accidental Stage Name Spelling** : In 1974, while married to Austrian actor Helmuth Sommer, Donna Sommer recorded "Denver Dream" for German label Lark Records. The single was accidentally pressed and labeled as "Donna Summer" (S-U-M-M-E-R). This spelling stuck and remained her stage name, even after her marriage ended three years later. "The single was pressed and labeled as Donna Sommer, S-U-M-M-E-R. The name with that spelling not only stuck, it outlasted Donna's first marriage."
**Recording "Love to Love You Baby" Moans** : For years, Donna Summer was asked by journalists if she had been pleasuring herself while recording the orgasmic moans in "Love to Love You Baby." She typically quipped that she had only been touching her knee, indicating the sounds were a deliberate, somewhat humorous, performance choice meant to highlight her versatility. "She usually quipped that she hadn't been touching anything more than her knee."
**Rock and Roll Hall of Fame Recognition** : "Love to Love You Baby" is enshrined in the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as one of its "songs that shaped rock and roll," acknowledging its significant impact on popular music and the evolution of the genre by signaling disco's coming of age and popularizing the extended mix. "Love to Love You Baby is now enshrined in the rock and roll Hall of Fame as one of its songs that shaped rock and roll."
**Pioneer of Album-Length Disco** : With the *Love to Love You Baby* album, Donna Summer and her producers reimagined disco as album-length music, featuring a full-side suite. This concept, unusual for disco at the time, drew parallels to album-oriented rock (AOR) and was seen as a deliberate commitment to Summer being an album artist. "The Love to Love You Baby album was the first of its genre to feature a full-size suite."
**"I Feel Love" as EDM Origin** : "I Feel Love" is generally agreed to have "single-handedly invented electronic dance music," being the first hit single with an entirely synthesized backing track, with the only organic element being Donna Summer's voice. Its innovative sound influenced post-punk and new wave groups and paved the way for genres like high energy, Italo, house, techno, and trance. "I Feel Love is generally agreed to have single-handedly invented electronic dance music."
**"I Feel Love" Vocal Challenge and Lyric Origin** : Giorgio Moroder noted that "I Feel Love" is a "difficult song to sing," indicating the complexity of its vocal melody, which Summer helped arrange. Pete Bellotte revealed that Donna Summer co-wrote the mantra-like lyric of "I Feel Love" the night she met her future husband, Bruce Sudano. "Balotti revealed that Summer co-wrote the mantra like lyric of I Feel Love, the night she met her future husband Bruce Sudano of the band Brooklyn Dreams."
**"I Feel Love" Gay Club Popularity** : Music critic Simon Reynolds noted that "I Feel Love" was a smash in discos, "particularly gay clubs," highlighting its significant impact and widespread acceptance within that community. "The record was a smash in discos, particularly Reynolds notes, gay clubs."
**Brian Eno's Prophetic Reaction to "I Feel Love"** : Brian Eno, upon first hearing Donna Summer's "I Feel Love," reportedly ran into David Bowie's room with the single in hand, exclaiming, "I've heard the future," and praising its "two tonic drumming" and "black voice." "I remember Brian Eno running into my, running into my room with a single in his hand, he said, I've heard the future."
**Cinderella Concept Album** : *Once Upon a Time* was a double album concept driven by Donna Summer herself, retelling the rags-to-riches story of Cinderella in disco form, demonstrating her creative ambition beyond just singles. "Its lyrics retold the rags-to-richest story of Cinderella in disco form. It was an idea driven by Donna herself."
**Film Acting Debut** : Donna Summer made her only film acting appearance in the musical film *Thank God It's Friday*, alongside young stars Jeff Goldblum and Deborah Winger. The film took place in a Los Angeles disco, effectively an LA answer to *Saturday Night Fever*. "Summer actually appears in the movie, her only film acting experience alongside young stars Jeff Goldblum and Deborah Winger."
**Oscar Win for "Last Dance" Songwriter** : "Last Dance" won an Oscar for its primary writer, Paul Jabara, in the year following its release, largely attributed to Summer's remarkable vocal performance, which was described as a "vocalist's trickle." "the following year, one an Oscar for its primary writer Paul Jabara."
**"MacArthur Park" Transformation** : Giorgio Moroder transformed Jimmy Webb's "ungainly" 1968 baroque pop song, "MacArthur Park," into a disco "thriller" for Donna Summer, incorporating a propulsive brass arrangement, ghostly synthesizers, screaming guitars, and melodramatic power vocals with a "witchy cackle," even in its radio edit. "Georgiom Marauder reckoned the ungainly song could be made into something else entirely."
**First Grammy for Best Female Rock Vocal Performance** : "Hot Stuff" won the very first Grammy Award for Best Female Rock Vocal Performance, triumphing over songs by Bonnie Raitt, Carly Simon, and Rickie Lee Jones, showcasing Summer's rock vocal prowess and ability to sing with a "snarl, more Pat Benatar than Gloria Gainer." "Hot Stuff would go on to win the very first Grammy award for best female rock vocal performance, over songs by Bonnie Raite, Carly Simon and Ricky Lee Jones."
**Crossover Appeal with *Bad Girls*** : The *Bad Girls* album cemented Donna Summer as the top crossover act of 1979. Previously, she had scored several Black radio hits but had often been more popular with white pop audiences; this album's success, including "Bad Girls" reaching number one on the R&B chart, changed that dynamic. "But the Bad Girls album made her the top crossover act of the year."
**Hot 100 Chart Blockade (1979)** : For the better part of two months in the summer of 1979, Donna Summer "blockaded the top of the chart" on the Hot 100, holding down the number one spot for five out of eight weeks and appearing in the top three for 11 weeks with her singles "Hot Stuff" and "Bad Girls." "Meanwhile, on the Hot 100 that Summer, for the better part of two months, Donna was blockating the top of the chart."
**"Dim All The Lights" as Rod Stewart Concept** : Donna Summer initially conceived and wrote "Dim All The Lights" for Rod Stewart, evident in the song's opening thirty seconds with a "raspy rocker crooning" style and a "saloon-like, almost country twang," before it transforms into a Latin-flavored dance record. "Summer, who wrote the song herself, later told music historian Christian John Waikain that she conceived Dim all the lights for Rod Stewart."
**Chart Battle with Herself** : Donna Summer's own single, the duet "No More Tears (Enough is Enough)" with Barbara Streisand, prevented her self-written "Dim All The Lights" from reaching number one, highlighting her intense dominance on the charts. "giving Summer her third number one hit of 1979 and actually preventing her own dim all the lights from reaching the top."

**Multilingual Background** : Born in the German-bordering Northern Italian province of South Tyrell, he spoke a polyglot mix of Italian, German, and Ladin. "born in the German bordering Northern Italian province, South Tyrell, speaking a polyglot mix of Italian, German, and Ladin."

"Moroder took home a Grammy less than four years ago for his contribution to the 2013 album Random Access Memories by Dafft Punk."
**Creative Partnership with Pete Bellotte** : Teamed with lyricist Pete Bellotte, they collaborated on writing a UK number one hit, "Son of my father." ## curiosities

**Early Breakthrough Singles** : By 1974-1975, lush club music began breaking on the charts, with "Love's Theme" by Barry White and his Love Unlimited Orchestra (1973) and "Rock the Boat" by The Hues Corporation (1974) signaling its early breakthrough. "Rock the Boat" is widely considered the first full-on disco number one. "One year earlier, a pair of singles had topped Billboard's Hot 100 that signaled the music's early breakthrough."

"Love to Love You Baby, both the single and the album, went a step further, reimagining disco as album length music and helping to popularize the concept of the extended mix."
