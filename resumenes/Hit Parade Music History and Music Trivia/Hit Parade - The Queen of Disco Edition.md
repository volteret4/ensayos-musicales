## albums
**Lady of the Night (1974) - Debut Album** : Donna Summer's debut album. Every song was written and produced by Giorgio Moroder and Pete Bellotte. The album mixed many styles to show the range of Summer's pop influences. The title track was a tribute to 60s girl group pop and became a number 4 Dutch hit and a minor top 40 hit in Germany. The first single, "The Hostage", reached number 2 in the Netherlands. It was a good start for the team, but not enough to end the trio's days as session musicians. "The album was a pastiche of styles, showcasing the breadth of Summers pop influences."
**Love to Love You Baby (1975) - First American Album** : Summer's first American album, released jointly by Moroder's Oasis label and Casablanca Records in late summer 1975. The 17-minute re-recorded title track filled all of Side A, which brought the album-oriented rock idea of the side-long suite (Pink Floyd, Genesis, Jethro Tull, Yes) to dance music. It was the first disco album to contain a full-size suite. It sold unusually well for a disco LP: it went Gold and peaked at number 11 on the Billboard album chart, next to LPs by the Electric Light Orchestra and Peter Frampton. In 1975 the full-length track could only be bought on this album. "The Love to Love You Baby album was the first of its genre to feature a full-size suite."
**A Love Trilogy (1976) - Side-Long Suite Follow-Up** : Released in March 1976 while Love to Love You Baby was still in the album top 40. It opened with the 18-minute track "Try Me, I Know We Can Make It", which filled all of Side A. Summer, Moroder and Bellotte stayed with the side-long format and with the idea that Summer was an album artist. A track from it reached number 1 on Billboard's Club Play chart, which tracked the songs New York DJs played most in the city's top discotheques. The album went gold within weeks of release but produced no top 40 pop hit. "let off with the 18-minute track, Triming, I know we can make it, taking up all of Side-Eye."
**Four Seasons of Love (1976) - Seasonal Concept Album** : Released only seven months after A Love Trilogy. It was a full concept album built from a suite of six- to eight-minute tracks named after the seasons, from "Winter Melody" to "Spring Affair". A track from it hit number 1 on Billboard's Club Play chart, which confirmed Summer as disco's rising queen. It went gold within weeks but had no top 40 pop hit, and it began to look as though "Love to Love You Baby" had been a fluke. "four seasons of Love was a full-blown concept album composed of a suite of six to eight-minute tracks named for the seasons"
**I Remember Yesterday (1977) - Past-to-Future Concept Album** : The concept came from Pete Bellotte. Side A recalled the 1940s, 50s and 60s. Side B used current sounds and ended with a song representing the future, "I Feel Love", which was almost an afterthought and became the lead single. The album established Summer as a pop force and established her, Moroder and Bellotte as all-time innovators. "a concept Balotti came up with called, I Remember Yesterday."
**Once Upon a Time (1977) - First Double Album, Cinderella Concept** : Summer's sixth album and her first double album. Its lyrics retold the Cinderella rags-to-riches story in disco form. Unlike the earlier single-album concepts, which were mostly Bellotte's, this idea came from Donna herself. It produced the top 40 pop hit and number 1 club hit "I Love You". It was her fifth gold album in a row, and although it peaked only at number 26, it arguably began her Imperial phase. "The fairy tale-like once upon a time was Summer's first double album."
**Live and More (1978) - Double Live Album and First Number One LP** : A two-record set that captured her eclectic concerts, which ran from disco to jazz to show tunes. Three vinyl sides held concert material and the fourth held a studio recording: a symphonic suite built around Jimmy Webb's "MacArthur Park". It became Donna's first number 1 album in the same week that "MacArthur Park" topped the Hot 100 in November 1978. It stayed on the album chart for nearly a year and a half and became her first platinum album. "The same week MacArthur Park topped the Hot 100, Live and More, became Donna's first number one album."
**Bad Girls (1979) - Double Platinum Peak** : Released May 1979. It was her third double album in a row and her second in a row to top the album chart. It was her longest studio album, her best seller (double platinum), and her most acclaimed and most varied work. Its 15 songs, about half written by Summer, ranged from the ballad "There Will Always Be a You" to the electro-dance of "Our Love". It made her the top crossover act of the year. Rock critics who disliked disco still praised it. "Even Rolling Stone grumbled that it, quote, ranks as the only great disco album other than Saturday Night Fever, unquote."
**On the Radio: Greatest Hits (1980) - Third Consecutive Number One Double Album** : A double greatest-hits compilation covering Donna's whole Casablanca era, with two new songs: the Barbra Streisand duet and the title track. It topped the chart in January 1980. This made Summer the only active recording artist to score three straight number 1 double albums, and it marked the end of her Imperial high point. "Having set a record as the only active recording artist to score three straight number one double albums, Summer's Imperial Highpoint was over."
**The Wanderer (1980) - First Geffen Album and New Wave Turn** : Summer's first album for Geffen, again produced by Giorgio Moroder and Pete Bellotte. It was her first full attempt at New Wave and made no effort to hide her makeover. The title track reached number 3, and "Cold Love" was a number 33 hit in early 1981. It turned out to be the last released album by the Summer–Moroder–Bellotte team. "The wanderer album, produced once again by George Eomeroder and Pete Balotti, was not timid about Summer's remodeling."
**I'm a Rainbow (1981) - Shelved Final Moroder/Bellotte Album** : The trio's last album together. Geffen was unhappy with its commercial prospects, judged it unfit for the market, and shelved it. Only a couple of tracks were released, including the synth-pop "Romeo", which appeared on the chart-topping Flashdance soundtrack. "Geffen, unsatisfied with the album's commercial prospects, decided I'm a rainbow was unfit for the marketplace and shelved the album."
**Donna Summer (1982) - Quincy Jones Production** : Geffen paired Summer with producer Quincy Jones. It was not the smash Geffen wanted, but it went gold and produced a solid top 10 hit, the synth-funk "Love Is in Control". In hindsight it was a near miss, because its sound anticipated what Jones would produce months later, with far greater success, on Michael Jackson's Thriller. "the album could be regarded as a near miss for Donna, as it echoed the sound Quincy would produce to much, much greater success just months later on Michael Jackson's Thriller"
**She Works Hard for the Money (1983) - Mercury Contractual Album** : When Summer left Casablanca for Geffen in 1980, she still owed Casablanca one album. She legally had to deliver it to Mercury Records, which had absorbed Casablanca. She made it with producer and songwriter Michael Omartian, and their co-written title track became her biggest pop hit of the decade and her biggest R&B hit ever. "When she left Casablanca for Geffen in 1980, Summer still owed the label one more album"
**Another Place in Time (1989) - Stock Aitken Waterman Comeback** : This album combined the Stock Aitken Waterman Europop style with Summer's vintage disco sound, released just as she was turning 40. Its lead single "This Time I Know It's for Real" was her last US top 10 hit. In the UK, where the SAW sound had developed, the album went gold and produced two more top 20 hits. "a savvy amalgamation of the Stock Aitken Waterman Europop style and Summer's vintage disco sound."

## songs
**If You Walk In Alone (1969) - Philips Northern Soul Single** : An early Northern Soul single on the German label Philips. It had a badly translated title. "A phrase Donna never actually sings in the song, although her young voice is potent."
**Sally Go Round the Roses (1971) - MCA UK Cover** : A one-off single for MCA Records, recorded in London in 1971 for MCA's UK subsidiary. The song had been a top 10 pop and R&B hit in 1963 for the Jaynetts, a one-hit-wonder act from the Bronx. Neither this nor her other early singles earned her a long-term contract or an album deal. "Donna's cover of the hit in 1971, Amped Up the Soul, and Pumped Up the Funk."
**Denver Dream (1974) - First Credit as Donna Summer** : Recorded for German label Lark Records. The single was pressed and labeled "Donna Summer" (S-U-M-M-E-R) by mistake instead of Sommer, and that spelling stuck. It was also her first collaboration with Giorgio Moroder and Pete Bellotte. "That single, Denver Dream in 1974, was pivotal, and not just because the single cover read Donna Sommer for the first time."
**The Hostage (1974) - Debut Album Lead Single** : The first single from Lady of the Night. It was a story song with melodramatic spoken-word sections, set to a hybrid of R&B and symphonic rock. It reached number 2 in the Netherlands. "a story song with melodramatic spoken word segments, and the music was a hybrid of R&B and Symphonic Rock."
**Lady of the Night (1974) - Girl Group Homage** : The title track of her debut album, a tribute to 60s girl group pop. It was a number 4 Dutch hit and a minor top 40 hit in Germany. "Its title track was an homage to 60s girl group pop"
**Love to Love You (1975) - Original Dutch Single** : Donna wrote the hook "Love to Love You Baby" and treated it partly as a joke, thinking it might make a demo for another artist. Moroder convinced her to keep it and record it herself. She dropped her powerful mezzo-soprano for a whispery "sex kitten" voice, the smallest her voice had sounded on record, and they deliberately camped it up with orgasmic moans. For years afterward she rolled her eyes when journalists asked whether she had pleasured herself during the vocal, and she usually joked that she had touched nothing more than her knee. It reached number 13 in the Netherlands in early 1975. "She usually quipped that she hadn't been touching anything more than her knee. But the fact was Love to Love You was unironically sexy."
**Love to Love You Baby (1975) - 17-Minute Breakthrough** : Moroder sent the track to Casablanca founder Neil Bogart, who reportedly played it at a party at his home, where guests asked to hear it again and again. Bogart called Moroder in the middle of the night, Germany time, asking for a version long enough to fill an LP side. He also suggested the new title. Summer, Moroder and Bellotte fully re-recorded it with a string section and extra backing singers. The result ran 17 minutes, five to six times the length of the Dutch single. Bogart's team got a five-minute edit onto radio, and it peaked at number 2 on the Hot 100 in February 1976. It helped popularize the extended mix and is enshrined in the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame as one of the songs that shaped rock and roll. "Donna Summer's global hit was not only her breakthrough, it was a watershed for popular music."
**I Feel Love (1977) - First Fully Synthesized Hit** : The closing track and lead single of I Remember Yesterday. It had no string section, no funk breakdown, no four-on-the-floor drumming, and no traditional instruments at all; the radio edit ran under four minutes and the album version under six. It reached number 6 on the Hot 100 (peaking November 1977) and was her first top 10 hit and first gold single since "Love to Love You Baby". It was also her first number 1 in England. It was the first hit single with an entirely synthesized backing track, partly inspired by Kraftwerk, and is generally credited with inventing electronic dance music. Moroder told Simon Reynolds the song was difficult to sing, and Summer helped arrange its complex vocal melody. Bellotte revealed in Reynolds' 40th-anniversary Pitchfork piece that Summer co-wrote the lyric on the night she met her future husband Bruce Sudano of Brooklyn Dreams. It was a smash in discos, especially gay clubs, and inspired Blondie, the Human League, David Bowie and Brian Eno. In an archival clip, a speaker recalls Eno running in saying he had "heard the future." Reynolds wrote that it influenced post-punk and new wave and opened the way for Hi-NRG, Italo, house, techno and trance. "If any one song can be pinpointed as where the 1980s began, its I Feel Love."
**Love's Unkind (1977) - UK Hit** : An ABBA-like single that reached number 3 in England as Summer became a steady hitmaker on both sides of the Atlantic. "In England, she reached number three with the Abba-esque Loves Unkind."
**I Love You (1977) - Once Upon a Time Single** : A lush track from Once Upon a Time that became a top 40 pop hit and a number 1 club hit. "generated another top 40 pop and number one club hit, with the Lush Track I Love You."
**Last Dance (1978) - Oscar-Winning Ballad-to-Disco Standard** : Her first hit of 1978. Its structure was unusual even for the period: it is a ballad for more than a third of its length and only speeds up after nearly a minute and a half. Co-producer Bob Esty proposed the structure, modeled on Diana Ross's "Ain't No Mountain High Enough", because he knew Summer could handle it. It appeared in the film Thank God It's Friday. It reached number 3 on the Hot 100 in summer 1978, and the next year it won an Oscar for its main writer, Paul Jabara, largely thanks to Summer's performance, from the dramatic opening to the powerful high note in the final minute. It cleared the way for Live and More. "What makes the song remarkable, what ultimately won Jabara his Oscar is Summers performance."
**MacArthur Park (1978) - First Number One Hit** : A remake of Jimmy Webb's 1968 song, the original of which was a number 2 hit for Richard Harris. Moroder believed the awkward song could become something completely different. He built it as a multi-part suite for the studio side of Live and More, with propulsive brass, ghostly synthesizers, screaming guitars, melodramatic power vocals and even a witchy cackle. Even the four-minute radio edit moves through several sections. It became Summer's first number 1, topping the Hot 100 in November 1978. "Donna Summers MacArthur Park was disco's own thriller."
**Heaven Knows (1979) - MacArthur Park Suite Spin-Off** : The middle section of the MacArthur Park suite, written by Summer, Moroder and Bellotte and released as a standalone single. It was co-credited to Brooklyn Dreams, whose leader Bruce Sudano later married Summer, and it was a number 4 hit in early 1979. "Co-credited to Brooklyn Dreams, the group whose leader Bruce Sudano would later marry Summers, Heaven Nose was a number four hit in early 1979."
**Hot Stuff (1979) - Rock/Disco Hybrid and Grammy First** : Written by Pete Bellotte with Keith Forsey (later Billy Idol's collaborator) and Harold Faltermeyer (later the "Axel F" keyboardist). It combined the crunch of rock with the thump of disco. Summer sang it with a snarl, and Jeff "Skunk" Baxter, formerly of Steely Dan and the Doobie Brothers, played a guitar solo. It was the lead single from Bad Girls and topped the Hot 100 in June 1979. It won the first-ever Grammy for Best Female Rock Vocal Performance, beating songs by Bonnie Raitt, Carly Simon and Rickie Lee Jones. "Summers sang Hot Stuff with a snarl, more Pat Benatar than Gloria Gainer"
**Bad Girls (1979) - First R&B Number One** : The chugging, chanting title track, co-written with future husband Bruce Sudano. It was released before "Hot Stuff" had even reached number 1, and the two climbed the Hot 100 together. Bad Girls replaced Hot Stuff at the top two weeks after Hot Stuff left the top spot, and it reached number 1 in the week ending July 14, 1979, with Hot Stuff a couple of places below, giving Summer two of the top three singles in America. It was also her first number 1 on the R&B chart, after years of being more popular with white pop audiences than Black audiences. Casey Kasem's American Top 40 noted her repeated stays at number 1 that summer. "This week, she makes it five out of the past eight weeks and here's the song that does it."
**Dim All the Lights (1979) - Multi-Genre Single** : The third single from Bad Girls, written by Summer alone. She later told music historian Christian John Wikane she had written it for Rod Stewart. It opens as a rock ballad with a saloon-like, almost country twang, then turns into a Latin-flavored dance record. Along the way it has one of the longest sustained notes in pop history, an electro-funk breakdown and a section for dancing the bump. It was compared to Led Zeppelin's "Over the Hills and Far Away" as a song covering all of an artist's modes. It peaked at number 2 in fall 1979, held off first by the Eagles' "Heartache Tonight" and then by Summer's own duet with Streisand. "Donna's voice explodes with one of the longest sustained notes in pop music history."
**No More Tears (Enough Is Enough) (1979) - Duet with Barbra Streisand** : Streisand, confirming Summer's status as the top vocalist on the charts, invited her to duet on this feminist anthem in late summer 1979. Released in the fall, it reached number 1 in under two months. It was Summer's third number 1 of 1979 and kept her own "Dim All the Lights" from the top spot. "giving Summer her third number one hit of 1979 and actually preventing her own dim all the lights from reaching the top."
**On the Radio (1980) - Compilation Title Track** : A new song on the greatest-hits double album, blending torch song and dance track in Summer's typical way. It reached number 5 on the Hot 100 by March 1980. "another of Donna's patented blends of Torch Song and Dance Jam reached number five on the Hot 100 by March."
**Starting Over Again (1980) - Dolly Parton Country Number One** : Co-written by Summer and her husband Bruce Sudano. It became a number 1 country hit for Dolly Parton in May 1980, as Summer broadened her range going into the 80s. "A song she co-wrote with her husband, Bruce Sudano, became a number one country hit in May 1980 for superstar Dolly Parton."
**The Wanderer (1980) - New Wave Makeover** : Her first full New Wave attempt. Its staccato vocals were a sharp break from her lush, full-throated disco singing. Other 70s stars tried similar makeovers, including Linda Ronstadt (a moderate hit) and Billy Joel (a number 1), but Summer was more closely tied to disco, which made her switch harder. It reached number 3 in fall 1980, a modest triumph. "When the wanderer soared into the top 10 in the fall of 1980, ultimately peaking at number three, it had to be regarded as a modest triumph."
**Cold Love (1981) - Rock-Leaning Single** : Written by the same Bellotte-led team behind "Hot Stuff". It sounded even more like rock and less like disco, and reached number 33 in early 1981. "it sounded even more like rock and even less like disco."
**Romeo - Flashdance Soundtrack Track** : A synth-pop song from the shelved I'm a Rainbow sessions that was released on the chart-topping Flashdance soundtrack. "including the synth pop Romeo, which wound up on the chart-topping Flash Dance soundtrack."
**Love Is in Control (1982) - Quincy Jones Top 10** : A trendy synth-funk track from the self-titled Quincy Jones-produced album that became a solid top 10 hit. "the on-trend synth funk track Love is in control."
**She Works Hard for the Money (1983) - Biggest R&B Hit and MTV Breakthrough** : Written with Michael Omartian, it was inspired by Summer's encounter with an exhausted attendant cleaning a ladies' room in Los Angeles. It was a number 3 pop and number 1 R&B hit in summer 1983, a season dominated by the Police, Eurythmics and Duran Duran. In the elaborate video, Summer wore Wayfarer sunglasses and a geometric dress and appeared with dozens of choreographed dancers in a city street, described as a cross between a feminist march, an aerobics workout and the movie Fame. She became the first African-American woman placed in heavy rotation on MTV, months after the channel embraced Michael Jackson and Prince. "Summer became the first African-American female to be placed in a heavy rotation"
**Unconditional Love - Reggae Duet with Musical Youth** : A minor mid-80s hit that showed her versatility. "scoring minor hits with the reggae song Unconditional Love, a duet with musical youth"
**There Goes My Baby (1984) - Synth-Pop Drifters Cover** : A synth-pop cover of the Drifters song that reached number 21 in 1984. "a synth pop cover of The Drifters There Goes My Baby, the latter a number 21 hit in 1984."
**This Time I Know It's for Real (1989) - Last US Top 10** : Lead single of Another Place in Time. Her voice had lost little power as she turned 40. It reached number 7 on the Hot 100 and went gold, her last US top 10 hit, and reached number 3 in the UK. "Donna's voice, just as she was turning 40, appeared to have lost little of its power."
**I Will Go With You (Con te partirò) (1999) - House Cover** : A house cover of Andrea Bocelli's hit. It was one of the five biggest club hits of 1999 and sold a quarter million copies in the US alone. "It was one of the five biggest club hits of 1999 and sold a quarter million copies in the U.S. alone."
**To Paris with Love (2010) - Final Club Play Number One** : Her last number 1 on Billboard's Club Play chart, reached in November 2010, completing number 1s in five different decades. "Her last club number one to Paris with Love reached the top of that list in November 2010."

## curiosities
**Birth and Childhood - Mission Hill, Boston** : Born LaDonna Adrian Gaines in Boston's Mission Hill neighborhood on New Year's Eve 1948. She sang in her church choir and was soloing on hymns by age eight. "she was born on New Year's Eve, 1948, and sang in her church choir, soloing on hymns as young as eight years old."
**New York, The Crow and the Hair Audition** : She arrived in New York City in 1967 at 18. Her first performing experience was with a blues-rock group called The Crow. When the band split, she auditioned for Broadway; the Broadway cast of Hair was full, so the producers offered her another opportunity. "Donna's first performing experience was with a blues rock combo called The Crow."
**Sheila in the Munich Production of Hair (1968)** : She played Sheila in the Munich production of Hair, which opened in October 1968, and sang "Wassermann", the German version of "Aquarius". The Hair cast album was the last Broadway cast recording to top the Billboard album chart. Moving to Germany was compared to what Hamburg did for the young Beatles and what Berlin later did for David Bowie. "What began as a break onto the stage for Donna changed the course of her life, both professional and personally."
**German and Viennese Theatre Career** : She quickly became fluent in German and appeared in German productions of Godspell, Show Boat and the rock musical The Me Nobody Knows (in German, "Ich bin ich"). After moving to Vienna in 1971 she sang with the Vienna Volksoper. "When she moved to Vienna in 1971, Donna even sang for the Viennese folk opera."
**First Marriage and the Accidental Stage Name** : In 1973 in Vienna she married Austrian actor Helmuth Sommer, her co-star in Godspell. The marriage lasted three years and produced her first daughter, Mimi. A 1974 Lark Records single was mislabeled "Summer" instead of "Sommer", and the new spelling outlasted the marriage. "The name with that spelling not only stuck, it outlasted Donna's first marriage."
**Meeting Moroder and Bellotte at a Three Dog Night Session (1973)** : Summer, Giorgio Moroder and Pete Bellotte, all expatriates living in Munich, met at a German recording session for Three Dog Night in 1973 and formed a partnership that changed 1970s dance music. They had already worked across proto-electro, show tunes, girl group, glam rock, Europop, funk and soul. "they formed a creative partnership that, without exaggeration, would change the sound of dance music in the 1970s."
**Queen of Disco - Accurate but Limiting** : No female artist dominated the charts more during disco's late-70s height, and among solo acts she may also have been its "king". The title is limiting because her roots were in rock, soul and Europop, and her hitmaking outlasted disco's collapse in the early 80s. She tried to expand disco rather than escape it. "She didn't try to escape disco. She sought to expand it."
**Three Consecutive Number One Double Albums** : She was the first artist to top the Billboard album chart with three consecutive double albums (Live and More, Bad Girls, On the Radio). Led Zeppelin, Pink Floyd, the Beatles and Tupac did not achieve this. "Actually, it wasn't a dude at all, but she was a badass."
**Imperial Phase 1978–1979** : Her Imperial period, when every project succeeded, fell in 1978–1979. Because it overlapped with a movement some people never liked, her standing in music history was somewhat diminished. "Because Donna Summer's Imperial period, in the years 1978 and 1979, coincided with a musical movement some people never liked and some would prefer to forget, her stature in music history has been somewhat diminished."
**Summer on Legitimacy (Archival Interview)** : In an interview she said she hoped to be respected for choosing her music rather than being limited by it, and that people had expected her first hit to be a flash in the pan. "I hope I stand in a light of legitimacy, I think, in a place where people will respect what I do and understand any songs that I make I do because I choose to and not because that is my limitation."
**Only Film Role - Thank God It's Friday (1978)** : She acted in Thank God It's Friday, her only film role, alongside young Jeff Goldblum and Debra Winger. The film is set in a Los Angeles disco as an LA answer to Saturday Night Fever. It did modest box office, was panned by critics and is mostly forgotten, unlike "Last Dance". "Summer actually appears in the movie, her only film acting experience alongside young stars Jeff Goldblum and Deborah Winger."
**First Signing to Geffen Records (1980)** : She was the first artist signed to David Geffen's new label, at a time when disco acts such as the Bee Gees and Village People were fading fast. "Signing Donna Summer in the year 1980 might appear to be like entering the Buggy Whip business in 1910."
**Club Play Chart Across Five Decades** : She kept topping the dance charts through the 90s and 2000s. After Madonna, she was one of the most consistent club DJ favorites around the turn of the century, and she is the only artist with a number 1 on Billboard's Club Play chart in five decades (1970s through 2010s). "Summer remains the only artist to score a number one hit on Billboard's club play chart in five different decades."
**1983 Anti-Gay Remarks Rumor** : A persistent rumor claimed Summer made disparaging remarks about gay people at a meet-and-greet after a 1983 concert. She had become a born-again Christian around the start of the 80s, and during the AIDS crisis the gay community, which had supported her completely in the 70s, took the claim seriously. Credible witnesses said the remarks came from fans, not Donna, and she spent years in interviews correcting the record and expressing devotion to her gay fans. Her revived club popularity suggested the rumor did not permanently damage her reputation. "Several credible witnesses to the event claimed that Summer had been misrepresented, that the disparaging remarks were made by fans not Donna herself."
**Rock and Roll Hall of Fame Snub** : She became eligible in 2000 and was nominated four times during her life (2007–2012), losing each time. The snub became more controversial after Madonna, whose career owed much to Summer, was inducted on her first ballot in 2008. Nominating committee insiders privately complained, regarding her as a pioneer of rock and especially electronic music. "old prejudices about disco and its relationship to rock and roll die hard."
**Death from Lung Cancer (May 17, 2012)** : She died at 63 of lung cancer despite never smoking. She had privately suspected she inhaled toxic fumes after 9/11 while living in New York City. Tributes came from Quincy Jones, Barbra Streisand, Aretha Franklin, Kylie Minogue and Moby. Elton John told the London Daily Telegraph her non-induction was a disgrace. "that she has never been inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame is a total disgrace. Her records sound as good today as they ever did"
**Posthumous Rock Hall Induction (2013)** : She was inducted on her fifth nomination, the year after her death. At the ceremony at the Nokia Theatre in Los Angeles, her widower Bruce Sudano and her three daughters accepted, while a screen showed her performing live in her 1978 prime. "She was somebody who wanted to push the envelope. She wanted to create something new at all times."

## songs
**Looky Looky (1969) - First Trans-European Hit** : Released under the single name "Giorgio". It made the top 40 in several countries and sold a million copies. It sounded like conventional pop but included synthesizer effects that were innovative for the time. "it included synthesizer effects that were innovative for the period."
**Son of My Father (1972) - UK Number One via Chicory Tip** : A glam-pop song Moroder wrote on synthesizer with lyricist Pete Bellotte. British band Chicory Tip's cover, with a heavy Moog synthesizer, topped the UK chart and reached the lower end of the Hot 100 in winter 1972. "Their version sported a fat-bottomed moge synthesizer, and it topped the UK chart"
**Take My Breath Away and Flashdance... What a Feeling - Oscar Winners** : After parting with Summer, Moroder had two more 80s chart-toppers and won two Oscars, for Berlin's "Take My Breath Away" from Top Gun and Irene Cara's "Flashdance... What a Feeling", a song Donna could easily have sung. "a song that could just as easily have been sung by Donna."

## curiosities
**Origins in South Tyrol** : Born Giovanni Giorgio Moroder in the northern Italian province of South Tyrol on the German border. He spoke a mix of Italian, German and Ladin, and released records as "Giorgio" from the mid-1960s. "My name is Giovanni Giorgio, but everybody calls me Giorgio."
**Grammy for Daft Punk's Random Access Memories** : He won a Grammy for his contribution to Daft Punk's 2013 album Random Access Memories; the duo owes much of its sound to him. "a chart-topping electro-robotic duo that owes vast swathes of its sound to Moroder."
**Persuading Summer to Keep Love to Love You** : When Donna brought him the hook as a possible demo for someone else, Moroder convinced her to record it herself. He later sent it to Neil Bogart for US distribution. "Georgio convinced her she should keep it and record it."
**Number One with Blondie** : While still working with Summer, he produced a number 1 hit with Blondie. "While still recording with Summer, he scored a number one smash with Blondie."

## curiosities
**British Expatriate Lyricist in Munich** : A British producer and lyricist who, like Summer and Moroder, had moved to Munich. He wrote lyrics for Moroder's "Son of My Father", which became a UK number 1. "an Italian producer, Georgioma Rotor, and a Brit, Pete Belotti."
**Architect of the Album Concepts** : Most of Summer's single-album concepts were his, including I Remember Yesterday. Once Upon a Time was the exception, coming from Donna. "unlike the previous string of single album concepts, which were largely conceived by Pete Balotti."
**Writer of Hot Stuff and Cold Love** : He co-wrote "Hot Stuff" with Keith Forsey and Harold Faltermeyer and led the same team on "Cold Love". "written by Pete Balotti with future Billy Idol collaborator Keith Forsey and future Axel F. keyboardist Harold Faltermeyer."

# genre - Disco

## curiosities
**Early Breakthrough 1974–1975** : Lush club music began reaching the charts in 1974–75 with "Love's Theme" and "Rock the Boat". Radio and discotheques had already embraced rawer funk and R&B such as Sylvia Robinson's "Pillow Talk" and the Chakachas' "Jungle Fever", which prepared the market for "Love to Love You Baby". "So the market was primed for Donna's sensual disco fantasia."
**Rockers Move onto Disco Turf** : By 1979, disco was drawing in rock acts, from the Rolling Stones to post-punk groups such as Blondie, and Summer answered with rock-disco hybrids. "If the rockers were going to move on to Donna's disco turf, she was within her rights moving onto theirs"
**Collapse After 1979** : Within a year of Disco Demolition Night, disco sales collapsed and dragged the record industry into a slump. Acts like the Bee Gees and Village People quickly fell off in 1980, and for seven years pop concealed its disco roots. "Within a year, disco sales would plummet, pulling the recording industry into a slump."
**Lasting Resilience** : Four decades after its peak, disco has shown its staying power as a musical form. "Disco has proved its resiliency as a musical form."

# genre - Electronic Dance Music

## curiosities
**Born with I Feel Love** : "I Feel Love" is generally credited with inventing electronic dance music and led to Hi-NRG, Italo, house, techno and trance. "I Feel Love is generally agreed to have single-handedly invented electronic dance music."

# genre - Album-Oriented Rock

## curiosities
**Side-Long Suites** : AOR dominated FM radio in the early 70s with 15–20 minute suites full of solos, jazzy breakdowns, string arrangements and even nature sounds, by Pink Floyd, Genesis, Jethro Tull and Yes. Summer, her producers and Neil Bogart brought this format to dance music. "In effect, Donna Summers, her producers, and record executive Neil Bogart were bringing concepts from album-oriented rock to dance music."

# label - Casablanca Records

## curiosities
**Neil Bogart's Midnight Call** : Founded by the legendary, hard-living Neil Bogart, Casablanca came to dominate the late-70s music business. Bogart reportedly played "Love to Love You" at a party, where guests demanded repeats, then called Moroder in the middle of the night asking for a side-long version. Casablanca was later absorbed by Mercury Records. "the label that would come to dominate the music business in the late 70s."

# concert - Disco Demolition Night

## curiosities
**Comiskey Park Record Bonfire (July 1979)** : A publicity stunt in which disco records were burned at Chicago's Comiskey Park during a White Sox doubleheader in July 1979. It became so violent and destructive that the second game was canceled. A crowd made up largely of straight white men publicly vented against a musical culture made up largely of women, gay men and people of color. It was an early sign of the American backlash against disco. "it was a leading indicator of Americans pushback against the now omnipresent music."

# curiosity

**Tom Moulton Invents the Extended Mix (1973)** : DJ Tom Moulton is credited with inventing the extended club mix in 1973 by playing his own acetates of club tracks with lengthened instrumental passages. "Legendary DJ Tom Molten is credited with inventing the form in 1973"
**The 12-Inch Single** : The 12-inch single emerged as a radio promotional tool around 1975 and reached record shops for consumers only in 1976. "Love to Love You Baby" was not released as a 12-inch, but it helped prepare buyers for the epic-length club track. "12-inch singles for consumers didn't hit record shops until 1976."
**Hair - Last Broadway Cast Album to Top the Chart** : The cast recording of Hair was the last Broadway cast album to reach number 1 on the Billboard album chart. "the smash theatrical hit whose album was the last Broadway cast recording to top the Billboard album chart"
**MTV and Black Artists** : MTV, launched in 1981, was barely playing Black artists by 1983, even as many white rock acts borrowed the rhythms of vintage dance and disco. "The video channel, which launched in 1981, was scarcely even playing black artists by 1983"
**Hidden Disco Strings in 80s Pop** : Disco-style strings showed up on hits such as Michael Jackson's "Billie Jean" and Billy Ocean's "Caribbean Queen", but were buried under post-new wave production until Stock Aitken Waterman embraced disco openly. "they were subsumed into more current post-new wave production elements."
