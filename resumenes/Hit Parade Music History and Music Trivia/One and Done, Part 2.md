# curiosity

**One-Hit Wonder Rule 1: Second Top 10 Hit** : A second hit that achieves a position within the top 10 of Billboard's Hot 100 automatically removes an artist from consideration as a one-hit wonder. This applies even if the artist does not achieve another top 40 hit after this second top 10 entry. "A second hit that makes the top 10 of Billboard's Hot 100 instantly removes an artist from 1 Hit Wonder's staff."
**One-Hit Wonder Rule 2: Second Top 40 Hit with Time Gap** : An artist is not considered a one-hit wonder if they score a second hit that reaches the top 40 on the Hot 100, unless that second hit occurs within six months of the first hit and is not followed by any subsequent top 40 hits. This rule is designed to differentiate genuine follow-up hits from "co-tails hits" that benefit from the momentum of a preceding major success. "An act that scores a second hit that makes the 40 on the Hot 100 shall not be considered a 1 Hit Wonder unless that second hit is scored within 6 months of the first hit and is never followed by another top 40 hit."
**One-Hit Wonder Rule 3: Album Chart Success** : Any act that achieves at least three top 10 albums or three platinum albums is entirely excluded from one-hit wonder classification. This rule acts as an "escape hatch" for artists whose popularity is primarily reflected through album sales rather than single chart performance, especially relevant for genres like classic rock. "Any act that scores at least three top 10 or platinum albums is removed from one-hit wonder consideration entirely."
**One-Hit Wonder Rules Scope - American Charts Only** : The established one-hit wonder rules are specifically designed for the American charts, and do not take into account hits achieved in other countries such as the UK, Australia, European, Asian, Latin American, or African charts. Success in international markets does not negate one-hit wonder status in America. "My rules are for the American charts, not those of the UK, Australia, any country in Europe or Asia, Latin America, or Africa."
**Billboard Hot 100 as Primary Metric** : The rules exclusively focus on the Billboard Hot 100, which is considered the flagship and all-encompassing pop chart in America. This approach avoids setting too low a bar for hitmaker status and acknowledges that artists prominent in genre-specific charts (like R&B or Country) might still be Hot 100 one-hit wonders. "My rules focus on the Billboard Hot 100, our flagship, all-encompassing pop chart."
**Co-tails Hit Phenomenon** : This phenomenon occurs when a second, often middling, hit gains momentary traction with US radio and the public primarily due to the momentum of a preceding major hit. Such hits, especially if they occur within a short "honeymoon period," may not reflect sustained artist popularity and are distinguished by Rule 2. "Almost all hit acts, even fleeting ones, are given a honeymoon period where a second hit will be momentarily embraced by US Radio and the public. That doesn't really mean the act has escaped the shadow of its 1 Hit."
**Streaming Era Impact on Charting** : In the 2020s, with the advent of streaming platforms like Spotify, YouTube, and Apple Music, it has become easier for artists, particularly rappers and pop stars, to chart numerous songs on the Hot 100 instantaneously upon an album's release. This shift in chart dynamics potentially alters the traditional definition and prevalence of one-hit wonders. "As we enter the 2020s, nearly a decade into the streaming era on the charts, you might think few artists are really one hit wonders anymore."
**Andy Warhol's Factory Scene Influence** : Andy Warhol's Factory scene served as inspiration for Lou Reed's song "Walk on the Wild Side," with the track detailing characters from this iconic artistic and social hub. "His David Bowie and Mick Ronson produced Story Song about characters from Andy Warhol's factory scene."
