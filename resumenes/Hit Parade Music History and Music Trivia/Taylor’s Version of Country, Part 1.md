## albums
**Taylor Swift (2006) - Self-Titled Debut Album** : Swift's self-titled debut album was released in 2006 and gradually gained sales, reaching number one on the country albums chart in its 39th week. By Christmas, it went double platinum and cracked the top five on the Billboard 200 pop chart in January 2008, in its 63rd week, continuing to spin off hits deep into that year. "And gradually, Swift's 2006 self-titled debut album kept selling deep into 2007."
**Fearless (2008) - Sophomore Album** : Fearless was Swift's sophomore album, for which she dropped advanced tracks in the fall of 2008 to build anticipation. The album was a "monster" upon its release, opening to 592,000 copies, including a record number of album downloads, a sales number much higher than any debut from contemporary stars like Katy Perry, Miley Cyrus, or Carrie Underwood, reaching a "Garth and Shania level number." It spent 11 weeks at number one on the Billboard 200, which remains the longest run by any of Swift's albums, and would be certified sextuple platinum over the next 18 months, later achieving Diamond status (10 million in sales plus streams) a decade later. "Swift's sophomore album would be called Fearless, and she spent the fall dropping advanced tracks to build anticipation."
**1989 (2014) - Album Titled After Birth Year** : Taylor Swift later titled one of her albums after the year of her birth, 1989. "who would later title one of her albums after the year of her birth, 1989."

## songs
**Tim McGraw (2006)** : This was Taylor Swift's very first single, written during her freshman year of high school and named after country superstar Tim McGraw. It served as a "sharp eyed entree into the world of country," a wistful song about a summer romance that the singer will always recall when hearing her favorite McGraw song. Released in the summer of 2006, it took six months to climb the charts, peaking in January 2007 at number six on Hot Country Songs and number 40 on the Hot 100. "Taylor Swift's Tim McGraw was a sharp eyed entree into the world of country, a wistful song about a summer romance."
**Teardrops on My Guitar (2007)** : This pop-friendly ballad was Taylor Swift's second single, released just as Carrie Underwood's "Before He Cheats" was peaking. Swift co-wrote this song with Liz Rose. By August 2007, it became a number two country hit, and six months later, following the "Before He Cheats" playbook for pop crossover, it reached number 13 on the Hot 100. "Just as before Heat Sheets was peaking, Swift released her second single, the very pop-friendly ballad, Teardrops on My Guitar."
**Our Song (2007)** : This jaunty track was Taylor Swift's third single and her first chart-topper. Unlike her previous singles, Swift composed "Our Song" alone. When it reached number one on Hot Country Songs in December 2007, a week after her 18th birthday, she became the youngest artist ever to both write and sing a country number one single by herself. "During those intervening months, Swift issued a third single that finally made her a chart-topper, the jaunty, our song."
**Should've Said No (2008)** : This was the final single released from Taylor Swift's self-titled debut album. It reached number one on the country chart in August 2008 and number 33 on the Hot 100. "Its final single should have said no reach number one on the country chart in August 2008 and number 33 on the hot 100."
**Change (2008)** : "Change" was Taylor Swift's first pop top 10 hit, debuting at number 10 on the Hot 100 in August 2008, fueled by 99-cent downloads. Anticipation for her next CD was so high that fans bought 131,000 copies of this first single from it, even without significant radio airplay. On the country chart, where downloads did not count, it peaked at number 57, indicating a shift in Swift's fanbase towards a younger, more mainstream audience. "The change, though not remembered as one of Swift's big hits, was her first pop top 10, debuting at number 10 on the Hot 100 in August 2008, fueled by 99 cent downloads."
**Fearless (2008) - Song** : This was an advanced track released from Taylor Swift's sophomore album, also titled Fearless, to build anticipation for the album. The song entered the pop chart at number 9. "including its title track, which entered the pop chart at number 9."

**White Horse (2008)** : "White Horse" was released as an advanced track from Taylor Swift's sophomore album, Fearless. It entered the pop chart at number 13 the same week the album was released. "And White Horse, which entered at number 13."
**15 (2008)** : "15" was one of seven total tracks from the Fearless album that debuted on the Hot 100 the same week the CD arrived. It would later become a number 23 hit. "Swift debuted six new singles and seven total tracks, including the future number 23 hit 15."
**Love Story (2008)** : This is one of Taylor Swift's top-selling singles, certified eight times platinum by the Recording Industry Association of America, with those eight million copies primarily being dollar downloads rather than streams. The original version topped the Hot Country chart in 2008 and later reached number four on the Hot 100 and number one on Billboard's mainstream pop chart in the winter of 2009. It remains the only single by any artist to be number one in country airplay one week and pop airplay another. "Love Story is one of the top selling singles by Taylor Swift."
**You Belong With Me (2008)** : First issued in the fall of 2008 as a teaser single ahead of the *Fearless* album, this "spritely single" is described as a "deeply relatable tale of a high school crush on a boy distracted by another girl." It spent nearly a year on the Hot 100. It followed "Love Story" to number one on Hot Country Songs in August 2009. That same week, it peaked at number two on the Hot 100, and just over a month later, reached number one on Billboard's Radio Songs Chart, tracking airplay across all radio formats. This made it the most played song on American radio, an achievement unprecedented in Billboard history for a country tune since "Islands in the Stream" in 1983. "First issued in the fall of 2008 as one of the teaser singles ahead of Fearless, you belong with me, would spend nearly a year on the Hot 100."
**Love Story (Taylor's Version) (2021)** : This re-recorded version of "Love Story" was released as part of Taylor Swift's initiative to re-record her early albums due to a dispute over master recordings ownership. Swift prefers fans consume this version, which she titled "Love Story, Taylor's version." In February 2021, it entered Billboard's Hot Country Songs at number one, becoming Taylor's second chart-topping version of the same song, matching a rare feat previously held only by Dolly Parton's "I Will Always Love You." Its high debut was thanks to heavy streaming and digital sales, factors not present during Dolly Parton's original chart runs. "She'd rather we consume this version, which she's even titled Love Story, Taylor's version."

## curiosities
**Early Artistic Exercise of Re-recording** : As part of a dispute over ownership of her master recordings, 31-year-old Taylor Swift began re-recording all of her early albums. This initiative is noted as a "fascinating artistic exercise" to hear Swift retake songs she wrote and recorded as a teenager. "Swift has begun re-recording all of her early albums as part of a dispute over ownership of her master recordings."
**Country Megastar Origins** : From her earliest days, Taylor Swift was a country megastar, with singles like "Our Song" sounding true to the country format and topping the country charts for weeks. These early country hits, often described as "twangy," were what made Swift a star in the first half-decade of her career before she turned 20. "It's a reminder that she was a country megastar back then."
**Unprecedented Country-to-Pop Crossover** : Taylor Swift's career is described as "basically unprecedented" because she is the only artist to not only successfully cross over from country music to pop but also attain pop success that measures up to, and in some ways surpasses, her country success. Most of her all-time bestsellers are still the CDs she released during her early country years. "Taylor Swift's career is basically unprecedented."
**Deliberate Pop Crossover Strategy** : Unlike other young country stars who struggled to maintain success on both pop and country charts after crossing over, Taylor Swift was "careful and deliberate" in her pivot to pop. She achieved this by degrees over several albums and many hits, blurring the lines between her pop and country sounds even before fully committing to pop in the 2010s. "Swift was so careful and so deliberate in her crossover to pop, doing it by degrees over several albums and many hits."
**Early Pop Infiltration** : Even in the "aughts" when she still sounded like a country star, Taylor Swift was "infiltrating the world of pop" by hanging out with fellow teen starlets, making mainstream TV appearances, and winning awards on shows that typically did not honor country artists. This included being nominated for Best New Artist at the 2008 MTV VMAs alongside Katy Perry and Miley Cyrus. "back in the aughts when she still sounded like a country star, Swift was already infiltrating the world of pop."
**Cultural Milestones with Young Audiences** : Over a decade of headlines, the cultural milestones Taylor Swift achieved often got lost, especially her ability to find a place in the hearts of young people who were not necessarily tuning into country radio. She consciously worked to prove country music could appeal to a younger demographic. "What's gotten lost in over a decade of headlines about Taylor Swift are the cultural milestones she was achieving, finding a place in the hearts of young people who weren't necessarily tuning in to country radio."
**Birth Date and Garth Brooks' First #1** : Taylor Swift was born just a week after Garth Brooks' song "If Tomorrow Never Comes" became his first country number one in December 1989. "Taylor Swift was born just a week after this song. If tomorrow never comes, became Garth's first country number one in December, 1989."
**Shania Twain as Primary Influence** : Growing up in Pennsylvania, young Taylor Swift regarded Shania Twain as a primary influence for her burgeoning interest in country music. Swift later told The Guardian that Twain's songs "make you want to just run around the block four times and daydream about everything." "I'm young Taylor Swift growing up in Pennsylvania, regarded Shania Twain as a primary influence for her budding interest in country music."
**LeAnn Rimes Concert Memory** : Taylor Swift recalled that one of her first memories of country music was attending a LeAnn Rimes concert when she was six years old, taken by her parents. She later shared this anecdote with her own concert audience, highlighting Rimes' early impression on her. "One of my first memories of country music was when I was 6 years old, and my parents took me to see Leanne Rines in concert."
**Observing Career Troubles of Influences** : Taylor Swift "observed all of these goings on watchfully," referring to the career troubles faced by Shania Twain, LeAnn Rimes, and The Chicks in the mid-2000s. She noticed that the rapid moves towards pop by her heroes, particularly Twain and Rimes, led to pushback from the Nashville establishment, influencing her own cautious approach to pop crossover. "Taylor Swift observed all of these goings on watchfully."
**Political Cautiousness Influenced by The Chicks** : More than a decade after becoming a star, Swift revealed that her early reluctance to discuss politics or even the country's history on the pop charts was motivated by what happened to The Chicks. The blacklisting of The Chicks by country radio after Natalie Maines' comments on the Iraq War made a lasting impression on Swift. "Swift revealed that her early weariness at even discussing the country's history of the pop charts. Even discussing politics was motivated by what happened to the chicks."
**Named After James Taylor** : Taylor Swift was named after the singer-songwriter James Taylor. "and named after singer-songwriter James Taylor"
**Early Songwriting and Nashville Dream** : Taylor Swift wrote her first songs by the age of 12. After watching a VH1 "Behind the Music" special about Faith Hill, she convinced her parents to take her to Nashville to try and get signed on Music Row. She famously walked up and down Music Row alone with demo CDs featuring songs by Dolly Parton, The Chicks, and LeAnn Rimes, handing them to receptionists while her mother and little brother waited in a rental car. "Swift wrote her first songs by age 12."
**Family Relocation to Nashville** : Around 2004, Taylor Swift's family relocated to a Nashville suburb to support her music career aspirations. "Eventually around 2004, Swift's family relocated to a Nashville suburb."
**Early Contracts and Discovery** : Taylor Swift signed a publishing contract at age 14. After performing at the renowned Bluebird Cafe, she was seen by Nashville Record Executive Scott Borchetta and subsequently signed a recording contract at age 15 with Borchetta's upstart country label, Big Machine Records. "She signed a publishing contract at age 14. And after a performance at the famed Bluebird Cafe, seen by Nashville Record Executive Scott Borsheda, she signed a recording contract at age 15. With Borsheda's upstart country label Big Machine Records."
**Plan to Attract Young Country Demographic** : Taylor Swift's early career strategy included a plan to prove that country music had a diverse range of fans and would embrace a teen star. She explicitly stated in a Country Music Television interview that she disagreed with the common perception that country music's demographic was solely "35 year old females," believing that other young girls who listened to country music would want songs directed more towards them. "This was part of Swift's plan all along to prove country music had all kinds of fans and would embrace a teen star."
**Youngest Self-Written Country #1** : When "Our Song" reached number one on Hot Country Songs in December 2007, a week after her 18th birthday, Taylor Swift became the youngest artist ever to both write and sing a country number one single by herself. "she became the youngest artist ever to both write and sing a country number one single by herself."
**2008 MTV VMA Appearance** : In September 2008, MTV started noticing Taylor Swift as her singles crossed over to teen pop fans, leading Big Machine Records to secure an invitation for her to work the red carpet at the 2008 Video Music Awards. At the event, she interviewed Katy Perry and Miley Cyrus, both of whom were bigger stars at the time and nominated with Swift for Best New Artist. This appearance is noted for its "layers of irony" given Swift's later feud with Perry. "Big Machine got MTV to invite Taylor to work the red carpet at the 2008 video music company."
**2009 MTV VMA Performance** : In September 2009, Taylor Swift performed her smash crossover hit "You Belong With Me" live at the MTV Video Music Awards in New York City. As a "show of how game she was and a gesture of welcome to New York," she performed in the New York City subway system, accompanied by an "army of fans," singing through a station, onto a train, out at another station, and onto the street in front of Radio City Music Hall. This "Broadway level extravaganza" added pizzazz to the music. "In the middle of this remarkable run in September 2009, Swift traveled to New York City to perform her smash crossover hit on the 2009 MTV VMAs."

## curiosities
**First Song to Top Country Chart Twice by Same Artist** : Dolly Parton's "I Will Always Love You" holds the unique distinction in Hot Country Songs chart history as the first song to be taken to number one twice by the same artist. She achieved this with her original 1974 recording and again with a second version in 1982 for a movie soundtrack. "It's the first song to be taken to number one twice by the same artist, Dolly herself."
**Limited Pop Crossover Success** : Despite her immense success in country music, Dolly Parton had remarkably few pop crossover hits. Only "9 to 5" topped both the country chart and the Hot 100 as a solo song, and only a couple more ("Islands in the Stream" with Kenny Rogers and "Here You Come Again") made the pop top 10. Very little of her work even made the pop top 40, and the legendary "I Will Always Love You" is largely known to pop audiences through Whitney Houston's cover. "Part.n had remarkably few pop crossover hits."
**Songwriting for Repeat Hits** : Dolly Parton's distinctiveness is also highlighted by the fact that she wrote her repeat number one country hit, "I Will Always Love You," by herself. "Still, the fact that both women wrote their repeat hits, each by herself is also remarkable."

**Come On Over (1997) - Uber Blockbuster Album** : This 1997 album by Shania Twain is described as an "Uber Blockbuster," one of the best-selling albums in chart history. It was certified for sales of 20 million copies in America alone, a figure equal to Fleetwood Mac's *Rumours*. The album spent nearly three years spinning off hits to both country radio (generating a string of number ones like "Love Gets Me Every Time") and pop charts, where Twain scored an "unprecedented number of top 40 hits," including top 10 ballads ("You're Still the One," "From This Moment On") and other popular tracks. **Up! (2002) - Multi-Version Album** : Shania Twain's 2002 CD, created with her then-husband Mutt Lang, was an attempt to triple down on her global star status. It was issued in three genre-coded versions: a green country CD, a red pop CD, and a blue international disc. Each track, including the lead single "I'm Gonna Getcha Good!", was remixed or re-recorded for its specific audience, with the international version featuring a "specifically South Asian, Bollywood style flavor." Despite selling quickly and strongly, its total sales were less than half of *Come On Over*, and its singles performed poorly on the charts, yielding no country number ones or pop top tens. "She and Mut tripled down with her 2002 CD up."
