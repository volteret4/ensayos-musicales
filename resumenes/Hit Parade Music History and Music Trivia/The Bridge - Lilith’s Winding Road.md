# artist - Paula Cole

## albums
**This Fire (1996)** : This album by Paula Cole was released in 1996 and served as the source for her song "Me." "That's me, a song by Paula Cole from her 1996 album This Fire."

## songs
**Me (1998)** : Issued as the third radio single from Paula Cole's 1996 album "This Fire," this song features lyrics about crossing a bridge into self-acceptance and self-love. Cole performed "Me" as a main stage act at Lilith Fair in both 1997 and 1998, with the festival helping to elevate her profile as a hitmaker. The single reached the top 40 on Billboard's Radio Songs Chart in the spring of 1998. "In the lyrics, Cole sings about crossing a bridge into self-acceptance and self-love."

# concert - Lilith Fair

## curiosities
**Visionary All-Woman Touring Festival** : Lilith Fair was described as a visionary and "big deal" "All Woman Touring Festival" that ran in 1997 and 1998. In its first year, it became the top-grossing touring festival, significantly boosting the profiles of artists like Paula Cole. "The All Woman Touring Festival helped raise her profile as a hitmaker."
**Strategic "Moneyballed" Lineup Approach** : A key factor in Lilith Fair's success was its strategic "moneyballed" approach to artist booking. Organizers conducted extensive research to determine how many people each artist (such as Amy Mann, Shawn Colvin, Lisa Loeb, and Sarah McLachlan) could draw in specific markets, ranging from 5,000 to 20,000 attendees. The festival aimed to create a strong bill by combining artists with fan bases that overlapped but were not identical. "they really put together a bill that they kind of moneyballed it."
**Creation of a Safe Community Space for Women** : A major contributor to Lilith Fair's success, a discovery made through audience interviews for an oral history, was its function as a safe space. Primarily, women and young women desired to attend festivals and concerts but often found existing spaces unsafe. Lilith Fair provided this crucial sense of safety, which fostered a strong desire for attendees to return, contributing to its multi-year success. "people wanted to go to a festival, and they say people, I mean, primarily women and young women, they wanted to go to a festival, they wanted to go to a concert, and those spaces fundamentally didn't feel very safe to them, and they thought they would be safe at Lilith, and they were."
**Challenging Venue Limitations for Women Artists** : Lilith Fair implicitly addressed a significant issue faced by women artists, which was a "ceiling" on the types and sizes of venues they could secure. Despite strong sales and audience numbers, artists like Sheryl Crow often encountered limitations that prevented them from playing larger venues, a barrier that the festival helped to overcome. "there was definitely a ceiling around what sort of venue a larger woman artist could get into."
