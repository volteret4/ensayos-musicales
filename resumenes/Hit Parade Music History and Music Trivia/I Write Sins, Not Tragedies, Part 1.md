## songs
**Sugar, We're Goin Down (2005) - Slow-Climbing Top 10 Breakthrough** : The song cracked the top 10 of Billboard's Hot 100 in the week ending October 15, 2005, after climbing the chart for nearly four months. It had also been rising on the modern rock chart for almost five months. It entered the top 10 the same week Green Day reached number six with "Wake Me Up When September Ends," which signalled that the emo strain of punk was now in the ascendant. "I'll be your number one with a bullet."

## albums
**Kerplunk (1992) - Lookout Records Breakout** : On this album, co-founders Billie Joe Armstrong (guitar, vocals) and Mike Dirnt (bass) added drummer Tré Cool as a permanent member and settled on a three-man lineup. Kerplunk became the fastest-selling and best-selling album on Lookout Records, the small punk label in Berkeley, California. Even on an indie label, the band's three-chord, hook-laden songs already sounded ready for the majors. "Crophunk became the fastest and best-selling album on the small Berkeley California punk label look out records."
**Dookie (1994) - Major Label Debut on Warner Music** : This was Green Day's major label debut after signing to Warner Music, and it proved pop punk's commercial potential almost immediately. The album was titled after a nickname for feces. Two weeks after Woodstock 94 it broke into the top 10 of the Billboard 200 and went platinum. It was quadruple platinum by the end of 1994, and its US sales eventually reached 10 million copies. "on its way to eventual sales of 10 million copies in America alone."

## songs
**Longview (1994) - Modern Rock Chart-Topper** : This bass-heavy, catchy ode to suburban ennui topped Billboard's Modern Rock chart in June 1994. Its success won Green Day a slot on that year's Lollapalooza touring festival and, more importantly, at Woodstock 94, that summer's most hyped mega-concert. In the week Longview reached number one, The Offspring's "Come Out and Play" cracked the modern rock top 10. "topped Billboard's Modern Rock Chart in June of 94."
**Basket Case (1994) - Post-Woodstock Number One** : This song was even catchier than Longview and is described as caffeinated and classically punk. The week after Green Day's Woodstock 94 set, it put the band back at the top of the Modern Rock chart, where it stayed for more than a month. "Basket case stayed at number one for more than a month."
**When I Come Around - Radio Hit Never Released as a Single** : The song reached the top 10 on Billboard's Radio Songs chart, which shows that even Top 40 radio was on board with pop punk. It drew about as much pop airplay as hits by Boyz II Men, Sheryl Crow, and TLC. It was never issued as a retail single, so like many 90s radio hits that remained album cuts, it was ineligible for the Hot 100. Had it been released as a single, Green Day would have had a massive pop hit. "The Green Day song was never issued as a retail single."
**Wake Me Up When September Ends (2005) - Final Top 10 Pop Hit** : In the week ending October 15, 2005, this song reached number six on Billboard's Hot 100. It was the final top 10 pop hit for Green Day, described as the reigning kings of pop punk. In the same week, Fall Out Boy's "Sugar, We're Goin Down" cracked the top 10, a moment that marked the rock zeitgeist turning toward emo. "reached number six on Billboard's Hot 100 with their final top 10 pop hit"

## curiosities
**Bay Area Underground Roots** : Green Day had been part of the Bay Area underground punk scene for nearly half a decade by the time Nirvana broke. One argument holds that Green Day, a straight-up punk band from Northern California, benefited more from Nirvana's breakthrough than Pearl Jam or Soundgarden did. "the act that benefited the most from Nirvana's breakthrough wasn't a grunge band at all"
**Inspired by Operation Ivy** : Billie Joe Armstrong said his fandom for Operation Ivy, the short-lived ska punk band that galvanized the late 80s Bay Area punk scene, inspired him to form Green Day. The antecedents of Green Day's sound go back a decade further, to catchy first-wave punk. "he was inspired to form Green Day by his fandom for Operation Ivy"
**British-Accented Singing** : Critics have noted that Billie Joe Armstrong's seemingly British-accented singing emulates the first wave of catchy British punk bands such as the Buzzcocks. "Billy Joe Armstrong's seemingly British accented singing emulates this first wave of catchy punk"
**Armstrong Hated the Term Pop Punk** : Billie Joe Armstrong hated the term "pop punk" and considered it an oxymoron. He later told the rock magazine Kerrang!: "you're either punk or you're not." "you're either punk or you're not"
**Woodstock 94 Mud Fight** : Partway through their Woodstock 94 set, the band that had named its album after a nickname for feces had a mud fight with the audience. The festival made Green Day's name. "engaged in a mud fight with the audience partway through their set."
**Establishing Punk as a Classic Genre** : Green Day's breakthrough in the mid-90s, even more than Nirvana's left-field success, established punk as a classic rock genre, and even classic pop, and severed its roots from the underground. "would establish punk as a classic rock genre."

# genre - Punk Rock

## curiosities
**Dictionary Definition** : Punk long ago entered the dictionary as a genre. "Rock music that is played in a fast, loud, and aggressive way and is often a protest against conventional attitudes and behavior"
**From Underground to Radio Format** : Punk's reason for being was rejecting the mainstream, yet it went mainstream two decades after it emerged. "20 years after it first emerged, punk went from an underground movement to a radio dominating format."
**Punk Was Never Pure** : Punk evolved almost immediately into post-punk and new wave. Its proto-punk roots in 60s garage rock and the Stooges made it a mongrel art form from the start. "punk was never pure. It was evolving almost instantly within its first year."
**CBGB and the Early Scene** : Hilly Kristal's downtown New York club CBGB fostered the early punk rock scene, which ranged from the New York Dolls to Patti Smith. "began fomenting the early punk rock scene in 1973 74 and 75"
**Pop Hooks from the Start** : Much punk has always had pop hooks. As punk splintered into hardcore, post-hardcore, noise rock, and ska-core, one strain kept the emphasis on classic, often Beatlesque songwriting. "A lot of it has pop hooks."
**Commercial Forms from Uncommercial Scenes** : The very commercial forms of punk grew out of scenes that saw themselves as defiantly uncommercial, including Dead Kennedys, Black Flag, Fugazi, Operation Ivy, and Flipper. "With rare exception none of this punk got anywhere near the billboard charts."

# genre - Pop Punk

## curiosities
**Mainstream Sound of the 2000s** : Punk fused with unabashed pop hooks, and by the 2000s the result was the mainstream. "pop punk became the main stream, competing with hip hop as the sound of young American."
**Green Day Perfected the Form** : Green Day did not invent pop punk, but they made it accessible, and the term became common after their success. "The very term pop punk became more common in the wake of Green Day."
**Bratty vs. Emotional** : In theory, pop punk was the bratty side of punk and emo the emotional side. "In theory pop punk was the bratty stuff."
**2021 Revival** : Generation Z artists, some barely born when these bands ruled the airwaves, adopted pop punk and emo's mix of head-thrashing release and soul-baring confession. "Even artists who used to call themselves rappers are now picking up guitars."

# genre - Emo

## curiosities
**A Name the Bands Hated** : None of the bands supposedly part of emo liked the name, partly because "punk" already seemed to fit their tempo and attitude. "a genre name that none of the bands that were supposedly a part of it liked at all."
**Emotional, Soul-Baring Punk** : Emo allowed itself more flourishes and broken vocals. As emo and pop punk merged, the line between them blurred. "it became hard to know where the frothy fury of pop punk ended"
**October 15, 2005 Turning Point** : The week Green Day had their final top 10 pop hit and Fall Out Boy's "Sugar, We're Goin Down" cracked the top 10, the rock zeitgeist moved fully toward emo, from skinny-jean fashions to smarty-pants song lyrics. "Signalling that the Emo strain of punk was now ascended."
**Early Seeds** : Indie acts like The Promise Ring, and even a platinum act that mixed brooding songs with bangers, planted the emotional scene years before it peaked. "The seeds of this more emotional scene had been shown years earlier"
