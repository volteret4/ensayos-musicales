# genre - Punk

## curiosities
**"Punk Happened" Decree** : Co-authors Jimmy Gooterman and Owen O'Donnell, in their 1991 book *The Worst Rock and Roll Records of All Time*, famously decreed "punk happened. Note tense." This implied punk was a finite movement of the 70s/early 80s, and later bands claiming to be punk were "posers." "Quote, punk happened. Note tense. Unquote."
**Dictionary Definition** : The common definition of punk describes it as "rock music that is played in a fast, loud, and aggressive way and is often a protest against conventional attitudes and behavior." This definition, as opposed to the idea that punk was a time-limited movement, allows for new versions of the music to be made at any time. "Rock music that is played in a fast, loud, and aggressive way and is often a protest against conventional attitudes and behavior as one dictionary puts it."
**Pop Hooks and Catchiness** : Despite the common perception of punk as unlistenable and challenging, much of it has always been catchy, with pop hooks that allow for head-bobbing and foot-tapping, not just slam dancing or head-thrashing. "Here's the dirty little secret. punk has always been catch."
**Never Pure, Always Evolving** : The idea that there's only one "original pure strain of punk" is a myth; punk was evolving almost instantly within its first year into post-punk and new wave. Its roots in 60s garage rock and early Detroit rockers show it was a "mongrel art form from the jump." "One more myth we should blow up about punk happened. Is the idea that there is only one original pure strain of punk to be sure the kind that broke in the mid 70s say the Ramones or the Sex Pistols was called at the time simply punk rock no hyphens no hybrids no qualifiers."
**Diverse Sounds from Birth** : From its inception in the early 70s CBGB scene, punk never had one singular sound, accommodating diverse artists like the cross-dressing New York Dolls and the equally androgynous poet-turned-rocker Patti Smith. This diversity allowed it to branch into myriad directions from the 1980s onward, laying groundwork for commercial and uncommercial forms. "The point is from its birth punk never really had one sound."
**Commercial Evolution from Uncommercial Roots** : The forms of punk that became very commercial in the late 20th and early 21st century evolved out of punk scenes that considered themselves defiantly uncommercial, such as the agit-prop of the Dead Kennedys, the relentless rock of Black Flag, the post-hardcore of Fugazi, and the ska-punk hybrids of Operation Ivy. "In fact the forms of punk we're going to discuss in this episode which were very commercial evolved out of punk scenes that thought of themselves as defiantly uncommercial"

# genre - Pop Punk

## curiosities
**Fusion of Punk and Pop Hooks** : Pop punk emerged as punk fused with "unabashed pop hooks," evolving from an underground movement to a radio-dominating format by the 2000s, where it competed with hip hop as the sound of young America. "Soon enough it fused with unabashed pop hooks into a genre that came to be called pop punk."
**Apex in the Aughts** : The pop punk revolution arguably reached its peak in the 2000s ("the aughts"), a period when the torch of rock zeitgeist began transitioning from traditional punk towards Emo, affecting everything from fashion to song lyrics. "But the apex of the pop punk revolution arguably came in the aughts when the torch was being passed from punk to Emo."
**Green Day's Perfection of the Genre** : Green Day did not invent pop punk but "more or less perfected it" and made it more accessible, leading to the term "pop punk" becoming more common in their wake. "Green Day didn't invent the pop punk genre, but they more or less perfected it, made it more accessible."
**Commercial Breakthrough and Legacy** : The breakthrough of pop punk, largely spearheaded by Green Day and The Offspring in the mid-90s, established punk as a "classic rock genre" (or classic pop), severing its roots from the underground and paving the way for other loud, fast bands to succeed on radio and charts. "The breakthrough of Green Day in the mid 90s, even more than Nirvana's left field success, would establish punk as a classic rock genre."
**Teen Pop Hero Translation** : By the end of the 90s, as pop radio leaned towards hip hop and dance-pop, pop punk was further mainstreamed and translated for the "teen pop hero" audience by bands like Blink-182. "But that's when another rising band took the main streaming of punk even further, translating it for the team pop hero."

# genre - Emo

## curiosities
**Pillory by Bands Tagged With It** : Emo was often "pilloryed by the bands tag with it" because there was already a suitable name, "punk," that matched the tempo and attitude of these bands. "Part of the reason Emo was so pilloryed by the band's tag with it was, there was already a perfectly good name that seemed to match the tempo and attitude of these bands."
**Emotional vs. Bratty Dichotomy** : In theory, pop punk was considered the "bratty stuff," while Emo was, as its name implied, the "emotional, soul-bearing stuff," allowing for more flourishes and brooding vocals. However, as the two styles merged, the lines between the "frothy fury of pop punk" and the "prolics profundity of Emo" became blurred. "In theory pop punk was the bratty stuff.
