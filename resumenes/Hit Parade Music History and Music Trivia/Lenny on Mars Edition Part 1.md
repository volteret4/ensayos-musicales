## albums
**Let Love Rule (1989) - Debut Album** : Lenny Kravitz's debut arrived in September 1989 on the then relatively new label Virgin America, after his self-produced demos had been rejected by numerous labels, some saying his music "wasn't black enough, others not white enough." Virgin executives, hungry for a hit and willing to entertain left-of-center artists, liked his Prince-meets-John-Lennon sound and let him turn his self-recorded demos into a finished album, which he wrote, produced and played on. Because it was an odd fit for pop or R&B radio, Virgin promoted it largely to rock radio, where it worked on both alternative and mainstream rock stations. Rolling Stone called it a "childlike musical collage of no particular point"; it was a sleeper hit, reaching number 61 on the album chart in February 1990, and was not certified gold for another five years. "True to his neo-hippy vibe Lenny called the album Let Love Rule."
**Mama Said (1991) - Sophomore Album** : Kravitz's 1991 second album built on his boomer-rock-nostalgia brand, opening with the vintage-style funk rock of "Always on the Run" featuring Slash. It produced his first pop and R&B hit, "It Ain't Over Till It's Over" (number 2 on the Hot 100), and a minor pop follow-up with the Lennon-esque "Stand by My Woman." The album went gold by the fall of 1991 and was certified platinum four years later. "Kravitz built on that brand on his 1991 sophomore album, titled Mama Said, which kicked off with a blast of uncut vintage style funk rock."
**Are You Gonna Go My Way (1993) - First Instant Platinum Album** : The album climbed to number 12 on the album chart and became the first Kravitz LP to go platinum right away. Kravitz filled it with riff rockers, including the title track (number 1 album rock), "Is There Any Love in Your Heart" (a top 20 album rock hit), and "Believe" (modern rock top 10, number 60 Hot 100), as well as the melancholy soul ballad "Heaven Help." With it Kravitz was crossing over at multiple formats. "The are you gonna go my way? Album climbed to number 12 and became the first Kravitz LP to go platinum right away."
**Circus (1995) - Commercial Dip** : Kravitz kicked off his 1995 album with the tongue-in-cheek "Rock and Roll Is Dead," which borrowed the riff from Led Zeppelin's "Heartbreaker," and also included the country-rock "Can't Get You Off My Mind." Neither single came close to the top 40 on the pop chart, and the album only went gold, suggesting Kravitz's hitmaking days were on the wane. The record was made while he was grieving the 1993 end of his marriage to Lisa Bonet and watching his mother, Roxy Roker, fight breast cancer. "the album only went gold, suggesting that Kravitz's hitmaking days were on the Wayne."
**5 (1998) - Sleeper Comeback and Longest Chart Run** : On his fifth album, Kravitz played with sequencers and samplers for the first time since "Justify My Love." It opened weakly with the trip-hop-flavored lead single "If You Can't Say No," which reached only number 39 on the modern rock chart. The album muddled around the middle of the album chart for months and took half a year to go gold, and AllMusic wrote that "Kravitz's Sly, Hendrix, Lenin, and Prince pastiches are a bore." It was revived by "Fly Away," a last-minute addition Kravitz had to push Virgin to include, after which the album climbed to a new peak of number 28 and went double platinum. Reissued in 1999 with the bonus track "American Woman," it spent 110 weeks on the Billboard 200, by far the longest run of any Kravitz album. "Five seemed commercially defunct, until Kravitz decided to try promoting a track he had added to the album at the very last minute."
**Greatest Hits (2000) - Highest-Charting and Top-Selling Album** : Virgin collected all of Kravitz's singles on a Greatest Hits CD just a few months after 5 fell off the chart in the fall of 2000. It debuted at number 2, Lenny's highest-charting album ever. Like Madonna's The Immaculate Collection a decade earlier, it was anchored by a new romantic song, "Again," which rose to number 4 on the Hot 100 by February 2001 and pushed the compilation to triple platinum. "Greatest Hits debuted all the way up at number two, Lenny's highest charting album ever."
**Lenny (2001) - Platinum Album** : The 2001 album produced "Dig In," which cracked the top 40 at number 31, and the album went platinum. "Dig in from 2001's Lenny album Cracked the Top 40 at No. 31 and the album went platinum."

## songs
**Let Love Rule (1989) - Debut Title Track** : The title track reached number 5 on Billboard's modern rock chart and number 23 on the album rock chart by December 1989. Alternative stations slotted it next to college rockers and their neo-psychedelia, while mainstream rock stations played it alongside boomer icons. Beatles fans noticed right away that it was built on the same chord progression as "Hey Jude," as later illustrated by a YouTube mashup. On the Hot 100 it reached only number 89, his sole Hot 100 entry before "It Ain't Over Till It's Over." "Beatles fans noticed right away that Let Love Rule, the song, was built out of the same chord progression as He Jude."
**I Build This Garden for Us (1989)** : A trippy, introspective, hippie-adjacent song inspired by Kravitz's bohemian life with Lisa Bonet. It followed "Let Love Rule" onto the modern rock chart, peaking at number 25. "I build this garden for us, followed Let Love Rule onto the modern rock chart, where it peaked at number 25."
**Flower Child (1989)** : Kravitz's smitten ode to Lisa Bonet, written during their bohemian period together. "Or his smitten ode to Bonay flower child."
**Mr. Cab Driver (1989)** : An urbane Hendrix reboot in which Kravitz channeled his admiration for Jimi Hendrix's guitar work; it was a number 50 mainstream rock hit. "Into the Urbane Hendrix reboot, Mr. Cab Driver, a number 50 mainstream rock hit."
**Justify My Love (1990) - Co-Written and Produced for Madonna** : Kravitz took the Funky Drummer beat (Clyde Stubblefield's break on James Brown's 1970 track, by way of Public Enemy's slowed-down 1988 "Security of the First World") and, in the studio with co-producer Andre Betts, paired it with an erotic love-letter poem written by Ingrid Chavez, evocative synthesizers, and his own wordless moaning. He brought the demo to Madonna, who was seeking new tracks for her greatest-hits album The Immaculate Collection. The song, known for its MTV-banned video and its sex-positive, queer-friendly perspective, was sold as a VHS video single and topped the Hot 100 in January 1991. It remains Kravitz's only number one pop hit as a writer and producer, and its production used hip-hop tools and anticipated trip hop. "It remains Lenny Kravitz's only number one pop hit as a writer and producer."
**Give Peace a Chance (1991) - The Peace Choir** : A remake of John Lennon's anthem, a benefit for Gulf War victims and an implied protest against that war, conceived by Kravitz with Yoko Ono and their 15-year-old son Sean Ono Lennon. Lenny and Sean open the video playing guitar side by side, and Kravitz's voice is one of the first heard. Credited to The Peace Choir, it featured more than 40 acts including MC Hammer, Axl Rose, Peter Gabriel, Iggy Pop and Michael McDonald, and it peaked at number 54 on the Hot 100. "it solidified Lenny's reputation as GeneX's premier Boomer Rock Nostalgia."
**Always on the Run (1991) - With Slash** : The opener of Mama Said, its fuzzy, funky groove recalled Sly and the Family Stone, while its main riff was played by Saul Hudson, aka Slash of Guns N' Roses, who, like Kravitz, is biracial. Slash said the song let him play with more funk than he normally did on G N' R records, and Lenny calls him out by name on the bridge. It peaked at number 8 on the modern rock chart and number 40 on the album rock chart in spring 1991. "Its fuzzy, funky groove directly recalled the sound of Sly and the family's stone."
**It Ain't Over Till It's Over (1991) - All-Time Hot 100 Peak** : Written as a plea to Lisa Bonet as their marriage was foundering, it was Kravitz in fluttery 70s slow-jam mode. Its string arrangement, sitar solo, and falsetto recalled peak Philly soul like The Spinners or The Stylistics, while its melody echoed Earth, Wind & Fire's "That's the Way of the World." It reached number 2 on the Hot 100 in the week ending August 24, 1991, his all-time peak, and number 10 on the R&B chart a month later. It kicked off the 20-year nostalgia cycle for 70s culture and directly foreshadowed Silk Sonic's "Leave the Door Open" 20 years later. "Lenny Kravitz, rock star, was finally a pop hitmaker."
**Stand by My Woman (1991)** : A Lennon-esque minor pop follow-up hit from Mama Said. "The Mama said album generated a minor pop follow-up hit with the Lennon-esque stand by my woman."
**Are You Gonna Go My Way (1993) - Number 1 Album Rock** : Kravitz's bid for air-guitar immortality, built on a Hendrix-worthy riff; critics noted the Hendrix echoes but were virtually all positive, one calling it "a rollicking fast-paced rock masterpiece that shows Lenny's mastery of a catchy riff." Its video, directed by Mark Romanek in a multi-tiered rotunda with flashing lights and dozens of thrashing dancers, won an MTV Video Music Award. At a time when songs by Black artists rarely topped mainstream rock radio, it hit number 1 on the album rock chart in early summer 1993 and number 2 on the modern rock chart. "At a time when songs by black artists rarely topped mainstream rock radio playlists, Kravitz's, are you gonna go my way? Did the improbable?"
**Is There Any Love in Your Heart (1993)** : A riff rocker from Are You Gonna Go My Way that became a top 20 album rock hit. "Kravitz filled the album with riff rockers like is there any love in your heart? Top 20 album rock hit."
**Believe (1993)** : An anthemic track whose melody was again compared to the Beatles, with a string coda recalling Derek and the Dominos' "Layla." It returned Kravitz to the modern rock top 10 and reached number 60 on the Hot 100. "Believe return Kravitz to the modern rock top 10 as well as reaching number 60 on the hot 100."
**Heaven Help (1993)** : A melancholy soul ballad that cracked the lower rungs of both the Hot 100 and the R&B chart; six years later it was covered by neo-soul singer Angie Stone. "Six years later it was even covered by Neo Soul Singer Angie Stone."
**Rock and Roll Is Dead (1995)** : The tongue-in-cheek opener of Circus cracked the top 10 at both mainstream and modern rock stations. It was doubly ironic because Kravitz borrowed its riff from Led Zeppelin's "Heartbreaker." "So even in pure rock mode Kravitz was trying on different rocker disguises."
**Can't Get You Off My Mind (1996) - Country Rock** : Kravitz tried country rock, combining his riffage with a lonely-highway lyric; it was a number 62 pop hit. He wrote it while grieving the 1993 end of his marriage to Lisa Bonet and watching his mother lose her fight with breast cancer. "Kravitz was seriously wounded when he wrote the song."
**If You Can't Say No (1998)** : Lead single from 5, it echoed the trip-hop sound of "Justify My Love" but only reached number 39 on the modern rock chart. "The lead single, if you can't say no, echoed that Madonna hits, trip hop sound."
**Fly Away (1998) - Double Rock Chart Number 1** : A last-minute addition that Kravitz had to push Virgin to include on 5, buried on the disc's back half, fusing Parliament-Funkadelic funk with classic rock riffage and a simple sing-along chorus. It took four months to climb the mainstream rock chart and three months on the modern rock chart, hitting number 1 on both simultaneously by November 1998, his only single to do so. Helped by inclusion on the first Now That's What I Call Music CD alongside Hanson and the Backstreet Boys, it climbed the Hot 100 for five months, peaking at number 12 in May 1999, his first major pop hit since 1991. It pushed 5 to a new peak of number 28 and double platinum. "The chameleon had fused all of his influences. Rock, funk, soul, pop, even a little grunge into a smash that sounded mostly like Lenny Kravitz."
**American Woman (1999) - The Guess Who Cover** : Recorded for the soundtrack of the second Austin Powers movie and added as a bonus track to the 1999 reissue of 5. Instead of adapting a style, Kravitz did a straight cover of The Guess Who's 1970 number one, keeping its crunchy riff and adding the late-90s sheen of "Fly Away." It hit the top 10 on both rock charts. "Only this time, Lenny didn't adapt a style. He borrowed a song wholesale."
**Again (2000) - Greatest Hits Lead Single** : A heartfelt new romantic song anchoring Greatest Hits, it sounded like Beatlesque classic rock crossed with neo soul but indebted to no one style. It rose to number 4 on the Hot 100 by February 2001, pushing the compilation to triple platinum. "The heart felt again, was the single that showed Lenny Kravitz' music had finally become its own genre."
**Dig In (2001)** : From the Lenny album, it cracked the top 40 at number 31. "Dig in from 2001's Lenny album Cracked the Top 40 at No. 31."
**Storm (2004) - With Jay-Z** : Kravitz tried hip hop again, teaming with Jay-Z; the song made the top half of Billboard's R&B/Hip-Hop chart. "In 2004, Kravitz tried his hand again at Hip Hop, teeming with rapper Jay-Z on Storm."
**Lady (2005)** : A return to riff rock that gave Kravitz one more top 40 pop hit, peaking at number 27. "a return to riff rock on Lady gave Kravitz one more top 40 pop hit, peaking at No. 27."
**I'll Be Waiting (2008) - Final Hot 100 Entry** : The number 73 hit marked what turned out to be the last time Kravitz made the Hot 100. "Justice Kravitz was making the Hot 100 for what would turn out to be the last time with the No. 73 hit, I'll be waiting."

## curiosities
**Birth and Generational Identity** : Leonard Albert Kravitz was born in New York City in May 1964, technically a tail-end baby boomer, though he publicly identifies more as a Generation Xer on the cusp. He was a flower-child hippie for Generation X before that vibe was considered cool. "Lenny was a flower-child hippie for Generation X, before that whole vibe was considered cool."
**Beatles Devotion - In My Life at His Wedding** : As a toddler in the mid-60s Kravitz loved the Beatles, especially "In My Life," which later played at his wedding. He loved Paul McCartney's melodic skill, but John Lennon was closest to his heart, including Lennon's early solo work with Yoko Ono. In his memoir Let Love Rule, he recalls his mother taking him to hippie rallies where protesters harmonized on "Give Peace a Chance." "But spiritually, John Lennon was closest to his heart."
**Showbiz Parents - Sy Kravitz and Roxie Roker** : Kravitz is the product of a biracial couple: Sy Kravitz, a white NBC TV news producer, and Roxie Roker, a Black theater actress acclaimed on Broadway who moved into TV in the 1970s, best known as Helen Willis on the CBS sitcom The Jeffersons opposite Sherman Hemsley's George Jefferson. Roker played classic soul by Gladys Knight and Stevie Wonder for him. "Kravitz was steeped in showbiz from a very young age."
**Duke Ellington Birthday Serenade at the Rainbow Room** : Sy Kravitz befriended jazz greats including Ella Fitzgerald, Bobby Short, and Duke Ellington, who at Sy and Roxie's request led the Duke Ellington band through "Happy Birthday" for young Lenny one night at the Rainbow Room. "led the Duke Ellington band through a rousing happy birthday for young Lenny one night at the Rainbow Room."
**Jackson Five Concert - Formative** : His father surprised him with tickets to a Jackson Five concert, which Kravitz called formative both for their music and their big afro style. "whom Kravitz called, quote, formative, both for their music and for their big afrode style."
**Black Dog Made His Head Explode** : After moving to LA with his mother when she got her TV role, Kravitz got into Jimi Hendrix and Led Zeppelin; he said his head exploded in junior high the first time he heard Zeppelin's "Black Dog." "Kravitz said his, quote, head exploded in junior high school the first time he heard Zeppelin's black dog."
**Prince - The Me I Wanted to Be** : By high school Kravitz was inspired by the young Prince, whom he called "the me I wanted to be," impressed that Prince did his own writing, most of the playing, and his own production, a model Kravitz later emulated. Friends said his early music sounded like Prince crossed with the lo-fi post-Beatles Lennon of Plastic Ono Band. "friends told him he sounded like prince crossed with John Lennon."
**Romeo Blue Alias and Blue Contact Lenses** : Hustling in LA in the 80s, Kravitz renamed himself Romeo Blue and tried blue contact lenses for a more exotic look. On The Mike Douglas Show, his mother said he wanted to be a musician and showman playing new wave; he led a band called Wave for a while. "Speaking of name changes when Lenny Kravitz began hustling his music in LA in the 80s he renamed himself Romeo Blue."
**Turning Down A&M's Black Duran Duran Plan** : In the mid-80s A&M Records, which had just signed Janet Jackson, wanted to make Romeo Blue and other musicians into a "Black Duran Duran"; he turned the deal down, one of several he rejected because he was uninspired by what major labels would invest in. "Lenny aka Romeo Blue turned the Ann M. deal down."
**Rejected Capitol Deal and Maggie's Dream** : An electro-pop band he'd recorded demos with was offered a firm Capitol Records deal; after weeks of dithering, Kravitz declined. The band found a new singer, a former member of Puerto Rican boy band Menudo, signed with Capitol as Maggie's Dream, and their album failed to chart. "The band by the way found a new lead singer a former member of the Puerto Rican boy band Menudo."
**Lisa Bonet and the Return to His Real Name** : By 1987, at the encouragement of girlfriend Lisa Bonet (Denise Huxtable on The Cosby Show and A Different World), he changed his name back to Lenny Kravitz. Bonet inspired him to hold out for a solo deal; they eloped in 1987 and immediately had a child, future actress Zoë Kravitz. Their marriage ended in 1993. "Bonay and Kravitz aloaked in 1987 and immediately had a child."
**Ingrid Chavez Songwriting Credit Lawsuit** : Several years after "Justify My Love," Ingrid Chavez sued for and won songwriting credit, claiming Kravitz hid her contributions out of fear that Lisa Bonet would suspect an affair. "several years later, Ingrid Shavez sued for and won songwriting credit on Justify My Love."
**Mother's Death** : Roxie Roker died of breast cancer in December 1995. "Rooker died in December 1995."
**Rip-Off Artist Criticism** : Critics took potshots at Kravitz for copying his elders from his debut on; he was tarred with the insult "rip off artist" more than once, and by 1998 critics tired of his "game of musical dress-up." "Kravitz in particular was tarred with the insult rip off artist more than once."
**Repeat Stage Run-Ins with Bruno Mars** : Kravitz first appeared at a Mars concert in Paris, was brought out by Mars at Madison Square Garden the following year, and in 2015 Mars made a surprise appearance at a Kravitz concert in Hollywood, singing from the audience. "This guy's Bruno my favorite, ladies and gentlemen, show some love to Mr. Lennie, Crabbit."

## curiosities
**Birth and Nickname** : Peter Gene Hernandez was born in October 1985 in Honolulu, Hawaii, and nicknamed Bruno by age two. His mother Bernadette was Filipina and his father Peter Hernandez half Puerto Rican, half Jewish; the whole family sang and played instruments. "young Peter Jean Hernandez born in October 1985 and nicknamed Bruno by age two."
**Little Elvis at Age Three** : Raised in Waikiki to revere Elvis Presley, with an uncle who was an Elvis impersonator, Bruno was impersonating Elvis himself by age three, still in kindergarten. "One of Bruno's uncles was an Elvis impersonator and by age three so was Bruno."
**Father's Band The Love Notes** : His father made his bones singing Little Richard songs in Honolulu cover band The Love Notes; Bruno's Little Elvis became the star attraction and began to eclipse them. "Bruno's little Elvis became the star attraction at his father's Love Notes concerts and began to eclipse them."
**Honeymoon in Vegas (1992) - First Big Break** : Bruno appeared in the Nicolas Cage and Sarah Jessica Parker film featuring a troupe of Presley impersonators called The Flying Elvises, with a showcase slot. "In 1992, Bruno scored his first big break when he appeared in the Nicholas Cage Sarah Jessica Parker movie Honeymoon in Vegas."
**Aloha Bowl, Arsenio Hall and MTV** : He performed at halftime of the Aloha Bowl, on The Arsenio Hall Show, and was interviewed on MTV by Pauly Shore as the world's youngest Elvis impersonator. "Bruno, it says right here you're the world's youngest Elvis impersonator."
**Michael Jackson Imitations** : Bruno had a knack for recreating Jackson's moves from "Billie Jean" and "Beat It," and at the Aloha Las Vegas revue in 2001 was filmed executing letter-perfect moves as Jackson. "At the Aloha Las Vegas review in 2001 he was captured on video executing letter-perfect dance moves as Michael Jackson."
**Self-Taught Guitar and Purple Rain Ritual** : As a teen Bruno taught himself guitar after listening to Jimi Hendrix and psyched himself up before shows by playing Prince's "Purple Rain." "before going on stage Bruno would psych himself up by playing Prince's purple rain."
**Move to LA and Name Change** : At 17 Bruno moved to LA to seek music fame and changed his performing name to Bruno Mars. "when he got there he changed his performing name to Bruno Mars."
**2008 Demo for First Hit** : In 2008, as a 22-year-old songwriter in LA, he recorded a demo for what would become his first hit, though not the song that made him a household name. "A 22 year old singer, songwriter who had moved to LA to find fortune in the music business was recording a demo for what would turn out to be his first hit."
**Cultural Appropriation Accusations** : Mars wrote hits for himself and other acts that owed a debt to his elders, and has been accused of cultural appropriation. "Mars has been accused of cultural appropriation."

# curiosity

**Funky Drummer Break** : Clyde Stubblefield's drum break on James Brown's 1970 "Funky Drummer" recurred as a sample on a slew of first-wave rap hits; in 1988 Public Enemy slowed it down for the instrumental "Security of the First World," which Kravitz used for "Justify My Love." "The monster drum break by Clyde Stubblefield on James Brown's 1970 track, Funky Drummer."
**First Now That's What I Call Music CD** : The first edition of the series included Kravitz's "Fly Away" alongside pop hits by Hanson and the Backstreet Boys, helping it cross over to top 40. "On the first edition of the Now That's What I Call Music CD series."
**20-Year Nostalgia Cycle for the 70s** : "It Ain't Over Till It's Over" kicked off the 20-year nostalgia cycle for 70s culture in 1991, before the rest of 90s pop culture mined the disco decade. "kicking off the 20-year nostalgia cycle for 70's culture."
