# genre - Yacht Rock

## curiosities
**Origin of the Term in 21st Century** : The name "Yacht Rock" is a modern invention, coined in the 21st century by a group of L.A.-based actors and writers. It originated at the dawn of the YouTube era as the title for a deliberately cheap-looking online TV series, but the term itself quickly gained widespread popularity. "The name Yacht Rock is a 21st century concoction, coined by a forceum of L.A. based actors and writers at the dawn of the YouTube era."
**Evolution and Broad Application** : Initially conceived as a label for a mini-genre of West Coast-based, studio-bred smashes from the late 1970s and early 1980s, "Yacht Rock" has since expanded dramatically. It has become a retro pop genre that is often broadly applied to encompass almost all 1970s and 1980s soft rock and JASI R&B, regardless of its original applicability. "In the decade and a half since this name took off, it's become the retro pop genre that ate everything."
**Chart Dominance at Turn of 80s** : Despite being a retroactively invented and sometimes overused term, the music later categorized as Yacht Rock was a dominant force on the charts around the turn of the 1980s, reflecting a significant trend in popular music. "Yacht Rock may be a slippery, overused term, but the music was dominant on the charts at the turn of the 80s."
**Absence of Explicit Nautical Imagery** : Counterintuitively, many of the early 1980s hits that retrospectively received the "Yacht Rock" label did not feature lyrics about beaches, piña coladas, or other nautical references, a notable characteristic given the genre's evocative name. "What you didn't hear in any of these early 1980 hits were lyrics about beaches, or piniacoladas, or nautical references."
**Timeframe of Dominance** : From 1976 to 1984, the radio airwaves were predominantly filled with the smooth music that would later become known as Yacht Rock, documenting a remarkable period of musical consistency and widespread popularity. "1976 to 1984, the radio airwaves were dominated by really smooth music, also known as Yacht Rock."

# curiosity

**"Peak Smooth" Era (Late 1970s - Early 1980s)** : The period around the turn of the 1980s, specifically highlighted by Christopher Cross's "Sailing" reaching number one on August 30, 1980, marked what was considered America's "peak smooth" in popular music. This era was characterized by sleek, jazzy, ultra-smooth sounds that emerged just after the disco boom and before the MTV revolution. "America had reached peak smooth."
**Dominance of Studio-Bred Performers** : In the transitional period between the disco era and the advent of MTV, studio-bred performers became the dominant force on the charts. Artists like Christopher Cross, Toto, Michael McDonald, and Steely Dan, often working with "armies" of sleek producers and sidemen, defined the sound of this musically significant moment. "studio-bred performers like Christopher Cross, Toto, Michael McDonald, Steely Dan, and their army of sleek producers and side men were kings of the charts."
**Shift in Contemporary Black Music** : During the early 1980s, even R&B performers, who had previously been known for popular funk and disco hits, adapted their sound. They began to shift towards smoother, "saltier" musical stylings deemed suitable for a cocktail lounge, reflecting a broader trend in pop music. "even the R&B performers of the day, previously known for funk and disco hits, were also shifting into smoother, saltier sounds suitable for a cocktail lounge."
