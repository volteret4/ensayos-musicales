# artist - Kate Bush

## songs
**Running Up That Hill (1985) - First Chart Run** : In its original 1985 run, "Running Up That Hill" peaked at number 30 on the Hot 100. It remains Kate Bush's only American Top 40 hit from that era. It was peaking on the Hot 100 at the same moment as the hits from Miami Vice, back when TV syncs had to reach the charts through physical record stores and an older generation of Top 40 radio. "Kate Bush's only American top 40 hit ever."

**Running Up That Hill (2022) - Stranger Things Season 4 Revival** : In the fourth season of Netflix's Stranger Things, the song is central to the plot. It has the power to break the character Max out of her prison in the dark realm known as the Upside Down. Unlike earlier TV syncs, it benefited from the exposure instantly. Billboard reported it as the number one download in America and the most-streamed song in the country, with more than 29 million streams. "The Song is practically a character."

**Running Up That Hill (2022) - Radio Airplay and Hot 100 Top Five** : The song also drew airplay at current Top 40 stations. In one week its radio audience totaled 2.4 million, nearly as large as current hits by Post Malone and Justin Bieber. Because it was strong in sales, streams and radio, the three components of the Hot 100, it became a 2022 mega-hit 37 years after Bush recorded it. It matched the top-five success of the Grey's Anatomy hits and gave Bush her first American top-five hit. "Kate Bush finally has an American top five hit."

**Running Up That Hill (2022) - Beating Previous TV Revivals** : Its digital performance surpassed earlier TV-driven revivals. Journey's "Don't Stop Believin'" peaked at number 21 in downloads even after The Sopranos. Badfinger's "Baby Blue", The Clash's "Should I Stay or Should I Go" and Limahl's "The NeverEnding Story" all failed to return to the Hot 100. "That's something that neither bad fingers baby blue nor the stranger songs by the clash and Lamal pulled off."

## curiosities
**Billboard Article on TV-Resurrected Songs** : Decades after "At This Moment", Billboard ran an article about songs resurrected by TV, citing Kate Bush's "Running Up That Hill". Radio consultant Sean Ross was quoted in it, tracing the trend back to Billy Vera. "songs resurrected by TV like Kate Bush's running up that hill."

**Fitting Gen Z Playlists** : In an era when Gen Z listeners build playlists where songs from any era compete equally, the 1985 track sits naturally alongside contemporary music. "Kate Bush's song just sounds like it fits."

# artist - Billy Vera & The Beaters

## members
Billy Vera

## labels
Rhino Records

## songs
**At This Moment (1981) - Original Release** : Veteran LA singer Billy Vera wrote this soulful saloon ballad in the 1970s. It was first issued as a single in 1981, when it charted briefly and peaked at number 79 on the Hot 100. "first issued as a single in 1981 when it actually charted briefly peaking at number 79 on the Hot 100."

**At This Moment (1986) - Discovered for Family Ties** : Five years later, an NBC producer caught Vera and his band in an LA nightclub. The producer was looking for a love theme for Alex P. Keaton, Michael J. Fox's character on Family Ties, and offered to license the song. Vera eagerly agreed but then had to get his out-of-print recording re-released. "The producer offered to license Vera's song at this moment and Vera eagerly said yes."

**At This Moment (1986) - Rhino Records Reissue** : It took Vera months to convince Rhino Records to reissue the song. By the time Rhino agreed, it had already aired on Family Ties twice. NBC and Vera himself were flooded with requests for it. "It took Vera months to convince a label in this case, Rhino Records, to reissue at this moment."

**At This Moment (1987) - Number One** : The song aired on Family Ties a third time in fall 1986. Only after that did the reissued single re-enter the Hot 100 and start climbing. After a dozen weeks, in January 1987, it reached number one, and NBC said it had never received so many letters and calls about one song. "After a dozen weeks in January 1987, at this moment by Billy Vera and The Beatles reached number one."

## curiosities
**Starting the TV Revival Trend** : Radio consultant Sean Ross credited "At This Moment" with opening a path to radio for songs that didn't sound like the rest of the format. Ross named UB40's "Red Red Wine" chart return of the late 80s as its most obvious successor. "I think at this moment had a lot to do with starting the trend because it established a route to radio for songs that weren't of a piece with what else was on the radio."

**Don't Stop Believin' (2007) - The Sopranos Finale** : HBO's The Sopranos finale cut to black as Tony Soprano listened to the song on a jukebox at Holsten's diner with his family. Digital sales rose nearly 400% in a week, with 41,000 downloads in that week alone. "digital sales of Don't Stop Believe in shot up nearly 400% in a week, 41,000 downloads sold in that week alone."

**Don't Stop Believin' - Blocked from the Hot 100 by Billboard Rules** : Billboard's rules at the time barred old songs without current pop radio airplay from re-entering the Hot 100. The song still reached number 21 on Billboard's Digital Song Sales chart, a sign it could well have re-entered the Hot 100, much as Kate Bush's song did in 2022. "the journey anthem did reach number 21 on Billboards digital song sales chart."

**Don't Stop Believin' - Best-Selling Catalog Song in iTunes History** : The song kept selling and became the all-time best-selling catalog song in iTunes history, with more than 7 million downloads. It was also featured prominently in the pilot of Fox's Glee. "It went on to become the all-time best-selling catalog song in iTunes history with more than 7 million downloads sold."

# curiosity

**The Miami Vice Model** : In the 1980s, after Miami Vice's success, TV music supervisors curated songs for maximum exposure and turned under-appreciated gems into hits. Several mid-80s shows, such as Moonlighting, tried to copy the model, with mixed results. "Certain mid-80s TV shows did try to replicate the Miami Vice model."

**Movies Led the Revival Trend Before TV** : After "At This Moment", movies initially did more than TV to bring back old hits. Examples include Ferris Bueller's Day Off, Stand By Me and Wayne's World. "Also at first movies more than TV took advantage of the trend."

**The Monkees and Partridge Family Precedent** : The fictional band The Heights followed The Monkees and The Partridge Family as TV-created acts that scored real number one hits. "like the monkeys or the Partridge family before them."

**Syncs in the New Millennium** : In the 2000s, TV showrunners and music supervisors looked further afield for syncs, meaning songs their shows could in a sense "own". Previously undiscovered or under-heralded songs were preferred. "If a song was previously undiscovered or underheralded and the show made it bigger, so much the better."

**Grey's Anatomy - King of Syncs** : Shonda Rhimes's long-running ABC medical drama Grey's Anatomy is described as the king of syncs among millennial TV shows. It essentially made two major hits of the 2000s, Snow Patrol's "Chasing Cars" and The Fray's "How to Save a Life". "Among millennial TV shows, the King of Sinks you might say was Shonda Rhymes's long-running ABC medical drama Grey's Anatomy."

**The O.C. and Indie Rock Curation** : Fox's 2003 teen drama The O.C. became known for curating a selection of fairly hip indie rock for its soundtrack. It broke Phantom Planet and Imogen Heap. "The OC, became renowned for curating a selection of fairly hip indie rock for its soundtrack."

**Billboard's Top-50 Rule for Old Songs** : Even in the streaming era, it is hard for an old song revived by TV to return to the Hot 100. Under Billboard's rules, an old song can only re-enter if its combined points would place it in the top 50. It also competes against current hits that have current radio airplay. "an old song can only come back if its combined points would put it within the top 50, the bar is very high."

**Streaming Services and Streaming TV** : Spotify, Apple Music and other streaming services now reach about a third of US households. Streaming TV services such as Netflix, Hulu, Disney+, Amazon, Apple TV+ and HBO Max together reach millions more people than premium cable did at its peak. "it's one thing to buy a single digital copy of Don't Stop Believe in. It's quite another to play it on a streaming service over and over again."

**Stranger Things' Music Approach** : The Duffer Brothers' teen sci-fi horror series is set in the early-to-mid 1980s and makes heavy use of period songs. Its music supervisors give special showcases to songs that weren't necessarily big hits the first time. "the Stranger Things music supervisors give special showcases to songs that weren't necessarily big hits the first time."
