## curiosities
**2001 Client Expansion - Usher, Ray J, Foxy Brown** : By 2001 the Neptunes had greatly expanded their clientele, producing hits that year alone for Usher, Ray J and Foxy Brown. Their most closely watched client that year was Britney Spears. "By 2001, the Neptune's, Farell Williams and Chad Hugo, had massively grown their clientele."
**2002 - High Watermark as Producers** : 2002 was the peak of the Neptunes' production career. They applied their bouncy, skittering hip-hop-meets-pop sound to hits by Busta Rhymes, boy band NSYNC and NSYNC singer turned soloist Justin Timberlake, plus Nelly's "Hot in Herre." "By 2002, which would prove the high watermark of the Neptune's career as producers, Ferel and Chad were pulling a range of acts over to their side of the hit-making street."
**Pop Success Without Losing Hip-Hop Credibility** : In 2002 the duo stayed versatile. Pop acceptance did not damage their rap standing, and the same year they produced the most acclaimed rap album, Clipse's Lord Willin'. "Their pop acceptance hadn't damaged their hip-hop credibility."
**A Recognizable Yet Varied Signature Sound** : Their productions sounded distinctly like them, yet every song had its own character. "You could often guess when a hit was a Neptune's track, but each song built its own little world."
**First Hot 100 Number One - Hot in Herre (2002)** : Nelly's "Hot in Herre" topped the Hot 100 in June 2002. It was the Neptunes' first ever pop number one and held the top spot for seven weeks. "becoming the Neptune's first ever number one pop single."
**Pushing the Envelope Sonically (2003-2004)** : At the end of 2003 they gave their protégé Kelis her biggest hit, "Milkshake." In 2004 the even more stripped-down "Drop It Like It's Hot" gave Snoop Dogg his first Hot 100 number one. "Behind the mixing board, the Neptune's were still, sonically, pushing the envelope."
**Rock Your Body and the Wardrobe Malfunction** : The Neptunes wrote and produced "Rock Your Body." Its notoriety came from the 2004 Super Bowl halftime show, but the societal fallout largely and unfairly fell on Janet Jackson rather than Justin Timberlake, and little of it reached the Neptunes. "not much rubbed off on the Neptune's either."
**First Number One for a Woman Performer - Hollaback Girl (2005)** : In spring 2005 Gwen Stefani's "Hollaback Girl" gave the Neptunes another number one pop hit. It was their first chart-topper for a woman performer. "this was their first for a woman performer."
**Last Chart-Topper - Money Maker (2006)** : Ludacris's "Money Maker" hit number one on the Hot 100 in October 2006. It was the Neptunes' last chart-topper as a production team. "the Neptune's, as a production team, never scored another chart-topping hit."
**Madonna's Give It 2 Me - A Reversal of Fortune (2008)** : The Neptunes produced the follow-up to Timbaland's "4 Minutes" for Madonna, but it reached only number 57 on the Hot 100. This showed how fortunes had reversed between Timbaland and Pharrell within a couple of years. "This was a stark indication of how fortune had reversed between Tim Beland and Farell in just a couple of years."
**Seesaw Careers With Timbaland** : The two teams never regarded each other as direct competition, but often when one was hot the other hit a low. Timbaland's slump in the early-to-mid 2000s coincided with Pharrell's rise. "often when one man's career was hot, the others hit a low."

## albums
**G I R L (2014) - Relaunch of Solo Career** : After "Happy," Pharrell relaunched his solo career with G I R L. It quickly spawned the top 40 follow-up "Come Get It Bae," a duet with Miley Cyrus, and also featured Justin Timberlake as a guest. "Farell relaunched his solo career, dropping a new album called Girl"
**Despicable Me Soundtrack (2010) - First Full Film Score** : At the invitation of film composer Hans Zimmer, Pharrell recorded the soundtrack for the hit animated film Despicable Me. He had produced movie soundtrack songs before, but this was his first time composing most of a single film's soundtrack and score. He brought in Robin Thicke, who sang on "My Life." "this was Farell's first time composing most of a single film's soundtrack and score."
**Despicable Me 2 Soundtrack (2013) - The Gru Challenge** : Pharrell returned to oversee the sequel's soundtrack. The directors set him a songwriting challenge: a song for Gru, the franchise's evil character, in a state of uncharacteristic happiness. "the film's directors issued a songwriting challenge to Farell."

## songs
**Frontin' (2003) - Solo Debut Single** : Released in summer 2003, this was Pharrell's first single under his own name. It is a languid hip-hop jam sung entirely in falsetto, with a supporting rap from his friend Jay-Z. It reached number 5 on the Hot 100 by the fall and suggested the tattooed producer could become a star in his own right. "A language hip-hop jam sung entirely in falsetto, with a supporting rap from his friend Jay-Z."
**Can I Have It Like That (2005) - Second Solo Single** : Gwen Stefani returned the favor for "Hollaback Girl" by singing the hook on Pharrell's second solo single. "Gwen Stefani returned the favor to Ferel, singing the hook on his second solo single, Can I Have It Like That?"
**Happy (2013) - Despicable Me 2 Song & Oscar Nominee** : Pharrell wrote it after nearly a dozen tries as a joyous slice of retro soul. The filmmakers were ecstatic, but Universal, the studio and soundtrack label, chose not to promote it in summer 2013 because Pharrell already had two songs at the top of the charts. In fall 2013 he released "24 Hours of Happy," billed as the first ever 24-hour music video, featuring scores of citizens and celebrities in Los Angeles singing along. That video propelled the song up the charts, and it reached number one on the Hot 100 by March 2014. It was nominated for the Oscar for Best Original Song, spent 10 weeks at number one, and ranked as Billboard's top song of 2014. "After nearly a dozen tries, Farell pulled off the assignment, a joyous slice of retro soul that he called simply happy."
**Come Get It Bae (2014) - Duet With Miley Cyrus** : The top 40 follow-up to "Happy" from G I R L. "a top 40 follow-up hit with Come Get It Bay, a duet with Miley Cyrus."

## curiosities
**Two Songs on Top of the Charts at Once (Summer 2013)** : For five weeks in summer 2013, "Blurred Lines" and "Get Lucky" held the number one and number two spots, with Pharrell as vocalist and co-songwriter on both. He had not been so ubiquitous since the Neptunes' peak, and never before as a credited artist, even though he played a supporting role on both tracks. "a hammerlock on the top of the Hot 100."
**First Person to Perform a Number One Song as an Oscar Nominee** : Pharrell performed "Happy" on the 2014 Oscars telecast while it was the number one song in the country. "he became the first person in history to have the number one song in the country as a nominee at the Academy Awards."
**From Quirky to Norm-Core** : After more than a decade of normalizing the unusual, Pharrell and Timbaland, both by then in their early 40s, were responsible for the squarely mainstream hits "Happy" and "Mirrors." "They were of norm-core themselves."
**Post-Neptunes 2010s Hit Streak** : Pharrell's post-Neptunes streak continued through the 2010s, if not at the torrid pace of 2002 through 2005. He produced hits for Beyoncé, Ariana Grande and Beck, plus Kendrick Lamar's 2015 Black Lives Matter anthem "Alright." "Farell had a pretty great 2010s, producing hits for Beyoncé. Ariana Grande and Beck."
**Striking Out on His Own (Late 2000s)** : While periodically releasing music with Chad Hugo as N.E.R.D, Pharrell began working solo as a writer-producer and artist. Meanwhile Timbaland was scoring a wave of hits in 2009 and 2010. "behind the scenes, Farell began to strike out on his own, both as a writer-producer and an artist."
**Producing Missy Elliott's Comeback Single (2015)** : Pharrell produced Missy Elliott's "WTF (Where They From)." "produced by none other than Forel Williams."
**Reminiscing for Missy's Video Vanguard Award (2019)** : In the video package for Missy's award, Pharrell recalled her impact on Virginia Beach. "She put our accent on the airwaves that feels like something."

## albums
**Shock Value (2007) - First Solo Album in Over Half a Decade** : Timbaland's first album under his own name in more than half a decade. Its lead single "Give It to Me" topped the Hot 100 in April 2007. The album spun off hits for the rest of the year, including "The Way I Are" and "Apologize." "Tim's first album under his own name in more than half a decade."

## songs
**Give It to Me (2007) - Feat. Nelly Furtado & Justin Timberlake** : Furtado and Timberlake were eager to return the favor for the hits Timbaland had produced for them and stepped up right away. The lead single from Shock Value reached number one on the Hot 100 in April 2007. "Give it to me, reach number one on the Hot 100 in April 2007."
**The Way I Are (2007) - Feat. Keri Hilson** : A bubbling electro-funk track featuring singer Keri Hilson that reached number three in August 2007. "the bubbling electro-funk track The Way I R, featuring singer Kerry Hilsen, a number three hit in August."
**Apologize (2007) - Feat. OneRepublic** : A number two hit in November 2007. Timbaland had only subtly remixed this melodramatic ballad by OneRepublic, but it was credited to him as lead artist, and the band did not mind taking second billing to the producer who gave them their breakthrough. "which in a flex was credited to Tim Beland featuring one republic."

## curiosities
**Turn Off the Light Remix (2001) - Seeds of a Comeback** : Timbaland remixed Nelly Furtado's 2001 hit "Turn Off the Light," bringing out the hip-hop elements of her pop hit. This planted the seeds for his later commercial return. "Tim's 2001 remix brought out the hip-hop elements in Fertado's pop hit."
**Dirt Off Your Shoulder and the Wilderness Years (2004-2006)** : After Jay-Z's "Dirt Off Your Shoulder" reached number five in early 2004, Timbaland entered a two-year wilderness period. Most of his productions missed the top 10 or even the top 40, though he kept busy with albums for Brandy, LL Cool J and Bubba Sparxxx. "there was a sense in the industry that maybe his production style had peaked."
**2006 Guardian Interview - Break the Box** : In a 2006 interview with The Guardian, coming out of his least productive hit-making period, Timbaland criticized the state of music. He said it was boring and watered down, that nobody was taking chances, and that making music is about taking risks. "Somebody's got to break the box, bust it open."
**Mosley Music Group Launch (2006)** : Timbaland, born Timothy Mosley, launched the vanity label Mosley Music Group with Universal Music in 2006. When Nelly Furtado's DreamWorks contract was absorbed into Universal, he signed her to it. "a new vanity label Mosley Music Group that Tim launched in 2006 with the universal music."
**Back-to-Back Chart-Toppers in 2006** : "Promiscuous" was his first Hot 100 number one as a producer since Aaliyah's "Try Again" in 2000. About a month later "SexyBack" took over. He went from virtually hitless to the biggest hitmaker on the charts in a few months, and "My Love" followed at number one. "Producer Tim Beland had gone from virtually hitless to the biggest hitmaker on the charts in just a few months."
**Vocal Cameos Across His Productions** : He traded lines with Furtado on "Promiscuous" (with featured billing) and with Timberlake on "SexyBack," and added small vocal touches to "Say It Right" and "What Goes Around... Comes Around." "Like a Hollywood author, Tim was making cameos across his body of work."
**Go-To Producer for the Unconventional** : His second act proved even more fruitful than his first. He was sought out by Rihanna, Björk, Duran Duran and former Soundgarden frontman Chris Cornell. Not all of these collaborations were hits. "the image of Tim Beland as the go-to producer for the excessively unconventional."
**2009-2010 Hits With Timberlake and Drake** : Timbaland scored another wave of hits in 2009 and 2010 with support from Justin Timberlake and the then newly emerging rapper Drake. "Tim Beland was scoring another wave of hits in 2009 and 2010, with support from Justin Timberlake. And the then newly emerging rapper Drake."
**Last Wave of Hits (Mid-2010s)** : Though he never returned to his late-2000s heyday, his work with Timberlake and Jay-Z gave him one last wave of hits, including "Holy Grail," the title track of Magna Carta Holy Grail. "his work with Timberlake and JZ gave him one last wave of hits in the mid-2010s"
**Memoir on the Missy Rift** : In his memoir, Timbaland theorized that he and Missy were both still mourning Aaliyah and were taking that sublimated grief out on each other. "In his memoir, Tim theorizes that they were, quote, both still mourning Aliyah"
**Missy Told Him "Sometimes Being Wrong Is Right"** : In the 2019 Video Vanguard package, Timbaland recalled that his Virginia sound didn't fit what New York or the West Coast were doing, but Missy encouraged him. "Coming from BA, my sound didn't fit into what New York was doing, what West Coast was doing, but Missy, she like nah Tim, sometimes being wrong is right."

# artist - Missy Elliott

## genres

## concerts

## albums
**Under Construction (2002) - Fourth Studio Album Dedicated to Aaliyah** : Missy dedicated the album to Aaliyah but made her commemoration an all-out party rather than something somber. It was led by her biggest pop hit, "Work It." Entering 2003 it was platinum on its way to double platinum, making it her all-time bestseller, and it also produced "Gossip Folks." "Missy decided her commemoration of Alia would be an all-out party"
**This Is Not a Test! (2003) - The Collaboration Frays** : Missy and Timbaland returned to the studio right away in 2003. They reportedly bickered, and Elliott rejected most of the beats he brought her. Released in fall 2003, the album went platinum on the strength of her reputation but fell short of the top 10, the only Missy album to do so. It was the last Missy Elliott album primarily produced by Timbaland. "But it fell short of the top 10, her only album to do so."
**The Cookbook (2005) - Last Album to Date** : Timbaland produced only a couple of deep cuts, making it her first album mostly not produced by him. Its lead single, the number three smash "Lose Control," was produced by Elliott herself. It remains her last full-length album, and her six studio LPs have a perfect platinum streak. "The cookbook from 2005 remains her last, and her six studio LPs have a perfect platinum streak."

## songs
**Work It (2002) - Biggest Pop Hit** : The song showed the public had acclimated to the Virginia Beach team's wackiness. The backwards line in the chorus is "I put my thing down, flip it, and reverse it" played backwards, right after Missy raps it forwards. It packed in animal noises for male body parts and references to Prince, Halle Berry and Kunta Kinte from Roots, plus apparently the first popular recorded use of "badonkadonk." It climbed to number two on the Hot 100 in November 2002 and held there for 10 straight weeks behind Eminem's 12-week "Lose Yourself." That is the longest runner-up run without reaching number one, a record tied with Foreigner's 1981 "Waiting for a Girl Like You." It topped the 2002 Pazz & Jop poll, Elliott's second straight year winning it, and two decades later ranked number 56 on Rolling Stone's greatest songs list. "The song was like a collection of memes before we had a word for memes."
**Work It Music Video (2002)** : The video featured Timbaland, a limber child dancer named Alyson Stoner, and kinetic dance moves by Missy and a troupe of breakdancers. It won Video of the Year at the MTV VMAs one year later. "It would go on to win video of the year at the MTV VMAs one year later."
**Gossip Folks (2003) - Feat. Ludacris** : A tongue-twisting number eight hit from Under Construction. "The album produced another tongue-twisting hit with Gossip Folks, a number eight team up with rapper Ludacris."
**Pass That Dutch (2003)** : The only hit from This Is Not a Test! was an attempt to recreate the zaniness of "Work It." It underperformed, peaking at number 27 on the Hot 100 and number 17 on the R&B chart. "an attempt to recreate the zaniness of work it underperformed on the charts"
**Lose Control (2005) - Self-Produced Smash** : The lead single from The Cookbook reached number three and was produced by Elliott herself. She later performed it at the 2015 Super Bowl halftime show. "the number 3 smash lose control, was produced by Elliott herself."
**WTF (Where They From) (2015) - Comeback Single** : Released after her Super Bowl appearance and produced by Pharrell Williams. It reached number 22 on the Hot 100, her first top 40 hit as a lead artist in over a decade. "Elliott's first top 40 hit as a lead artist in over a decade."

## curiosities
**Tweet's Oops (Oh My) - Female Sexual Agency** : Missy wrote "Oops (Oh My)" for Tweet about a woman's self-admiration and arousal at the sight of her own beauty. Timbaland produced it. "Still finding new ways to express female sexual agency"
**Throwing Herself Into Work After Aaliyah's Death** : Despite the lingering pain of Aaliyah's 2001 death, Missy and Timbaland distracted themselves by pouring themselves into work, both for others and for Missy herself. "To distract themselves, Missy and Tim poured themselves into their work"
**Lady Marmalade (2001) - Uncredited Mistress of Ceremonies** : On the number one remake from Moulin Rouge, credited to Christina Aguilera, Lil' Kim, Mýa and Pink, Missy produced and acted as MC. She was the only vocalist not credited. "the only vocalist not credited was its producer and mistress of ceremonies Missy Elliott."
**Lifting Up Other Women Artists** : As early as 2001 her goal was producing hits primarily for other women, and after The Cookbook she stopped recording for herself while writing and producing for others. She co-wrote and rapped on Ciara's number two "1, 2 Step." She produced and guested on Keyshia Cole's "Let It Go" (number seven, 2007). She produced and rapped on Jazmine Sullivan's "Need U Bad" (R&B number one, 2008), nearly a decade and a half before Sullivan won a Grammy for Heaux Tales. She produced Monica's R&B chart-topper "Everything to Me," Monica's first number one on that chart in seven years. "Especially other black women artists giving them a leg up as others had done for her early in her career."
**Katy Perry Remix (2011)** : Missy's remix of Katy Perry's "Last Friday Night" took that song the last mile to number one on the Hot 100. "Missy agreed to do a remix of Katie Perry's Last Friday Night that took that song the last mile to number one"
**Graves' Disease Revelation (Summer 2011)** : Elliott revealed that her absence from recording was largely due to Graves' disease, a thyroid disorder causing severe tremors. She later had successful treatment. "a thyroid disorder that caused her severe tremors while driving or even just holding a pen."
**Ellen Surprise for a Superfan (2018)** : On Ellen DeGeneres' talk show, Elliott surprised Mary Halsey, who had gone viral with a flawless karaoke performance of "Work It." "Elliott surprised a superfan named Mary Halsey"
**Janet Jackson Guest Role (2016)** : She made a guest appearance on a Janet Jackson single in 2016. "her guest role on a Janet Jackson single in 2016."
**MTV Video Vanguard Award (2019)** : MTV presented her with its highest commemorative honor. The night became one long tribute, and she performed a medley of her material. "In 2019, MTV finally acknowledged her singular influence by presenting her with their Video Vanguard Award"
**Visual-First Songwriting** : In the Video Vanguard package Missy explained that she needs to see a song's video before believing in it. "If I can't see the visual of what the video is going to be, then that's a record that I just got to throw away."
