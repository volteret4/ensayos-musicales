## albums

It was a sleeper hit, never rising above No. 56 on the album chart but quietly going gold, and spinning off minor hit songs at multiple radio formats. "The debut album in Search Up was 2002's Sleeper Hit, never rising above No. 56 on the album chart, but quietly going gold, and even spinning off minor hit songs at multiple radio formats."

## albums
**Lord Willin' (2002)** : The most acclaimed rap album of the year, produced by The Neptunes for Clipse, a pair of brothers who had known Pharrell since their teenage years in Virginia Beach. The album generated two cutting-edge hits, "Grindin'" and "When the Last Time," which reached the top 10 on the R&B chart and top 40 on the pop chart. "The Neptune's produced the year's most acclaimed rap album, Lord Willen by Clips, a pair of brothers who'd known Ferel since their days as teenagers in Virginia Beach."

## songs
**I'm a Slave 4 U (2001)** : The lead single from Britney Spears' 2001 album "Britney," originally demoed by The Neptunes for Janet Jackson. When Jackson passed on it, Spears, eager to shed her teen pop image, recorded the track, marking a significant R&B-leaning departure for her and proving The Neptunes' ability to work with mainstream pop stars. "I'm a slave for you. Was a track for real and Chad had originally demoed for Janet Jackson."
**Hot in Herre (2002)** : An explosive hit from St. Louis rapper Nelly, produced by The Neptunes. They built the track by interpolating the hook from Go Go pioneer Chuck Brown's 1979 R&B number one smash "Bustin' Loose," adding their percolating percussion and Nelly's rap. It became the 2002 song of the summer, topped the Hot 100 for seven weeks, and was Billboard's third biggest hit of that year, marking The Neptunes' first ever number one pop single. "The Neptune's flipped this hook, added their percolating percussion and Nelly's rap, and turned it into the 2002 song of the summer hot and here."
**Grindin' (2002)** : A cutting-edge hit from Clipse's Neptunes-produced album "Lord Willin'," which reached the top 10 on the R&B chart and top 40 on the pop chart. "The CD by Clips generated two cutting-edge top 10 R&B top 40 pop hits, Grindin'."
**When the Last Time (2002)** : Another cutting-edge hit from Clipse's Neptunes-produced album "Lord Willin'," which reached the top 10 on the R&B chart and top 40 on the pop chart. "And when the last time."
**Beautiful (2003)** : A number 6 hit for Snoop Dogg, featuring vocals from Pharrell Williams and Chad Hugo, showcasing their continued work producing for other artists while Pharrell was also launching his solo career. "including Snoop Dogg's number 6 hit Beautiful."
**Change Clothes (2003)** : The first single from JAY-Z's "The Black Album," produced by The Neptunes, further solidifying their presence behind the mixing board for major hip-hop artists. "And the debonair changed clothes, the first single from Jay-Z's The Blackout."
**Milkshake (2003)** : Kelis's biggest ever hit, a number 3 smash at the end of 2003, which The Neptunes produced. The track was notably built off of a skeletal synthesizer beat, demonstrating their innovative sound. "At the end of 03, they gave their protege, Collise, her biggest ever hit with milkshake. A number 3 smash built off of a skeletal synthesizer beat."
**Rock Your Body (2003)** : The third single from Justin Timberlake's "Justified" album, a sumptuous dance track written and produced by The Neptunes that reached number 5. It later became infamous for Timberlake's performance at the 2004 Super Bowl halftime show, where it triggered the "wardrobe malfunction" involving Janet Jackson. "Unfortunately, Rock Your Body later became infamous when Timberlake performed it at the 2004 Super Bowl halftime show, while triggering the so-called wardrobe malfunction on Janet Jackson."
**Drop It Like It's Hot (2004)** : An even more stripped-down track produced by The Neptunes in 2004, which gave Snoop Dogg his first ever Hot 100 number 1 hit. "And in 2004, the even more stripped down drop it like it's hot, gave Snoop Dogg his first ever hot 100 number 1 hit."
**Hollaback Girl (2005)** : Gwen Stefani's biggest hit from her solo debut album "Love, Angel, Music, Baby," which reached the top of the Hot 100 in just six weeks and was produced by The Neptunes. Stefani claimed she wrote it in response to derogatory comments from Courtney Love, with Pharrell channeling that sass into a track built like a cheerleader chant, borrowing from Queen's "We Will Rock You" and Tony Basil's "Mickey," and its titular phrase from Fabolous's "Young'n (Holla Back)." "Hollaback girl was a clapback song before clapbacks."
**Money Maker (2006)** : A Ludacris smash hit that The Neptunes scored as producers in 2006, reaching number one on the Hot 100 in October of that year. It was notably sandwiched between Timbaland's hits "SexyBack" and "My Love" in the chart rankings. "In 2006, they scored one more chart-topper as producers, manning the boards for the ludicrous smash money maker."
**Give It 2 Me (2008)** : A track produced by The Neptunes for Madonna's "Hard Candy" album, released as a follow-up to her Timbaland-produced hit "4 Minutes." This track, however, was not nearly as successful, only reaching number 57 on the Hot 100, indicating a reversal of fortunes between The Neptunes and Timbaland at the time. "Only the track Farell Williams and Chad Hugo produced for Madonna was not nearly as big a hit."

## curiosities
**Early Clientele Growth (2001)** : By 2001, The Neptunes, consisting of Pharrell Williams and Chad Hugo, had massively grown their clientele, producing hits for a diverse range of artists including Usher, Ray J, and Foxy Brown that year alone. "That year alone, they produced hits for everyone from usher to Ray J to Foxy Brown."
**Versatility and Credibility (2002)** : The year 2002 was remarkable for The Neptunes due to their versatility; their pop acceptance had not diminished their hip-hop credibility, as they produced both chart-topping pop hits and the year's most acclaimed rap album. "What made 2002 so remarkable for the Neptune's was their versatility. Their pop acceptance hadn't damaged their hip-hop credibility."
**Distinctive Sound** : The Neptunes developed a distinctive sound that made their handiwork recognizable, a bouncy, skittering hip-hop meets pop style, yet each song they produced managed to create its own unique sonic world. "All of their handiwork sounded distinctly like them. You could often guess when a hit was a Neptune's track, but each song built its own little world."
**Wardrobe Malfunction Incident (2004)** : The Neptunes wrote the line "gonna have you naked by the end of this song" for Justin Timberlake's 2003 hit "Rock Your Body," which became infamous when Timberlake performed it at the 2004 Super Bowl halftime show, triggering the "wardrobe malfunction" with Janet Jackson. Despite the song's infamy, not much negative impact rubbed off on The Neptunes compared to Janet Jackson, who bore the brunt of the societal backlash. "The line, going to have you naked by the end of this song, the Neptune's wrote that line."
**End of Chart-Topping Era (2006)** : After producing Ludacris's number one hit "Money Maker" in October 2006, The Neptunes, as a production team, never scored another chart-topping hit for the rest of the decade. "But after money maker, the Neptune's, as a production team, never scored another chart-topping hit."
**Reversal of Fortune (Late 2000s)** : The late 2000s saw a stark reversal of fortune between The Neptunes (Pharrell and Chad Hugo) and Timbaland; while Timbaland experienced a resurgence in hits, The Neptunes' chart success waned, exemplified by the underperformance of their Madonna production "Give It 2 Me" compared to Timbaland's "4 Minutes." "This was a stark indication of how fortune had reversed between Tim Beland and Farell in just a couple of years."

## curiosities
**Early Career - Virginia Beach** : Timothy "Timbaland" Mosley was one of three friends from Virginia Beach, alongside Pharrell Williams and Chad Hugo (The Neptunes) and Missy Elliott, who possessed quirky musical tastes and an innate talent for crafting hooks. "Three friends from around Virginia Beach with quirky tastes in music and genius ears for a hook."
**Mourning Aaliyah (2001-2003)** : After the death of Aaliyah in 2001, Timbaland and Missy Elliott poured themselves into their work to distract from the lingering pain. Timbaland later theorized in his memoir that their bickering during the production of Missy's "This Is Not a Test" album in 2003 was a result of them both "still mourning Aliyah" and taking that "sublimated grief out on each other." "But Missy and Tim poured themselves into their work, both for others and for Missy herself."
**"Wilderness Period" (2004-2006)** : After "Dirt Off Your Shoulder" reached number 5 in early 2004, Timbaland entered a two-year period where most of his produced tracks failed to hit the top 10 or even the top 40. Despite staying busy working on albums for artists like Brandy, LL Cool J, and Bubba Sparxxx, there was a sense in the industry that his production style might have peaked. "But after Dirt reached number 5 on the hot 100 in early 2004, Tim Beland went into a two-year wilderness period, where most of the tracks he produced failed to hit, missing the top 10 or even the top 40."
**Opinion on Contemporary Music (2006)** : In a 2006 interview with British newspaper The Guardian, Timbaland expressed dissatisfaction with the state of music, calling it "boring, too watered down" and feeling that "nobody's taken chances." He emphasized his belief that "making music is about taking risks" and that someone needed to "break the box, bust it open." "I don't really like where music is at right now. It's boring, too watered down. Nobody's taken chances."
**Comeback and Reinvention (2006)** : Timbaland's "hitless period" ended dramatically in 2006, partly due to the launch of his new vanity label, Mosley Music Group, but primarily because of the seeds of his commercial return planted years earlier, culminating in his successful reinvention with Nelly Furtado's "Loose" album and Justin Timberlake's "FutureSex/LoveSounds." "But Tim Beland's hitless period was about to end in high style."
**Musical Cameos (2007)** : By 2007, Timbaland was making small vocal touches and "cameos" across his body of work as a producer, notably on Nelly Furtado's "Say It Right" and Justin Timberlake's "What Goes Around... Comes Around," akin to a "Hollywood author" appearing in their own creations. "Like a Hollywood author, Tim was making cameos across his body of work."
**Second Act More Fruitful (2007)** : Timbaland's second act as a producer became even more fruitful than his first, as he was sought out by a wide range of artists across pop, rock, and R&B, including Rihanna, Björk, Duran Duran, and Chris Cornell, solidifying his image as the go-to producer for the "excessively unconventional." "Tim's second act was now even more fruitful than his first, as he was sought out by a range of artists across the spectrum of pop rock and R&B."
**Reversal of Fortune (Late 2000s)** : The late 2000s saw a reversal of fortune where Timbaland's career became hot while Pharrell Williams' hit a low, although the two never considered their work direct competition. This was exemplified by the relative success of Timbaland's Madonna production "4 Minutes" compared to The Neptunes' "Give It 2 Me." "This was a stark indication of how fortune had reversed between Tim Beland and Farell in just a couple of years."
**Virginia Beach Musical Visionaries** : In 2019, Timbaland, Missy Elliott, and Pharrell Williams reminisced about their shared history as musical visionaries and natives of Virginia Beach. Timbaland specifically recalled how Missy encouraged his unique sound: "Coming from BA, my sound didn't fit into what New York was doing, what West Coast was doing, but Missy, she like nah Tim, sometimes being wrong is right."
**"Norm-Core" Hitmakers (Mid-2010s)** : By the mid-2010s, after more than a decade of normalizing the unusual, Timbaland and Pharrell Williams, both in their early 40s, became responsible for squarely mainstream hits like "Mirrors" and "Happy," effectively becoming "kind of a huge hit" and "norm-core themselves." "They were of norm-core themselves."
