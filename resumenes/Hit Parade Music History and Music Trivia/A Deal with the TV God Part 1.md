# artist - Kate Bush

## genres
Art Rock

## albums
**Hounds of Love (1985) - Best-Selling Studio Album** : Kate Bush's 1985 album became her best-selling studio album. Its first single, "Running Up That Hill," was issued in the summer of 1985. By then Bush had already had a string of hit singles and albums in the UK since the late 70s. "what would become her best-selling studio album, Hounds of Love."

## songs
**Running Up That Hill (A Deal with God) (1985) - Original Release** : This was the first single from Hounds of Love, issued in the summer of 1985. Its full title was "Running Up That Hill (A Deal with God)." It is described as a cross between an intimate meditation and a thunderous anthem. It cracked Billboard's top 40 for the first time at No. 39 in the week ending November 9, 1985. That was the same week Jan Hammer's "Miami Vice Theme" hit No. 1 on the Hot 100 and the Miami Vice soundtrack topped the album chart. It peaked at No. 30 in November 1985, unable to get past hits by Dire Straits, Sting and The Cars. "Running Up That Hill topped out at No. 30 on the chart by November 85."
**Running Up That Hill (2022) - Stranger Things Revival** : In June 2022, after the song was featured in TV's Stranger Things, it re-debuted on the Hot 100 at No. 8. The following week it leapt to No. 4. This gave Bush, at age 63, her first-ever American top 10 hit, and a top 5 one. The show alone made it a top five hit, which places it instantly among the biggest TV-spawned hits of all time. It is described as the byproduct of decades of evolution in how TV makes hits. "single-handedly made a top five hit by TV's Stranger Things."

## curiosities
**UK Star Without a US Hit** : Since the late 70s Bush had a string of hit singles and albums in her UK homeland, but as of 1985 she had never scored a major pop hit in America. "Running Up That Hill" was the song that changed that, although in 1985 it could only do so much to break her in the US. "Bush had, as of 85, yet to score a major pop hit in America."
**First US Top 10 at Age 63** : The 2022 revival gave Bush her first-ever American top 10, and in fact a top 5, 37 years after the song's original release. The comeback was catalyzed mainly by television rather than by radio or a growing audience alone. "At age 63, Kate Bush has her first ever American top 10 hit, Top 5."
**A Deal with the TV God** : In the fall of 1985 Bush could not have known that her song would later become a TV-driven smash. The episode frames this as a bargain with the television, a play on the song's subtitle. "Let's just call it a deal with the TV God."

## songs
**I Want to Hold Your Hand (1964) - Already No. 1 Before Sullivan** : The Beatles arrived in New York for The Ed Sullivan Show already holding a US No. 1. The song was in its third week atop the Hot 100 when they performed it live on TV. Because of the data lag in Billboard's charts, it had really been No. 1 for about a month. The Sullivan appearance probably helped accelerate its popularity, and it spent seven weeks on top, though whether it would have done so without TV is debatable. The Beatles went on to dominate the charts for the next three months. "I want to hold your hand was in its third week atop the Hot 100 when they performed it live on the telly."
**All You Need Is Love (1967) - Written for Global Television** : John Lennon wrote the song for Our World, the first-ever global live televised satellite broadcast, watched by some 600 million people worldwide. The Beatles performed it live in a studio, backed by a small orchestra. It topped the Hot 100 about eight weeks after the broadcast. By 1967 the Beatles understood television well enough to use it to create and market a hit song, unlike "I Want to Hold Your Hand," which was already a hit before their TV appearance. "All you need is love only existed because of television."

## curiosities
**Ed Sullivan Debut - 73 Million Viewers** : The Beatles appeared on The Ed Sullivan Show on February 9, 1964. A then-record 73 million Americans watched, more than a third of the country. The appearance fueled Beatlemania in the US and secured the group's popularity there permanently. It is called probably the most famous musical moment in TV history. "watched by a then record 73 million Americans, more than a third of the country."
**Outcharted by The Monkees in 1967** : The Beatles took over a year to break on the Billboard charts. In 1967, the year of Sgt. Pepper and the Summer of Love, the act with the most weeks at No. 1 on the album chart was not the Beatles but The Monkees, a TV group created to capitalize on the Beatles' success. "the group with the most weeks at number one on the album chart was not the Beatles. It was the monkeys."

**Sixteen Tons (1955) - Fastest-Selling Single to Date** : Ford was a five-day-a-week TV personality on NBC's daytime schedule as well as a recording artist. "16 tons sold a million copies in just three weeks, the fastest-selling single of the recording industry to date."

**Plot-Explainer Themes Failed on the Charts** : In the 60s, TV themes tried to summarize a show in about a minute, as in Gilligan's Island, The Beverly Hillbillies and Green Acres. These were not chart hits, and neither, decades later, was the Fresh Prince of Bel-Air theme. To succeed on radio, a theme needed a personality that didn't depend on the show. "These explanatory theme songs, Gilligan's Island, the Beverly Hillbilly's, Green Acres were not chart hits."
**TV in Two-Thirds of US Homes by 1955** : "The Ballad of Davy Crockett" was released near the dawn of television, when only two-thirds of American homes had a TV set. "TVs were in just two-thirds of American homes by 55."
**Syncs - Hollywood Term for Song Placements** : Song placements in shows are called synchronizations, or "syncs" in Hollywood. They boost musicians' careers, but a song only becomes a true hit when it reaches beyond TV viewers to radio listeners, record buyers and digital listeners. "Song synchronizations like these or sinks in the parlance of Hollywood certainly help these musicians careers."
**The 70s - Decade of Polished TV Themes** : In the 70s, TV themes finally became polished enough to stand on their own as radio hits. Producers realized that themes with lyrics could prolong a show's life if they weren't too plot-driven. "What really took off in the 70s was the TV theme song, which finally got really polished."
**Rise of the Music Supervisor** : Songs unrelated to TV that were later revived by hit shows began appearing at the start of the 80s. That was when Hollywood music supervisors gained the clout to look further afield for songs that could drive a plot. This category eventually led to Kate Bush's 2022 comeback. "These only started to emerge at the dawn of the 80s when Hollywood music supervisors gained the clout to search farther afield for songs that could motivate a plot."
**General Hospital - The Song-Reviving Jukebox** : General Hospital was TV's top-rated soap from 1979 to 1988 and was once even more popular than The Young and the Restless. Its long-running romance between Luke Spencer (Anthony Geary) and Laura Spencer (Genie Francis) produced two Hot 100 No. 1s: Herb Alpert's "Rise" (1979) and Patti Austin and James Ingram's "Baby, Come to Me" (1983). Both were chosen by music supervisor Jill Phelps. "TV's top rated soap from 1979 to 1988 was a song reviving jukebox."
**Miami Vice - "MTV Cops" Origin Story** : NBC's 1984 show Miami Vice supercharged the General Hospital model in prime time. Its apocryphal origin story has NBC president Brandon Tartikoff scribbling only the phrase "MTV cops" on a notepad. Like MTV, its music sequences were highly stylized and impressionistic. "NBC president Brandon Tartikoff scribbling in a notepad just the phrase MTV cops."
**Miami Vice Soundtrack - 11 Weeks at No. 1** : The Miami Vice soundtrack hit No. 1 on the album chart in fall 1985. That same month Glenn Frey reached No. 2 and Jan Hammer's theme reached No. 1 on the Hot 100. The soundtrack spent 11 weeks at No. 1, still the longest run for any TV soundtrack. It established how important the music supervisor was to a TV series, though Miami Vice proved hard to replicate. "The Miami Vice soundtrack spent a whopping 11 weeks at number one on the album chart."
**Family Ties and the Template for the Stranger Things Approach** : A resurrected hit one year after Miami Vice's peak, unlikelier than the General Hospital hits and neither sleek nor cutting-edge, is described as the real template for today's Stranger Things approach to TV placement. It came via the Michael J. Fox sitcom Family Ties. "the Michael J. Fox Family sitcom plays Kingmaker on the charts."
