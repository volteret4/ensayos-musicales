# artist - Lisa Lisa and Cult Jam

## members
Lisa Lisa
Paul Anthony
Lucien Boleget-Lue George

## albums
**Lisa Lisa and Cult Jam (Debut)** : The group's self-titled debut album was released in late 1986 and featured the heartbroken piano ballad "All Cried Out" as its third single. "The third single from their self-titled debut album was a heartbroken piano ballad called All Cryed Out."
**Spanish Fly (1987)** : This sophomore album was pushed into the album charts' top 10 by the back-to-back number one singles "Head to Toe" and "Lost in Emotion". It quickly achieved platinum status, a feat no other freestyle act had accomplished at that time. "These two back-to-back number one singles pushed Lisa Lisa and Colt Jam's sophomore album Spanish Fly into the album charts top 10, and it went platinum practically out of the box."
**Straight To The Sky (Third Album)** : The group's third album featured the mid-tempo R&B track "Little Jackie Wants To Be a Star" as its lead single, which only reached number 29 on the charts, indicating a shift away from their freestyle sound. "Their third album, Straight To The Sky, led off with a mid tempo R&B track called Little Jackie Wants To Be a Star, which only reached number 29."

## songs
**All Cried Out (Late 1986)** : The third single from their debut album, this heartbroken piano ballad was transformed into a female-male duet between Lisa Lisa and Full Force members Paul Anthony and Lucien Boleget-Lue George, breaking the group into the top 10 with a peak at number 8. It was a traditional pop ballad with booming syn drums, showing their sonic versatility and willingness to move away from freestyle for chart success. "All Cryed Out finally broke Lisa Lisa into the top 10, peaking at number 8."
**Head to Toe (1987)** : Broadly classified as a freestyle song, this track is more accurately a hybrid that applied elements of freestyle production and 80s syncopation to a sound that resembled 60s Motown, specifically Diana Ross and the Supremes. It reached number one on the Hot 100 for the week ending June 20, 1987, making history as freestyle's first ever number one pop hit. "When it reached number one on the Hot 100 for the week ending June 20, 1987, it earned a place in history as freestyle's first ever number one pop hit."
**Lost in Emotion (1987)** : Following "Head to Toe", this smash hit leaned even more heavily into the Motown sound and less into freestyle, characterized by synthesizer-run zylafeng and kongas. It topped the Hot 100 in October 1987, contributing to the platinum success of their album "Spanish Fly". "Lost in Emotion was another smash, topping the hot 100 in October of 87."
**Can You Feel The Beat (Unspecified Year)** : This song's entire chorus was interpolated as a breakdown in Nina Sky's 2004 number 12 hit "Move Ya Body". It was also interpolated in the Black Eyed Peas' 2020 collaboration with Maluma, "Feel The Beat," despite that track having no other sonic connection to freestyle. "Nina Skye quote the entire chorus of Lisa Lisa and Colt Jam's Can You Feel The Beat as a breakdown in the middle of the song."
**Passenger (2022)** : Lisa Lisa's 2022 single openly imitates the sound of vintage Latin freestyle, demonstrating her continued engagement with the genre. "Her 2022 single Passinger openly imitates the sound of vintage Latin freestyle."

## curiosities
**Pop Potential Maximization** : The Full Force production team strategically maximized Lisa Lisa's pop potential by subtly referencing freestyle elements without creating full-on freestyle jams, a trend that aligned with the pervasive influence of freestyle in 1987. "Full force had figured out how to maximize Lisa Lisa's pop potential, allude to freestyle without creating full on freestyle jams."
**Evolving Away From Freestyle** : Despite Full Force's efforts to cross Lisa Lisa over to pop audiences, the group moved almost entirely away from freestyle on their third album, "Straight To The Sky." Later, on her subsequent album, Lisa Lisa sang over hip house beats provided by producers from C+C Music Factory, further illustrating her shift in sound. "Even Lisa Lisa and Colt Jam, which full force was always trying to cross over with pop audiences, and almost entirely away from freestyle."
**Current Activity** : Lisa Lisa remains active as a performer, participating in successful concert tours since the mid-2010s, often packaged with other vintage freestyle acts for audiences who remember the 80s. "Lisa clearly isn't giving up on the genre."

# genre - Freestyle

## curiosities
**Hybrid Genre Definition** : Freestyle is defined as a hybrid genre that blends rap-style break beats, hectic synthesizers, and florid lyrics focusing on romantic longing, originating from post-disco dance music. "A hybrid genre that blended rap style break beats, hectic synthesizers, and florid lyrics about romantic longing."
**Main Ingredient in Billboard Crossover Chart** : By 1987, freestyle was the primary component of Billboard magazine's new "crossover chart," which was created to track the significant presence of dance, R&B, and pop on radio. "Freestyle was the main ingredient in this new chart."
**First Number One Pop Hit on Hot 100** : "Head to Toe" by Lisa Lisa and Cult Jam became the first freestyle song to reach number one on the Hot 100 for the week ending June 20, 1987, marking a historical milestone for the genre. "It earned a place in history as freestyle's first ever number one pop hit."
**Ubiquitous Influence in 1987** : By 1987, freestyle's influence was so pervasive that it seemed to permeate all aspects of pop music, even impacting superstar singles like Madonna's "Who's That Girl." "Freestyle was now so ubiquitous, it seemed to pervade everything."
**Miami Freestyle - "Uncut Stuff"** : In Miami, Latin freestyle completely dominated the airwaves, known for its "uncut stuff" – cheaply, synthetically, but artfully recorded music designed to boom from cars or pump on dance floors. "In Miami, where Latin freestyle had utterly taken over the airwaves, they liked the uncut stuff."
**Gateway to Mainstream for New Acts** : Freestyle served as a crucial means for newly emerging pop acts with national ambitions, such as Taylor Dayne and Debbie Gibson, to break into mainstream fame. "Newly emerging pop acts, with national ambitions, who leveraged freestyle as their means of breaking into mainstream fame."
**Tempo Not Strictly Necessary for Affiliation** : The existence of "freestyle ballads" like Exposé's "Seasons Change" demonstrated that tempo was not a strict requirement for a song to be affiliated with the freestyle genre. "Tempo was not necessary strictly speaking for a hit to be affiliated with freestyle."
**Radio Preference for Ballads (1988-89)** : An ominous trend emerged between 1988 and 1989, as radio programmers increasingly favored freestyle ballads over its dance jams, leading to slow songs consistently performing better on the charts for artists like Safire and Will To Power. "Radio programmers seemed to prefer freestyle ballads over its dance jams."
**Competition from New Jack Swing** : Towards the late 1980s, freestyle faced significant competition from New Jack Swing, a more rap-influenced R&B style that emerged in 1987 and 1988, for the pop-friendly blend of dance and hip hop market. "In the category of pop friendly blends of dance and hip hop, freestyle no longer had the field to itself."
**Winding Down as a Pop Force (End of 1990)** : By the end of 1990, coinciding with Stevie B's number one hit, freestyle began to decline as a mainstream pop force, a shift implicitly signaled by Billboard's termination of its crossover 30 chart. "Freestyle was winding down as a pop force."
**Last Chart Topper Associated (March 1991)** : Timmy T's ballad "One More Try," which reached number one in March 1991, marked the last song in any way associated with freestyle to top the charts, even though it wasn't a true freestyle track itself. "The last chart topper in any way associated with freestyle, even though it wasn't really freestyle at all."
**Last Pure Freestyle Top 10 Hit (1991)** : Corina's "Temptation," reaching number 6 in 1991, is considered possibly the last pure freestyle hit to crack the Top 10. "Possibly the last pure freestyle hit to crack the top 10."
**Off the Charts by 1993** : By the time Robin S's deep house classic "Show Me Love" reached the top 5 in 1993, freestyle had largely disappeared from the pop charts. "Freestyle was basically off the charts."
**Echoes in 1990s Club Tracks** : Throughout the remainder of the 1990s, remnants of freestyle's sound could still be heard in certain club tracks, such as Lena Santiago's "Feel So Good," Angelina's "Release Me," and Lil Susie's "Can't Get You Out Of My Mind." "For the rest of the 90s, you could still hear freestyle's echoes in certain club tracks."
**Specific Mid-to-Late 80s Phenomenon** : Within the landscape of pop mini-genres, freestyle is distinctly associated with a specific period, its characteristic trebly beats and blippy synthesizers firmly dating it as a mid-to-late 1980s phenomenon. "Its trebly beats and blippy synthesizers date it firmly as a mid to late 80s phenomenon."
**Minimal Legacy in 21st Century** : In the 21st century, with bass-heavy genres like trap music and reggaeton dominating, evidence of freestyle's legacy is scarce, often appearing only as brief allusions or interpolations within unrelated tracks. "You have to dig pretty deep to find evidence of freestyle's legacy."
**Liminal Genre Bridging Eras** : Freestyle functioned as a transitional genre, bridging the disco era (when disco was seen as unfashionable) and the emerging hip hop and house eras, thereby providing a valuable contribution to the history of dance music. "It was a liminal genre that bridged the disco era at a moment when disco was considered uncool. And the hip hop and house eras, in so doing, freestyle provided a valuable service to the history of dance music."
**Source of Happy Memories for Vintage Listeners** : For listeners of a certain age, freestyle, with its impassioned lyrics and irresistible synthesized beats, continues to be a cherished source of nostalgic memories. "Freestyle with its impassioned lyrics and irresistible synthesized beats will always be a source of happy memories."
**Vintage Acts Remain Active** : Since the mid-2010s, nearly all original freestyle acts, including Exposé, Lisa Lisa, and Stevie B, remain active and are featured in highly successful packaged concert tours. "The good news for the vintage freestyle acts. They're virtually all still active."
**Concert Audience Demographics** : The audiences attending these contemporary freestyle concert tours are largely composed of 40 and 50-somethings who fondly recall their teenage years in the 1980s, actively singing and dancing along. "Yes, the audiences are largely composed of 40 and 50 somethings who remember their teenage years from the 80s."
