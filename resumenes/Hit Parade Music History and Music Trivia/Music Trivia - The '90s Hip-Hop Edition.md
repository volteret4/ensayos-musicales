## albums
**Saturday Night Fever (1977) - Soundtrack of Disco Hits** : The soundtrack produced many of the Bee Gees' disco No. 1s in 1977 and 1978, which were the later entries in their run of nine Hot 100 chart-toppers. The film was rated R when it came out. Its opening scene was shot in Bensonhurst, Brooklyn, a neighborhood described as "Travolta Central in the 70s." "the many disco hits on Saturday Night Fever in 1977 and 78."
**Spirits Having Flown (1979) - Final No. 1s as Artists** : This album gave the Bee Gees their last No. 1s as recording artists, with three straight chart-toppers: "Too Much Heaven," "Tragedy," and "Love You Inside Out." The Gibbs kept writing No. 1 hits for other artists in the 1980s, but they never again topped the Hot 100 under their own name. "their final number ones as artists came in 1979 from the spirits having flown album."

## songs
**Jive Talkin' (1975) - Written Crossing a Miami Bridge** : The song was a No. 1 hit in the summer of 1975. Barry Gibb wrote it while crossing the Julia Tuttle Causeway in Miami, Florida, described in the transcript as connecting Miami to Biscayne Bay. The rhythm of the causeway's slats found its way into the song's opening rhythm. In the Bee Gees' career, the hit was a bridge to their new phase as disco kings. "this song was written while Barry Gibb was crossing a bridge in Miami, Florida."
**Jive Talkin' (1975) - The Speeding Lore** : According to bridge lore, the rhythm at the start of the song shows the brothers were speeding over the causeway. The story goes that the slats only make that "trick-a-trick" sound above the 55 mph speed limit. "that is the lore of the bridge story on Give Talking."
**Spicks and Specks - Early Australian Era** : This was an early hit from the Bee Gees' Australian period. It came up as an example of whether the early Bee Gees can be told apart from the "Jive Talkin'" era and the later Bee Gees. "Spicks and specs, Bee Gees from Jive Talking Bee Gees, from later Bee Gees."
**I Was a Lover, a Leader of Men (c. 1963-64) - Signature Tone Already Present** : On this early song, the Gibbs' distinctive vocal tone is already audible, even outside the falsetto. Their sound is "hard to hear" on the material from when they were trying to sound like the Everly Brothers around 1960, but it emerged quickly afterward. "You can already hear it's not just the falsetto."
**I Started a Joke - Robin Gibb Lead** : Robin Gibb sang lead on this track. It was part of a 1960s pattern in which Robin's leads climbed a little higher in pitch with each hit. "It really had been ramping up all through the 60s on the songs that Robyn sang, lead on, like I started a joke."
**How Can You Mend a Broken Heart (1971) - First Hot 100 No. 1** : This was the first of the Bee Gees' nine Hot 100 No. 1s. It was also cited as one of the Robin-era tracks on which their singing crept steadily higher before the full leap into falsetto. "all nine of the bg's number ones on the hot 100 from How Can You Man To Broken Heart in 1971."
**Massachusetts (Late 1960s) - Pre-Disco Hit** : This was a big Bee Gees hit from the late 1960s, though it is not one of their famous disco-era songs. "Massachusetts is the title of a pretty big bg sit from the late 60s."
**Too Much Heaven (1979) - First of Three Straight No. 1s** : This was the first of three consecutive No. 1 hits from *Spirits Having Flown*. "after too much heaven and tragedy."
**Tragedy (1979) - Second of Three Straight No. 1s** : This was the second consecutive No. 1 from *Spirits Having Flown*, following "Too Much Heaven" and preceding "Love You Inside Out." "That was the third of three straight number ones from the spirits having flown album after too much heaven and tragedy."
**Love You Inside Out (1979) - Last No. 1 as Artists** : This was the Bee Gees' final Hot 100 No. 1 as performers and the third straight chart-topper from *Spirits Having Flown*. Donna Summer's "Hot Stuff" was named as the song it traded the No. 1 spot with in 1979. "Love You Inside Out was their last as artists."

## curiosities
**Nine Hot 100 No. 1s** : The Bee Gees scored nine No. 1 hits on the Hot 100. The run began with "How Can You Mend a Broken Heart" in 1971, included the disco hits from *Saturday Night Fever* in 1977-78, and ended with "Love You Inside Out" in 1979. "I talked about all nine of the bg's number ones on the hot 100."
**Songwriters of 1980s No. 1s for Others** : After their run as artists ended, the Gibbs wrote 1980s No. 1 hits for Barbra Streisand, Kenny Rogers, and Dolly Parton. "the Gibbs would write 80s number ones for Barbara Streisand and Kenny Rogers and Dolly Parton."
**The Leap Into Full Falsetto in 1975** : Their vocals had been creeping higher with each hit through the 1960s, especially on Robin's leads. In 1975 they fully committed to falsetto, which marked their disco transformation. "then they make this leap over the cliff into full-blown falsetto in 75."
**A Signature Vocal Tone Beyond the Falsetto** : Even in a normal middle-range male voice, the Gibbs' vocals have a distinctive quality. That gives them a recognizable sound before they reach the top of their range. Their earliest recordings, around 1960, were attempts to sound like the Everly Brothers. "The Gibbs voices have a certain brain quality."
**Australian and Isle of Man Roots** : The brothers were described as lads from Australia "with a little bit of the Isle of Man." Their high falsetto cheer was credited with acting as a balm. "these lads from Australia with a little bit of the Isle of Man that inherently acts as a bomb."
**Chart Dominance in a Dark Era** : The Bee Gees ruled the charts during a grim stretch of 1970s American history. The period was marked by inflation, the post-Watergate malaise, New York's fiscal crisis ("Ford to New York: Drop Dead"), and crime in Brooklyn. "The Bee Gees were dominating the charts at a really dark time in American history."
**Saturday Night Fever Rated R** : *Saturday Night Fever* carried an R rating on release, so young children who heard the soundtrack constantly could not see the film. "A little bit of trivia when the movie came out, it was rated R."

**Ice Ice Baby (1990) - First All-Rap Hot 100 No. 1** : In the fall of 1990, "Ice Ice Baby" became the first full-on rap song by a rapper to top the Hot 100. "before the fall of 1990 when ice ice baby went all the way, no full-on rap song had gone to number one on the hot 100."

# genre - Hip Hop

## curiosities
**Rap's Slow Climb to No. 1** : Rap songs began appearing on the Hot 100 in the 1980s. No all-rap song by a rapper topped the chart until Vanilla Ice's "Ice Ice Baby" in the fall of 1990. "it took until the 1990s for an all-wrap song by a rapper to top the hot 100."
**SoundScan and the 1990s Rap Boom** : The early-'90s switch to a more accurate system for tracking sales and airplay gave rap a huge boost on the charts. It helped make the 1990s a great decade for hip hop by capturing what people were actually buying and listening to. "the 90s were a decade that was great for rap, great for hip hop on the charts. And more accurate data had a lot to do with that."

# curiosity

**Billboard Converts to SoundScan (1991)** : In 1991, one year after "Ice Ice Baby," Billboard converted its flagship charts to the new, more accurate SoundScan system. The week the Hot 100 converted, the chart was immediately topped by a rap-pop crossover hit, P.M. Dawn's "Set Adrift on Memory Bliss," on the chart dated November 30, 1991. "Billboard converted its flagship charts to the new more accurate sound scan system, and it gave a huge boost to rap on the charts."
**Rap Hits That Stalled at No. 2** : Before the fall of 1990, Tone Loc's "Wild Thing," MC Hammer's "U Can't Touch This," and Digital Underground's "The Humpty Dance" all peaked without reaching No. 1. Tone Loc's No. 2 in early 1989 was the high-water mark. "none of them got higher than number two."
**1983 Samples Fuel Multiple Hip-Hop No. 1s** : More than one hip-hop song reached No. 1 on Billboard by sampling a 1983 hit. Examples are P.M. Dawn with Spandau Ballet's "True," Puff Daddy with The Police's "Every Breath You Take," and Puff Daddy and Mase with Matthew Wilder's "Break My Stride." "More than one hip hop song snagged the number one billboard spot by sampling a song from 1983."
