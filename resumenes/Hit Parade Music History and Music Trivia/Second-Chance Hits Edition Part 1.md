"With rock songs in particular, the recording industry tends to try pushing them on rock fans first before attempting to cross them over with pop fans."

# curiosity

**Second Chance Hit - Definition** : A "second chance hit" refers to a song that gains significant chart success after an initial failure or underperformance. These records often take on a life of their own, sometimes becoming more associated with the year of their return than their original release, and can be boosted by various catalysts such as increased artist fame, media exposure, DJ advocacy, or public demand. "You may be shocked to learn how many now legendary hit songs needed two or more tries to reach their chart destiny."
**Bring Back Hits - Terminology** : "Bring back hits" is a term used by radio programmers to describe returning records that achieve renewed chart success after an initial run, often benefiting from various catalysts like increased artist fame, media exposure, or public re-discovery. These songs can sometimes be more associated with their return year than their original release. "Whatever the catalyst, these returning records, which radio programmers call Bring Back Hits, take on a life of their own."
**Public Collective Decision (1973)** : Sometimes, there is no single explicable reason for an old record's resurgence on the charts; the public simply and collectively decides they want to hear it again. This phenomenon was observed with "Monster Mash" in 1973 and compared to the unexplained success of the TV show "Suits" in 2023. "Honestly, sometimes the public just collectively decides they want to hear an old record again."
**Legacy Hits - Definition** : "Legacy hits" are songs that may not have achieved significant chart success upon their original release but have become well-known and culturally recognized over time. They can also be "second chance hits" if their labels made multiple attempts to get them onto the charts. "As we discussed in our legacy hits episode of Hit Parade, about songs that never had success on the charts, but are now well known anyway."
**Post-Stardom Level-Up Category** : This category of "second chance hits" describes instances where an early single by an artist flops, but then the artist achieves greater fame with a subsequent hit. Their higher profile enables the re-release of the earlier, often identical, recording to successfully scale the charts. "Many second chance hits are ones that benefit from a budding hitmaker's higher profile."
**Media Exposure Category** : This category of "second chance hits" involves songs that gain a renewed shot at success through exposure in movies, TV shows, advertisements, or trailers. Such placements can transform previously underperforming or obscure tracks into major hits. "Category 2, media exposure, movies, TV shows, even adverts and trailers all can take a previously underperforming hit and give it another shot."
**General Hospital's Impact (1982)** : The soap opera "General Hospital" played a crucial role in reviving the song "Baby, Come to Me" by Patti Austin and James Ingram in 1982. After its initial flop, the show's inclusion of the song brought it back to the Hot 100, where it climbed all the way to number one. "When GH brought the song back later that year, Austin and Ingram climbed all the way to number one, a quintessential second chance hit."
**1986 - Year for Revived Hits** : The year 1986 was particularly notable for revived hits, largely due to movies bringing back early 1960s singles. Films like "Ferris Bueller's Day Off" and "Stand By Me" propelled classic songs like The Beatles' "Twist and Shout" and Ben E. King's "Stand By Me" back onto the charts, albeit sometimes in lower positions. "In general, 1986 was a good year for revived hits as movies brought back to early 60s singles, albeit in lower chart positions."
**Format Evolution Category** : This category of "second chance hits" describes how changes in radio programming strategy can lead to a song's renewed success. Particularly with rock songs, the industry might first push them to rock-oriented formats before attempting to cross them over to pop audiences, leading to delayed but ultimately successful chart runs. "Category 3, format evolution. With rock songs in particular, the recording industry tends to try pushing them on rock fans first before attempting to cross them over with pop fans."
**Promotional Reboot Category** : This category of "second chance hits" involves record labels or artists actively reissuing or re-promoting singles, often to support albums like compilations, greatest hits, or live recordings. This can include remixes or re-recordings of old material. "Category 4, the promotional reboot."
**Marketplace Activism (DJs and Fans)** : Beyond label decisions, the marketplace itself—driven by proactive DJs or the collective "wisdom of crowds" (fans)—can reboot songs. DJs are known for picking hits, sometimes from B-sides, while the streaming and social media era has seen viral memes trigger major chart revivals. "What about when the second chance activist is the marketplace, either a DJ or the fans?"
**Streaming and Social Era Influence** : The streaming and social media era of the last decade has proven to be a powerful catalyst for rebooting hits, with numerous songs experiencing viral revivals via platforms like TikTok. These organic resurgences demonstrate the immense power of collective public interest in bringing older music back to prominence. "But as powerful as DJs are, nothing is more powerful than the wisdom of crowds, as we have seen in the streaming and social era of the last decade."
**TikTok's Revival Power (2020s)** : TikTok has become a significant force in reviving older songs, turning them into "second chance hits" through viral memes and dance challenges. Examples include Fleetwood Mac's "Dreams," Lady Gaga's "Bloody Mary," and Tyler, the Creator's "See You Again," all experiencing chart re-entries years after their initial release due to TikTok trends. "Numerous hits have been rebooted by viral memes in the internet age, including famously Fleetwood Max Dreams, a 1977 number 1 hit revived in 2020 by a TikTok video of Nathan Apodaka skateboards surfing the back of a truck while drinking a bottle of ocean spray."
**Wednesday Netflix Series Impact (2022)** : The Netflix series "Wednesday" significantly contributed to the revival of Lady Gaga's "Bloody Mary" in 2022. TikTok users mashed up the song with a dance sequence from the show, causing the decade-old track to re-enter the Hot 100. "Lady Gaga's song, Bloody Mary, originally released in 2011, was revived on TikTok a decade later by TikTok users mashing up the song with a dance sequence from the Netflix series Wednesday."
**Seasonal Perennials Category** : This category encompasses holiday songs that repeatedly return to the charts, particularly in December. In the streaming era, the true scale of Christmas music's popularity has become evident, with many festive songs achieving unprecedented chart runs, even for tracks that initially only appeared on specialized holiday surveys. "Category 6, Seasonal Perennials."
**Streaming Era and Christmas Music (2018 onwards)** : The streaming era has revealed the immense scale of Christmas music's popularity, transforming seasonal songs into "multi-chance hits" that return annually with chart runs comparable to major contemporary artists. The 2018 Hot 100 debut of Andy Williams' "It's the Most Wonderful Time of the Year" in the top 10 was a key moment signaling this trend. "only in the streaming era have we fully grasped just how big Christmas music is."
**Nielsen Ratings (2023)** : The TV show "Suits," a pulpy legal drama, set a record in the Nielsen ratings in 2023, becoming the number one most watched show on streaming TV for a dozen consecutive weeks. This unprecedented success for an older show highlights how media re-exposure can create new phenomena, similar to "second chance hits" in music. "Suites set a record in the Nielsen ratings. Suites was the number one most watched show on streaming TV in 2023 for a dozen consecutive weeks."
