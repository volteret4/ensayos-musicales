**Duotones (1986)** : Kenny G recorded this album for Arista in 1986, featuring more instrumentals than previous efforts. Its success was significantly boosted by the instrumental "Songbird." Duotones immediately hit the top 10 and went double platinum."
**Kenny G Live (1990)** : This album included the track "Going Home," which became a standard in the People's Republic of China, routinely played over public address systems at the end of the Chinese work day. "Going Home, a track from his 1990 album Kenny G Live, that became a standard in the People's Republic of China."
**Breathless (1992)** : Kenny G's sixth album, released in 1992, became a "chart colossus." It sold 6 million copies in its first year and was later certified for sales of 12 million. In 1993, it spent 11 weeks at number 2 on the Billboard 200 album chart, behind only Whitney Houston's "The Bodyguard" soundtrack. The album spawned the Grammy-winning hit "Forever in Love." "In 1992, Kenny's sixth album Breathless, which spawned the Grammy winning number 18 hit Forever in Love, became a chart Colossus."
**Miracles (1994) - The Holiday Album** : This holiday album achieved significant commercial success, scoring a number 1 CD position and going octuple platinum. "In 1994, Kenny finally scored a number 1 CD with Miracles, the holiday album, which went octopal platinum."
**Faith (1999) - A Holiday Collection** : In an attempt to maintain commercial relevance at the turn of the millennium, Kenny G produced this second collection of holiday standards. It contained a cover of "Auld Lang Syne," which was later released in a special "Millennium Mix." "In a bid for continued commercial relevance, in 1999, he produced a second collection of holiday standards called Faith."

## songs
**Love on the Rise (1985)** : A vocal R&B track from the "Gravity" album, featuring singer Kashif. It reached number 24 on Billboard's Hot Black Singles chart. "Kenny and Kashif's love on the rise from the 1985 Kenny G album Gravity reached number 24 on Billboard's Hot Black singles chart."

**Songbird (1987)** : This instrumental track was unexpectedly performed by Kenny G on Johnny Carson's "The Tonight Show," despite instructions to play a vocal single. Its acclaimed performance led Arista to promote it to both R&B and Top 40 pop stations. It scaled the Hot 100 in spring/summer 1987, ultimately topping out at number 4, and was the last top 10 pop instrumental of the 1980s. "The Tonight Show performance of songbird was highly acclaimed, and Clive Davis and the team at Arista Relented. They agreed to promote songbird, not just to R&B stations, but to top 40 pop stations."
**Silhouette (1988)** : This instrumental track continued Kenny G's chart success after "Songbird," reaching number 13 on the Hot 100 in 1988. "In 1988, his silhouette reached number 13 on the Hot 100."
**Going Home (1990)** : A track from his "Kenny G Live" album, which became a unique cultural phenomenon in the People's Republic of China, routinely played over public address systems at the end of the Chinese work day. "Going Home, a track from his 1990 album Kenny G Live, that became a standard in the People's Republic of China."
**Forever in Love (1992)** : A Grammy-winning hit from the "Breathless" album, which peaked at number 18. "Breathless, which spawned the Grammy winning number 18 hit Forever in Love."
**Auld Lang Syne (Millennium Mix) (1999)** : A kitschy version of the New Year's standard, featuring an audio collage of 20th-century sound bites and keyboards from Yani. It was produced by "Team Kenny" in anticipation of the year 2000. It debuted on the Hot 100 just before Christmas 1999 and made a massive 47-point leap to number 7 in the week of New Year's 2000, becoming Kenny G's last major pop hit. "Team Kenny produced a second version of Oddlang Zine, dubbed the Millennium Mix, featuring an audio collage of sound bites from 20th century history... made a massive 47-point leap up to number 7."

## curiosities
**Early Career Vocal Push** : Initially, Kenny G was signed by record mogul Clive Davis to Arista in the early 1980s, where label executives insisted he would sell better if his singles featured vocalists, despite his personal preference to showcase his vocal-free soprano sax playing. "after he was signed by record mogul Clive Davis to his aristal label in the early 80s, the label brass insisted Kenny would sell better if his singles had vocals."
**The Tonight Show Performance (1987)** : Kenny G recounted a story in the 2021 HBO documentary "Listening to Kenny G" where he defied instructions on Johnny Carson's "The Tonight Show" by playing his instrumental "Songbird" instead of the assigned vocal single, to the initial anger of producers. "In the 2021 HBO documentary Listening to Kenny G, Kenny recounts a story of appearing on Johnny Carson's The Tonight Show for the first time, being instructed to play his vocal single, but playing an instrumental song instead, much to the anger of the tonight show producers."
**Defying One-Hit Wonder Status** : Kenny G successfully defied the typical one-hit wonder status often associated with instrumental hitmakers by consistently producing successful instrumental tracks and albums after his breakthrough with "Songbird." "He defied the typical one hit wonder status of most instrumental hit makers."
**Business Model for Instrumentalists** : Kenny G established a successful business model that other instrumentalists, like New Age keyboardist Yani and composer John Tesh, imitated. This model combined accessible "pseudo-pop" with a "virtuosic gloss" and a "high energy live show." "He also built a business model other instrumentalists imitated, accessible pseudo-pop with a virtuosic gloss and a high energy live show."
**Fan Perception and Playing Style** : Kenny G attracted fans who perceived his playing, characterized by circular breath-held notes and aggressively melodic delivery, as a feat of "athletic prowess" akin to Whitney Houston's singing, aiming to "wow" rather than just soothe them. "Kenny G attracted fans who saw his circular breath held notes and aggressively melodic playing as the work of a diligent competitor."
**Presidential Fan** : Kenny G became an institution, loved by the public, including sax-playing President Bill Clinton. "loved by the public, including sax playing President Bill Clinton, Kenny G had become an institution."
**Career Decline and Last Major Hit** : By the turn of the millennium, Kenny G's career as a hitmaker saw a decline, with his 1999 "Millennium Mix" of "Auld Lang Syne" marking his last major pop hit. "By the turn of the millennium, even Kenny G's career as a hitmaker had fallen off a bit."

# curiosity

**Instrumental Music Decline in 1980s** : The 1980s marked a period where instrumental music began diminishing as a pop force, largely due to major market FM programmers' preference for songs with vocals that listeners could sing along to. This preference depressed instrumental record sales and chart performance. "the instrumental is diminishing as a pop force... Major market FM programmers preferred songs listeners could sing along to, which depressed these instrumental's record sales and subsequent chart performance."
**Medley Craze of 1981-1982** : Sparked by Stars on 45's number one hit in 1981, an 18-month "medley craze" saw numerous acts compiling familiar tunes into danceable mashups, though it was considered a fleeting fad rather than an enduring pop form for instrumentals. "the Stars on 45's unexpected rise to number one in 1981 prompted a roughly 18-month medley craze that saw a range of random acts compiling familiar tunes into a danceable mashup."
**TV and Movie Themes as Instrumental Route (early 80s)** : For the first half of the 1980s, the most reliable path to instrumental chart success was through theme songs for television and movies, as demonstrated by composers like Mike Post, Vangelis, Harold Faltermeyer, David Foster, and Jan Hammer. "For the first half of the 80s, the surest route to instrumental chart success continued to be TV and the movies."
**Most Innovative 80s Instrumentals Not Pop Hits** : Some of the 1980s' most innovative instrumental music, including tracks by Herbie Hancock, The Art of Noise, and Yello, failed to achieve top 40 pop hit status, often peaking much lower on the Hot 100 despite critical acclaim and MTV exposure. "This was a shame, as some of the 80s most innovative music was instrumental... None of these instrumental New Wave classics were top 40 pop hits."
**Instrumental Drought on Hot 100 (1985-2013)** : Following Jan Hammer's "Miami Vice Theme" hitting number 1 in 1985, no pure instrumental (a song without any words) reached number 1 on the Hot 100 for 38 years, until Bauer's "Harlem Shake" in 2013, which itself contained a few instructional words. "In the 38 years since this song topped the Hot 100, no pure instrumental. I.E. a song without any words at all has gone to number 1."
**Instrumentals as Niche Focus (80s/90s)** : A likelier explanation for the decline of instrumentals in the 1980s and 1990s is that they, like much of popular music, became more niche-focused, finding a home in emerging radio formats like smooth jazz. "the instrumental, like much of popular music in the 80s and 90s, became more niche focused."
**90s Instrumental Flukiness** : Beyond Kenny G, instrumental pop hits in the 1990s became increasingly rare and "fluky," with exceptions like David A. Stewart and Candy Dulfer's "Lily Was Here" (#11), Robert Miles's "Children" (#21), and U2's rhythm section's "Mission: Impossible Theme" (#7), the latter being the decade's only Top 10 instrumental. "The instrumental became more fluky than ever."
**Classic Instrumental Sampling and Rebooting (90s)** : By the 1990s, classic instrumentals were frequently "sampled, remixed and rebooted," demonstrating their enduring influence through new interpretations, such as Quincy Jones's "Soul Bossa Nova" becoming the "Austin Powers" theme and sampled by The Dream Warriors, or Herb Alpert's "Rise" being re-imagined as The Notorious B.I.G.'s "Hypnotize." "By the 90s, classic instrumentals were being sampled, remixed and rebooted."
**Post-Millennium Instrumental Rarity** : Since the turn of the 21st century, instrumentals have become "exceedingly rare on the charts," with film placements like Tomoyasu Hotei's "Battle Without Honor or Humanity" in "Kill Bill" making an impact but rarely reaching the Hot 100. "Since the turn of the century, Instrumentals have become exceedingly rare on the charts."
**Instrumentals in Dance Club Culture** : While rare on mainstream charts, instrumentals remain "fairly omnipresent" in the dance club genre, with DJ producers like Daft Punk, Fatboy Slim, Darude, Tiësto, and Skrillex creating innovative, club-driven hits. "there is one genre where the Instrumental is still fairly omnipresent, the dance club."
**Billboard's YouTube Rule Change (2013)** : Bauer's "Harlem Shake" immensely benefited from Billboard's new rule, implemented in 2013, which began counting music plays on YouTube for the first time. This change allowed viral phenomena to directly impact chart performance, powering "Harlem Shake" to number 1 with over 100 million streams in a single week. "Bauer was benefiting from a new billboard rule, counting music plays on YouTube for the first time."
**Instrumental Comeback in TikTok Age?** : The possibility of a more prominent and permanent chart comeback for instrumentals in the "age of TikTok" is considered, suggesting that new viral platforms could potentially alter historical trends. "Will the instrumental ever make a more prominent and permanent chart come back in the age of TikTok, anything's possible?"
**Historical Serendipity of Instrumental Hits** : Across 70 years of chart history, instrumental hits have predominantly been "moments of serendipity, strokes of luck, and mad inspiration," rather than consistent commercial trends, even for highly successful artists like Kenny G. "across the last 70 years on the charts, instrumental hits have almost always been moments of serendipity, strokes of luck, and mad inspiration."
**Cultural Meta Resonance of Enduring Instrumentals** : Instrumentals that achieve lasting cultural impact often possess a "cultural meta resonance," where the melody is important, but so is its "package"—the associated imagery or context that helps it live on beyond just being an "earworm." "The Instrumentals that live on as more than just earworms have a certain cultural meta resonance."
