## albums
**Gravity (1985) - Vocal-Driven Arista Album** : Kenny G's 1985 album for Arista, made at a time when label brass insisted his singles needed vocals to sell. It featured "Love on the Rise" with singer-songwriter Kashif, which reached number 24 on Billboard's Hot Black Singles chart. Kenny's albums were still selling only modestly at this point, and he was eager to showcase his soprano sax without vocals. "Kenny's albums were still selling modestly, and he was eager to showcase his melodic brand of soprano sax playing Vocal Free."
**Duotones (1986) - Commercial Breakthrough** : Recorded for Arista in 1986, this album featured more instrumentals than its predecessors, but Arista still chose a vocal track as the first single: a cover of Junior Walker's "What Does It Take (To Win Your Love)" featuring Ellis Hall, which hit number 15 on the black singles chart. After the instrumental "Songbird" took off on pop radio in 1987, Duotones immediately hit the top 10 and went double platinum. It went on to sell 5 million copies. "duo tones immediately hit the top 10 and went double platinum. It went on to sell 5 million copies."
**Kenny G Live (1990) - Live Album Featuring Going Home** : Live album that included "Going Home," an instrumental that became a standard in the People's Republic of China. "a track from his 1990 album Kenny G Live, that became a standard in the People's Republic of China."
**Breathless (1992) - Sixth Album and Chart Colossus** : Kenny's sixth album spawned the Grammy-winning number 18 hit "Forever in Love." It sold 6 million copies in its first year alone and was later certified for sales of 12 million. In 1993 it spent 11 weeks at number 2 on the Billboard 200, kept from the top in all of those weeks by Arista labelmate Whitney Houston's Bodyguard soundtrack. "Breathless sold 6 million copies in its first year alone and was later certified for sales of 12 million."
**Miracles: The Holiday Album (1994) - First Number 1 Album** : In 1994 Kenny finally scored a number 1 CD with this holiday collection, which went octuple platinum. "In 1994, Kenny finally scored a number 1 CD with Miracles, the holiday album, which went octopal platinum."
**Faith (1999) - Second Holiday Collection** : Kenny G's second collection of holiday standards, produced in 1999 when his hitmaking career had fallen off and he was seeking continued commercial relevance. Buried near the end of the CD was a cover of "Auld Lang Syne" featuring keyboards from fellow instrumentalist Yanni. "in 1999, he produced a second collection of holiday standards called Faith."

## songs
**Love on the Rise (1985) - with Kashif** : A vocal R&B track with Kashif from the album Gravity, recorded because Arista insisted that Kenny's singles carry vocals. It reached number 24 on Billboard's Hot Black Singles chart. "This worked well enough."
**What Does It Take (To Win Your Love) (1986) - with Ellis Hall** : A cover of the Junior Walker hit featuring singer Ellis Hall. Arista chose it as the first single from Duotones even though the album leaned more toward instrumentals. It hit number 15 on the black singles chart. "for the first single, Arista went with yet another vocal track"
**Songbird (1987) - Breakthrough Instrumental** : Kenny was told to play his vocal single on his first appearance on The Tonight Show Starring Johnny Carson. He played the instrumental "Songbird" instead, angering the show's producers. The performance was highly acclaimed, and Clive Davis and Arista relented, promoting the song to both R&B and top 40 pop stations. It climbed the Hot 100 through spring and summer 1987 and peaked at number 4. Casey Kasem introduced it on American Top 40 as the first top 10 instrumental since Jan Hammer's Miami Vice theme. It was the last top 10 pop instrumental of the 1980s. "Here's the first top 10 instrumental since Jan Humber hit number 1 back in 1985 with a Miami Vice theme, Kenny G with songbird."
**Silhouette (1988)** : Reached number 13 on the Hot 100 in 1988, showing that Kenny G avoided the one-hit-wonder status typical of instrumental hitmakers. "In 1988, his silhouette reached number 13 on the Hot 100."
**Going Home (1990)** : A track from Kenny G Live that became a standard in the People's Republic of China, where it is routinely played over public address systems at the end of the work day. "It is routinely played over public address systems at the end of the Chinese work day."
**Forever in Love (1992)** : A Grammy-winning single from Breathless that reached number 18. "the Grammy winning number 18 hit Forever in Love"
**Auld Lang Syne (Millennium Mix) (1999)** : The original version on Faith featured Yanni on keyboards. Team Kenny expected Americans to feel wistful about the end of the 20th century around New Year's 2000 (the century technically ended at the start of 2001). They produced a "Millennium Mix" featuring an audio collage of sound bites from 20th-century history. It debuted on the Hot 100 just before Christmas 1999, then made a 47-place leap to number 7 in the week of New Year's 2000. It fell off the chart before the end of January and was Kenny G's last major pop hit. "the week of New Year's 2000 made a massive 47-point leap up to number 7."

## curiosities
**Real Name and Origins** : Born Kenneth Gorelick, he was a soprano saxophone player from Seattle who branded himself Kenny G and became the signature artist of smooth jazz. "an earnest soprano saxophone player from Seattle named Kenneth Gorlick, who would become the signature artist of smooth jazz."
**Forced to Use Vocalists by Arista** : Kenny G was signed to Arista by Clive Davis in the early 1980s. He did not want to break his music with vocalists, but label brass insisted he would sell better with vocal singles, so he recorded R&B tracks with singers like Kashif. "the label brass insisted Kenny would sell better if his singles had vocals."
**Tonight Show Defiance (Recounted in Listening to Kenny G, 2021)** : In the 2021 HBO documentary Listening to Kenny G, Kenny recounts playing an instrumental on his first Tonight Show appearance instead of the vocal single he was told to perform, which angered the show's producers. "being instructed to play his vocal single, but playing an instrumental song instead, much to the anger of the tonight show producers."
**Defying One-Hit-Wonder Status** : Unlike most instrumental hitmakers, Kenny G kept scoring hits after "Songbird." He still recorded occasionally with vocalists such as Motown legend Smokey Robinson, but typically did better with instrumentals. "He defied the typical one hit wonder status of most instrumental hit makers."
**Playing as Athletic Feat** : Like Whitney Houston, who turned singing into a feat of athletic prowess, Kenny G attracted fans who saw his circular-breathed held notes and aggressively melodic playing as the work of a diligent competitor. "Kenny didn't just soothe his fans. He aimed to wow them."
**Fan in the White House and Business Model** : Kenny G's fans included sax-playing President Bill Clinton, and he became an institution. His formula of accessible pseudo-pop with virtuosic gloss and a high-energy live show was imitated by other instrumentalists, leading to best-selling albums by Yanni and John Tesh. "Kenny G had become an institution."
**Random Hits Despite Careerism** : Even with mega-platinum success, Kenny G's pop hits were random and fluky. "Even when an act as careerist as Kenny G turned Instrumentals into mega platinum, his hits were random and fluky."

# artist - Vangelis

## songs
**Chariots of Fire (1981) - Biggest Instrumental of the 1980s** : Vangelis composed and performed this theme for the film Chariots of Fire. It debuted on the Hot 100 in December 1981, at the start of movie awards season, and took until May 1982 to reach number 1. That was more than a month after Vangelis won the Oscar for Best Original Score and the film won Best Picture. It was one of only two instrumentals to hit number 1 in the entire 1980s and was much parodied. "Chariots of Fire was one of only two instrumental to hit number 1 in the entire 1980s."

# genre - Smooth Jazz

## curiosities
**Definition** : Smooth jazz rewarded artists who straddled jazz, R&B and pop, from George Benson's guitar solos to Sade. AllMusic defines it as an outgrowth of jazz fusion that emphasizes its polished side and relies on rhythms and grooves instead of improvisation. "an outgrowth of jazz fusion that emphasizes its polished side and relies on rhythms and grooves instead of improvisation."
**Never Really Cool** : Smooth jazz tried to project cool, but to both its lovers and haters it was never really cool music. "Smooth jazz tried to project cool, but to both its lovers and haters, it was never really cool music."
**Radio Format Launched in Chicago (1987)** : The format launched at a Chicago station in 1987. It blended R&B instrumentalists like Najee, the more ambient tracks of pop acts like Sade, and the most accessible jazz fusion players, including David Sanborn, Dave Grusin, David Benoit and Dave Koz. It rarely crossed over to the pop charts but thrived on instrumentals. "launched at a Chicago station in 1987, Smooth Jazz stations blended R&B instrumentalists like NaJay."

# curiosity

**Radio Turns Away From Instrumentals in the 1980s** : The instrumental shrank as a pop force in the 1980s mainly because major-market FM programmers preferred songs listeners could sing along to, which depressed instrumental sales and chart performance. "It wasn't as if artists were avoiding instrumental tracks. It was more that radio was turning away from them."
**Three Screen Instrumentals in the 1985 Top 20** : In 1985, three wordless hits from film and TV composers made the Hot 100 top 20: Harold Faltermeyer's "Axel F," David Foster's "Love Theme from St. Elmo's Fire," and Jan Hammer's "Miami Vice Theme." "in 1985, three wordless hits from score composers for visual media made the top 20 on the Hot 100."
**Instrumentals Go Niche** : Another explanation for the decline is that instrumentals, like much popular music of the 1980s and 1990s, became niche-focused, aimed at smooth jazz and new age markets. "the instrumental, like much of popular music in the 80s and 90s, became more niche focused."
**Sampling and Rebooting in the 1990s** : By the 1990s, classic instrumentals were being sampled, remixed and rebooted, for example Quincy Jones's "Soul Bossa Nova" and Herb Alpert's "Rise." "By the 90s, classic instrumentals were being sampled, remixed and rebooted."
**Instrumental Hits as Serendipity** : Across 70 years of the charts, instrumental hits have almost always been moments of luck and inspiration. The ones that endure have a cultural resonance in which the packaging matters as much as the melody. "instrumental hits have almost always been moments of serendipity, strokes of luck, and mad inspiration."
