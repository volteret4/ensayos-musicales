# curiosity

**Heterosexualization of Bisexual Artists** : Historically, bisexual artists, such as Janis Joplin, were often "heterosexualized" during their lifetimes. This meant their non-heterosexual relationships were kept secret, and their public image was often presented as straight, even if they were part of free-love counter-culture. "Also, complicating matters is that bisexual artists were to quote one Janice Joplin historian often heterosexualized."
**American Idol as a Launchpad and Context for Coming Out** : American Idol served as a launchpad for artists like Clay Aiken and Adam Lambert, whose experiences post-show highlighted evolving expectations for queer artists. Aiken came out years after his Idol moment, while Lambert made history by coming out publicly weeks after being runner-up, before his debut album. "Recall that 2003 Idol runner up Clay Aiken came out years after his Idol moment."
**Queer-Coding in Music and Videos** : "Queer-coding" refers to the subtle or overt ways in which songs and videos express LGBTQ themes or identities without explicit verbal declarations. Examples include Right Said Fred's "I'm Too Sexy" and Pet Shop Boys' "Rent," which conveyed queer sensibilities through lyrics or visuals. "By that time, Richard, their lead singer, was already out as bisexual and the song and video were unabashedly gay-coded and fabulous."
**Police Sting as Outing Event** : In 1998, George Michael was outed due to a "police sting in Beverly Hills, California," for solicitation in a public park men's room. This event, though "less than flattering," forced his sexuality into the public sphere, to which he later responded defiantly through his music. "until finally in 1998, a police sting in Beverly Hills, California snared Michael for solicitation in a public park men's room."
**The Zillennial Generation's Openness** : The "zillennial generation" is characterized as being "unabashed in sharing everything," including their "gender presentation, their sexual orientation, their pronouns." This openness is influencing both recording artists and pop audiences, making it not only possible but "mostly expected" for chart-topping musicians to come out. "The zillennial generation is unabashed in sharing everything, their gender presentation, their sexual orientation, their pronouns, and neither recording artists nor the pop audience seem limited by this."
**Dividing Line in LGBTQ Chart Acceptance** : Adam Lambert's number one album "Trespassing" in 2012 is seen as a "dividing line in LGBTQ chart acceptance." His success paved the way for numerous openly queer artists across various genres to subsequently top the Billboard 200, signaling a significant shift in the music industry. "As for Lambert's solo chart achievement in 2012, with hindsight, it can now be seen as a dividing line in LGBTQ chart acceptance."
**Progress in Fits and Starts** : The history of LGBTQ representation in pop music demonstrates that "progress has come in fits and starts over the decades." Despite significant advancements, challenges persist, with the LGBTQ+ community, particularly trans Americans, facing societal and legal assaults in 2025. "To be sure, progress has come in fits and starts over the decades."
