## albums
**Special Things (1980)** : This album showcased The Pointer Sisters' versatility, leading off with a post-disco R&B track reminiscent of Quincy Jones's style. "Their 1980 album, Special Things, leads off with a post-disco R&B track in high-quincey Jones style."
**Black & White (1981)** : This LP reached number 12 on the album chart, becoming The Pointer Sisters' highest-charting album at the time and earning a gold certification. It showcased the group's "Try Anything's spirit," featuring innovative elements like rapping breakdowns on "What a Surprise" and a retro homage to 60s girl groups on "Should I Do It." "Slow Hand came from the sisters LP Black and White, which rose to number 12 on the album chart, their highest position to date, and went gold."
**So Excited (1982)** : This album was released during The Pointer Sisters' bumpy transition to RCA Records and initially underperformed on the album chart, despite producing two medium-sized hits. "The sisters' next LP underperformed on the album chart."
**Breakout (1983)** : This LP would eventually live up to its title, elevating The Pointer Sisters from mid-tier pop status, but initially seemed unexceptional. By early 1984, it had stalled at number 75 on the album chart, appearing to fare no better than its predecessor. After the success of "Automatic," it began scaling the charts, cracking the top 30 in the spring of 1984 and achieving gold certification, marking a significant turnaround for the LP, eventually going double platinum on its way to triple platinum by June 1985. "At the start of 1984, Breakout had stalled on the album chart at number 75."
**Breakout (1983) - Reissue with "I'm So Excited"** : The "Breakout" LP was reissued in record stores with a remixed version of "I'm So Excited" tacked on, following the momentum of "Jump For My Love." This strategic move helped "I'm So Excited" finally become the signature smash hit it was intended to be. "the breakout LP was reissued in record stores with I'm so excited tacked on."
**Contact (1985)** : Released in the fall of 1985 as the follow-up to the highly successful "Breakout," this LP was certified both Gold and Platinum within a month, yet The Pointer Sisters struggled to extract hit singles from it. "the contact LP was certified both Gold and Platinum in just one month, the pointer sisters had a hard time pulling hits from the album."
**Hot Together (1986)** : This album featured "Goldmine" as its first single, which became The Pointer Sisters' last top 40 pop hit as a lead act. "Goldmine, the first single from their Hot Together album."

## songs
**Could I Be Dreaming (1980)** : Featured on the "Special Things" album, this post-disco R&B track was noted for channeling The Jacksons and managed to reach No. 22 on the R&B chart and just miss the pop top 40. "Could I Be Dreaming manage to reach No. 22 on the R&B chart and just miss the pop top 40?"
**He's So Shy (1980)** : This track from "Special Things" became The Pointer Sisters' first 80s blockbuster, characterized by a poppy keyboard hook and a sound aligned with the burgeoning Yacht Rock trend. Originally conceived as "She's So Shy" by co-writer Tom Snow for Leo Sayer, with lyrics by Cynthia Wilde, producer Richard Perry flipped the gender, recorded it with West Coast session musicians, and featured June Pointer on lead vocals. It soared to No. 3 on the Hot 100 and No. 10 on the R&B chart and was notably the group's first hit to receive a music video treatment, predating MTV's launch by a year. "He's so shy rose to No. 3 on the Hot 100 and No. 10 on the R&B chart."
**Slow Hand (1981)** : This song, written by John Bettis and Michael Clark, was initially not intended for The Pointer Sisters or even a female vocalist, but producer Richard Perry envisioned it as a sensual, seductive track akin to their earlier hit "Fire." Anita Pointer's lead vocal infused it with assertive sex positivity, which was subtly cutting edge. Debuting in spring 1981, it became The Pointer Sisters' biggest pop hit, climbing to number two on the Hot 100 for three weeks and reaching number seven on the R&B chart, embodying female empowerment. "Slow Hand rose to number two on the Hot 100 and stayed there three weeks, The Pointer's biggest ever pop hit."
**Slow Hand (1981) - Country Crossover** : This song, despite being a soulful slow jam, marked The Pointer Sisters' second successful foray into country music, roughly half a decade after "Fairytale," effectively functioning as a country hit disguised in another genre. "The Pointer's sisters had basically hit with another country song. This time disguised as a soulful Slow Jam."
**What a Surprise (1981)** : Featured on the "Black & White" album, this track demonstrated The Pointer Sisters' willingness to experiment by incorporating rapping breakdowns, a novel inclusion just a couple of years after rap began emerging as a recorded medium. "What a surprise, for example, featured breakdowns where the girls were rapping."
**Should I Do It (1981)** : This single from the "Black & White" album served as a "new kind of throwback" for The Pointer Sisters, offering a perky homage to the sound of 1960s girl groups and doo-wop, and successfully reached number 13 on the pop chart in early 1982. "Released as a single, it hit number 13 on the pop chart in early 1982."
**American Music (1982)** : This bouncy yacht-style pop ditty was a hit from The Pointer Sisters' "So Excited" album, reaching number 16 on the charts. "a bouncy yacht-style pop did he called American Music, which reached number 16."
**I'm So Excited (1982) - Original Version** : Co-written by the three Pointer Sisters and associate producer Trevor Lawrence, this original four-minute version from the "So Excited" album was a synth-driven club track with hedonistic lyrics, peaking at a modest number 30 on the Hot 100 during the Planet Records transition to RCA. Although a pop classic, it would later require a remix to achieve its full cultural recognition and chart success. "I'm So Excited was a four-minute party. It had the post-disco sound of synth-driven club music and fun-loving hedonistic lyrics."
**I Need You (1983)** : Serving as the first single from the "Breakout" album, this stately heartache ballad achieved only modest success, reaching number 13 on the R&B chart and number 48 on the Hot 100, suggesting the album might not outperform its predecessor. "I need you, a stately heartache ballad that served as the first single from Breakout was only a modest hit."
**Automatic (1984)** : This "geekier" track, which was already receiving early airplay from club DJs as an album cut, became the second single from "Breakout" and a major success. Featuring Ruth Pointer's low alto lead vocal, the pulsating electro-rock song with lyrics about robotic and cybernetic love, debuted in January 1984 and climbed to number five on the Hot 100. Its synthesizer-driven digital funk sound resonated with both pop audiences, fitting in with contemporary new wave rock acts like The Cars, Nana, and Eurythmics, and Black audiences, reaching number two on the R&B chart (their biggest R&B hit since 1975) and number two on the UK singles chart, becoming their biggest British hit. "Automatic was pulsating electro rock, and its lyrics were about robotic love."
**Jump For My Love (1984)** : Originally titled "Jump" on the album, this uptempo track was renamed "Jump For My Love" due to Van Halen's concurrent number one hit with a song also titled "Jump." The Pointer Sisters supported this single with a heavily rotated music video featuring them in slinky dresses, intercut with footage of track and field athletes and basketball stars Julius Erving and Magic Johnson, capitalizing on 1984 Olympics fever. It peaked at number three on both the Hot 100 and R&B chart in July 1984, coinciding with the Los Angeles Olympics, and became "Breakout's" biggest hit. "Jump For My Love, peaked at number three on both the Hot 100 and the R&B chart in July, just as the summer Olympics were opening in Los Angeles."
**I'm So Excited (1984) - Remix Version** : This remixed version of the 1982 single was added to the reissued "Breakout" LP and, propelled by the success of "Jump For My Love," finally became a signature smash, reaching number nine on the Hot 100. Despite a lower chart peak than "Automatic" and "Jump For My Love," it is now considered The Pointer Sisters' legacy hit, boasting over 425 million Spotify streams and tens of millions of music video views featuring Ruth and Anita in lingerie and June in a bubble bath. "The I'm so excited remix eventually reached number nine on the hot 100."
**Neutron Dance (1985)** : Originally written by Ali Willis for the "Streets of Fire" soundtrack and rejected, "Neutron Dance" found its home on the "Breakout" LP after Ruth Pointer sang it. It became "commercial gold" when pitched to the producers of "Beverly Hills Cop," released as a single weeks before the movie. It peaked at number six on the Hot 100 in early 1985, becoming the fourth consecutive top 10 hit from "Breakout," and its inclusion on the "Beverly Hills Cop" soundtrack extended its popularity throughout 1985. "Neutron Dance scaled the Hot 100 into early 1985, peaking at number 6, the 4th straight top 10 hit from Breakout."
**We Are the World (1985)** : The three Pointer Sisters were invited to join the chorus of USA for Africa's charity single "We Are the World," reflecting their pervasive pop presence at the time. "The three sisters were invited to join the chorus on USA for Africa's We Are the World."
**Dare Me (1985)** : The lead single from the "Contact" album, released in September 1985, "Dare Me" peaked at number 11, narrowly missing the pop top 10. However, its 12-inch remix achieved significant success, becoming The Pointer Sisters' only number one on Billboard's Club Play chart that fall. "Dare Me just missed the pop top 10, peaking at number 11 in September 1985."
**Freedom (1985)** : This follow-up single from the "Contact" album reached number 25 on the R&B chart but only number 59 on the pop chart, reflecting the group's difficulty in generating major hits from the album. "Freedom, the follow-up, got to number 25 R&B, but only number 59 pop."
**Twist My Arm (1986)** : Released in early 1986 from the "Contact" album, "Twist My Arm" failed to reach the top 40 on either the pop or R&B charts. "And in early 1986, Twist My Arm missed the top 40 on both charts."
**Goldmine (1986)** : Released as the first single from the "Hot Together" album, "Goldmine" peaked at number 33 just before Christmas 1986, becoming The Pointer Sisters' last top 40 pop hit as a lead act. "Goldmine, the first single from their Hot Together album, peaked at number 33 just before Christmas 86."
**Sexual Power (1987)** : This electro disco single reached number seven on Billboard's Club Play chart in 1987, demonstrating The Pointer Sisters' continued presence on the dance floor. "Their 1987 electro disco single Sexual Power reached number seven on Billboard's Club Play chart."
**Respect Yourself (1987)** : The Pointer Sisters provided backing vocals for Bruce Willis's cover of The Staple Singers' "Respect Yourself," with June Pointer even singing a verse. This collaboration marked the last time any Pointer-adjacent hit would crack the pop top 10. "The pointer sisters backed up the self-styled Bruno and June pointer even sang a verse."
**Be There (1987)** : The Pointer Sisters recorded this catchy song for the soundtrack of "Beverly Hills Cop 2" in an attempt to replicate their previous movie soundtrack success, but it only peaked at number 42. "But the catchy B there peaked at number 42."
**He Turned Me Out (1988)** : Keeping pace with contemporary trends, The Pointer Sisters released this New Jack Swing-styled track in 1988, which became a number 39 R&B hit, following Janet Jackson's influence on the genre. "The pointer sisters came back in 1988 with the new Jack styled He Turned Me Out, a number 39 R&B hit."
**Friends Advice (1990)** : As R&B shifted towards house and club music in the early 1990s, The Pointer Sisters incorporated a house beat into this single, which became a number 36 R&B hit. "the pointer sisters added a house beat to their 1990 single Friends Advice, a number 36 R&B hit."
**Chain of Fools (1994)** : On the 1994 album "Rhythm Country and Blues," The Pointer Sisters collaborated with country megastar Clint Black on a roadhouse-style cover of Aretha Franklin's classic "Chain of Fools," showcasing their continued engagement with country music as the genre exploded in popularity. "the pointer sisters teamed up with country megastar Clint Black on a roadhouse style cover of Aretha Franklin's classic Chain of Fools."
**Christmas in New York (2005)** : This holiday single, released by the trio version of The Pointer Sisters (Ruth, Anita, and Issa Pointer), achieved a minor hit on the adult contemporary chart in 2005. "This version of the pointer sisters scored a minor adult contemporary hit in 2005 with the holiday single Christmas in New York."
**Jump For My Love (2003) - Girls Aloud Cover** : British girl group Girls Aloud covered The Pointer Sisters' "Jump For My Love" in 2003, taking their version to number two on the UK singles chart, contributing to a reappraisal of the original group's legacy. "girls allowed the hit making British girl group took a cover of the pointer's jump for my love to number two on the UK singles chart."
**Pinball Number Count (Year TBD) - Remix** : A remix of The Pointer Sisters' "Sesame Street jam," "Pinball Number Count," became a minor dance hit a couple of years after 2003, further highlighting their diverse legacy. "a remix of the sister's Sesame Street jam pinball number count became a minor dance hit."
**Yes We Can Can (1973)** : This totemic 1973 hit by The Pointer Sisters, known for anticipating Barack Obama's campaign slogan, was frequently played at his rallies during his presidential campaign in 2008, contributing to a reappraisal of the group's legacy. "Yes We Can Can, which anticipated Obama's campaign slogan."

## curiosities
**Early Career Versatility** : The Pointer Sisters, a family vocal troupe, explored a wide range of genres in their early years as a quartet, including jazz, big band music, strutting funk, children's music, and twangy country, demonstrating significant musical adaptability. "trying everything from jazz and big band music to strutting funk, children's music, and even twangy country."
**Transition to Trio in 80s** : By the turn of the 1980s, The Pointer Sisters transitioned from a quartet to a trio, consisting of Ruth, Anita, and June Pointer, as they prepared to pursue a new wave of synth pop hits under the guidance of producer Richard Perry. "They are a trio of Ruth, Anita, and June pointer."
**First Music Video (1980)** : The Pointer Sisters' hit "He's So Shy" was their first song to receive a music video treatment, a significant achievement given it was a full year before the official launch of MTV. "It was also the first Pointer Sisters hit to receive music video treatment, a year before the launch of MTV."
**Female Empowerment in "Slow Hand"** : Critic Elizabeth Vincentelli noted that even without explicit lyrics about female empowerment, The Pointer Sisters bravely embodied it in songs like "Slow Hand," presenting themselves as women free of preconceptions and boundaries through their music. "even when The Pointer Sisters weren't singing specifically about female empowerment, they bravely embodied it."
**Reinvention with "Automatic"** : With the release of "Automatic" in 1984, The Pointer Sisters underwent another reinvention, aligning themselves with the synthesizer-driven new wave rock trend of the era and demonstrating their continued ability to adapt and remain relevant. "The Pointer Sisters had been reinvented yet again, and they were once again on trend."
**Title Change for "Jump For My Love" (1984)** : The Pointer Sisters' track, originally titled "Jump" on their album, was officially retitled "Jump For My Love" for its single release to differentiate it from Van Halen's synth-rocker, which had also reached number one on the Hot 100 with the title "Jump." "Team Pointer added a parenthetical to their track. The ladies next single would be titled Jump For My Love."
**"Jump For My Love" Music Video (1984)** : The Pointer Sisters produced a music video for "Jump For My Love" featuring them in slinky dresses in a steamy room, interspersed with footage of track and field athletes and basketball stars Julius Erving and Magic Johnson. This sporty video received heavy rotation on video channels, strategically leveraging the "1984 Olympics fever" in America. "They shot a video of the ladies singing in slinky dresses in a steamy room, intercut with footage of limber track and field athletes, as well as basketball stars Julius Irving and Magic Johnson."
**"I'm So Excited" Music Video (1984)** : The music video for the remixed "I'm So Excited" features Ruth and Anita Pointer in lingerie and June Pointer in a bubble bath, contributing to its tens of millions of views. "Its music video featuring Ruth and Anita in lingerie and June in a bubble bath has also been watched tens of millions of times."
**Legacy Hit Recognition of "I'm So Excited"** : Despite charting lower than "Automatic" and "Jump For My Love," the remixed "I'm So Excited" is now widely regarded as The Pointer Sisters' legacy hit, evidenced by its overwhelming popularity on Spotify with approximately 425 million streams, more than double that of "Jump For My Love." "today I'm so excited is regarded as the pointer sister's legacy hit."
**Anita Pointer's Vocal Opportunity (1984)** : After Ruth took the lead on "Automatic" and June on "Jump," Anita Pointer specifically sought a turn on lead vocals, leading producer Richard Perry to remix and reissue "I'm So Excited" with her on the lead. "after a Ruth lead vocal on Automatic and a June lead vocal on Jump, Anita pointer wanted a turn."
**Pop Ubiquity in Mid-80s** : By the mid-1980s, The Pointer Sisters achieved widespread pop recognition, evidenced by their invitation to join the chorus of the charity supergroup USA for Africa for the song "We Are the World." "The pointers pop ubiquity now seemed absolute."
**Adaptability to New Jack Swing (Late 1980s)** : The Pointer Sisters demonstrated their ability to keep up with music trends by adopting the New Jack Swing style in 1988, influenced by Janet Jackson's "Control" album, leading to hits like "He Turned Me Out." "they kept up with trends when Janet Jackson helped usher in the new Jack Swing era in 1986 and 1987 with the hits from her control album."
**Adaptability to House and Club Music (Early 1990s)** : As R&B evolved towards house and club music in the early 1990s, The Pointer Sisters incorporated a house beat into their 1990 single "Friends Advice," showcasing their continued versatility. "When R&B turned toward house and club music at the turn of the 90s, the pointer sisters added a house beat to their 1990 single Friends Advice."
**Continued Engagement with Country Music (1990s)** : The Pointer Sisters maintained a connection to country music throughout the 1990s as the genre's popularity surged, notably collaborating with Clint Black on a cover of "Chain of Fools" for the "Rhythm Country and Blues" album. "They even kept a toe dipped in country music as that genre exploded in popularity in the 90s."
**Broadway Debut and Touring (Mid-1990s)** : As their radio hits declined in the mid-1990s, The Pointer Sisters found a new artistic outlet on the legitimate stage, accepting roles in a touring company of the Broadway musical "Ain't Misbehaving," where their harmony singing skills were well-suited and earned them favorable notices and CNN coverage for their formal stage debut. "the pointers sought refuge on the legit stage, accepting roles in a touring company of the Broadway musical Ain't Misbehaving."
**Historic "Firsts"** : Over their 22-year career, The Pointer Sisters achieved several "firsts," including being the first contemporary group to play Guarper House and the first black woman to perform at the Grand Ole Opry. "The first contemporary group to play this happens is Guarper House, the first black woman to perform at the Grand Ole Opry."
**June Pointer's Addiction and Departure (Circa 2000)** : June Pointer's long-standing cocaine addiction led her to miss numerous performances during the "Ain't Misbehaving" tour. This ultimately compelled Ruth and Anita to remove her from the group around 2000 due to her ongoing struggles. "Long plagued by a cocaine addiction, youngest sister June pointer missed numerous performances on the Ain't Misbehaving tour. Eventually, circa 2000, Ruth and Anita were compelled to kick June out of the group altogether due to her ongoing addiction."
**Family Dispute and Rival Groups (2002-2003)** : Following June Pointer's departure, an internal family conflict arose when June and Bonnie Pointer performed gigs billed as "The Pointer Sisters" in 2002 and 2003, leading Ruth and Anita to sue the promoter for fraud. "There was an internison family battle, as June and Bonnie pointer teamed up in 2002 and 03 for some gigs build as the pointer sisters. Ruth and Anita sued the promoter for fraud."
**New Trio Lineup with Issa Pointer (2002)** : In 2002, Ruth and Anita Pointer continued performing as The Pointer Sisters by forming a new trio that included Ruth's daughter, Issa Pointer. "Starting in 2002, Ruth and Anita began performing as a trio with Ruth's daughter, Issa pointer."
**Period of Strife and Loss (2000s-2020s)** : The Pointer Sisters faced two decades of "strife and loss" that led to most original members being replaced, including June's death in 2006, Bonnie's death in 2020, and Anita's death in 2022. "the pointer sisters were beset by two decades of strife and loss."
**Current Lineup (2010s-Present)** : For most of the last decade, Ruth Pointer, the last remaining original member, has continued to perform The Pointer Sisters' gigs with her daughter, Issa Pointer, and her granddaughter, Sadeco Johnson. "Ruth pointer has performed pointer sisters gigs with not only her daughter Issa, but also her granddaughter, Sadeco Johnson."
**21st Century Reappraisal of Legacy (2000s-Present)** : Despite personal tragedies, the 21st century has brought a reappraisal of The Pointer Sisters' legacy, marked by cover successes (Girls Aloud, "Pinball Number Count" remix) and the political relevance of "Yes We Can Can" during Barack Obama's 2008 campaign. "the last two decades have brought a reappraisal of the pointer sister's legacy."
**Musical Adaptability and Fearless Attitude** : The Pointer Sisters are remembered for their exceptional adaptability and "game attitude," consistently trying diverse genres and defying industry doubts throughout their career, resulting in a stellar roster of 16 Top 40 hits, nearly half of which cracked the Top 10. "if the pointer sisters are remembered for anything besides their stellar roster of hits, 16 that made the top 40, nearly half of which cracked the top 10, it is their adaptability and their game attitude."
**"Yes We Can" Mantra** : The Pointer Sisters' fearless approach to the music business, constantly proving doubters wrong and achieving new hits, is encapsulated by their unwavering declaration: "yes, we can." "At every turn, when the music business doubted their prowess, or thought they couldn't come back with more hits, the pointer sister said, yes, we can."

## curiosities
**Producer's Vision for "He's So Shy"** : Producer Richard Perry recognized "He's So Shy" as a smash hit upon hearing it, particularly noting its trendy keyboard hook, which was at the peak of Yacht Rock's popularity. He changed the song's gender from "She's So Shy" to "He's So Shy" and guided The Pointer Sisters to record it, solidifying their status as pop stars. "When Richard Perry heard Snow and Wilde's song, it sounded to him like a smash."
**Producer's Vision for "Slow Hand"** : Richard Perry perceived "Slow Hand" as a sensual and seductive song, similar to The Pointer Sisters' earlier hit "Fire," and encouraged Anita Pointer to deliver a lead vocal that infused the track with subtly cutting-edge sex positivity. "Richard Perry heard Slow Hand as a sensual seductive song in the vein of The Pointer's previous hit Fire."
**Sale of Planet Records (1982)** : By 1982, Richard Perry sold his vanity label, Planet Records, to RCA Records, though he continued to oversee the artists on the imprint, including The Pointer Sisters, whom he credited for the label's establishment. "By 1982, with Planet Records established, thanks in no small part to The Pointer's sisters, Richard Perry sold his vanity label to RCA Records."
**Single Selection for "Jump For My Love"** : Richard Perry and The Pointer Sisters initially wanted to release an uptempo album cut simply titled "Jump" as their next single, but changed the title to "Jump For My Love" after Van Halen had a number one hit with their own song titled "Jump." "For their next single, The Sisters and Richard Perry wanted to go with an uptempo cut that on the album was simply titled Jump."
**Remix and Reissue Strategy for "I'm So Excited" (1984)** : Believing that the 1982 version of "I'm So Excited" hadn't reached its full potential and responding to Anita Pointer's desire for a lead vocal, Richard Perry had the single remixed and reissued the "Breakout" LP with the new version, leading to its eventual signature hit status. "Perry had the 1982 single remixed, and the breakout LP was reissued in record stores with I'm so excited tacked on."
