## songs
**Man of Constant Sorrow (2000) - Cover Anchoring O Brother, Where Art Thou?** : The Soggy Bottom Boys' cover of the traditional American folk song "Man of Constant Sorrow" anchored both the soundtrack and the plot of the Coen Brothers' satirical comedy *O Brother, Where Art Thou?* The band did not exist. It was a fictional group, and star George Clooney lip-synced their performance on screen. "But the soggy bottom boys were a fiction."

## albums
**O Brother, Where Art Thou? (2000) - Blockbuster Soundtrack to the Coen Brothers Film** : The soundtrack reached record stores in early December 2000, ahead of the film's release just before Christmas. The film was a modest success: it made back its budget and a decent profit, got favorable reviews and earned two nominations at the 2001 Academy Awards, winning neither. The soundtrack spent more than two years on the album chart and sold seven million copies in the US alone. It won the 2002 Grammy for Album of the Year and reached number one on the Billboard 200 just after the Grammys, in its 63rd chart week. "But the soundtrack? That was a blockbuster."

# artist - Simon & Garfunkel

## members
Paul Simon
Art Garfunkel

## genres
Folk Rock

## albums
**The Graduate (1968) - Soundtrack That Rewrote the Rules** : The soundtrack to Mike Nichols' film starring Dustin Hoffman contained the incomplete version of "Mrs. Robinson." In spring 1968 it spent nine weeks at number one on the Billboard album chart, trading the top spot with *Bookends*. For most of those weeks *The Graduate* was also number one at the box office. Afterwards, soundtracks could promote pop songs that carried the glow of a Hollywood movie even when the film was not a musical. Unlike the era of *Blackboard Jungle*, those non-diegetic hits could now be packaged on lucrative soundtrack LPs. "trading the number one spot back and forth."
**Bookends (1968) - Home of the Complete Mrs. Robinson** : The finished version of "Mrs. Robinson" appeared on the duo's 1968 album *Bookends*. It spent seven weeks at number one on the Billboard album chart, alternating with *The Graduate* soundtrack. "The complete Mrs. Robinson appeared on Simon and Garfunkel's 1968 album Bookends."

## songs
**Mrs. Robinson (1968) - Number One on the Hot 100** : The song reached number one on Billboard's Hot 100 in the week ending June 1, 1968. It began as an incomplete song Paul Simon already had, called "Mrs. Roosevelt." Mike Nichols persuaded the busy Simon to retitle it after the suburban parent and femme fatale played by Anne Bancroft, with whom Benjamin Braddock has an affair. Simon gave Nichols the half-finished song, so in the film it has only a chorus and some "dee dee dee"s. The duo did not finish writing or recording it until three months after the movie opened. "Actually, it was an incomplete song Simon already had lying around, that he called Mrs. Roosevelt."
**The Sound of Silence (1965) - Opening Scene of The Graduate** : This existing hit had reached number one on the Hot 100 nearly two years before *The Graduate*. Nichols's editor used it as temporary music for the opening scene, in which Benjamin Braddock passes forlornly through the airport on his way home from college. Nichols expected to replace it with new music but kept it. "Nichols assumed these old songs would eventually be replaced. But then he just left them in."
**Scarborough Fair/Canticle (1968) - Benjamin's Heartsickness** : This baroque medley plays as Ben longingly pursues Elaine Robinson on her college campus, and it expresses his heartsickness. It was a number 11 Hot 100 hit. "the song expressing his heart sickness is Simon and Garfunkel's Baroque medley, Scarborough Fair, Canticle"

## curiosities
**The Three Songs That Were Never Written** : Mike Nichols originally wanted new Simon & Garfunkel songs to serve as a kind of score for *The Graduate*. His producer negotiated a deal for Paul Simon to write three new songs. The duo was busy touring in 1967, and Simon never wrote them. "Paul Simon never got around to writing new songs."
**Pop Songs as a Greek Chorus** : In *The Graduate*, the Simon & Garfunkel songs worked like a Greek chorus. They commented on the characters and voiced their longings, wistfulness and regrets. Because they were well-known pop songs rather than incidental score, audiences could not miss them. The songs were non-diegetic: Ben Braddock doesn't hear them, but the audience does. They commented on the action and on the film's themes of youth alienation and the generation gap. "Film makers hadn't really used current pop songs in this way before."
**The Atlantic's 50th Anniversary Assessment (2018)** : In a 2018 article in *The Atlantic* marking the film's 50th anniversary, journalist Kristin Marguerite Doidge wrote that where other directors might have seen a song's existing associations as a burden or a distraction, Mike Nichols embraced the song's meaning. That choice influenced popular films for decades afterwards. "For Nichols, the soundtrack was meant to be heard, a choice that would go on to influence popular films for decades to come."

# genre - Show Tunes

## curiosities
**Charting Twice: Cast Album and Soundtrack** : In the '50s, original Broadway cast albums often charted as well as or better than film soundtracks. Many stage musicals were later filmed, so some shows topped the chart twice. *South Pacific* hit number one in 1949 as a cast album and in 1957 as a film soundtrack. "a show could scale the album chart twice"
**Oscar-Winning Soundtracks of the '50s** : Oscar-winning soundtrack LPs that topped the album chart included *Gigi*, *Around the World in 80 Days* and *An American in Paris*. *An American in Paris*, the Gene Kelly film, was Best Picture for 1951, and its soundtrack reached number one in 1952. The soundtracks to *Show Boat* (1951) and *The King and I* (1956) also topped the chart. "The best picture Oscar went to a musical several times during the 50s."

# curiosity

**Snow White - The First Soundtrack Album (1937)** : Walt Disney's first full-length animated feature produced the first movie soundtrack album, with songs including "Whistle While You Work," "Heigh-Ho" and "Someday My Prince Will Come." It came out before Billboard's weekly charts existed, so it has no chart data. "would generate the first movie soundtrack album."
**Singles Era of Movie Music** : In the 1940s, people who wanted movie music usually bought singles. Soundtrack albums were fairly rare on 78 RPM, and soundtrack LPs only took off after the 33⅓ RPM LP arrived in the 1950s. "Soundtrack albums were fairly rare in the age of 78 RPM."
**West Side Story (1961) - Longest-Running Number One Album** : The film soundtrack of the Jerome Robbins, Leonard Bernstein and Stephen Sondheim musical spent a combined 54 weeks at number one on Billboard's separate mono and stereo album charts. The film also won Best Picture for 1961. "longer than any album before or since."
**Doctor Zhivago (1965) - Instrumental Chart-Topper** : The romantic drama starred Omar Sharif and Julie Christie. Composer Maurice Jarre won an Oscar for its score, and the vocal-free soundtrack topped the album chart a year after the film opened. "You might say Dr. Javago was the Titanic of its day, a romance you could relive musically."
**My Fair Lady (1964) - Ghost-Sung Hit** : The soundtrack to the Broadway adaptation starring Audrey Hepburn ranked fourth among albums in 1965. "with vocals dubbed by Ghosts Singing Legend, Marney Nixon."
**Billboard Mid-'60s Headline** : A Billboard article noted that four of the top six albums of 1965 were soundtracks: *Mary Poppins*, *The Sound of Music*, *My Fair Lady* and *Goldfinger*. "Soundtracks are big business, read a mid-60s headline in Billboard magazine."
**Bond Themes as Chart Hits** : Not long after *Dr. No* (1962, starring Sean Connery), Bond themes became cultural touchstones and chart hits. Only one Bond song has ever reached number one on the Hot 100. The documentary *The Sound of 007* is recommended as an introduction. "only one Bond song has ever reached number one on the Hot 100"
**Diegetic vs. Non-Diegetic** : Diegetic music is part of the film's world, such as a character singing or a song playing in a bar. Non-diegetic sound does not come from the film's world. "Non-diagetic sound is any sound in a film that doesn't originate from the world of the film."
**Late-'60s Holdouts** : After *The Graduate*, most hit soundtracks for the rest of the '60s were still musicals like *Funny Girl* and *Oliver!* or score albums. *2001: A Space Odyssey* was one of the top-selling LPs of 1968 and 1969. "The effects of this were not immediate"
**Pulp Fiction (1994) and the Streaming Decline** : Quentin Tarantino's *Pulp Fiction* soundtrack collected his favorite songs. Streaming and playlists have made compilation albums less relevant, so a soundtrack like that is less likely to be made now. "A soundtrack like that is just less likely to exist now."
**One Battle After Another (2025)** : Paul Thomas Anderson's film is full of needle drops such as Steely Dan's "Dirty Work." Jonny Greenwood's score has been released, but none of the film's pop songs appear on it. "none of the movie's pop songs are on that soundtrack."
**Soundtrack Drought of the 2020s** : For most of the 2020s, months have gone by without a soundtrack in the top 10. That made it unusual to have *Wicked: For Good* and *KPop Demon Hunters* in the top five at the same time. "For most of the 2020s, we've gone months without any soundtracks touching the top 10."
**City of Angels Soundtrack Promo** : A vintage ad for the *City of Angels* soundtrack. "The number one soundtrack in America, City of Angels in stores now."
