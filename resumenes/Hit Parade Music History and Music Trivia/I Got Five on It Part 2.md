**Jive Talkin' (1975) - Breakthrough Disco Smash** : Every day, as the Gibbs drove to Criteria Studios across the causeway between Biscayne Bay and Miami, their car wheels made a distinctive "chicka chicka" rhythm. ## songs
**I Feel Love (1977) - The Invention of EDM** : "I Feel Love" was the brainchild of producers Pete Bellotte and Giorgio Moroder and reached number 6 on the Hot 100. It is generally agreed to have single-handedly invented electronic dance music, and it took inspiration from contemporaneous electro-rock bands like Kraftwerk. It was the first hit single with an entirely synthesized backing track; the only organic element was Summer's voice. Summer helped arrange the complex vocal melody, because, as Moroder told critic Simon Reynolds, it is a difficult song to sing. "I Feel Love was the first ever hit single with an entirely synthesized backing track."

## curiosities
**Queen of Disco Who Transcended Disco** : Donna Summer was a 1970s vocal queen and an author of club culture. She was a hitmaker for two decades and a dance-floor deity for more than three. Her collaborations with Giorgio Moroder and Pete Bellotte were formative in dance, electronic, and rock music, and she became the ultimate disco diva before transcending that role altogether. "She was a hitmaker for two decades and a dance floor deity for more than three."

## curiosities
**The Concept of "The One"** : In rhythm and blues, rock and roll, and soul, the emphasis had been on the second and fourth beats of the bar. James Brown stressed the first beat, which became the bedrock of funk. To the Godfather of Soul, the One was also a cosmic concept: getting on the one was a mode of living. "James Brown did was to stress the first beat. This became the bedrock of funk music, the rhythm of the one."

# curiosity

**The Great War Against the Single** : In the 1990s the music business ran a decade-long experiment to eliminate retail singles and push consumers to buy full-length albums. Hitmakers from MC Hammer to Alanis Morissette, the Rembrandts, No Doubt, Chumbawamba, and Sugar Ray underperformed on or vanished from the Hot 100 because only their albums were for sale. The tactic led to consumer backlash and, arguably, Napster. "It was a tactic that led to consumer backlash and, arguably, Napster."
**The AC/DC Rule** : This chart phenomenon describes a slow-growing album followed by an instant-smash successor that gives the act its first or only number one. Examples include Jimi Hendrix, Billy Joel, Pat Benatar, Jackson Browne, Boston, Van Halen, Depeche Mode, Lady Gaga, and most famously AC/DC. "slow-growing albums that are followed by instant smash successors."
**Defining the One-Hit Wonder** : Chart watchers have long debated what qualifies an act as a one-hit wonder: whether a second Hot 100 hit should count, and how high it has to go. Michael Sembello and a-ha are test cases. "whether a second hot 100 hit should count, how high that second hit has to go to remove the act from one hit wonder status."
**1967 Number Ones That Began as B-Sides** : "To Sir with Love," "Incense and Peppermints," and "Ode to Billie Joe" all started out as B-sides; "Light My Fire" did not. "Which of these 1967 number one hits did not start out as the B side to another song?"
**Chart Data Collectors** : Billboard magazine, Nielsen, MRC, and Luminate are among the data collectors throughout chart history. "We are not Billboard magazine, or Nielsen, or MRC, or Luminate, or any of the other data collectors throughout chart history."
