## curiosities
**Groundbreaking All-Women Tour (1997)** : Lilith Fair was a traveling festival that debuted in the early summer of 1997, spearheaded by a team of women. It was considered the first of its kind, specifically designed to defy the conventional industry wisdom that concert tours should feature no more than one or two women on the bill, instead presenting an exclusive lineup of all-women and women-fronted bands. "It was a traveling festival that defied conventional wisdom, the idea that no concert tour should have more than one or two women on the bill."
**Blockbuster Commercial Success (1997)** : During its inaugural run in 1997, Lilith Fair achieved blockbuster success, becoming the highest-grossing touring festival of the year. This commercial triumph was underpinned by the fact that all of Lilith's main stage acts were certified gold, platinum, or multi-platform record sellers, which also extended to platinum-selling R&B and hip hop artists as the roster expanded. "The highest grossing touring festival of the year on its very first run."
**Initial Concert and National Sell-out (1997)** : Lilith Fair played its inaugural show at the Gorge Amphitheater in Washington State during the week ending July 5th, 1997. This debut was quickly followed by widespread success, as the festival managed to sell out venues across the entire nation within a month, marking a significant triumph in popular music. "The same week her Lilith Fair festival played its first show at the Gorge Amphitheater in Washington State."
**Context of 90s Female Success** : At the time of its launch, Lilith Fair was widely regarded as the culmination of a decade—the 1990s—and a genre—90s rock—that had been exceptionally favorable to women performers. Many female artists were topping radio and Billboard charts during this era, and Lilith further diversified its lineup to include successful R&B and hip hop artists. "At the time, Lilith Fair was received as the consummation of a decade and a genre, 90s rock, that had been very good to women performers."
**Irony of Radio's Shift** : A poignant irony surrounded Lilith Fair's success: even as the festival was selling out venues nationwide, rock radio, in particular, was simultaneously pivoting away from showcasing women performers.
