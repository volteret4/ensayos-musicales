## songs
**Building a Mystery (1997) - Modern Rock Chart Debut** : Sarah McLachlan's "Building a Mystery" debuted on Billboard's Modern Rock Chart in the week ending July 5th, 1997. That was the same week her Lilith Fair festival played its first show, at the Gorge Amphitheatre in Washington State. The single's chart arrival and the festival's launch coincided at the start of her most commercially dominant summer. "building a mystery by Sarah McLaughlin made its debut on Billboard's Modern Rock Chart."

## curiosities
**Founder of Lilith Fair** : The Canadian singer-songwriter founded Lilith Fair, a traveling festival launched in the early summer of 1997. It was built entirely around women artists and women-fronted bands. Its purpose was to correct the industry perception that women could not sell tickets or keep radio listeners from changing the station. "Founded by Canadian singer-songwriter Sarah McLaughlin, Lilith Fair would prove a blockbuster success."

**Summer 1997 Chart Peak - Top Album in the Country** : One month after "Building a Mystery" debuted and Lilith Fair played its opening show, McLachlan had the top album in the country. At the same time, Lilith Fair was selling out venues across the nation, which made it a double commercial triumph. "One month later, McLaughlin would have the top single artist album in the country, while Lilith sold out venues across the nation."

# concert - Lilith Fair

## curiosities
**First Festival of Its Kind (1997)** : Lilith Fair was mounted in the early summer of 1997 by a team of women. It was a traveling festival made up entirely of women and women-fronted bands. This defied the conventional wisdom that no concert tour should have more than one or two women on the bill. "It was a traveling festival that defied conventional wisdom, the idea that no concert tour should have more than one or two women on the bill."

**Highest-Grossing Touring Festival of 1997** : On its very first run, Lilith Fair was the highest-grossing touring festival of the year. Every one of its main stage acts was certified gold, platinum, or multi-platinum. "The highest grossing touring festival of the year on its very first run."

**Opening Show at the Gorge Amphitheatre** : Lilith Fair played its first show at the Gorge Amphitheatre in Washington State. That was the week ending July 5th, 1997, the same week Sarah McLachlan's "Building a Mystery" debuted on Billboard's Modern Rock Chart. "The same week her Lilith Fair festival played its first show at the Gorge Amphitheater in Washington State."

**Expansion into R&B and Hip Hop** : Lilith Fair later expanded its roster to include women at the forefront of R&B and hip hop. Those artists were also platinum sellers. "when Lilith expanded its roster to include artists at the forefront of R&B and hip hop, those women were platinum sellers too."

**Consummation of a Decade** : At the time, Lilith Fair was received as the culmination of a decade and of a genre, 90s rock, that had been very good to women performers. Many of the biggest acts on radio and the Billboard charts were women, including some who were not on the Lilith Fair bill. "Lilith Fair was received as the consummation of a decade and a genre, 90s rock, that had been very good to women performers."

**Sold Out While Radio Turned Away** : At the very moment Lilith Fair was selling out venues nationwide, radio was pivoting away from women performers, rock radio in particular. "At the very moment, Lilith was selling out venues nationwide, radio. Rock Radio in particular was pivoting away from women performers."

# genre - 90s Rock

## curiosities
**A Decade Good to Women Performers** : 90s rock was a genre in which many of the biggest acts on radio and the Billboard charts were women. Women released some of the decade's most acclaimed albums and had some of its biggest hits. Even so, they spent the 90s convincing radio programmers and tour promoters that they could sell tickets and keep listeners from changing the station. "They still spent the 90s convincing radio programmers and tour promoters they could sell tickets and keep listeners from changing the station."

**Women Treated as Footnotes to Rock History** : Generations of women helped shape what rock became and inspired countless artists. Despite this, they were frequently treated as footnotes or sidebars to rock history. "They were frequently treated as footnotes or sidebars to rock history."

**"Women in Rock" Is Not a Genre** : Generations of female performers have said they are sick of women in rock being treated as a genre. The music of the 1990s was far more varied than the stereotype of the soul-bearing singer-songwriter with diary-like lyrics. Women rockers, rappers, and iconoclasts of the era undercut that stereotype by topping the charts. "generations of performers who happen to be female have said they are sick of women in rock being treated as a genre."
