# curiosity

**History of Number One Debuts on Hot 100** : For most of the 20th century, a song debuting at number one on the Hot 100 was considered impossible. However, in the 21st century era of digital music, this feat became not only possible but more commonplace, though many such "insta hits" have proven to be flukes. "For most of the 20th century, this explosive chart feat was considered impossible."
**Sean Ross Radio Data** : Radio consultant Sean Ross investigated the weekly radio spins for dozens of number one debuts across chart history to see which ones are still receiving airplay in 2025. His data revealed results "all over the map," with some songs from as little as two years ago receiving few to no spins, while others endured significantly. "Sean checked the weekly radio spins for the dozens of number one debuts across chart history to see which ones are still receiving airplay in 2025."
**"Superstar Momentum" Number One Debuts** : This category describes songs that opened at the top of the Hot 100 mainly because the artist was "on a roll," leveraging their existing fame and anticipation for instant chart success, sometimes leading to quick burnout. "These are songs that opened on top mainly because the artist was on a roll."
**"History Making Hit" Number One Debuts** : This category includes songs that opened at number one thanks to a "meta-narrative" bigger than the song itself, such as a tribute to a world-renowned figure or a groundbreaking cultural achievement like a song primarily in a non-English language. "These songs opened on top thanks to a meta-narrative, bigger than the song itself."
**"Spin-Off" Number One Debuts** : This category refers to seemingly new soloists who enter at number one because they are "spinning off from an already established group," leveraging their prior success with the group for immediate solo chart dominance. "These are seemingly new soloists who enter at number one because they are spinning off from an already established group."
**"Weird Number One Debuts"** : This category encompasses chart-toppers that are unusual or unexpected, often fueled by phenomena outside traditional music metrics, such as viral memes, video game integrations, or unadorned acoustic recordings that tap into a specific cultural moment. "Let's talk about another category, Weird Number One Debuts."
**Billboard Policy Change - YouTube Views (2013)** : A significant Billboard policy change in 2013 began counting YouTube views for the Hot 100 for the first time. This change allowed viral phenomena like Baauer's "Harlem Shake" to debut at number one, driven by massive video views rather than traditional radio airplay or sales. "Billboard policy change in 2013, when the magazine began counting YouTube views for the Hot 100 for the first time."
**Fortnite Battle Royale - Music Integration** : The online video game "Fortnite Battle Royale" served as a platform for Travis Scott to premiere his song "The Scotts" with Kid Cudi as a soundtrack to a special appearance. This integration leveraged the game's "massive gamer audience" during the 2020 pandemic to drive the song's number one debut. "Scott premiered The Scots... within Fortnite before releasing it as a single."
**Impact of American Idol Coronation Songs** : Most "American Idol coronation songs" are cited as examples of the "lamest number one debuts," none of them being considered great or enduring hits. "my bucket of lamest number one debuts includes most of the American Idol coronation songs I already mentioned."
**Longest Number One Song in Hot 100 History** : Taylor Swift's "All Too Well (10 Minute Version)" became the longest number one song in Hot 100 history by running time (over 10 minutes), surpassing Don McLean's "American Pie" and The Beatles' "Hey Jude." "It's the longest number one song in hot 100 history, longest as in running time."
**Number One Debuts Named Billboard's #1 Hit of the Year** : It is harder for a number one debut to also become Billboard's number one hit of the whole year, as "generally slower growing songs tend to take the title." Only two songs have achieved this feat: Ed Sheeran's "Shape of You" (2017) and Drake's "God's Plan" (2018). "Shape of You and Gods Plan are the only two number one debuts in Hot 100 history to be named Billboard's number one hit of the whole year 2017 and 2018 respectively."
**Fan Loyalty and Chart Success** : The process of chart success ideally begins with "loyal fans" who drive initial streams and purchases, causing songs to "explode onto the charts." If the song is great, the "larger public keeps it burning for weeks, months, and years to come." "Ideally, this is how the charts are supposed to work."
