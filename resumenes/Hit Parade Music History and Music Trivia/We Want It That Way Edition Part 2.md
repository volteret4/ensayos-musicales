## albums
**Backstreet Boys (1997) - US Debut** : This self-titled album was finally released in US music stores in the late summer of 1997, after the success of "Quit Playing Games With My Heart." It initially included the romantic ballad "As Long As You Love Me" and was later reissued in 1998 with "Everybody (Backstreet's Back)" added. "just as the Backstreet boys' self-titled debut album finally reached US Music stores."
**Millennium (1999) - Second US Album** : This album was the group's second US and third European release, with its launch in the spring of 1999 expected to do well given the group's recent chart explosion. It was strategically led by "I Want It That Way" and opened to an unprecedented 1.1 million copies in its first week, setting a new record for album sales at that time. "When the Backstreet Boys readyed the release of their second US and third European album, Millennium."
**Black & Blue (2000)** : Released in December 2000, this album followed "Millennium" and opened with 1.6 million sales in its first week. Although this figure would have been groundbreaking a year prior, it fell short of NSYNC's then-unbeatable record, indicating a shift in the boy band landscape. "In December, Black and Blue opened to 1.6 million in sales in its first week, a number that would have been mind-blowing a year earlier, but now fell short of NSYNC's seemingly unbeatable record."

## songs
**We've Got It Going On (1995)** : The group's first single, co-written by Max Martin and his mentor Dennis Popp, was described as sounding like "two Scandinavians approximating the sound of American R&B." It was a top five hit across Europe and in the UK, but only peaked at number 69 on the Hot 100 in the US due to the declining popularity of boy bands in America at the time. "We've Got It Going On in 1995."
**Quit Playing Games With My Heart (1997)** : Co-written by Max Martin, this mid-tempo ballad marked the Backstreet Boys' breakthrough on the Hot 100 in the US, peaking at number two in late summer 1997. Its success showed that this style was an easier sell for US audiences compared to their earlier R&B-wannabe sound. "The Backstreet boys finally broke through on the hot 100 with Quit Playing Games With My Heart, co-written once again by Max Martin."
**As Long As You Love Me (1997)** : This romantic ballad was the second hit from their self-titled US debut album, released soon after "Quit Playing Games With My Heart" broke through. As a radio-only track, it reached number four on Billboard's Radio Songs Chart, indicating Jive Records' strategy to encourage full-length CD sales. "The album spawned a second hit right away with the similarly romantic as long as you love me."
**Everybody (Backstreet's Back) (1997)** : This song was the group's third US top 40 hit, showcasing their original white R&B sound and serving as a self-referential boy band anthem. Co-written by Max Martin and Dennis Pop, it was initially intended to hype international fans and was kept off the US debut album by Jive Records President Barry Weiss due to its incongruous nature, but after Canadian and US radio play, it was added to a 1998 reissue and reached number four on the Hot 100 in May 1998. "Everybody, parentheses, Backstreet's Back, was a headscratcher for American audiences."
**I Want It That Way (1999)** : This song, described by Rolling Stone as a "genre transcending classic," was one of Max Martin's greatest and most nonsensical pop songs, with its inscrutable lyrics never fully explained by Martin and co-writer Andreas Carlson. Despite its sterling melody and frequent high rankings in polls of greatest pop and boy band songs, Jive Records chose not to issue it as a retail single in America, leading it to peak at a modest number six on the Hot 100 while compelling thousands of fans to buy the "Millennium" album. "Rolling Stone later called, I wanted that way. Quote, a genre transcending classic, unquote."
**Shape of My Heart (2000)** : This song served as the lead single from the "Black and Blue" album, representing an attempt to replicate the success and "magic" of "I Want It That Way." It topped out at number nine on the charts, suggesting that the initial fervor for boy bands was beginning to wane. "The Backstreet albums lead single and attempt to recreate the I wanted that way magic called Shape of My Heart, topped out at number 9."

## curiosities
**Creation by Lou Pearlman** : The Backstreet Boys were the first group formed by Lou Pearlman, a figure described as a "shadowy, svengali slash con man," who was inspired to enter the boy band business by the success of New Kids on the Block. "Pearlman formed the Backstreet Boys first."
**Origin of Name** : The band was named by Lou Pearlman after the Backstreet Market, a flea market in Orlando, Florida, known as a hangout spot for teenagers. "He named them after an Orlando flea market, the Backstreet market, where teenagers hung out."
**Legal Battle Against Lou Pearlman** : In 1998, the members of the Backstreet Boys sued Lou Pearlman for fraud, alleging that he had withheld most of their earnings and treated them as "indentured servants," becoming the first of Pearlman's groups to escape his control. "In 1998, they sued Perlman for fraud, claiming he'd kept most of their money and treated them as, quote, indentured servants."
**Chart Explosion and Imperial Peak** : By May 1998, with "Everybody (Backstreet's Back)" reaching number four on the Hot 100, the group was established as hitmakers and was approaching their "Imperial peak" in popularity and chart dominance. "a sign that the Backstreet Boys were now established hitmakers approaching their Imperial peak."
**Millennium Album's Record-Breaking Sales** : The "Millennium" album's debut with 1.1 million copies sold in its first week set a new record for the largest opening week for any album to that date, marking the beginning of a vibrant summer for pop music in 1999. "Backstreet Boys Millennium opened to 1.1 million copies in its first week, the largest week for any album ever to that date."
**Dominance in 1999** : During the summer of 1999, the Backstreet Boys "lorded over" the pop music scene, selling the most CDs, dominating MTV's Total Request Live (TRL) countdown, and setting the cultural pace for Millennial Pop. "But the Backstreet Boys lorted over all that summer, selling the most CDs, dominating the TRL countdown, and setting the pace for Millennial Pop."
**Cultural Impact of Millennium** : The "Millennium" album went on to sell 13 million copies and was instrumental in defining the "zeitgeist" as the year 2000 (Y2K) approached. "The Millennium album went on to sell 13 million copies, and it defined the zeitgeist as why 2K approached."
**Hiatus and Reunion** : The group went on hiatus for about three years after 2002. They scored a number one album in 2019, and continue to tour as "Backstreet Men," demonstrating the enduring appeal of boy bands. "The Backstreet Boys for their part were no slouches in 2000."

"NSYNC recorded their second album, New Strings Attached, while they were extricating themselves from their original contract."
**Celebrity (2001) - Final Album** : The group's final album, released in the summer of 2001, aimed to show they were "in on the joke" regarding the criticism of boy bands. It opened to 1.7 million in sales but only stayed at number one for a week and did not produce a chart-topping hit, reflecting the fading popularity of the boy band era. "You might say that was also the premise of what turned out to be NSYNC's final album Celebrity."

## songs
**Tearin' Up My Heart (1997)** : Co-written by Max Martin and his Swedish colleagues, this song served to define NSYNC's sound, which was characterized as being a bit more R&B-forward than the Backstreet Boys' style, while still blending easily with the emerging shiny boy band sound. It eventually became a number 15 US radio hit. "Terran Up My Heart, an eventual number 15 US radio hit, defined in sync's sound."

"Not long after TRL launched, In sync scored their first ever US top 10 hit, with the ballad God must have spent a little more time on you, which peaked at number 8."
**Bye Bye Bye (2000)** : As the lead single from "No Strings Attached," this song became an instant top five hit and served a dual purpose: a romantic kiss-off and a metaphorical "screw you" to Lou Pearlman, letting fans in on the group's struggles to free themselves from his control. It also reminded fans how long it had been since the group released new material. "So did the album's lead single, Buy Buy Buy, which doubled as both a romantic kiss-off song and a screw you to Perlman."
**It's Gonna Be Me (2000)** : Co-written by Max Martin and his team of Swedish songwriters, this "meme-tastic" follow-up single to "Bye Bye Bye" went all the way to the top of the Hot 100 in the summer of 2000, becoming a chart-topping hit for the group. "The meme-tastic, it's gonna be me."
**Pop (2001)** : The lead single from their final album "Celebrity," this song saw the group directly address their critics who were demeaning boy bands for their populist music. "On its lead single, Pop, the group took aim at their critics who were debasing the boy band for their populist music."
**Gone (2001)** : This sole ballad from the "Celebrity" album performed well, nearly cracking the top 10 on both Billboard's R&B chart and the pop chart, showcasing Justin Timberlake's emerging R&B credibility. "The sole ballad, Gone, did almost as well on Billboard's R&B chart as it did on the peak just outside the top 10 on both charts."
**Girlfriend (2001)** : Featuring rapper Nelly, this track was a "legit hip-hop dance track" and the final single from "Celebrity." It made the pop top 5 and reached number 23 on the R&B chart, further highlighting the R&B influences in the group's later work and setting up Justin Timberlake's solo career. "And the album's final single, Girlfriend, featuring rapper Nelly, was a legit hip-hop dance track."
**Better Place (2023)** : This song was recorded for the "Trolls Band Together" animated movie franchise, marking the group's reunion. It reached number 25 on the Hot 100 and even entered the top 10 on the adult contemporary chart, catering to their original fanbase who were then in their 30s and 40s. "The song from Trolls Band Together titled Better Place reached number 25 on the Hot 100 and even crapped the top 10 on the adult contemporary chart."
