## albums
**The Writing's On the Wall (1999) - Sophomore Album** : This album served as the group's second release, successfully obliterating any notion of a "sophomore slump" and selling 8 million copies in America alone. It built upon the legacy of earlier 1990s girl groups like En Vogue and TLC, but critics, such as John Perellis of The New York Times, noted the group's subtle sonic advances, particularly Beyoncé's voice and the dynamic way melodies interacted with rhythm tracks. The album made good on the group's long-standing effort to fuse Beyoncé's potent R&B singing with hip hop rhythms. "The Writing's On the Wall finally made good on this bespoke formula."
**Survivor (2001) - Third Album** : Destiny's Child's third album continued their hybridization of genres, notably scoring their biggest hit by sampling a rock hook, demonstrating their innovative approach to incorporating diverse musical elements. "Destiny's Child were still doing their part to hybridize genres on their third album 2001's Survivor."

## songs
**Bills, Bills, Bills (1999)** : This smash hit led off "The Writing's On the Wall" and featured syncopated four-part harmonies, blending elements of a traditional girl group with a rap crew sound. The song, which dressed down an individual unable to pay his own rent, rose to number one on the Hot 100 in just five weeks, breaking through in the competitive summer of 1999. "It all led off with the smash, Bills, Bills, Bills, a jam that dressed down a scrub, unable to pay his own rent."
**Bugaboo (1999)** : A minor pop hit from "The Writing's On the Wall," this percolating track successfully managed to crack the R&B Top 20, showcasing the group's unified sound and subtle sonic advances. "From the percolating bugaboo, a minor pop hit that managed to crack the R&B top 20..."
**Jumpin', Jumpin' (1999)** : This metronomic club track from "The Writing's On the Wall" became a Top 10 hit on both the pop and R&B charts, further solidifying Destiny's Child's reputation for potent permutations of their hybrid sound. "to the top 10 pop and R&B hit Jump and Jumpin, a metronomic club track, the club is full of pilots and they practice full of bugs."
**Say My Name (2000)** : A multi-week number one smash, this song was a signal moment in the history of singing and rapping hybridization, showcasing Beyoncé's "aerobic vocals." Its vocals possessed the range and melody of R&B but the distinct cadence of rap, featuring triplets and numerous syllables, creating a dizzying flow. The song spent three weeks on top of both the Hot 100 and the R&B chart. "Say my name is a signal moment in the history of singing and rapping hybridization."
**Bootylicious (2001)** : This body-positive hit from the album "Survivor" incorporated a fierce guitar riff sampled directly from Stevie Nicks' 1982 number 11 pop hit, "Edge of 17," and successfully took it to number one on the charts. "Destiny's took that riff to number one on their body positive hit, Bootylicious."

## curiosities
**Early Group Name - Girls Tyme** : In the early 1990s, before becoming Destiny's Child, the group was known as Girls Tyme and made an early appearance on TV's "Star Search," where they first attempted to fuse Beyoncé's potent R&B singing with hip hop rhythms. "From their inception in the early 90s when the group was known as Girls Time and wrapped their way onto TV's star search, Destiny's Child had been trying to fuse Beyoncé's potent R&B singing to hip hop rhythms."
**Internal Drama and Lineup Changes** : Despite serving up a unified sound, the group itself was "anything but unified," experiencing significant internal drama during the promotion of "The Writing's On the Wall." Members Latoya Luckett and Latavia Roberson accused manager Matthew Knowles of favoring his daughter Beyoncé and family friend Kelly Rowland, leading to their dismissal and replacement by vocalists who lip-synced their parts in videos. "Destiny's Child kept serving up potent permutations of a unified sound, even though the group itself was anything but unified."
**Managerial Favoritism Allegations** : Matthew Knowles, the group's manager and Beyoncé's father, faced accusations of favoring Beyoncé and Kelly Rowland over other members, which resulted in the dismissal of Latoya Luckett and Latavia Roberson during the promotion of their second album. "Latoya Luckett and Latavia Roberson accused Matthew Knowles of favoring his daughter Beyoncé and their family friend Kelly Knowles wound up dismissing Luckett and Roberson in the middle of promoting the album..."

## albums
**I Am... Sasha Fierce (2008) - Double LP** : This album, a musically varied double LP, garnered Beyoncé a "queenly reception" and significant Grammy nominations, including a win for "Single Ladies" in the Song of the Year category. Other tracks on the album, such as "Diva," explored more experimental sounds closer to straight-up rap. "In late 2008, Beyonce issued her album I Am Sasha Fears, a musically varied double LP that earned her a queenly reception, including some top nominations at the Grammys."
**4 (2011) - Musical Foundation Shift** : With this album, Beyoncé began to deconstruct the established foundations of her music, featuring experimental tracks like the martial chant "Run the World (Girls)," the synthy hip hop track "Party" (with Kanye West and Andre 3000), and the intricately built love song "Countdown." "By the time of her 2011 album, four, Beyonce was starting to pull apart the foundations of her music..."
**Beyoncé (2013) - Surprise Drop Album** : This self-titled album pioneered the "surprise drop strategy," being released unexpectedly on iTunes in the closing weeks of 2013. Beyoncé used this album to get personal and "go for broke," focusing less on radio hits and more on stretching herself thematically, lyrically, and stylistically. It was her most acclaimed album to date, earning a Grammy nomination for Album of the Year in 2015. "queen bee pioneered the surprise drop strategy when she unleashed herself titled Beyonce album on iTunes in the closing weeks of 2013."
**Lemonade (2016) - Magnum Opus** : Her second consecutive surprise album, "Lemonade" is considered a magnum opus and a deep meditation on fidelity, released in the wake of rumors about her husband Jay-Z's unfaithfulness. The album showcased fiercelyricism and hip-hop attitude, spanning an eclectic palette of sounds and styles from art pop to shredding rock, country music, and electro-R&B. It was nominated for top Grammy categories in 2017. "Lemonade was a meditation on fidelity. In the wake of rumors that her husband Jay-Z had been unfaithful."

## songs
**03 Bonnie & Clyde (2002)** : This duet with Jay-Z served as a semi-official debut for Beyoncé as a soloist outside of Destiny's Child. An interpolation of 2Pac Shakur's 1996 track "Me and My Girlfriend," it became Jay-Z's biggest pop hit to date, reaching number four, and was produced by an up-and-coming Kanye West. "Jay Z and Beyonce's O3 Bonnie and Clyde was important to both of their careers."
**Crazy in Love (2003)** : Heralded as the "official product launch" for solo Beyoncé, this song was a massive hit featuring a full troop of dancers in the video, a frenetic chant-along chorus, and a sharp rap bridge from her then-boyfriend Jay-Z. Its most innovative aspect was the track's unusual beat, constructed by producer Rich Harrison from an unorthodox horn blast sample from a 1970 soul classic by The Soul Searchers. "If O3 Bonnie and Clyde was the beta test for solo Beyonce, crazy in love was the official product launch."
**Baby Boy (2003)** : Featuring Sean Paul as a guest, this song was Beyoncé's own blend of dancehall with R&B, reaching number one on the charts just four months after Sean Paul's "Get Busy." "With Beyonce, who grabbed Paul as a guest for Baby Boy, her own spin on dancehall crossed with R&B."
**Check On It (2006)** : This number one hit saw Beyoncé refining her syncopated bounce and collaborating with rapper Slim Thug during a period when her husband, Jay-Z, was on hiatus from rap. "Jay left the sing-rapping to his girlfriend Beyoncé, who was refining her syncopated bounce and teaming with actual rappers like Slim Thug on her 2006 number one hit, Check On It."
**Single Ladies (Put a Ring on It) (2008)** : This highly successful single marked Beyoncé's last completely solo Hot 100 number one and her final undeniably mass-appeal pop smash. It notably won Song of the Year at the Grammys, which remains Queen Bey's only top category win at the awards. "Single ladies put a ring on it even took home song of the year."
**Diva (2008)** : Featured on the "I Am... Sasha Fierce" album, this track represented Beyoncé's closest approach to straight-up rap at that point. To Queen Bey, the term "Diva" signified a "female version of a hustler," and she delivered her bars with conviction. "Other tracks on the Sasha Fears album were more experimental, most especially Diva, the closest Beyonce had come to that point to straight up rap."
**Run the World (Girls) (2011)** : This track from the album "4" was characterized as a "martial chant," indicative of Beyoncé's shift towards more experimental and foundational music. "run the world girls was a martial chant."
**Party (2011)** : A synthy hip hop track from "4," this song featured collaborations with Kanye West and Andre 3000, further demonstrating Beyoncé's exploration of new musical territories. "Party was a synthy hip hop track with Kanye West and Andre 3000."
**Countdown (2011)** : Identified as the standout track from the album "4," this dizzying love song was constructed from a chopped-up Boys II Men sample, hashtag-style lyrics, and marching band horns, showcasing a brilliant blend of pop and R&B elements drawing from rap tools. "the album's standout track Countdown was a dizzying love song built out of a chopped up boys to men's sample, hashtag style lyrics and marching band horns."
**Drunk in Love (2013)** : The lead single from her self-titled album, this number two hit featured her spouse, Jay-Z, and explored the theme of keeping the fire alive in their marriage. Beyoncé herself engaged in nearly as much "bars dropping" as Jay-Z and coined the hashtag-worthy term "SURFBORT." "On its lead single, the number two hit drunk in love, Be teamed up again with her spouse in a song about keeping the fire alive in their marriage, but she did nearly as much bars dropping as Jay did."
**Partition (2013)** : A follow-up hit from her self-titled album, this song was described as a "lascivious ode to sex" and was more than half rapped, showcasing Beyoncé's stylistic evolution and willingness to incorporate more rap elements into her music. "The follow-up hit Partition, a lascivious ode to sex, was more than half wrapped."
**Formation (2016)** : Introduced with a surprise appearance at the 2016 Super Bowl halftime show, this song was perhaps Beyoncé's most political single ever, serving as a Black Lives Matter anthem. It managed to scrape the Top 10 weeks after its debut and set the stage for her "Lemonade" album. "Formation managed to scrape the top 10 a few weeks after the Super Bowl, and it set up Beyonce's second consecutive surprise album and Magnum Opus, Lemonade."

## curiosities
**Eccentric Vocalist with Original Approaches** : Critic Jodie Rosen, writing in The New Yorker, praised Beyoncé as an "eccentric, a vocalist with truly weird and original melodic and rhythmic approaches," specifically noting the "slippery rap style syncopations in Say My Name." Rosen suggested these sounds didn't exist before Beyoncé and that her influence, along with her followers, "retrained our ears." "Years later, critic Jodie Rosen wrote in the New Yorker Beyoncé is an eccentric, a vocalist with truly weird and original melodic and rhythmic approaches."
**Cool Hunter of the 2000s** : Her ability to absorb new styles and collaborate with current hitmakers established her as one of the 2000s' ultimate "cool hunters," demonstrating her keen ear for emerging musical trends. "Bees' absorption of new styles and current hit makers made her one of the decades ultimate cool hunters."
**Pioneer of the Surprise Album Drop** : Beyoncé revolutionized album release strategies by pioneering the "surprise drop strategy" in 2013, unleashing her self-titled album on iTunes without prior announcement, which had a significant impact on the industry. "queen bee pioneered the surprise drop strategy when she unleashed herself titled Beyonce album on iTunes in the closing weeks of 2013."
**2016 Super Bowl Halftime Show Performance** : Beyoncé made a dazzling surprise appearance at the 2016 Super Bowl halftime show, where Coldplay was the official performer. She marched onto the field with a full drill team to debut her political single, "Formation," an anthem for Black Lives Matter. "In fact, less than one year later, she made a surprise appearance at a much bigger television event than the Grammys. The official performer of the 2016 Super Bowl halftime show was Coldplay, but the British band seated a large chunk of their time to guests Bruno Mars and most dazzlingly, Beyonce."
**Repeated Grammy Album of the Year Snubs** : Despite releasing highly acclaimed and top-selling albums like "Beyoncé" (2013) and "Lemonade" (2016), Beyoncé consistently lost the Album of the Year Grammy. This was attributed to generalist Grammy voters perceiving her as a "de facto rapper," despite her being a consummate singer and commanding artist, a pattern also observed with other acclaimed hip-hop artists. "She was to the generalist Grammy voter, a de facto rapper."

## albums
**Country Grammar (2000) - Solo Debut** : This solo debut album was an instant sensation, helping to put St. Louis on the hip hop map with Nelly's uniquely twangy melodic flow. The album, which featured some of his "Sing Songiest" hits, spent a month atop the album chart and achieved eight-times platinum status by 2001. "St. Louis Bourne Cornell Haynes Jr. Better known as Nelly was an instant sensation in 2000 with his solo debut Country Grammar."
**Nellyville (2002) - Sophomore Album** : On his second album, Nelly delved even deeper into melody, continuing his trend of blending singing and rapping, notably with the hit "Hot in Herre." "A year later, on his sophomore album Nellyville, the singer slash rapper went deeper into melody on Hot In Here..."

## songs
**Country Grammar (Hot S**t) (2000)** : The album's title track, described as a "spicy nursery rhyme set to music," was based around the age-old children's clapping game "Down Down Baby" and its memorable refrain, "shimmy shimmy koko pop." It became a Top 10 pop hit and also reached the Top 5 on the Hot R&B/Hip Hop Songs chart. "His albums title track Country Grammar, parentheses hot shit, was like a spicy nursery rhyme set to music based around the age old kid's clapping game song Down Down Baby, with its memorable refrain, shimmy shimmy koko pop."
**Ride Wit Me (2000)** : The biggest hit from the "Country Grammar" album, this track was a "pure pop confection" driven so strongly by its melody that it seemed odd to label it as rap. Notably, Nelly himself did not rap straight on the track, with only his guest, City Spud, delivering traditional bars on the bridge. Both "Country Grammar" and "Ride Wit Me" were Top 10 pop hits. "the album's biggest hit, Ride With Me, was a pure pop confection, so driven by its melody it seemed odd to call it rap at all."
**Hot in Herre (2002)** : Produced by The Neptunes, this song from "Nellyville" was adapted from a 1979 Charles Brown Go-Go classic. Nelly delivered double-time melodic rhymes, and the track subsequently topped the Hot 100 for seven weeks, solidifying his melodic rap style. "A year later, on his sophomore album Nellyville, the singer slash rapper went deeper into melody on Hot In Here, produced by The Neptune's and adapted from the 1979 Charles Brown Go Go classic and released with Nelly dropping double time melodic rhymes."
**Dilemma (2002)** : A duet with Kelly Rowland, this song was an interpolation of Patti LaBelle's 1983 hit "Love, Need and Want You." While Kelly Rowland was clearly singing, Nelly's flow, though closer to rap, also drove the melody, showcasing the increasing hybridization of genres. "Nelly and Kelly's version was called Dilemma, and while she was clearly singing and his flow was closer to rap, both of them were driving the melody."
