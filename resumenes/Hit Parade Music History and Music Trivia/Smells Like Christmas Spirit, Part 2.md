# artist - Michael Jackson

## genres
Pop

## labels
Epic

## albums
**Bad (1987) - Blockbuster Predecessor** : This album was a blockbuster released in 1987, preceding his 1990s album, Dangerous. "The follow-up to his 1987 blockbuster Bad."
**Dangerous (1991) - First New Album of the 90s** : Released in November 1991, just before Thanksgiving, this album was greeted with immense hype, comparable to that of the Olympics. It debuted at number one on the Billboard 200 chart in early December 1991. Despite being knocked off the top spot by Nirvana's Nevermind, it never returned to number one but generated six more top 40 hits, including a number two hit. The album sold seven million copies in the US and ranked as the second biggest album of 1992, behind Garth Brooks' "Ropin' the Wind" but ahead of Nirvana's "Nevermind". "The new disc was called Dangerous, and it was greeted with a level of hype more akin to the Olympics than a pop album."

## songs
**Black or White (1991)** : This was the first single from the "Dangerous" album, kicking off its campaign two weeks before the album's release. It was accompanied by a very expensive video that premiered simultaneously on MTV, VH1, BET, and Fox in America, and BBC's Top of the Pops in the UK, reaching an estimated global audience of 500 million viewers across 27 countries. The song is a discourse on racism in the form of a pop song, featuring a guitar riff that echoed John Mellencamp's "Hurts So Good" and a mid-song rap by producer Bill Bottrell. It quickly rose to number one on the Hot 100 in just its third week. "Black or White was Michael Jackson's discourse on racism in the form of a pop song, with a brain-colonizing guitar riff that echoed John Mellon Camp's Hurts So Good."

## curiosities
**"King of Pop" Title** : For the first time ever, Jackson's label, Epic, insisted the media refer to him as the King of Pop, a title that most journalists and fans subsequently adopted. "Jackson's label, Epic, insisted the media refer to him for the first time ever as the King of Pop, and most journalists and fans dutifully obliged."
**"Black or White" Video Event** : The music video for "Black or White" was an event in itself, featuring cameos by Macaulay Culkin, George Wendt (from NBC's Cheers), and Supermodel Tyra Banks. It notably showcased the then-novel "Morphing" CGI technology for face-swapping sequences. "The global audience for Black or White across 27 countries was estimated at 500 million viewers."
**"Black or White" Video Controversy and Edit** : The video generated controversy due to an extended segment after the song where an angry Jackson, presumably incensed by racism, smashed a car, zipped his fly, and touched himself. This segment disturbed many parents, leading MTV and other outlets to mostly stop playing it. Jackson eventually apologized and released an edited version that CGI'd anti-racist messages onto the car windows. "The closing segment disturbed enough parents that MTV and other outlets mostly stopped playing the tail end of the clip, and Jackson apologized and issued an edit to the video that CGI'd in anti-racist messages on the car windows."
**Christmas Buying Season King** : In the music industry's 1991 Christmas buying season, Michael Jackson was considered the dominant artist, poised to rule the album charts deep into winter with his "Dangerous" album. "In short, Michael Jackson was the King of the 1991 Christmas Buying Season."

## albums
**Nevermind (1991)** : Released in September 1991, this album was initially expected to sell modestly, possibly around 100,000 copies, similar to a Sonic Youth album. It took more than three months to reach number one on the Billboard 200. Despite predictions that it had peaked at number six, it unexpectedly surged from number six to number one on the Billboard 200 in early January 1992, knocking Michael Jackson's "Dangerous" off the top spot. Its sales grew by 193,000 copies in a single week, a record for the SoundScan system at a time when most albums typically sold fewer copies post-Christmas. The album, featuring an "insolent punk worthy and now iconic image" of a baby swimming after a dollar, became platinum certified and went quadruple platinum by June 1992, eventually achieving diamond certification for 10 million in sales. It ranked as the third biggest album of 1992. "Nirvana leapt all the way from number six to number one on the Billboard 200, knocking out Michael Jackson's dangerous."

## curiosities
**Unplanned Chart Coup** : Nirvana's rise to number one on the Billboard charts was not planned; their label, DGC Records, had modest sales expectations, hoping "Nevermind" might sell around 100,000 copies. The unexpected chart dominance against Michael Jackson was widely covered by the media, using verbs like "pushed, shoved, steamrolled, and knocked out" to describe their feat, though the band had not plotted the attack. "But of course, nothing about Nirvana's coup was planned."
**"Palace Coup" Recognition** : Longtime Billboard chart beat columnist Paul Grine described Nirvana's dethroning of Michael Jackson as "an astonishing palace coup." Industry insiders, including Billboard's album sales analyst Jeff Mayfield, were amazed by the feat. "Long time Billboard chart beat columnist Paul Grine wrote, quote, Nirvana pulls off an astonishing palace coup by dethroning the King of Pop, unquote."
**Post-Christmas Sales Rulers** : Nirvana became the "rulers of post Christmas" in 1991, indicating that consumers, primarily teenagers, were spending gift cards or exchanging CDs for "Nevermind" in the days following the holiday. The album's massive sales growth between Christmas and New Year's occurred when high school and college students were home. "Nirvana were the rulers of post Christmas. And what is post Christmas? In the music world, it's when consumers, mostly teenagers, spend the gift cards they've received, or maybe exchange a CD or a tape they were given for one they like better."
**Christmas Song Promo for RuPaul (Early 1990s)** : The trio, including Kurt Cobain, Krist Novoselic, and Dave Grohl, recorded evidence of them singing a Christmas song during the shooting of a promo for RuPaul's early 90s TV talk show. This is the only known recording of them singing a Christmas song while Cobain was alive. "That's Kurt Cobain, Chris Novicellich, and Dave Grohl, with the only recorded evidence while Cobain was still alive of the trio singing a Christmas song."
**No Christmas Song Release (Stylistic Choice)** : Nirvana never released a Christmas song, as it would not have been consistent with their style, "not even ironically." "Maybe Nirvana never released a Christmas song. It wouldn't have been their style, not even ironically."

Despite industry skepticism about such a late release, it debuted with sales of 670,000 copies, one of the top 10 biggest weeks of the SoundScan era, and codified a new industry practice for hip-hop album drops aimed at post-Christmas fans. "Flesh of my flesh, blood of my blood, debuted sales of 670,000 copies, one of the 10 biggest weeks of the sound scan era to date."
**...And Then There Was X (1999) - Top Seller** : DMX's third album was released on December 21, 1999, exactly one year after his previous strategic Christmas week release. It knocked Celine Dion from her album chart position and became his top-selling album, featuring hits like "What's My Name" and "Party Up." **King of Hardcore Rap** : DMX emerged as the "new king of Hardcore Rap" in the late 90s, following the deaths of Tupac Shakur and The Notorious B.I.G. **Million-Dollar Bonus Challenge** : The president of Def Jam challenged DMX to record and release a second full studio album before the end of 1998, promising him a million-dollar bonus for doing so, a unique approach given major rappers typically took at least a year between albums. "He promised the rapper a million-dollar bonus if he could record and release a second album before the end of the year."
**Unprecedented Late-Year Album Release (1998)** : The release of "Flesh of My Flesh, Blood of My Blood" just three days before Christmas was "essentially unprecedented" according to Billboard's Jeff Mayfield, as conventional wisdom held that such a late release would get lost or struggle to reach shelves. Despite skepticism, it proved successful. "This was essentially unprecedented. **Codified Post-Christmas Hip-Hop Drop** : DMX's second album, "Flesh of My Flesh, Blood of My Blood," codified a new industry practice: the hip-hop album drop specifically timed for the post-Christmas fan. This strategy transformed Nirvana's earlier accidental timing into a deliberate marketing approach. "More importantly, DMX's second album codified a new industry practice, the hip hop album drop intended for the post Christmas fan."
**"Dog Whistle" to Young Fans** : Releasing "Flesh of My Flesh, Blood of My Blood" nine days before the end of the year was described as a "dog whistle to young music fans," signaling that the CD was intended for them, rather than as a gift from parents. "Dropping it 9 days from the end of the year was like a dog whistle to young music fans. This CD is for you, Merry Christmas."
