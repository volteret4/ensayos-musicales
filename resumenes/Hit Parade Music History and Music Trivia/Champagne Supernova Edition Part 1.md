## songs
**Connection (1995) - Number 2 US Modern Rock Hit** : In May 1995 "Connection" rose to number 2 on Billboard's Modern Rock Tracks chart. It was everywhere on US alternative radio and crossed over to some Top 40 pop stations, thanks to a danceable beat and a bit of punk attitude. Most American listeners missed that the song was a cheeky homage to earlier waves of British art punk. In a move compared to Noel Gallagher's habits, it borrowed its rhythmic hook from Wire's 70s track "Three Girl Rhumba," and Justine Frischmann did not deny the resemblance. The song drove the Elastica album to gold status in America. "Connection was a cheeky homage to previous waves of British art punk."
**Stutter (1993) - Debut Single** : Elastica's punky debut single arrived in fall 1993 and drew near-instant acclaim. "As debut single, The Punky Stutter arrived in the fall of 1993 to near instant acclaim."
**Line Up (1994) - UK Top 20 Follow-Up** : Released in winter 1994, "Line Up" showed Elastica's bespoke sound, which drew heavily on late-70s post-punk bands like Wire and The Stranglers. It reached the UK Top 20 and built feverish anticipation for a debut album that took another year to arrive. "Line Up cracked the UK Top 20 and prompted feverish excitement for an Elastica debut album."
**Waking Up (1995) - Biggest UK Hit to Date** : In early 1995 "Waking Up" became Elastica's biggest UK hit up to that point, peaking at number 13. The long-awaited debut album followed. "Elastica, whose single Waking Up became their biggest UK hit to date at number 13."

## albums
**Elastica (1995) - Record-Breaking Self-Titled Debut** : Like Parklife and Definitely Maybe before it, the album entered the British album chart at number 1. Its opening sales beat Definitely Maybe's, making it the fastest-selling British debut album in history up to that point. It also went gold in America on the strength of the number 2 Modern Rock hit "Connection." "Elastica's opening sales eclipsed definitely maybe as the fastest-selling British debut album in history to that date."

## curiosities
**Origins in Suede** : Justine Frischmann co-founded Suede in 1988 with then-boyfriend Brett Anderson and was its original guitarist before Bernard Butler joined. Frischmann left in 1992, before Suede's formal recorded debut, and formed Elastica with Justin Welch, who had briefly been Suede's drummer. Bassist Annie Holland and guitarist Donna Matthews completed the lineup, making Elastica a mostly female band. "a mostly female band whose leader had previously been a member of Swade."
**Britpop's It Couple** : Justine Frischmann had been dating Blur's Damon Albarn since the early 90s. The pair fascinated Fleet Street and the British tabloids as a musical power couple. "became an object of Fleet Street fascination, Britpop's It Couple."
**Lollapalooza 1995** : Elastica joined the touring Lollapalooza festival in summer 1995. To American audiences they looked like just the latest band offering catchy punk-adjacent rock at the height of the alternative era. "The band would even join the Traveling Lala Paloza Festival that summer."

## songs
**There's No Other Way (1991) - First Major Hit** : This was Blur's first major hit, reaching number 8 in the UK and number 5 on the US Modern Rock chart in mid-1991. It came out during the rave-rock wave and does not represent the sound Blur later became known for, so it is not really Britpop's first hit. "It's tempting to call this song Brit pop's first hit, but that would be incorrect since this is not exactly how blur wound up sounding."
**Bang (1991) - Stalled Follow-Up** : The polyrhythmic follow-up single stalled at number 24 in the UK and went nowhere in the States, which made Blur look like a possible flash in the pan. "it looked like Blur might be a flash in the pan."
**Popscene (1992) - Punky New Direction** : After moving away from the Madchester rhythm, Blur released the punky "Popscene" in 1992. It reached only number 32 in the UK and went nowhere in the US. Critics later named both its sound and its title as formative for what Britpop became. "Critics would later point to Pop scene, both it sound and it's title as formative for what Britpop became."
**For Tomorrow (1993) - Primrose Hill Single** : The lead single from Modern Life Is Rubbish was a Top 30 UK hit about London's famous Primrose Hill. "a top 30 UK hit about London's famed Primrose Hill."
**Chemical World (1993) - US Modern Rock Entry** : The follow-up single from Modern Life Is Rubbish reached the Top 30 of the US Modern Rock chart, peaking at number 27. "managed to crack the top 30 on the US Modern Rock Chart, peaking at number 27."
**Girls & Boys (1994) - Disco Trojan Horse** : Pitchfork described Parklife as launched with a disco song, and this was it. Nothing else on the album sounded like it, yet it carried the LP's cheeky attitude. The in-joke was that it sounded like an Ibiza club song of the kind only a soused British lad or lass would fly to Ibiza to dance to. It was Blur's biggest hit so far: number 5 in the UK, number 4 on the US Modern Rock chart, and number 59 on the Hot 100. "Even on their most danceable hit, Blur were taking the piss."
**Parklife (1994) - Phil Daniels Title Track** : The title track was a UK Top 10 hit and has been called the most British single ever. Actor Phil Daniels, known for playing Londoners in the film Quadrophenia and the TV soap EastEnders, speak-sang the verses. The lyrics are about little more than lazing around London, watching joggers and feeding pigeons in the park. Packed with pub slang, it made no sense outside England, and the British loved it. "A wash in pub slang, The Song would make no sense to anyone outside England."
**Country House (1995) - Chart Battle With Oasis** : "Country House" went head to head with Oasis's "Roll With It" on the UK Official Charts Company chart, with the result announced the week of August 26, 1995. It was called the most epic British chart battle since Beatles vs. Stones or Slade vs. Wizzard. The two bands created a media frenzy by deliberately playing chicken with their release dates. "The two rival bands had ginned up a media frenzy by deliberately playing chicken with their songs and only one hit could wind up atop the heap."

## albums
**Leisure (1991) - Debut Album** : When Blur released their debut in 1991, they did not look like world conquerors. It got respectable reviews from British music magazines such as Q, New Musical Express and Smash Hits, but nobody would have told Blur apart from the Charlatans or Inspiral Carpets. The band later felt dissatisfied with it. "Didn't seem like world conquerors when they issued their debut album, Lesure, in 1991."
**Modern Life Is Rubbish (1993) - Reinvention** : Critics called Blur's second album a reinvention. It rejected baggy and shoegaze styles and openly embraced a Kinks-like Englishness. It was also seen as a response to Suede's rise, which had stirred Damon Albarn's competitive feelings. "an unabashed embrace of a kinks like English style."
**Parklife (1994) - Britpop's Catalyst** : Released in April 1994, the same month Kurt Cobain died, it is arguably the landmark Britpop album. Pitchfork called it "Britpop's catalyst," with "a colorful pop-centric palette of great scope and eclecticism." It entered the UK album chart at number 1, produced four UK hits and went quadruple platinum. It never entered the Billboard 200 in America. It won four statuettes at the 1995 Brit Awards, including Album of the Year. "Park Life never entered the Billboard 200 album chart in America at all."

## curiosities
**Evolving Away From Madchester** : By late 1991 the Madchester sound had gone fully mainstream. Blur lost interest in the fading trend, were unhappy with their debut, and began moving away from the Madchester rhythm. "Blur, disinterested in continuing with this waning trend and dissatisfied with their debut, began evolving away from the Mad Chester rhythm."
**First Canonical Britpop Band on the Charts** : Of the canonical Britpop bands, Blur charted first, but Suede were the band that signaled a real sea change. "among the canonical Britpop bands, Blur hit the charts first."
**Rivalry With Oasis** : The tabloids and music weeklies presented Blur and Oasis as Britpop's main rivalry. Damon Albarn and Noel Gallagher fed reporters snarky jibes. "band leaders Damon Alborn and Noel Gallagher obliging reporters with snarky jibes."
**One Battle, Not the War** : Blur and Oasis each claimed a kind of victory: one band won the chart battle, and the other arguably won the war at home and in the States. "One band won the battle. The other you might say won the war, both at home and in the States."

Oasis lost a plagiarism claim because its verse melody was taken from the folk-era Coca-Cola jingle and New Seekers hit "I'd Like to Teach the World to Sing." "Lost a plagiarism claim for stealing its versimelody from the old Foki era Coca-Cola jingle."
**Cigarettes & Alcohol (1994) - T. Rex Homage** : This decadent, strutting single from fall 1994 reached number 7 in the UK. It was an obvious homage to T. Rex's glam classic "Bang a Gong." "Was an obvious homage to T. Rex's glam classic Bang a Gump."
**Whatever (1994) - Neil Innes Credit** : For their Christmas 1994 single, Oasis had to add Neil Innes to the songwriting credits. Innes's satirical 1973 song "How Sweet to Be an Idiot" was a clear antecedent. Because Innes is best known for playing a faux John Lennon in the Beatles parody band The Rutles, even some of Oasis's Beatles allusions were secondhand. "So even some of Oasis's Beatles illusions were second hand."
**Live Forever (1994) - First UK Top 10** : This soaring anthem was the third single from Definitely Maybe and the band's first UK Top 10 hit. It drove the album's success. It also did well in America, reaching number 2 on the Modern Rock chart in winter 1995 and entering the Top 40 at pop radio. On the Modern Rock chart it ran alongside the Stone Roses' comeback single "Love Spreads." "Live Forever was such an undeniable single. It even did well in America."
**Roll With It (1995) - Chart Battle With Blur** : This single faced Blur's "Country House" in the head-to-head UK chart war, with the result announced the week of August 26, 1995. "A head-to-head singles war between this song, Country House by Blur and this song. Roll with it by Oasis."

## albums
**Definitely Maybe (1994) - Debut Smash** : The debut contained most of the early Oasis hits and was an immediate smash in Britain. Like Parklife, it debuted at number 1, driven mainly by "Live Forever." Elastica's debut later beat its opening sales. "Like Blur's Parklife, definitely maybe debuted at number 1."

## curiosities
**Noel Gallagher, Inspiral Carpets Roadie** : Noel Gallagher toured as a drum tech and roadie for Inspiral Carpets before Oasis. Afterward Noel agreed to join the band Liam Gallagher was already fronting, on condition that Noel write all the material and control the sound. "On the condition that Noll could write all the band's material and take control of their sound."
**Name From a Tour Poster** : The band took the name Oasis from a venue listed on an Inspiral Carpets tour poster. "were renamed Oasis after a venue on an Inspiral Carpets tour poster."
**Brute-Force Commercial Songwriting** : Noel Gallagher wrote brute-force rock songs with stadium-sized hooks and anthemic, stripped-down, heavily distorted playing. Noel's instincts were ruthlessly commercial, and the material worked because it felt instantly familiar, perhaps too familiar. "Noll's instincts were ruthlessly commercial and spot-on."
**The Beatles Running Joke** : The Oasis–Beatles link became a running joke, including a gag in Danny Boyle and Richard Curtis's 2019 film Yesterday: if the Beatles had never existed, neither would Oasis. Noel Gallagher was openly a Beatles fan, and the songs reference "Tomorrow Never Knows," "The Fool on the Hill" and "I Feel Fine." Liam Gallagher's sneering vocals sound less like Lennon or McCartney and more like Johnny Rotten, and Noel borrowed from many sources. "if the Beatles had never existed, neither would Oasis."
**Conquering America, Then Hubris** : For a moment Oasis were the biggest rock band on the American charts, but their hubris eventually undid them on both sides of the Atlantic. "But the hubris of Oasis ultimately did them in on both sides of the Atlantic."

**Suede (1993) - Self-Titled Debut** : The debut arrived in spring 1993 with a cover of two androgynous people kissing whose genders cannot be determined. **Dog Man Star (1994) - Sophomore Album** : Suede returned to the studio at the end of 1993 to consolidate their command of British rock. The album won critical acclaim in 1994 and includes Butler's guitar, but Butler's departure set the band back and left the field open for others to take up Britpop. "his departure set Swade back and essentially cleared the field for others to pick up the emerging Britpop mantle."

## curiosities
**Camp and Chorus-Skating Rock** : Led by the androgynous Brett Anderson, Suede reworked 70s glam and 80s indie, and never had anything to do with Madchester. John Harris, author of Britpop! (also titled The Last Party), wrote that Suede spoke "in the elegant Utreh language about Siderdom." The Anderson–Butler pairing was compared to Morrissey and Johnny Marr in the Smiths, and Suede's elliptical lyrical outlook was uniquely British. "like the Smiths, Swade's elliptical lyrical perspective was uniquely British."
**Band Tensions** : Relations inside Suede deteriorated during the sessions for the second album, and Bernard Butler left soon after. "But relations within the band were deteriorating."

# genre - Britpop

## curiosities
**A Defiantly English Mania** : Blur, Oasis, Elastica and others created a chart craze in the UK that was defiantly English. The movement aimed to bring UK music back to the center of rock after years of American grunge dominance. It borrowed from both British Invasions and gave them an even more British spin. "a mania that was defiantly English."
**More Movement Than Genre** : AllMusic's definition: "Britpop's bands gave the sense that they were creating the soundtrack to the lives of a new generation of British youth." Britpop celebrated British youth culture and musical heritage with little concern about whether that specificity would put off Americans. "Britpop was less a genre than a movement, a sensibility."
**Family Tree of Influences** : Britpop's influences include the Beatles, the Rolling Stones, the Kinks, the Who, David Bowie's glam period, T. Rex's Marc Bolan, the Buzzcocks, the Jam, Squeeze and XTC. All of these acts were UK hits but not US hits. "All were hits in the UK and non-hits in the US."
**Commercial UK Indie** : Britpop was essentially a more commercial version of UK indie descended from the Smiths. "It was a more commercial version of UK Indie."
**Short-Lived Peak in 1995** : In Britain, Britpop was short-lived. 1995 is remembered as its dizzying peak. "In Britain, Britpop was a remarkably short-lived phenomenon."

# genre - Madchester

## curiosities
**Psychedelic Rock Meets Acid House** : Madchester combined psychedelic rock and acid house beats, fueling drug-driven raves in northern England around 1989–1990. It was significant that it grew in Manchester, not London. "the blend of psychedelic rock and acid house dance beats that inspired many a drug-fueled rave."

**Hooks Under Guitar** : A subset of UK indie that buried pop hooks under layers of guitar, pioneered by the Jesus and Mary Chain and perfected by My Bloody Valentine. "a rumbling form of rock that buried pop hooks under layers of guitar."

# curiosity

**The Haçienda** : The Manchester club co-founded by Tony Wilson and New Order was the center of the Madchester scene. "The epicenter of the Madchester scene was the club The Hacienda."
**Billboard Modern Rock Chart (1988)** : The chart launched in 1988 and became a refuge for British bands that never reached the US pop Top 40. "Billboard's Modern Rock Chart, which had launched in 1988."
**Blur vs. Oasis Chart Battle (August 1995)** : This was the most epic British chart battle since Beatles vs. Stones or Slade vs. Wizzard, with the result announced the week of August 26, 1995. "the most epic British chart battle since Beatles vs. Stones or Slade vs. Wizard."
**Second British Invasion Acts** : The Human League, Duran Duran, Eurythmics, Culture Club, Wham! and Tears for Fears all scored big Hot 100 hits in the 80s. "All scored big hits on the Hot 100."
**Melody Maker Hype** : The UK music press, including Q, NME, Smash Hits and Melody Maker, drove Britpop hype. Melody Maker put Suede on its cover before their first single came out. "England's hyperactive music magazines like Q, new musical express, and smash hits."
