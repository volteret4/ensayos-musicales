## albums
**Faith (1987) - Debut Solo Album** : Released in October 1987 after "I Want Your Sex" served as an attention-getting teaser, Faith dominated the charts for the next 15 months. It spun off five more top five singles, including four number ones, and according to Billboard it was the top album of 1988, while its title song was the top single of the year. It also topped Billboard's R&B chart and produced a string of crossover hits on both the Hot 100 and the R&B chart. It made George Michael the biggest solo male music star of the late 1980s, ahead of even Michael Jackson, whose 1987 Thriller follow-up Bad sold millions fewer copies in the United States than Faith did. "Faith was the title song of George Michael's debut solo album."
**Listen Without Prejudice Vol. 1 (1990) - Withdrawal From the Spotlight** : Released in late 1990, three years after Faith. Michael's face was not on the cover, he appeared in no videos and did no interviews. Its first single was the mournful dirge "Praying for Time," followed by "Freedom 90." The album became the subject of Michael's war with Sony Music. "Michael's face was not on the cover of the album."
**1996 Album - First Post-Sony Album** : After his split with Sony Music, Michael did not release another album until 1996, six years after Listen Without Prejudice. Its first two singles, the torch ballad "Jesus to a Child" and the uptempo dance jam "Fastlove," were number one hits in England and top ten hits in America. "After a split with Sony Music, he didn't release another album until 1996, six years after listen without prejudice."
**Patience (2004) - Final Studio Album** : Released in 2004, it turned out to be George Michael's last studio album. Its release was the occasion for his BBC interview with veteran interviewer Michael Parkinson. "George Michael recorded that BBC interview in 2004 on the release of what would turn out to be his last studio album, Patience."

## songs
**Careless Whisper (1985) - Breakthrough Ballad** : A conversational soft rock ballad that made pop fans around the world take the young singer-songwriter more seriously, serving the same career purpose that "Your Song" had for Elton John. It topped the Hot 100 for three weeks in February 1985 and was later named Billboard's Top Single of the Year. It even reached number eight on Billboard's R&B chart, notable since the prior single "Wake Me Up Before You Go-Go" had no traction on Black radio. "Carolis Whisper was, in essence, George Michael's your song."
**A Different Corner (1986) - First Truly Solo Hit** : Included on the final Wham! album, it was Michael's first truly solo top ten hit in America and a chart-topper in the UK. Michael wrote, produced, arranged and performed it entirely by himself, and it was very obviously personal. Critic Alfred Soto, commemorating Michael after his death, wrote that it admits falling in love is terrifying and that "Straight men don't sing and write songs like a different corner." In a 2009 interview Michael explained the moroseness of songs like this by saying his depression at the end of Wham! came from realizing he was gay, not bisexual. "In 1986's A Different Corner, George Michael admits that falling in love is terrifying."
**I Want Your Sex (1987) - Most Controversial Hit of 1987** : Issued first as a single from the summer 1987 blockbuster Beverly Hills Cop II, it peaked at number 2 on the Hot 100 and would have gone all the way if more radio stations had played it. It was banned by the BBC in England and in America was either left off playlists or played only in a censored "I Want Your Love" version. Michael insisted it was about monogamy; in the video he appeared with then-girlfriend Kathy Jeung in various states of undress, with "explore monogamy" written in lipstick on her back. For several weeks it was the biggest selling single in America and went platinum, highly unusual for the US singles market at the time. "I want your sex was arguably the most controversial hit single of 1987."
**Faith (1987) - Rockabilly Title Track** : The rockabilly-flavored title track that kicked off the album's run of number one hits. According to Billboard it was the top single of 1988. "Faith's run of number one hits kicked off with its rockabilly flavored title track."
**Father Figure (1988) - Gospel-Inflected Torch Ballad** : The first of a pair of torch ballads from Faith that crossed over to both the Hot 100 and the R&B chart, described as sultry and gospel-inflected. "First, the sultry gospel-inflicted father figure."
**One More Try (1988) - Pop and R&B Number One** : A soulful, organ-drenched lament that quickly followed "Father Figure" and hit number one on both the pop chart and the R&B chart. "the soulful organ-drenched lament, One More Try, which actually hit number one on both the pop chart and the R&B chart."
**Monkey (1988) - Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis Remix** : An up-tempo chart-topper that topped the Hot 100 in the summer of 1988, holding the top spot the week ending August 27, 1988, and keeping Elton John's "I Don't Wanna Go On with You Like That" at number two. In its original incarnation on Faith it was a more ethereal, skeletal pop-funk track and required a remix to be worthy of single release. Producers Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis, former Prince associates fresh off Janet Jackson's Control, completely rearranged it, pumping up the intro and making it a hot summer hit. It was one of Michael's least likely hits, chosen when his label went deeper into the album to find more singles. "In its original incarnation on the Faith album, Monkey was a more ethereal skeletal pop-funk track."
**Praying for Time (1990) - Mournful Lead Single** : The first single from Listen Without Prejudice Vol. 1, described as a beautiful but mournful dirge. "its first single was a beautiful but mournful dirge titled, Praying for Time."
**Freedom 90 (1990) - Farewell to the Trappings of Fame** : A joyous autobiographical dance track on which Michael said goodbye to the trappings of fame, as Elton had on the cover of Reg Strikes Back. He did not appear in the music video and asked director David Fincher to set fire to his famous Faith guitar, jukebox and leather jacket. "asking director David Fincher to set fire to his famous faith guitar, jukebox and leather jacket."
**Don't Let the Sun Go Down on Me (1991) - Live Duet With Elton John** : Recorded at the final show of the Cover to Cover tour at Wembley Arena in London on March 23, 1991, where Michael brought out Elton John as a surprise guest, introduced him triumphantly and, unlike at Live Aid, let Elton sing a verse. Elton didn't think it would be a big hit, and Sony initially balked because it wasn't tied to a live album. Released just before Christmas 1991, it reached number one on the Hot 100 at the end of January 1992, Elton's first credited number one since his Kiki Dee duet nearly 16 years earlier, and the last time George Michael topped the Hot 100. "George Michael's instincts about the live version of Sun proved right on."
**Jesus to a Child (1996) - Memorial for Anselmo Feleppa** : A torch ballad and the first single from Michael's 1996 album, a number one in England and a top ten hit in America. Michael wrote it as a memorial for his former lover Anselmo Feleppa, who had died in 1993. "Jesus To a Child was particularly affecting, written by Michael as a memorial for Ensalmo Philippa."
**Fastlove (1996) - Uptempo Dance Jam** : The second single from the 1996 album, a number one in England and a top ten hit in America. "The Uptempo Dance Jam, Fast Love, were number one hits in England and top ten hits in America."
**Outside (1998) - Response to the Beverly Hills Arrest** : Recorded after Michael was outed by a 1998 police sting, it contained lyrical references to al fresco lovemaking. Its music video featured Michael dancing in a men's loo in a police uniform. "a music video featuring Michael dancing in a men's Lou in a police uniform."
**White Light (2012) - Final UK Top 40 Hit** : Michael's final British top 40 hit, bringing him into the era of electronic dance music. Its lyrics depicted a man in middle age refusing to quit. "I'm not through, George says. I'm alive."

## curiosities
**Birth Name and Origins** : George Michael was born Georgios Kyriacos Panayiotou in North London, half-British and half-Greek, and first topped the UK charts at age 20 with Wham! in the summer of 1983. "born Georgios, Caracos, Panayatu, who first topped the UK charts at age 20 with his duo WAM in the summer of 1983."
**Imperial Year 1988** : In 1988 Michael reigned over the charts; anyone listening to American Top 40 radio that year was probably listening to him. "for George Michael, 1988 was what pop critics call an Imperial year."
**Successor to Elton John** : He was to the 1980s what Elton John had been to the 1970s, the premier male pop vocalist of his generation, with deep appreciation for American Black music including R&B and early hip hop. "George Michael was, to the 80s, what Elton John had been to the 70s, the premier male pop vocalist of his generation."
**Straight Presentation at Launch of Solo Career** : In the mid-'80s, at the height of the AIDS crisis, Michael was still dating women and feared revealing his sexuality amid stardom would be hard on his family, so in 1987 he doubled down on a straight presentation. "he doubled down on his straight presentation."
**Battle With Sony Music - "A Piece of Software"** : Sony acquired Michael's contract when it bought CBS Records in 1987. Michael accused Sony of not promoting him properly and, in an eventual court case, claimed the conglomerate was treating him like "a piece of software," more than a decade before iTunes and Spotify. "Michael claimed the conglomerate was treating him like, quote, a piece of software."
**Anselmo Feleppa - Private Grief** : Michael's lover Anselmo Feleppa was diagnosed HIV positive in 1991 and died two years later; still not public about his sexuality, Michael grieved privately. "Still not public about his sexuality, Michael grieved privately."
**Cover to Cover Tour (1991)** : Not a promotional tour for Listen Without Prejudice, which he and Sony were warring over, but a tour built around covers of other artists' songs, from Stevie Wonder's "Superstition" to the Doobie Brothers' "What a Fool Believes," plus Elton John's "Don't Let the Sun Go Down on Me." "Michael was structuring the tour around covers of other artist's songs."
**1998 Beverly Hills Police Sting - Outed** : In 1998 a police sting in Beverly Hills, California snared Michael for solicitation in a public park men's room, outing him as a gay man under unflattering circumstances. He leaned into the tabloid exposure, laughing off the revelation in interviews and recording "Outside." "Michael was finally outed as a gay man, under less than flattering circumstances."
**2004 Michael Parkinson BBC Interview** : Michael sounded bemused and relieved to be open, joking that it was "probably the most glamorous toilet in the world" and acknowledging his subconscious led him into the situation. He also noted his US career had cooled several years before the incident. "I think my subconscious definitely led me into that situation."
**Later Career** : He recorded sporadically in his final 16 years, never scored another Hot 100 hit after the mid-'90s, but had a handful of modest UK hits in the 2000s, a couple reaching the top five, while living just outside London as a treasured British pop veteran. "In America, Michael never scored another hot 100 hit after the mid-90s."
**Feud and Reconciliation With Elton John** : In the early 2000s Elton publicly admonished Michael for his voluminous marijuana use; Michael fired back that his elder should mind his own business, and Fleet Street chronicled the sniping for years. In the early 2010s they reconciled as Michael played Elton's AIDS charity concert. "John and Michael reconciled, as George played Elton's AIDS charity concert."
**Death on Christmas Day 2016** : Michael was found dead of heart failure by his boyfriend Fadi Fawaz on Christmas Day 2016, a day when radio in America and England was already power-rotating "Last Christmas." Tributes came from Madonna, Boy George and Adele, who performed one of his songs at the following February's Grammy Awards. "Michael died on a day when radio stations in both America and England were already power rotating WAM's 1984 classic Last Christmas."

## albums
**Fantastic (1983) - Debut Album** : Wham!'s debut album topped the UK charts but made little impression in America. "WAM's debut album, Fantastic, topped the UK charts but made little impression in America."
**Make It Big (1985) - US Breakthrough Album** : One month after "Careless Whisper" topped the Hot 100, Make It Big began a three-week run atop the album chart and was quadruple platinum by year's end. "It would be quadruple platinum by year's end."
**Final Wham! Album (1986) - Farewell Victory Lap** : Wham!'s final album, issued late 1985 with a string of singles into 1986 before the duo amicably broke up. It contained George Michael's solo single "A Different Corner." "The final WAM album contained another George Michael solo single, a different corner."

## songs
**Young Guns (1982) - First UK Top Ten** : The first of four straight British top tens for Wham!, featuring George Michael rapping in the manner of Melle Mel, barely three years after rap broke through with "Rapper's Delight," at a time when British musicians favored New Romantic acts like Spandau Ballet and A Flock of Seagulls. "Young guns was the first of four straight British top tens for WAM."
**Wham Rap (1983) - Heavier Rapping** : The follow-up to "Young Guns," which made even heavier use of Michael's rapping skills. "the follow-up WAM rap, which made even heavier use of Michael's rapping skills."
**Wake Me Up Before You Go-Go (1984) - American Breakthrough** : Written by Michael, it broke Wham! in America in fall 1984, making nostalgic reference to early rock and soul and an incongruous reference to the 1930s dance the jitterbug, much like Elton John's "Crocodile Rock." Its exuberant video featured Michael and Ridgeley in white "Choose Life" T-shirts, neon gloves and short shorts, making Wham! a teenybopper sensation in the year of peak Duran Duran. "Wake me up before you go go, made stars of WAM in America."
**Last Christmas (1984) - Holiday Standard** : Released in England the same week "Go-Go" spent its final week atop the Hot 100. It is one of very few songs from the last two decades of the 20th century in the holiday canon, alongside Mariah Carey's "All I Want for Christmas Is You," and one of the best-selling British singles of all time, certified for nearly two million UK sales. It has been covered by dozens of artists; it wasn't a US hit in 1984 because it would have confused the market, but it now dominates US radio every December. "Last Christmas is one of the best-selling British singles of all time, certified for sales of nearly two million copies in the UK alone."
**Everything She Wants (1985) - Third Straight Hot 100 Number One** : An R&B-flavored mid-tempo jam that hit number one by May 1985, Wham!'s third straight Hot 100 chart-topper and a number 12 R&B hit. On it Michael lamented an imagined acquisitive, pregnant wife, paralleling Elton's "Someone Saved My Life Tonight." "everything she wants, which was WAM's third straight number one on the Hot 100."

## curiosities
**School Friends Formed as Teenagers** : Michael formed Wham! with secondary school friend Andrew Ridgeley while still teenagers; Ridgeley shared some co-writing, played some guitar and served as moral support, but Michael was lead composer, producer, main singer and instrumentalist. "George Michael, for most purposes, was WAM."
**The Exclamation Point** : The group's name came complete with an exclamation point, and older pop fans thought they might be flashes in the pan. "whose group title came complete with an exclamation point."
**First Western Pop Group Invited to China** : Wham! were invited to perform in China, the first Western pop group so honored by the People's Republic. "the first Western pop group so honored by the people's republic."
**Amicable Breakup (1986)** : After a final victory lap of an album and singles into 1986, the duo amicably broke up. "before amicably breaking up as a duo."

## albums
**Honky Château (1972) - First Number One Album** : Reached the top of the Billboard album chart the same week in July 1972 that "Rocket Man" reached number six, the first of seven consecutive number one albums — still the longest streak by anyone except the Beatles. It produced top ten hits "Rocket Man" and "Honky Cat." "It would become Elton John's first of seven consecutive number one albums."
**Don't Shoot Me I'm Only the Piano Player (1973) - Second Consecutive Number One** : His second consecutive number one album, arriving within half a year of Honky Château alongside his first US number one single, "Crocodile Rock." "his second consecutive number one album, with Don't Shoot Me I'm Only the Piano Player."
**Goodbye Yellow Brick Road (1973) - Double LP** : A chart-topping double LP that yielded "Saturday Night's Alright for Fighting," the title track, and its biggest hit "Bennie and the Jets." It also contained the original "Candle in the Wind." "From his next chart topping album, the double LP, Goodby Yellow Brick Road, John scored a string of smash hits."
**Greatest Hits (1974) - Number One Into 1975** : His late-1974 greatest hits album was atop the album chart as 1975 began. "the latter with his late 74 greatest hits album."
**Captain Fantastic and the Brown Dirt Cowboy (1975) - First Album to Debut at Number One** : Released in May 1975 and hailed as a magnum opus, the confessional, soul-bearing, elaborately packaged album was the first in Billboard history to debut at number one — something no album by the Beatles, Rolling Stones or Led Zeppelin had achieved. It spent seven weeks on top and included "Someone Saved My Life Tonight." "It debuted on top, the first album in Billboard chart history ever to start its life at number one."
**Rock of the Westies (1975) - Second Album to Debut at Number One** : Less than four months after Captain Fantastic's run ended, it also debuted at number one, the second album in chart history to do so. It looked rushed: the cover was a close-up of Elton's face, unshaven. Its lead single "Island Girl" topped the Hot 100 simultaneously. "he hadn't even shaved for the occasion."
**Blue Moves (1976) - First Studio Album to Miss Number One Since 1971** : Released in October 1976, just weeks after the Rolling Stone interview, it peaked at number three and spent only five months on the chart, less than any prior Elton album. Its only major single was "Sorry Seems to Be the Hardest Word." "it became John's first new studio album to miss the number one spot since 1971."
**Ice on Fire (1985) - Album With George Michael Lead Single** : Elton brought in George Michael for its lead single "Wrap Her Up" after struggling to generate radio hits post-"Sad Songs." "so he brought in George Michael for the lead single of his next album, Ice On Fire."
**Reg Strikes Back (1988) - Comeback Album** : Following the worst-selling album of his career, Elton wanted back in the game. The self-deprecating title and cover — a photo of piles of flamboyant costumes he was auctioning for charity — signaled the comeback. It produced "I Don't Wanna Go On with You Like That." "The album's self-deprecating title was Reg Strikes Back."
**The Lion King Soundtrack (1994) - Disney Collaboration With Tim Rice** : Written with lyricist Tim Rice, it topped the Billboard album chart for most of summer 1994, giving Elton partial claim to a number one album for the first time since Rock of the Westies in 1975. "The soundtrack topped the Billboard album chart for most of the summer of 1994."

## songs
**Your Song (1970) - First Major Hit** : The first major pop hit for Elton and lyricist Bernie Taupin, with almost conversational lyrics. It debuted at number 38 on American Top 40 in December 1970 and reached number eight by January 1971. "your song, which was the first major pop hit not only for its performer, but also its co-writer, a lyricist named Bernie Toppen."
**Border Song (1970) - Aretha Franklin Cover** : Elton's gospel-flavored single, remade by Aretha Franklin with the subtitle "Holy Moses," debuted at number 37 the same week "Your Song" debuted one notch lower, briefly making it the highest-charting Elton composition. It reached number five on Hot Soul Singles, making Elton's first US top ten as a songwriter an R&B hit. "Elton John's first top 10 hit in America as a songwriter was on the R&B chart."
**Tiny Dancer (1972) - Missed the Top 40** : Now one of Elton's most beloved songs, it peaked at number 41 in early 1972. "Tiny Dancer, peaked at number 41 in early 1972, actually missing the top 40."
**Rocket Man (1972) - Return to the Top Ten** : Reached number six in July 1972, the same week Honky Château hit number one; described with "Bowie-esque alien folk rock." "The same week in July 1972 when Rocket Man reached number six."
**Honky Cat (1972) - Honky Château Top Ten** : A top ten smash from Honky Château. "Rocket Man and Honki Cat, the hits for John started coming faster."
**Crocodile Rock (1973) - First US Number One Single** : A pastiche of '50s rock and roll that introduced flamboyant Elton, with lyrics referencing "the record machine" and "rockin' round the clock." Elton and Taupin were ahead of the '70s nostalgia wave — Grease was just launching on Broadway, months before American Graffiti and years before Happy Days or The Rocky Horror Picture Show. "Its very first lyric, I remember when Rock was young."
**Saturday Night's Alright for Fighting (1973) - Pub Rock Smash** : A hard-driving pub rock number from Goodbye Yellow Brick Road, later covered by The Who. "a hard-driving pub rock number who would later cover Saturday nights all right for fighting."
**Goodbye Yellow Brick Road (1973) - Title Track** : A dreamy ballad wedding Wizard of Oz imagery of Dorothy with Beach Boys-style harmonies. "A dreamy ballet that wedded Dorothy from Wizard of Oz imagery with gorgeous Beach Boys style harmonies."
**Bennie and the Jets (1974) - Broke on Black Radio in Detroit** : An album cut Elton resisted releasing as a single until it broke on Black radio in Detroit. A blend of strutting R&B and glam rock with fake crowd noise and arena echo, its Taupin lyric is a fan letter to an invented band fronted by a woman in electric boots and a mohair suit. It topped the Hot 100 and reached number 15 on Hot Soul Singles, earning Elton a Soul Train invitation a year later. "It was a blend of strutting R&B and glam rock, plus fake crowd noises and arena echo that imitated a live concert."
**Don't Let the Sun Go Down on Me (1974) - Shared Song With George Michael** : Peaked at number two in 1974. Elton performed it with George Michael at Live Aid in 1985, ceding the lead vocal; at the start of the second verse Elton appears ready to sing but George keeps going. The 1991 live duet reached number one in January 1992, and Elton now displays George's photo while performing it at Caesars Palace, his delivery channeling George's phrasing. "John ceded the spotlight to Michael, letting him take the lead vocal alone."
**Philadelphia Freedom (1975) - Tribute to Philadelphia Soul and Billie Jean King** : Premiered on Soul Train for host Don Cornelius, it was a tribute to the music of Philadelphia and to Billie Jean King of the Philadelphia Freedoms tennis team. It topped the Hot 100 by April 1975 and scraped the top 40 on the R&B chart. "It's a tribute to the music of Philadelphia from myself and the band, and also to a lady called Billie Jean King."
**Lucy in the Sky with Diamonds (1975) - Beatles Cover** : His cover of the Beatles song was atop the singles chart as 1975 began. "the former with his cover of the Beatles song, Lucy and the Sky with Diamonds."
**Someone Saved My Life Tonight (1975) - Escaping a Marriage** : From Captain Fantastic, Taupin's lyric was a thinly veiled story of Elton nearly marrying a woman in the 1960s before his fame. It fed speculation about his sexuality, though there was remarkably little media attention. "Bernie Toppins lyric was a thinly veiled story about his dear friend Elton, who nearly married a woman in the 1960s prior to his fame."
**Island Girl (1975) - Replaced Himself at Number One** : The laid-back lead single from Rock of the Westies; when it topped the Hot 100 it knocked out Neil Sedaka's "Bad Blood," issued on Elton's Rocket Records with Elton on backing vocals. "John had, in effect, replaced himself on top of the Hot 100."
**Don't Go Breaking My Heart (1976) - Duet With Kiki Dee** : A cheeky male-female duet that topped the charts in summer 1976 and was Elton's first ever British number one. It remained his last US or UK number one until 1992. "The single, Don't Go Breaking My Heart, even topped the charts in Elton's home country, his first ever British number one."
**Sorry Seems to Be the Hardest Word (1976) - Last Top Ten for Three Years** : The only major single from Blue Moves, it reached number six and was his last top ten hit for nearly three years. "It would be John's last top ten hit for nearly three years."
**I'm Still Standing (1983) - Number 12 Hit** : "These included the 1983 number 12 hit, I'm still standing."
**I Guess That's Why They Call It the Blues (1984) - Number Four Hit** : "The early 1984 number four hit, I guess that's why they call it the blues."
**Sad Songs (Say So Much) (1984) - Number Five Hit** : After this, Elton found it difficult to generate radio hits. "And the summer 1984 number five hit, Sad Songs, say so much."
**Wrap Her Up (1985) - Duet With George Michael** : Lead single of Ice On Fire, prominently featuring George Michael, on which the two run down a list of famous female sex symbols they'd like to take home. "the two men run down a list of all the famous female sex symbols they'd like to take home with them."
**That's What Friends Are For (1986) - AIDS Charity Record** : Elton sang on Dionne Warwick's 1986 number one AIDS charity record, credited to Dionne and Friends. "The chart topper had been credited to Dion and friends."
**I Don't Wanna Go On with You Like That (1988) - Blocked by Monkey** : His comeback hit from Reg Strikes Back, peaking at number two behind George Michael's "Monkey" the week ending August 27, 1988 — still his biggest hit in a dozen years. "But it wasn't the number one he craved."
**Can You Feel the Love Tonight (1994) - Oscar Winner** : From The Lion King; Elton and Tim Rice accepted the Best Original Song Oscar at the 67th Academy Awards in 1995. "John and Rice accepted the best original song Oscar for the sentimental Can You Feel The Love Tonight."
**Candle in the Wind 1997 (1997) - Tribute to Princess Diana** : Inspired by Diana's August 1997 death, Taupin rewrote the 1973 Marilyn Monroe tribute ("Goodbye Norma Jean" became "Goodbye England's Rose"), with orchestration by George Martin. Elton performed it at the funeral for the only time. It debuted at number one in the US and UK, was certified diamond in America, surpassed Band Aid as the UK's best-selling single, and with 30 million sales ranks second only to Bing Crosby's "White Christmas" per Guinness. The original was a modest UK hit never issued as a US single; only a 1987 Australian live version had charted. "Candle 1997 sold better than any single in modern pop history."
**This Train Don't Stop There Anymore (2002) - Homage to His Imperial Period** : An homage to his '70s imperial period and the loneliness at the top. The video depicts a 1975-era Elton, played by Justin Timberlake in a loud striped jacket and feather boa, sad and trapped backstage. "was an homage to his 70s imperial period and the loneliness he felt at the top."

## curiosities
**Born Reginald Dwight in Middlesex** : Born Reginald Dwight in Middlesex, England, he was initially more popular in the US than the UK — a half dozen US number ones and 15 top tens by 1976, nearly twice his UK count. He didn't score a UK number one until the mid-'70s and landed most of his British chart-toppers in the 1990s. "the man-born, regional Dwight in Middlesex, England, was even more popular here in the US at first than he was in his home country."
**Love of American R&B** : His genuine absorption of American R&B was key to his US success. "what also made Elton John a success here was his genuine love and absorption of American R&B."
**Flamboyant Image** : Around "Crocodile Rock," his jackets sported stripes and wider lapels and his platform shoes grew higher. "The platform shoes grew higher."
**1975 - Most Imperial Year** : Three number one singles and three chart-topping albums, two of which debuted at number one. "He had three number one singles and three chart-topping albums."
**1987 Interview - "Fruitful"** : In a 1987 British interview, Elton described his imperial period and its abrupt 1976 end, joking about being "fruitful and like a fruit." "Did you catch Elton's self-deprecating joke to the interviewer about fruitful and like a fruit?"
**1976 Rolling Stone Interview With Cliff Jahr** : In fall 1976, openly gay freelancer Cliff Jahr became the first interviewer to ask directly about his sexuality; Elton insisted on staying on the record, saying "I think everybody's bisexual to a certain degree." Walter Cronkite covered it on the evening news. "Walter Cronkite covered it on the evening news."
**Post-Interview Chart Collapse** : Within weeks he went from chart-dominating demigod to a second-tier act. A Provo, Utah reader's letter in Rolling Stone called him a "moral midget." The backlash's swiftness was compared to the Dixie Chicks' 2003 fall-off. "Elton John in the space of a few weeks in 1976 went from the premier, chart dominating Demigod to a modest second-tier pop-act."
**Cautionary Tale for Gay Pop Stars** : His 1976 experience made an impression on Freddie Mercury, who put himself back in the closet during Queen, and David Bowie, who all but renounced his bisexuality. "his 1976 experience was the cautionary tale for gay pop stars considering coming out."
**Mentor to George Michael** : By mid-1985 he praised George publicly, contrasting his own youth. "It's quite frightening at 21, I was a hopeless mess."
**Marriage to Renate Blauel** : In the mid-'80s he married his friend and studio collaborator Renate Blauel; they divorced less than four years later. "a companionship that ended in divorce less than four years later."
**Coming Out in 1988 and Stronger Voice** : Around Reg Strikes Back, having divorced, he told the press he was comfortable being gay; after treatment before recording he came back with his voice stronger. "Elton told the press he was comfortable being gay."
**Knighthood and David Furnish** : Knighted by Queen Elizabeth II in 1998, he settled with Canadian filmmaker David Furnish, whom he married after civil partnerships and same-sex marriage were legalized in England. "He was knighted by Queen Elizabeth II in 1998."
**Teetotaler Warning Flares** : A recovered addict and vigilant teetotaler, he used the press to warn friends, admonishing George Michael for marijuana use and similarly Billy Joel. "He did similarly with Tormate Billy Joel in this period."
**Rocket Hour Tribute to George Michael** : He dedicated an episode of his Beats 1 show Rocket Hour to Michael. "the kindest, most generous man, and one of the most brilliant songwriters England has ever produced."

# curiosity

**Week Ending August 27, 1988** : "Monkey" and "I Don't Wanna Go On with You Like That" held the top two Hot 100 positions. "held down the top two positions on the Hot 100."
**Imperial Period** : A term for a phase when an artist reigns over the charts and can do no wrong; examples include Elvis Presley, Michael Jackson, Justin Bieber, Prince (late '80s) and Lady Gaga (mid-2010s). "One where he rained over the charts and, essentially, could do no wrong."
**Billboard Chart Methodology Before SoundScan** : Before Nielsen SoundScan computerization, Billboard phoned or faxed retailers, so albums typically debuted mid-chart. "it has become routine for albums to debut at number one."
**Best-Selling Singles** : Candle in the Wind 1997 surpassed Band Aid's "Do They Know It's Christmas"; only Bing Crosby's "White Christmas" has sold more. "only white Christmas by Bing Crosby has sold more copies."
**Changing Attitudes Toward Homosexuality** : By the mid-2010s both the US and UK legalized gay marriage, reflected in the music business by Adam Lambert's 2012 number one. "public attitudes toward homosexuality in both the US and UK were undergoing a remarkable metamorphosis."
