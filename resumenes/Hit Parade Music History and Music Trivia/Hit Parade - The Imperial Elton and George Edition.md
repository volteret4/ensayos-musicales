## albums
**Faith (1987) - Debut Solo Album** : George Michael's solo debut album was released in October 1987 and went on to dominate the charts for 15 months, spinning off five top five singles, including four number ones. It made him the biggest solo male music star of the late 1980s, outselling Michael Jackson's *Bad* album in the United States. The album's singles were crossover successes on both the Hot 100 and R&B charts. "Michael's solo debut album, which landed in October 1987 and went on to dominate the charts for the next 15 months."
**Listen Without Prejudice Vol. 1 (1990) - Follow-up to Faith** : This album arrived in late 1990, three years after the release of *Faith*. George Michael chose not to appear on the album's cover, in its music videos, or to do any interviews to promote it, signaling a withdrawal from the spotlight and the trappings of fame. "Michael's face was not on the cover of the album. He appeared in no videos and did no interviews."

## songs
**Careless Whisper (1985)** : This song served as George Michael's equivalent of Elton John's "Your Song," acting as a conversational soft rock ballad that garnered the young singer-songwriter serious critical attention worldwide. It became a US pop smash, topping the Hot 100 for three weeks in February 1985, and was named Billboard's Top Single of the Year. It also impressively reached number eight on Billboard's R&B chart, especially notable given that WAM's previous single, "Wake Me Up Before You Go-Go," had not gained traction on Black radio. "Carolis Whisper was, in essence, George Michael's your song."
**A Different Corner (1986)** : This was a solo single by George Michael featured on the final WAM album, marking his first truly solo top ten hit in America and a chart-topper in the UK. The song was particularly notable for being entirely written, produced, arranged, and performed by Michael himself, and for its deeply personal lyrics, hinting at his then-unrevealed sexuality. Critic Alfred Soto described it as reflecting the experience of navigating a queer identity in pop music, with its terrifying admission of falling in love. "In 1986's A Different Corner, George Michael admits that falling in love is terrifying."
**I Want Your Sex (1987)** : Arguably the most controversial hit single of 1987, George Michael insisted the song was about monogamy. Its music video featured him with then-girlfriend Katie Jung in various states of undress, writing "explore monogamy" on her back in lipstick. Released initially as a single from the 1987 film *Beverly Hills Cop 2*, it peaked at number two on the Hot 100 and likely would have reached number one if more radio stations had been willing to play it. The song was banned by the BBC in England and either removed from American radio playlists or played only in a censored "I Want Your Love" version, yet it became the biggest-selling single in America for several weeks, achieving platinum status, which was rare for US singles at the time. "I want your sex was arguably the most controversial hit single of 1987."
**Faith (1987)** : This rockabilly-flavored title track kicked off the run of number one hits from George Michael's debut solo album, *Faith*. The song contributed to the album's dominance on the charts for 15 months and its success as a crossover smash on both the Hot 100 and R&B charts. "Faith's run of number one hits kicked off with its rockabilly flavored title track."
**Father Figure (1988)** : A sultry, gospel-inflected ballad, "Father Figure" was one of the crossover smash singles from George Michael's *Faith* album. It contributed to the album's success on both the Hot 100 and R&B charts. "First, the sultry gospel-inflicted father figure."
**One More Try (1988)** : This soulful, organ-drenched lament was another crossover smash from George Michael's *Faith* album, achieving number one status on both the pop chart and the R&B chart. Its success highlighted Michael's dominance in the summer of 1988. "And it was quickly followed by the soulful organ-drenched lament, One More Try, which actually hit number one on both the pop chart and the R&B chart."
**Monkey (1988)** : One of George Michael's least likely, and thus most "imperial," hits, "Monkey" topped Billboard's Hot 100 in the summer of 1988. In its original form on the *Faith* album, it was an ethereal, skeletal pop-funk track but required a remix by super producer duo Jimmy Jam and Terry Lewis for single release. They completely rearranged the track, pumping up the intro and turning it into a hot summer hit after it gained traction on Black radio, much like Elton John's "Benny and the Jets." "Monkey is perhaps less well-remembered among George Michael's big hits, but it did top Billboard's Hot 100 in the summer of 1988."
**Praying for Time (1990)** : This beautiful but mournful dirge was the first single released from George Michael's 1990 album, *Listen Without Prejudice Vol. 1*. The song's introspective tone aligned with Michael's decision to step back from public promotion, with no videos or interviews for the album. "And its first single was a beautiful but mournful dirge titled, Praying for Time."
**Freedom! '90 (1990)** : A joyous, autobiographical dance track, "Freedom! '90" was released as a single from *Listen Without Prejudice Vol. 1*. In its music video, directed by David Fincher, George Michael explicitly renounced the trappings of his *Faith*-era fame, symbolically having his famous *Faith* guitar, jukebox, and leather jacket set on fire. "He said goodbye to the trappings of fame, leaving the music video to a possible end. And asking director David Fincher to set fire to his famous faith guitar, jukebox and leather jacket."

## curiosities
**Birth Name and Heritage** : George Michael's birth name was Georgios Kyriacos Panayiotou, and he was described as "half-breddish, half-greek." "half-breddish, half-greek man, born Georgios, Caracos, Panayatu."
**Musical Versatility and Influences** : George Michael was an enormous fan and assimilator of American music, especially African American music, including R&B and the then-new genre of Hip Hop. He was noted for his facility in and appreciation for a range of popular song forms. "George Michael was an enormous fan and a simulator of American music, particularly African American music, including not just R&B, but the then-new genre Hip Hop."
**Rapping Skills in Early WAM** : George Michael showcased his rapping skills in early WAM hits like "Young Guns" and "WAM Rap," a bold move in the early 1980s when many leading British musicians were focused on New Romantic styles. This happened barely three years after "Rapper's Delight" had broken through on the charts. "Michael was spitting over a beat, barely three years after rap had broken through on the charts with rapper's delight."
**Exuberant Video Style** : The video for "Wake Me Up Before You Go-Go" made WAM stars in America, featuring Michael and Andrew Ridgley in distinctive white "choose life" t-shirts, neon gloves, and short shorts, reflecting their understanding of the power of a sassy ensemble. This visual style made WAM a teeny-bopper sensation during the peak of Duran Duran's popularity. "Thanks in part to its exuberant video in which Michael and Ridgley, co-ordered in white, choose life t-shirts, neon gloves and short shorts."
**China Tour with WAM (1985)** : In a historic event, George Michael and Andrew Ridgley, as WAM, were invited to perform in China, becoming the first Western pop group ever to be honored by the People's Republic. This tour marked a significant cultural milestone in their final year as a duo. "Michael and Ridgley were invited to perform in China, the first Western pop group so honored by the people's republic."
**Concealed Sexuality in the Mid-80s** : In the mid-1980s, at the height of the AIDS crisis, George Michael actively maintained a straight-identified public image, even though he was dating women and beginning to realize he was gay, not bisexual. In a 2009 interview, he explained that his depression at the end of WAM was due to this realization, as he feared the impact on his family if he revealed his sexuality amid his stardom. "my depression at the end of WAM was because I was beginning to realize I was gay, not bisexual."
**Marketing Controversy for "I Want Your Sex"** : George Michael's 1987 single "I Want Your Sex" faced widespread censorship and bans, including by the BBC, and was often removed from American radio playlists or played only in a censored "I Want Your Love" version. Despite Michael's insistence that the song was about monogamy, its provocative title and video content led to significant controversy, yet it became the biggest-selling single in America, going platinum. "I want your sex was either left off American radio stations playlists, or played only in a censored, I want your love version."
**Label Dispute with Sony Music** : Following the release of *Listen Without Prejudice Vol. 1*, George Michael became embroiled in a legal battle with his label, Sony Music (which had acquired his contract after buying CBS Records in 1987). Michael accused Sony of improperly promoting him and claimed the conglomerate was treating him like "a piece of software." "Michael accused Sony, which had acquired his contract when it bought CBS records in 1987, of not promoting him properly."

## albums
**Fantastic (1983) - Debut Album** : WAM's debut album topped the UK charts but achieved little recognition in America. To break into the US market, the duo would need a slicker approach. "WAM's debut album, Fantastic, topped the UK charts but made little impression in America."

The album spawned multiple number one singles, cementing WAM's pop stardom. "WAM's album, MakeItBig, began a three week run atop the album chart."

## songs
**Young Guns (1982)** : This song was the first of four consecutive British Top Ten hits for WAM, showcasing George Michael's early rapping skills and marking their initial success in the UK. "Young guns was the first of four straight British top tens for WAM."
**WAM Rap (1982)** : Following "Young Guns," this track made even heavier use of George Michael's rapping skills, further distinguishing WAM's early sound in the British pop scene. "including the follow-up WAM rap, which made even heavier use of Michael's rapping skills."
**Wake Me Up Before You Go-Go (1984)** : This single, written by George Michael, marked WAM's breakthrough in America by drawing on nostalgic references to early rock and soul, including a reference to the 1930s dance, The Jitterbug. Its exuberant music video, featuring Michael and Andrew Ridgley in white "choose life" t-shirts and neon accessories, established WAM as a teeny-bopper sensation during the peak popularity of Duran Duran. "Wake me up before you go go, made stars of WAM in America."
**Last Christmas (1984)** : Released in England as a massive holiday single, this song is one of the best-selling British singles of all time, certified for sales of nearly two million copies in the UK alone. It cemented WAM's enduring compositional ability and is one of the few songs from the last two decades of the 20th century to enter the holiday music canon alongside Mariah Carey's "All I Want for Christmas Is You." Although not a hit in America in 1984 due to the market being focused on "Wake Me Up Before You Go-Go," it has since become WAM's most enduring perennial, dominating US radio playlists every December. "Last Christmas is one of the best-selling British singles of all time, certified for sales of nearly two million copies in the UK alone."
**Everything She Wants (1985)** : An R&B-flavored mid-tempo jam, this song became WAM's third consecutive number one hit on the Hot 100 and a number 12 hit on the R&B chart. George Michael's lyrics on this track lamented an imagined, acquisitive, and pregnant wife, drawing parallels to Elton John's "Someone Saved My Life Tonight" in its personal reflection on relationships. "George Michael was lamenting and imagined, acquisitive, and pregnant wife."

"While Ridgley shared some co-songwriting duties, played a bit of guitar, and served as co-conspirator and moral support for his shy friend."

## albums
**Honky Château (1972)** : This album reached the top of the Billboard album chart in July 1972, becoming Elton John's first of seven consecutive number one albums. This achievement remains the longest streak of chart-topping albums by anyone except The Beatles. It featured top 10 singles like "Rocket Man" and "Honky Cat." "Its album, Honki Shatto, reached the top of the Billboard album chart."
**Don't Shoot Me I'm Only the Piano Player (1973)** : Following the success of *Honky Château*, this album became Elton John's second consecutive number one album in the US, released less than half a year later. It also spawned his first American number one single, "Crocodile Rock." "John would score not only his second consecutive number one album, with Don't Shoot Me I'm Only the Piano Player, but also his first American number one single."
**Goodbye Yellow Brick Road (1973) - Double LP** : This double LP saw Elton John score a string of smash hits, demonstrating his versatility across mini-genres. It included a hard-driving pub rock number, "Saturday Night's Alright for Fighting," a dreamy ballad with Beach Boys-style harmonies in the title track, and the album's biggest hit, "Benny and the Jets." "From his next chart topping album, the double LP, Goodby Yellow Brick Road, John scored a string of smash hits."
**Captain Fantastic and the Brown Dirt Cowboy (1975)** : Hailed as a magnum opus upon its release in May 1975, this confessional, soul-bearing album made history by debuting at number one on the Billboard album chart. It was the first album ever to start its life at number one in Billboard chart history, an achievement neither The Beatles, The Rolling Stones, nor Led Zeppelin had accomplished. The album completed a seven-week run atop the chart. "The confessional soul-bearing album debuted on the album chart all the way up at number one."
**Rock of the Westies (1975)** : Less than four months after *Captain Fantastic and the Brown Dirt Cowboy* completed its chart run, Elton John's next album repeated history by also debuting at number one, becoming the second album in chart history to do so. Despite its rushed appearance, with an album cover featuring a close-up of an unshaven Elton, its lead-off single, "Island Girl," was simultaneously atop the Hot 100. "John's next album, Rock of the Westies, repeated the feat and also debuted at number one."
**Greatest Hits (1974)** : This album topped the album charts in early 1975, contributing to Elton John's "imperial year" alongside his number one singles. "the latter with his late 74 greatest hits album."
**Blue Moves (1976)** : Released in October 1976, this album peaked at number three on Billboard, marking Elton John's first new studio album to miss the number one spot since 1971. It spent only five months on the chart, less than any of his previous albums, indicating the swift backlash following his Rolling Stone interview where he openly discussed his bisexuality. "When that album peaked at number three in Billboard, it became John's first new studio album to miss the number one spot since 1971."
**Ice On Fire (1985)** : This album featured "Wrap Her Up" as its lead single, a duet with George Michael. Elton John brought in George Michael for this track as he was finding it difficult to generate worthy radio hits after "Sad Songs (Say So Much)." "Elton issued a single, prominently featuring George, hitching his wagon to Michael's rising star."
**Reg Strikes Back (1988)** : This album marked Elton John's hunger for a comeback, with a self-deprecating title and an album cover featuring a photograph of piles of flamboyant costumes he was auctioning for charity. Released around the same time he publicly announced he was comfortable being gay and had undergone vocal cord surgery, it aimed to signal his return to form. "The album's self-deprecating title was Reg Strikes Back and the cover was a photograph of piles of flamboyant costumes John was auctioning for charity."

## songs
**Your Song (1970)** : This was the first major pop hit for Elton John as a performer and for his co-writer, lyricist Bernie Toppen. It debuted at number 38 in the American Top 40 in December 1970 and broke into the pop Top 10 by January 1971, reaching number eight. The conversational lyrics contributed to its success on US airwaves. "your song, which was the first major pop hit not only for its performer, but also its co-writer, a lyricist named Bernie Toppen."
**Border Song (1970)** : This gospel-flavored single, written by Elton John, was covered by Aretha Franklin, who subtitled it "Holy Moses." Franklin's remake became Elton John's highest-charting composition at the time, breaking into the top 10 on the R&B chart (then Hot Soul Singles) and peaking at number five. This meant Elton John's first top 10 hit in America as a songwriter was on the R&B chart. "For a brief period just before Christmas 1970, the highest charting Elton John composition ever was the Queen of Souls remake of his gospel-flavored single, Border Song, which she subtitled Holy Moses."
**Tiny Dancer (1972)** : Now one of Elton John's most beloved songs, "Tiny Dancer" originally peaked at number 41 in early 1972, surprisingly missing the Top 40. Its iconic status would grow over time, but it did not immediately follow "Your Song" as a major hit. "Another single that's now one of Elton's most beloved, Tiny Dancer, peaked at number 41 in early 1972, actually missing the top 40."
**Rocket Man (1972)** : This song reached number six on the charts in July 1972, coinciding with its album, *Honky Château*, reaching the top of the Billboard album chart. It was one of two top 10 smashes from the album. "The same week in July 1972 when Rocket Man reached number six, its album, Honki Shatto, reached the top of the Billboard album chart."
**Honky Cat (1972)** : One of the top 10 smash hits from the album *Honky Château*, this song contributed to Elton John's rising popularity and the album's success. "After Honki Shatto and its top 10 smashes, Rocket Man and Honki Cat, the hits for John started coming faster."
**Crocodile Rock (1973)** : Elton John's first American number one single, "Crocodile Rock" introduced his flamboyant persona, with jackets sporting stripes and wider lapels, and higher platform shoes. The song itself was pure kitsch, a pastiche of 1950s rock and roll signifiers, with lyrics referencing "when Rock was young" and "rockin' around the clock," positioning John and Toppen ahead of the 1970s wave of 50s nostalgia. "Rocket Man and Honki Rock Crocodile Rock, in essence, introduced flamboyant Elton."
**Saturday Night's Alright for Fighting (1973)** : A hard-driving pub rock number from the double LP *Goodbye Yellow Brick Road*, this song demonstrated Elton John's ability to adapt to various mini-genres. "including a hard-driving pub rock number who would later cover Saturday nights all right for fighting."
**Goodbye Yellow Brick Road (1973)** : The title track from the album, this was a dreamy ballad that combined imagery from *The Wizard of Oz* with gorgeous Beach Boys-style harmonies. It was one of the smash hits from the double LP. "A dreamy ballet that wedded Dorothy from Wizard of Oz imagery with gorgeous Beach Boys style harmonies, the album's title track, Goodby Yellow Brick Road."
**Benny and the Jets (1974)** : This curious album cut became a number one hit for Elton John, initially resisting release as a single until it broke on Black radio in Detroit. It blended strutting R&B with glam rock, incorporating fake crowd noises and arena echo to imitate a live concert. Bernie Toppen's lyrics imagined a fan letter to a fictional band led by a woman in "electric boots and a mohair suit." Its success on Black radio led to it reaching number 15 on Billboard's Hot Soul Singles, in addition to topping the Hot 100. "Benny was a pinnacle of John's and Topin's adaptive approach to songwriting."
**Philadelphia Freedom (1975)** : This single was dedicated to Elton John's favorite tennis player, Billie Jean King, who played for the Philadelphia Freedoms tennis team, and served as a tribute to the music of Philadelphia. Like "Benny and the Jets," it made both the Hot 100, where it topped the chart, and the R&B chart, where it scraped the top 40 in early 1975. "Yes, it's a song written about Philadelphia. It's a tribute to the music of Philadelphia from myself and the band, and also to a lady called Billie Jean King, who used to play tennis for the Philadelphia Freedom's tennis team."
**Lucy in the Sky with Diamonds (1974)** : Elton John's cover of this Beatles song topped the singles chart in early 1975, contributing to his "imperial year" where he led off the year atop both the single and album charts. "the former with his cover of the Beatles song, Lucy and the Sky with Diamonds."
**Someone Saved My Life Tonight (1975)** : A hit from his *Captain Fantastic* album, this song featured soul-bearing lyrics about a man escaping a loveless marriage to a controlling woman. Bernie Toppen's lyric was a thinly veiled account of Elton John's own experience, nearly marrying a woman in the 1960s before his fame, for which he was grateful he had not. "Bernie Toppins lyric was a thinly veiled story about his dear friend Elton, who nearly married a woman in the 1960s prior to his fame, and was grateful he hadn't."
**Don't Go Breaking My Heart (1976)** : A cheeky male-female duet with British vocalist Kiki Dee, this song topped the charts, becoming Elton John's first-ever British number one hit. It was released in the summer of 1976, just before the controversial Rolling Stone interview that would significantly impact his career. "The single, Don't Go Breaking My Heart, even topped the charts in Elton's home country, his first ever British number one."
**Sorry Seems to Be the Hardest Word (1976)** : This was the only major single released from the *Blue Moves* album, reaching number six on the charts. It marked Elton John's last top ten hit for nearly three years, symbolizing the abrupt end of his imperial period following his controversial Rolling Stone interview. "Sorry, seems to be the hardest word, reached number six."
**I'm Still Standing (1983)** : This song was a number 12 hit, representing one of a string of respectable successes for Elton John in the first half of the 1980s, even if he hadn't reached the heights of his 1970s megastardom. "These included the 1983 number 12 hit, I'm still standing."
**I Guess That's Why They Call It the Blues (1984)** : An early 1984 number four hit, this song was part of Elton John's consistent chart presence in the early 1980s, contributing to his respectable but not "imperial" run of success. "The early 1984 number four hit, I guess that's why they call it the blues."
**Sad Songs (Say So Much) (1984)** : This track was a summer 1984 number five hit, marking one of Elton John's last significant radio successes before he began finding it difficult to generate worthy radio hits, leading him to collaborate with George Michael on "Wrap Her Up." "And the summer 1984 number five hit, Sad Songs, say so much."
**Wrap Her Up (1985)** : This duet with George Michael was the lead single from Elton John's album *Ice On Fire*. It was described as a "very 80s sounding and regrettable single" where the two men listed famous female sex symbols they'd like to take home. It was not considered a highlight for either artist. "Wrap Her Up was not a highlight of either man's 1985."
**That's What Friends Are For (1986)** : Elton John sang on this number one AIDS charity record with Dion Warwick, credited to "Dion and Friends." While a chart-topper, it was not his solo hit, and he craved to be back in the game with his own material. "Although John did sing with Dion Warwick on her 1986 number one AIDS charity record, that's what friends are for."
**I Don't Wanna Go On with You Like That (1988)** : This song peaked at number two on the charts, becoming Elton John's biggest hit in a dozen years. Released from his comeback album *Reg Strikes Back*, it symbolized his renewed effort to achieve chart success after a period of lower sales. However, it was kept from the number one spot by George Michael's "Monkey," preventing Elton from achieving the top spot he craved. "When I don't want to go on with you like that peaked at number two, it was still Elton's biggest hit in a dozen years."

## curiosities
**US Dominance in Early 70s** : Elton John was remarkably more popular in the United States than in his home country, the United Kingdom, during the first half of the 1970s. By 1976, he had accumulated half a dozen number one hits and 15 Top 10 hits in the US, nearly twice as many as in the UK at that time. "the man-born, regional Dwight in Middlesex, England, was even more popular here in the US at first than he was in his home country."
**Unique Songwriting Process with Bernie Toppen** : Elton John's songwriting partnership with lyricist Bernie Toppen involved an unusual "lyrics first" system. Toppen would send fully penned lyrics to John, who would then set them to music, often without further consultation. This method, adapted from a Tin Pan Alley format for rock, produced songs that felt intensely personal despite reflecting two distinct sensibilities. "Bernie Toppen would send Elton John fully penned lyrics, which Elton would then set to music, often without consulting Bernie further."
**Flamboyant Persona and 50s Nostalgia** : With the release of "Crocodile Rock," Elton John's flamboyant persona became more pronounced, featuring striped jackets, wider lapels, and higher platform shoes. The song itself, a pastiche of 1950s rock and roll, positioned John and Toppen ahead of a broader 1970s wave of 50s nostalgia, predating major cultural touchstones like *Grease* on Broadway, *American Graffiti*, and *Happy Days*. "Rocket Man and Honki Rock Crocodile Rock, in essence, introduced flamboyant Elton."
**First Album to Debut at Number One (1975)** : Elton John's *Captain Fantastic and the Brown Dirt Cowboy* was the first album in Billboard chart history to debut at number one in May 1975. This unprecedented feat, which even major acts like The Beatles, The Rolling Stones, or Led Zeppelin had not achieved, was repeated by John later that year with *Rock of the Westies*, making him the first artist to have two albums debut at number one. "It debuted on top, the first album in Billboard chart history ever to start its life at number one."
**Replacing Himself on the Charts (1975)** : When Elton John's "Island Girl" topped the Hot 100, it knocked out "Bad Blood," a number one song by his friend Neil Sedaka. Remarkably, Sedaka's single had been issued on Elton John's own label, Rocket Records, and featured backing vocals by John himself, meaning Elton John had, in effect, replaced his own influence at the top of the Hot 100. "John had, in effect, replaced himself on top of the Hot 100."
**Imperial Period Defined (1970s)** : Elton John's "imperial period" in the first half of the 1970s was characterized by an extraordinarily prolific and concentrated output of smash albums and singles released almost monthly. He later summarized this time as when he "could do no wrong," which came to an abrupt end in 1976. "Elton didn't say the word Imperial, but he defined it perfectly."
**Rolling Stone Interview and "Coming Out" (1976)** : In October 1976, Elton John gave a pivotal Rolling Stone interview where he was directly asked about his sexuality for the first time by openly gay freelancer Cliff Jar. John openly admitted to being bisexual, stating, "there's nothing wrong with going to bed with somebody of your own sex. I think everybody's bisexual to a certain degree." This interview was an explosive public event, covered by Walter Cronkite on the evening news, and marked a turning point where "America's love affair with Captain Fantastic ended." "Elton replied, there's nothing wrong with going to bed with somebody of your own sex. I think everybody's bisexual to a certain degree."
**Career Impact of "Coming Out"** : The 1976 Rolling Stone interview, where Elton John confirmed his bisexuality, abruptly halted his "imperial" chart dominance in America. In a matter of weeks, he transitioned from a chart-dominating demigod to a more modest second-tier pop act, with his next album *Blue Moves* failing to reach number one and his single "Sorry Seems to Be the Hardest Word" being his last top ten hit for nearly three years. This backlash served as a cautionary tale for other pop stars. "in the space of a few weeks in 1976 went from the premier, chart dominating Demigod to a modest second-tier pop-act."
**Marriage to Renate Blouel (Mid-80s)** : In the mid-1980s, during the height of the AIDS crisis, Elton John surprised friends by marrying his friend and studio collaborator, Renate Blouel, while actively living a straight-identified life. This companionship ended in divorce less than four years later. "Elton surprised friends by marrying a woman, his friend and studio collaborator Renate Blouel, a companionship that ended in divorce less than four years later."
**Publicly "Coming Out" as Gay (1988)** : Around the release of his 1988 album *Reg Strikes Back*, Elton John, having divorced Renate Blouel, told the press he was comfortable being gay. This public statement marked a definitive moment in his personal life, occurring as he was actively working on his career comeback. "Around the release of the album, having divorced Blowell, Elton told the press he was comfortable being gay."
**Vocal Surgery (1988)** : Just before recording *Reg Strikes Back*, Elton John underwent what is implied to be vocal surgery (referred to as "full support from John" but contextually "came back with his voice stronger"), which resulted in a stronger voice, evident on his comeback hit "I Don't Wanna Go On with You Like That." "just before recording the album, he underwent full support from John. And he came back with his voice stronger as evidenced by his comeback hit."
