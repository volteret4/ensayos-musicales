## albums
**Run-D.M.C. (1984) - Hard-Edged Self-Titled Debut** : Co-produced by Run's brother Russell Simmons and hip-hop producer Larry Smith, the album put rock elements front and center like no rap recording had before. It never cracked the top 50 of Billboard's album chart, but it was certified gold just before Christmas 1984 for sales of half a million copies, only nine months after release. Before this, the only gold rap album was Kurtis Blow's self-titled 1980 debut, certified more than four years earlier. The album's rock-heavy sound and the group's presentation inspired NYU student Rick Rubin. "It was a remarkable milestone for a rap album."
**King of Rock (1985) - Sophomore Album** : Released by Profile in early 1985, its title track found Run-DMC dissing the biggest acts in rock and pop. The album went gold even faster than the debut: the RIAA certified it in June 1985, less than four months after release. "The album went gold even faster than its predecessor."
**Raising Hell (1986) - Platinum Breakthrough** : Released in May 1986, this was Run-DMC's third album and the first produced entirely by Rick Rubin. Rubin said that after finishing it "it felt like there was something missing," which led to the "Walk This Way" remake. The album peaked at No. 3 on the album chart the week "Walk This Way" peaked on the Hot 100. It was certified platinum in July, double platinum in September and triple platinum the following spring, sales milestones no rap album had seen before. Its hits included "My Adidas," "Walk This Way," "You Be Illin'" and "It's Tricky." "All sales milestones that no rap album had ever seen."
**Tougher Than Leather (1988) - Film and Album** : Run-DMC followed the path of Elvis Presley, Madonna and the Spice Girls by starring in their own movie at their peak. Rick Rubin directed it, his only directorial effort. The film was an odd fiction-biopic hybrid and left theaters quickly. The album went platinum immediately, mostly on pent-up demand, but spent only two weeks in the top 10 and about six months on the chart, less than half as long as any of their first three albums. Critics and the rap community were mixed: Public Enemy's Chuck D praised its directness on the lead single "Run's House," while the Monkees remake "Mary, Mary" was considerably less inspired than "Walk This Way." Run-DMC were the only major second-wave act to release an album in 1988. "It was out of theaters quickly."
**Back from Hell (1990) - Commercial Low Point** : Run-DMC tried to pivot toward a post-gangsta and New Jack Swing sound. The album peaked at No. 81 and was their first not to go gold. "Became their first album not to even go gold."
**Down with the King (1993) - Sixth Album** : The tracks were produced by a who's who of 90s rap, including Pete Rock, Q-Tip, EPMD, Naughty by Nature, the Bomb Squad and Jermaine Dupri, with guitar from Rage Against the Machine's Tom Morello. The album worked as a thank-you from the rap community to the group that had laid a foundation for so many of them. Reviews were mixed, with critics noting Run and DMC's attempts to adapt to younger MCs' styles. It still returned Run-DMC to the album top 10, went gold and produced the trio's final top 40 hit. "The album was in effect a thank you to the group that had provided a foundation for so many of them."

## songs
**Sucker MC's / It's Like That (1983) - Debut Single** : Run-DMC's debut single, "Sucker MC's," was paired with the flip side "It's Like That." It broke virtually every pattern rap had followed to that date and kicked off rap's second wave. It was minimalist where earlier hip-hop had been lavish, glowering where it had been festive, and it carried a more sharply defined MC personality. Even Grandmaster Flash and Melle Mel's "The Message" had a lusher backdrop. "Run DMC's music was a line in the sand."
**Rock Box (1984) - Third Single from Debut** : The third single from the debut album featured squealing metal-style guitar by Eddie Martinez, who went on to play with everyone from David Lee Roth to Robert Palmer. "Featured squealing metal style guitar."
**King of Rock (1985) - Title Single** : The lead single from the second album dissed the biggest acts in rock and pop. In the video, the group crashes an imaginary museum of rock artifacts, years before the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame existed. The Beastie Boys later borrowed this approach for the "No Sleep till Brooklyn" video. "The song and the album were unsuttely titled King of Rock."
**My Adidas (1986) - Ode to Shell-Toe Sneakers** : The first single from Raising Hell commemorated the group's Live Aid appearance and took a victory lap. Backed by the B-side "Peter Piper," it reached No. 5 on Hot Black Singles, Run-DMC's biggest R&B hit, and led to a sponsorship deal with Adidas. It re-established the group's credibility at Black radio ahead of "Walk This Way." "Actually spawned a sponsorship deal with the shoemaker."
**Walk This Way (1986) - Rap-Rock Summit with Aerosmith** : Run-DMC's remake of Aerosmith's 1970s hit features additional guitar and vocals from Steven Tyler and Joe Perry. It was rap's first top 10 hit on the pop charts. In the week ending September 27, 1986, it reached its No. 4 peak on the Hot 100 (and No. 8 on Hot Black Singles), behind Lionel Richie's "Dancing on the Ceiling," Carl Anderson and Gloria Loring's "Friends and Lovers," and Huey Lewis and the News' "Stuck with You." Aerosmith got no artist credit on the single, the 45 sleeve or Billboard's charts. Tyler and Perry were credited only as songwriters, the sole songwriters, because the remake counted as a new arrangement rather than a rewrite. Russell Simmons and Rick Rubin received the only production credits. Profile released it as Raising Hell's second single in mid-summer 1986, and its video pushed Run-DMC to platinum stardom. "That's the rap rock summit heard around the world."
**You Be Illin' (1986) - Comic Story Song** : This Raising Hell single was a No. 29 pop hit in fall 1986. "The comic story song UBLA, a number 29 pop hit."
**It's Tricky (1987) - Tongue-Twisting Hit** : This Raising Hell single was a top 20 R&B hit in winter 1987, and its video was a regular favorite on Dial MTV. "Another hit video that was a regular Dile MTV favorite."
**Christmas in Hollis (1987) - A Very Special Christmas** : Run-DMC were the only rap act invited onto the multi-platinum Special Olympics benefit album A Very Special Christmas. Their track was the album's only new composition; every other performer, from Sting to Stevie Nicks to Bruce Springsteen, recorded traditional carols, hymns or covers of holiday rock classics. The royalties mostly go to charity, and the song still gets holiday airplay every year. In 2017 alone, according to Nielsen Music, it was played more than 3,000 times on US terrestrial radio, about a thousand more than "Walk This Way." "Christmas in Hollis remains Run DMC's perennial."
**Run's House (1988) - Tougher Than Leather Lead Single** : Some hip-hop heads, including Public Enemy's Chuck D, praised the directness of this lead single. "Praised the album's directness."
**Mary, Mary (1988) - Monkees Remake** : Rubin and Run-DMC tried to repeat the rap-rock formula by reaching back to the 1960s for a well-known album cut by the pre-fab pop band the Monkees, who, like Aerosmith, were enjoying an 80s renaissance. The remake was considerably less inspired than the 1986 experiment. "Run DMC's redo was considerably less inspired."

## curiosities
**Rock Ambitions from the Start** : For years before "Walk This Way," Run, DMC and Jam Master Jay presented themselves as the future of both rap and rock and roll. They demanded a seat at rock's table and used rock elements from the start. They did cross over, but their own time at the top was relatively short, while others benefited more. "Run DMC were the giving tree of hip hop."
**Signature Look** : Run-DMC dropped the close-fitting denim and breakdance gear of rap's first wave for a uniform as spare as their music: black jackets, black fedoras, gold chains and unlaced white sneakers. Darryl's giant square-framed glasses were part of the statement. "Run DMC, like their music, were no joke."
**Only Rap Act at Live Aid (July 13, 1985)** : Run-DMC were the only rap act invited to Live Aid, at JFK Stadium in Philadelphia, and they took the role of rap ambassadors seriously. On stage, DMC said, "we have no band, just Jam Master J." The group cherished the moment for the rest of their careers. "It was a moment Run DMC would cherish the rest of their careers."
**No Hot 100 Hits Before 1986** : Despite the acclaim, as of the end of 1985 none of Run-DMC's singles had even entered the Hot 100. "None of their singles had so much as cracked the Hot 100."
**Initial Skepticism About Walk This Way** : The rappers knew the opening beat and riff because Jam Master Jay looped and scratched it, but they barely knew the rest of the song; Run says they didn't even know the title. Rubin had to convince them the idea wouldn't be corny. DMC later told the Washington Post: "hell no, this ain't gonna happen... This is hillbilly gibberish, country bumpkin bullshit." Run objected to hokey lyrics like "backseat lover." "Rubin had to convince the rap trio that the idea wouldn't be corny."
**Walk This Way Video - Breaking Down the Wall** : The video reunited Run-DMC and Aerosmith in an empty theater in Union City, New Jersey. Director Jon Small built an actual wall on stage between the two rehearsing groups. Jam Master Jay scratches over the beat, Run and DMC drown out Tyler and Perry, and Tyler finally smashes a hole in the wall with his mic stand just in time for the chorus. MTV still played almost no rap (Yo! MTV Raps was two years away), but the pairing was "MTV catnip": the clip dominated Dial MTV and went into power rotation. "Director John Small actually erected a wall on stage between the two cruise rehearsing."
**Skipping 1987** : Apart from the Christmas single, Run-DMC took 1987 off, their first year without an album since 1984. The trio came to regret this momentum-killing move. "A momentum killing move the trio came to regret."
**Rock and Roll Hall of Fame Induction (2009)** : Run-DMC were inducted three years before the Beastie Boys, with one member missing because Jam Master Jay had died. Eminem inducted them, the leading white rapper of the 21st century honoring the leading Black crew of rap's second wave. Run's acceptance speech credited Russell Simmons for telling them to "put the rock with the rap" and Rick Rubin for knowing what to do. "We'd be somewhere, but we wouldn't be in the rock and roll hall of fame."
**Murder of Jam Master Jay (2002)** : Jason Mizell was shot and killed in 2002 by an unknown assailant. He had been the most active member in the decade after the group's last hits, producing for acts including platinum rappers Onyx and, in his final act, helping to discover 50 Cent. The case officially remains unsolved, with theories involving a drug dealer and Mizell's earlier beef with 50 Cent. "Myzel was murdered in 2002, shot and killed by an unknown assailant."
**Breakup and DMC's Solo Performances** : Since Jam Master Jay's death in 2002, Run-DMC have officially been broken up. DMC still performs occasional live shows with a small stage crew, including an outdoor event in Red Hook, Brooklyn, where he performed the group's hits. As at Live Aid, he needs little more than two turntables, a microphone and now a laptop, but unlike at Live Aid he now has "Walk This Way," a crowd-pleasing rock song more associated with Run-DMC than with Aerosmith. "He continues to uphold the mantle of Run DMC as kings and pioneers of rock."

## albums
**Toys in the Attic (1975) - Million-Selling Breakthrough** : Driven by "Sweet Emotion," the album sold a million copies. It also contained "Walk This Way," whose groove made "Sweet Emotion" seem almost sedate. "It helped spur their album Toys in the Attic to a million in sales."
**Done with Mirrors (1985) - Failed Comeback** : This was Aerosmith's first album on Geffen Records after Joe Perry's return. It underperformed, was widely considered the band's least inspired, and barely scraped the top 40 of the album chart. "By general agreement, the band's least inspired."
**Permanent Vacation (1987) - Post-Walk This Way Resurgence** : With the hits "Dude Looks Like a Lady," "Angel" and "Rag Doll," it went double platinum within months and is now quintuple platinum. "Went double platinum within months."
**Pump (1989) - Multi-Platinum Follow-Up** : With the hits "Love in an Elevator," "What It Takes" and "Janie's Got a Gun," it went quadruple platinum within a year and is now septuple platinum. Every new Aerosmith album through the 1990s went multi-platinum, selling heavily to Gen Xers who were toddlers when "Dream On" was a hit. "Sold truckloads to Gen Xers."

## songs
**Dream On (1973/1976) - Hit on the Second Try** : The original 1973 release stalled well short of the top 40. After "Sweet Emotion" broke, Columbia reissued it in early 1976 and it reached the top 10. "It took Dream on two tries to become a hit."
**Sweet Emotion (1975) - First Top 40 Hit** : Aerosmith's first top 40 hit, in summer 1975. It stood out for Steven Tyler's syncopated, Black-derived vocal rhythms on the verses, punctuated by the band's blues-derived start-stop rhythms. "The classic Sweet Emotion was Aerosmith's first top 40 hit."
**Walk This Way (1975/1977) - Original Version** : First released in summer 1975 as the follow-up to "Sweet Emotion," it also took two tries to become a hit. Joe Perry said the boogie-rock groove was inspired by New Orleans funk legends the Meters. Steven Tyler put nonsense words over Perry's riff and turned them into tongue-twisting lyrics about a high school boy losing his virginity, writing them almost as percussion and essentially rapping them on the record. Tyler also laid down the original kick-drum rhythm, which drummer Joey Kramer later played and embellished with hi-hat. It peaked at No. 10 on the Hot 100 in February 1977, Aerosmith's last top 10 single for a long time. "Tyler said he wrote them practically as percussion."

## curiosities
**The Toxic Twins** : By the late 1970s Aerosmith were infamous for their drug use, and the music industry nicknamed Tyler and Perry the Toxic Twins. "The music industry dubbed Tyler and Perry the Toxic Twins."
**Wilderness Years (1979-1985)** : Joe Perry left in 1979 and stayed away for five years. Between 1979 and 1985 the band released only three studio albums, and none of their tracks, with or without Perry, connected at radio. When Rick Rubin approached them in 1986, they were as out of favor as a formerly multi-platinum rock band could be. "They needed Run DMC even more than Run DMC needed them."
**Tyler and Perry Join the Remake** : Steven Tyler was a minor hip-hop fan: during his drug years, while scoring on New York street corners, he bought rap mixtapes and noticed the growing rap scene. Joe Perry saw a link between rap and the blues he loved. Both were wary of the idea but had little to lose. Perry noticed the recording had no bass part and borrowed a bass from the Beastie Boys, who were hanging around the studio. They left charmed and bemused, unsure the record would ever come out. "They also had very little to lose."
**Reinvented by Walk This Way** : The 1986 collaboration did more than revive Aerosmith's career; it reinvented it. Approaching 40 in 1987, the band became bigger than ever, outselling their first decade, which had produced only a handful of gold albums and a couple of platinum ones. "It was most good to the people who could scurry back to the rock side of the rap rock divide."

## albums
**Licensed to Ill (1986) - Debut Album and First No. 1 Rap Album** : Produced by Rick Rubin, the album mixed rap with crunching hard rock and mocked rock poses. Rubin and Russell Simmons talked the group out of some of their worst title ideas, including a gay slur the band later regretted. The group still hid an Easter egg on the cover: held up to a mirror, the airplane's tail reads "Eat Me." Deep cuts like "Slow Ride," "Time to Get Ill" and "Girls" became bar-room standards. It entered the top 10 a month after Christmas, and in March 1987 it knocked Bon Jovi's Slippery When Wet out of the No. 1 slot, staying there seven weeks. It matched Raising Hell's triple platinum by spring 1987, ranked as Billboard's third-biggest album of 1987 behind Bon Jovi and Paul Simon, and is now diamond-certified for 10 million US sales, one of the best-selling single albums in rap history. "License to ill went where no rap album, including Rundee and C's Raising Hell, had gone before, all the way to the top."
**Paul's Boutique (1989) - Dust Brothers Sample Collage** : Released three years after Licensed to Ill, an eternity in hip-hop, because of the legal fight to leave Def Jam. It was made with the West Coast production team the Dust Brothers, who, like Public Enemy's Bomb Squad, built dense collages out of samples. Critics hailed it as a high-culture magnum opus, but it peaked outside the top 10 in summer 1989 and went gold, a comedown from Licensed to Ill. It became a word-of-mouth hit and took a full decade to reach double platinum. With so many samples, it could not be made today because clearing them would cost too much. "It was hailed as a high-culture magnum opus."
**Check Your Head (1992) - Return to Instruments** : To reboot their career after Paul's Boutique, the Beasties picked up their instruments again, mixing the punk-derived rock of their teens with MC rhymes. As alternative rock boomed after Nirvana and Pearl Jam, it reached the top 10 and went platinum in about a year. Its songs, including "So What'cha Want," were welcomed on modern rock radio. "Check Your Head righted the ship for the Beasties commercially."
**Ill Communication (1994) - Second No. 1 Album** : The album that produced the shrill punk anthem "Sabotage" was their first No. 1 album since Licensed to Ill. "The Beasties' first number one album since license to ill."

## songs
**Cooky Puss (1983) - Prank Call Record** : In early 1983 the band prank-called the New York ice cream chain Carvel to order a custom birthday cake, recorded it and turned it into a record. Mixing Jerky Boys-style antics (a decade before the Jerky Boys), dumbed-down rap lingo, hip-hop beats and samples, it became an underground hit. The EP did well enough that the band dropped hardcore punk and committed fully to rap. "They recorded the prank call and they turned it into a record."
**Rock Hard (1984) - First Def Jam Single** : Produced by Rick Rubin, the Beasties' first 12-inch for Def Jam lifted the core riff of AC/DC's "Back in Black" and had the group rap over it. The sample was unauthorized, and the single didn't stay in stores long. It set the template for Rubin's Beasties sound: raw like LL Cool J but more maximalist and dense, and more openly rock than Run-DMC. "The ACDC sample was not legally authorized."
**She's on It (1985) - Krush Groove Soundtrack** : A rap-rock single on the 1985 soundtrack to the Def Jam-affiliated hip-hop film Krush Groove. It earned MTV exposure during the Madonna tour. "Particularly for their rap rock single She's On It."
**Hold It Now, Hit It (1986) - Lead-Off Single** : Released six months ahead of the debut album, it got Black radio airplay in spring 1986 and peaked at No. 55 on Hot Black Singles. It sampled Trouble Funk, Kool & the Gang, Bob James, Kurtis Blow and Doug E. Fresh. "They and Rick Rubin pushed sampling technology in bold new directions."
**She's Crafty (1986) - Led Zeppelin Riff** : Opens with an unmistakable riff taken from Led Zeppelin's "The Ocean," from Houses of the Holy. "Bold and, again, brazen."
**Slow and Low (1986) - Co-Written with DMC** : Darryl McDaniels co-wrote this track, which echoes Run-DMC's flow. "They'd even gotten help from Run DMC."
**(You Gotta) Fight for Your Right (To Party!) (1986) - Frat Parody** : Written mainly by Adam Yauch and produced by Rick Rubin with prominent guitar, it was meant to parody frat-boy antics but was enjoyed unironically by the group's growing dude-bro fanbase. The video shows a food fight spiraling out of control, with cameos from Rick Rubin, LL Cool J and a then-unknown Tabitha Soren, later an MTV VJ. It peaked at No. 7 in March 1987. "Was meant to be a parody of Frat Boy Shenatigans."
**No Sleep till Brooklyn (1986) - Slayer Guitar Cameo** : Features guitar licks from Kerry King of Slayer, a thrash metal band Rubin had signed to Def Jam and whose album Reign in Blood he produced. Like Run-DMC's "King of Rock" video, the clip mocks rock fans who didn't think rap was real music. "Taking the piss out of rock fans who didn't think rap was legitimate music."
**Girls (1986) - Deep Cut** : A Licensed to Ill deep cut that, along with "Slow Ride" and "Time to Get Ill," became a bar-room standard. "Even the deep cuts on license to ill... became bar room standards."
**Hey Ladies (1989) - Paul's Boutique Lead Single** : The funky lead single peaked at No. 36. "It's lead single The Funky Hey Ladies peaked at number 36."
**Sabotage (1994) - Punk Anthem** : A shrill punk anthem played on modern rock radio and taken from Ill Communication. "The Shrill punk anthem Sabotage."
**Intergalactic (1998) - Format-Crossing Smash** : The Beasties still rapped but blended it with digital rock, reaching formats from alt-rock to top 40 to rhythmic and dance. "Enabling them to blanket radio formats."

## curiosities
**Hardcore Punk Origins** : Formed in 1981 as a hardcore punk trio led by Michael Diamond, then not quite 16, with John Berry on guitar and Kate Schellenbach, "the only non-boy Beastie," on drums. They soon added 17-year-old bassist Adam Yauch. By 1982 they were opening for Bad Brains and the Dead Kennedys at clubs like Max's Kansas City and CBGB. At a late-1982 gig they met 17-year-old Adam Horovitz, son of playwright Israel Horovitz, who took over guitar when Berry left. "The Beastie Boys formed as a hardcore punk trio in 1981."
**Kate Schellenbach's Departure** : Drummer Kate Schellenbach left in 1984, leaving a trio who took the rap names Mike D, MCA and Ad-Rock. She later joined the hip-hop-flavored indie rock band Luscious Jackson in the 90s and had hits like "Naked Eye." "She later made out quite well."
**Rick Rubin as DJ** : One of the DJs the band hired for live shows was Rick Rubin, who offered to sign them to his new Def Jam label. Like Sam Phillips at Sun Records, Rubin and Simmons had found "a trio of hip-hop Elvis Presleys." "Rick Rubin and Russell Simmons had found themselves a trio of hip-hop Elvis Presley's."
**Opening for Madonna (1985)** : Before they had an album out, Madonna, then the biggest pop star in America, invited them to open dates on her Virgin Tour. Her arena crowds did not take kindly to their loud antics, but the tour brought them promotion and MTV exposure. "The army of Madonna wannabes... did not take kindly to the loud antics."
**Long Beach Riot (1986)** : Supporting Run-DMC on the Raising Hell tour with LL Cool J and Whodini, the Beasties drew a rowdy following that was a prime cause of a riot near the end of the tour in Long Beach, California. "The Beasties Routing fans were a prime cause of the chaos."
**Together Forever Tour Excesses (1987)** : Sharing equal billing with Run-DMC, the Beasties drew mostly negative headlines. Early shows featured a 21-foot hydraulic inflatable penis, later retired before the tour ended, plus women in cages and the band spraying the audience with Budweiser. "Early stops on the tour featured a 21-foot hydraulic inflatable penis."
**LL Cool J's Criticism (1987)** : In a 1987 interview, LL Cool J complained the Beasties' behavior was making things hard for other rappers: "I wish they'd learn how to act. They need some self-control." "I wish they wouldn't run around the country and make it hard for us."
**Split from Def Jam** : After a cameo in Tougher Than Leather, the Beasties had a major falling out with Def Jam and Rick Rubin, moved from New York to Los Angeles, and after months of legal wrangling signed with Capitol Records. "The Beastie Boys had a massive falling out with Def Jam and Rick Rubin."
**Rock and Roll Hall of Fame Induction (2012)** : LL Cool J and Public Enemy's Chuck D inducted the Beastie Boys, showing the late-80s feud was long over. LL joked that when Rubin introduced them he thought "what a bunch of punks," and that he had "about 30 years to write my speech." Adam Yauch, ill with throat cancer, couldn't attend, and Ad-Rock read a letter from him. "My buddies, the Beastie Boys have made rock, rap, and just plain old music history."
**Death of Adam Yauch** : MCA converted to Tibetan Buddhism in the 90s and led the group to renounce their earlier sexist and homophobic antics. He died less than a month after the 2012 induction, at 47. "Less than one month later, Yaut died. He was 47."

## albums
**Radio (1985) - Debut Album** : Recorded with Rick Rubin within months of LL turning 17 and released in November 1985, it was praised for its sparse, uncluttered production. The cover is a simple photo of a huge boom box, and the back carries one prominent credit, "Reduced by Rick Rubin," the same wording used on the label of debut single "I Need a Beat." In April 1986 it became Def Jam's first gold album. "The album was critically acclaimed for its sparse and resolutely uncluttered production style."
**Bigger and Deffer (1987) - Sophomore Album** : Recorded in late 1986 and released in spring 1987, it was made with Los Angeles producer DJ Pooh, who later produced Ice Cube and Tha Dogg Pound, and whose sharp sound helped LL keep his street credibility. Lead single "I'm Bad" pushed the album to No. 3, matching Raising Hell's peak. "I Need Love" took it to double platinum, and it eventually sold three million copies. "DJ Poo's sharp sonic approach helped LL maintain his street credibility."
**Walking with a Panther (1989) - Third Album and Backlash** : LL kept his lover-man persona with lead single "I'm That Type of Guy," but the album was seen as soft and lightweight, with novelty tracks like "Big Ole Butt." He was arguably the first rapper to face a serious backlash from the rap community. "Walking with a Panther was regarded as soft and lightweight."
**Fourth Album - Marley Marl Comeback** : After Walking with a Panther underperformed, LL teamed with Eric B. & Rakim associate Marley Marl. The album won praise from rap, rock and pop listeners, and its title track contains what is now considered one of the greatest opening lines in hip-hop history. "Produced a fourth album that won praise across the music spectrum."

## songs
**I Need a Beat (1984) - Debut Single** : LL's debut single, carrying the Def Jam credit on its label. "LL's debut single, I need a beat."
**I Can't Live Without My Radio (1986) - Title Single** : The lead single from Radio reached No. 15 on Hot Black Singles in February 1986. "Reached number 15 on Billboards Hot Black singles chart in February."
**Rock the Bells (1986)** : Released weeks after "I Can't Live Without My Radio," it peaked at No. 17 in April 1986. "Peaking at number 17 in April."
**I'm Bad (1987) - Bigger and Deffer Lead Single** : Reached the R&B top five by summer 1987 and pushed the album to No. 3. "I'm Bad had reached the R&B chart top five."
**I Need Love (1987) - First Rap Ballad and First No. 1 Rap Single** : Rap's first hit ballad, and the most emotionally vulnerable any rapper had been since Melle Mel's "The Message," though in a more bourgeois romantic setting. At a time when even Black radio preferred smoother acts like Freddie Jackson, Whitney Houston and Janet Jackson, it worked as a Trojan horse. It reached No. 1 on Hot Black Singles in September 1987, rap's first chart-topping single, and No. 14 on the Hot 100. "LL's kinder gentler I Need Love was the Trojan horse."
**Going Back to Cali (1987) - Less Than Zero Soundtrack** : A sharp, jazz-inflected Rick Rubin production with witty lyrics, staccato horns and plenty of scratching, on the soundtrack to the Brat Pack film Less Than Zero. The soundtrack, mostly rock acts like the Bangles and Poison, went gold just before the 1987 holidays. The single reached No. 12 R&B and No. 31 on the Hot 100 by early 1988. "LL's Sharp, Jazz Inflected, Rick Rubin produced single Going Back to Callie."
**All I Have (2003) - With Jennifer Lopez** : LL's only Hot 100 No. 1, as the featured rapper on Jennifer Lopez's hit, at the peak of the R&B boom led by Ja Rule and Nelly. "LL scored his only Hot 100 number one hit in 2003."

## curiosities
**Origins of Ladies Love Cool James** : Born James Todd Smith, he was 16 in 1984 and grew up in Hollis, Queens, minutes from Run-DMC's neighborhood. He had been rapping since age nine and saw himself as an irresistible lothario, which he largely was. "He gave himself the nickname Ladies Love Cool James."
**Booed at the Apollo (1989)** : At a 1989 concert at Harlem's Apollo Theater, LL was booed off the stage and needed a comeback. "LL was booed off the stage."
**Pop Chameleon** : With no rock career to fall back on, LL changed styles as pop trends shifted. Not every change worked, but he scored some of his biggest pop hits in the mid-90s at the height of sultry R&B. "He nonetheless proved a pop chameleon."
**Acting, Grammys and Hall of Fame Nominations** : LL took up acting in film and TV in the mid-90s and hosted the Grammy Awards five times in the 2010s. He still releases music and sells decently for a middle-aged rapper. He has been nominated for the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame four times without being inducted. "He hosted the Grammy Awards five times in the 2010s."

## curiosities
**It's Yours (1983) - Dorm-Room Production** : In 1983, at age 20, NYU student Rubin, who had been soaking up the hip-hop club scene, teamed with John Byas (DJ Jazzy Jay) on "It's Yours." The breakbeat- and scratch-heavy single was produced in Rubin's NYU dorm room and rapped by Clarence Keaton, aka T La Rock, with unusually meta lyrics. It was 27-year-old Russell Simmons's favorite rap record, and Simmons was stunned when a bearded, scraggly white kid was pointed out as the producer. "Simmons asked again to meet the producer of The T. LaRocke record not believing it was Rubin."
**Founding Def Jam** : Rubin started Def Jam Recordings in his dorm room to release the 12-inch of "It's Yours." Simmons partnered with him, turning Def Jam into a full label and giving Rubin an indirect link to Run-DMC, who were signed to Profile. Rubin was inspired by Run-DMC's rock-flavored sound, which he believed could take rap to the next level. "He called it Death Jam Recordings."
**The Idea Behind Walk This Way** : Rubin assembled the remake while producing a Run-DMC album for the first time, wanting the experiment to be legitimate rather than another unauthorized sample. He told the Washington Post that after finishing Raising Hell "it felt like there was something missing," and that he wanted a familiar, hip-hop-friendly piece of music to bridge the gap with people who didn't consider rap to be music. When his friend Tim Sommer suggested having Aerosmith play on it, Rubin replied: "they'll never do it. Old white guys don't get this rap thing." "I was looking for a way to bridge that gap."
**Producer of Slayer** : Rubin signed thrash metal band Slayer to Def Jam and produced their album Reign in Blood. "Whose album Rain in Blood Rubin himself produced."
**Directing Tougher Than Leather (1988)** : The Run-DMC film was Rubin's only directing job. "His only directorial effort."
**Def American and Rock Career** : After his own falling out with Def Jam, Rubin started Def American, later renamed American Recordings, and signed mostly rock acts, from Danzig to the Black Crowes to the Jesus and Mary Chain. He still produced rappers such as Sir Mix-a-Lot and Jay-Z. By the turn of the millennium he was earning Grammy nominations for producing the Red Hot Chili Peppers and reviving veterans like Johnny Cash. "Rick Rubin II was a code switcher in the 90s."

# artist - Russell Simmons

## labels
Def Jam Recordings

## curiosities
**Manager and Def Jam Co-Founder** : Run's brother Russell Simmons was a manager and up-and-coming Black entrepreneur who managed Run-DMC, Kurtis Blow, Whodini and the Fearless Four. At 27 he partnered with Rick Rubin to launch Def Jam. He co-produced Run-DMC's debut with Larry Smith and, with Rubin, got the only production credits on "Walk This Way." Run credited him at the Hall of Fame for telling the group to "put the rock with the rap." "A manager and budding black entrepreneur whose brother was in run DMC."

# artist - The Sugarhill Gang

## members
Michael "Wonder Mike" Wright
Henry "Big Bank Hank" Jackson
Guy "Master Gee" O'Brien

## labels
Sugar Hill Records

## songs
**Rapper's Delight (1979) - First Major Rap Hit** : Released in fall 1979 by a crew from Englewood, New Jersey, assembled "X Factor style" by soul singer and mogul Sylvia Robinson and signed to her Sugar Hill Records. It reached No. 4 R&B and No. 36 on the Hot 100, rap's first top 40 hit. Its opening line helped popularize the term "hip hop." Its rhymes were lifted from rapper Grandmaster Caz and its hook from Chic's 1979 No. 1 "Good Times." The bassline was replayed in the studio rather than sampled, but it owed so much to Bernard Edwards that he and Nile Rodgers were credited as co-writers within months. "It is unquestionably the first major rap hit."
**Apache (1981) - Cover of a Cover** : Their version traces back to a 1960 country- and surf-flavored instrumental by UK act Cliff Richard and the Shadows, which the Incredible Bongo Band turned into a funk version in 1973. "Was a cover of a cover of a rock song that dated back to 1960."

# artist - The Fatback Band

## genres
Funk

## songs
**King Tim III (Personality Jock) (1979) - Candidate for First Rap Single** : The New Jersey funk group had been scoring funk hits since 1973, including an instrumental early hip-hop DJs loved for its loopable drum breaks. "King Tim III" has rap's basic hallmarks, including an invitation to clap and King Tim spelling his name and boasting. Still, it was a funk band rapping almost as a novelty, and Sugarhill Gang's bigger single arrived weeks later. "Fatback's King Tim III is an almost accidental progenitor of rap."

# artist - Grandmaster Flash and the Furious Five

## members
Grandmaster Flash
Melle Mel

## labels
Sugar Hill Records

## songs
**Freedom / The Birthday Party - Early Singles** : The group's first singles were fairly simple party records mixing hip-hop with R&B and disco. "Relatively straight forward party records."
**The Message (1982)** : One of the most influential recordings of the rock era, it was covered or interpolated by Duran Duran, Stiff Little Fingers, Depeche Mode and Puff Daddy. Phil Collins and producer Hugh Padgham borrowed Melle Mel's chant for Genesis's 1983 hit "Mama," and Lin-Manuel Miranda's Hamilton references it nightly. It reached No. 4 R&B, which was the ceiling for rap at Black radio then, but only No. 62 on the Hot 100 in November 1982. "The message remains one of the most influential recordings of the rock era."
**White Lines (1983)** : Credited to Grandmaster Flash even though he didn't take part, the song was conceived by Melle Mel and Sylvia Robinson as a new wave crossover built on an interpolation of "Cavern" by underground dance-punk band Liquid Liquid. Despite heavy play on New York pop and dance stations, it peaked at No. 47 on Hot Black Singles and missed the Hot 100 entirely. "It missed the Hot 100 entirely."

## songs
**Bring the Noise (1987) - Less Than Zero Soundtrack** : The Long Island group's track appeared on the Less Than Zero soundtrack and was later ranked by Rolling Stone and Pitchfork among the top singles of all time. It previewed their second album and signaled that rap's second wave was being overtaken. "Was a warning flare to rap's second wave."

## albums
**It Takes a Nation of Millions to Hold Us Back (1988)** : Released in June 1988, it unofficially began rap's golden age, raising the level of sonic range, political militancy and Afrocentrism. It and the conscious and gangsta rappers who followed changed hip-hop the way Nirvana changed rock in 1991, and the way Run-DMC had in 1984. "Nation of millions unofficially inaugurated rap's Golden Age."

# genre - Hip-Hop

## curiosities
**Commercial Dominance in the 2010s** : Rap is the top genre in recorded music in the 2010s. In 2017 hip-hop and R&B made up a quarter of all music consumed in America, remarkable for a form that didn't exist on record 40 years earlier and was long dismissed as a fad. At the 2018 Grammys, many of the strongest performances came from Kendrick Lamar and Cardi B (supporting Bruno Mars), but the major awards went to pop singers like Mars and Ed Sheeran. "Rap in the 2010s is the undisputed top genre in all of recorded music."
**Pivotal Years** : Fans organize rap history around key years. The golden era is usually dated from 1988 (Public Enemy, N.W.A) through 1994 (Nas, Tupac, the Notorious B.I.G.). 1986, the year rap first went platinum, is often overlooked but marks the start of rap's pop dominance. "But one pivotal year for rap that sometimes goes unmentioned is 1986."
**Origins in 1970s Black Music** : Naming the first rap single is like naming the first rock and roll song. Hip-hop grew out of 1970s funk and disco, and for about half a decade it existed only as live performance at New York street parties, with no MCs on vinyl. "Rap, at that time, was a live performance medium at New York City Street parties."
**Omnivorous Borrowing** : From the start rap borrowed from all of contemporary music. Chic's "Good Times" bassline fed both "Rapper's Delight" and Queen's 1980 No. 1 "Another One Bites the Dust," written by John Deacon. Afrika Bambaataa transformed Kraftwerk's 1977 "Trans-Europe Express" into the 1982 electro-rap hit "Planet Rock." "Hip hop emerged from the same stew as Rock, disco, funk, and R&B."
**The Chart Glass Ceiling (1983-1984)** : Rap influenced both white and Black radio, but rap singles stalled on Billboard. "Rapper's Delight," "Planet Rock" and Kurtis Blow's "The Breaks" all peaked at No. 4 R&B. Meanwhile rap seeped into other hits: Wham! and Malcolm McLaren's "Buffalo Gals" in England, and Frankie Smith's "Double Dutch Bus," Zapp's vocoder electro jams including "More Bounce to the Ounce," and Cameo's "She's Strange" on US R&B charts. "The actual rap singles would hit a glass ceiling in Billboard."
**Second Wave and Third Wave** : The first wave showed rap's potential and shifted focus from the DJ to the MC. Run-DMC's 1983 debut launched the second wave. 1988 brought third-wave debuts from N.W.A, Slick Rick, MC Lyte, EPMD, Big Daddy Kane and Biz Markie. "The emphasis had begun to shift from the DJ to the MC."

# label - Def Jam Recordings

## curiosities
**Early Rap Label** : One of the first labels specializing in rap, started by Rick Rubin in his NYU dorm room and co-founded with Russell Simmons. Its first two major signings, LL Cool J and the Beastie Boys, both proved pivotal. Radio became its first gold album in April 1986, and Licensed to Ill its first No. 1. Both Rubin and the Beastie Boys later fell out with the label. "One of the first labels specializing in rap."

# label - Profile Records

## curiosities
**Run-DMC's Label** : Profile signed Run-DMC before Def Jam existed and released their debut, King of Rock, Raising Hell and the "Walk This Way" single. "The rap trio was already signed to Profile, another early rap label."

# label - Sugar Hill Records

## curiosities
**Sylvia Robinson's Label** : Founded by soul singer and mogul Sylvia Robinson, it signed the Sugarhill Gang, whom she assembled, and released early Grandmaster Flash and Melle Mel singles including "White Lines," which she co-conceived. "Signed to her Sugar Hill Records label."

# concert - Live Aid 1985

## curiosities
**Run-DMC as Rap's Sole Representative** : On July 13, 1985, Run-DMC were the only rap act on the bill, performing at JFK Stadium in Philadelphia with no band, only Jam Master Jay. "They made history by being the only rap act invited to perform at Live Aid."

# concert - Together Forever Tour 1987

## curiosities
**Run-DMC and Beastie Boys Co-Headline** : With both Raising Hell and Licensed to Ill topping the charts, the two crews shared equal billing, but the Beasties' stunts dominated the mostly negative coverage. "The two crews shared equal billing."

# curiosity

**Chart Week of September 27, 1986** : When "Walk This Way" peaked at No. 4, the songs above it were Lionel Richie's "Dancing on the Ceiling" (No. 3), Carl Anderson and Gloria Loring's "Friends and Lovers" (No. 2) and Huey Lewis and the News' "Stuck with You" (No. 1). Casey Kasem noted that Aerosmith had taken the song to No. 10 in 1977. "Now run DMC rapid to number four."
**MTV and Rap in 1986** : By 1986 MTV had moved past some of the institutional racism of its early years, regularly playing Prince, Whitney Houston, Lionel Richie and Janet Jackson, but it still played little to no rap. Yo! MTV Raps arrived two years later. "MTV was still playing little to no rap."
**Sue Cummings and the Spin Connection** : Spin writer Sue Cummings, profiling both Run-DMC and Aerosmith in early 1986, noticed the rappers knew "Walk This Way." She asked Rick Rubin for a Run-DMC tape to give Aerosmith and suggested the groups meet, making her one of several people who claim credit for the remake. "They say success has many fathers and mothers."
**Radio Resistance to Rap** : Through the mid-1980s, Def Jam, Profile and other rap labels fought radio's reluctance to play rap, at Black stations as well as pop stations, which preferred Freddie Jackson, Whitney Houston and Janet Jackson. "Even black radio stations preferred the smoother or pop-friendly or sounds."
