## curiosities
**Song Rejection by The Beatles** : Harrison was "typically limited to just two harrassongs per LP" by The Beatles, and many of his songs were rejected by John Lennon and Paul McCartney, even as his material kept getting better. "By then, Harrison was used to songs of his getting rejected by the beetles."

"But that's when George Harrison said, in essence, hold my beer."
**Constipation Analogy for Songwriting (1970)** : George Harrison cheekily and "rather queasily" analogized the release of his "All Things Must Pass" album in Craig Rosen's "The Billboard Book of Number One Albums," stating he had a "backlog of songs" that needed to be released. "I've always looked at all things must pass like somebody who's had constipation for years, and then finally they get diarrhea. I had a backlog of songs."
**My Sweet Lord Plagiarism Case (1971)** : "My Sweet Lord" was the basis for the most infamous song plagiarism case in 20th century pop, being a "note for note rewrite" of The Chiffons' 1963 number one hit "He's So Fine." Bright Tunes, the production company behind The Chiffons' record, sued Harrison in a case that dragged on for years. Though Harrison pleaded that the replication was accidental, the judge, while convinced of his good intentions, "could not excuse his transgression," charging him with "subconscious plagiarism" and ordering him to pay Bright Tunes. This case remains the "archetypal music plagiarism case" for two songs in different styles but with identical melodies. "He's so fine. He was a number one hit for Girl Group The Shafans in 1963, and the basis for what became the most infamous song plagiarism case in 20th century pop."
**First Simultaneous #1 Single and Album (1971)** : George Harrison was the first former Beatle to score a simultaneous number one single and album, with "My Sweet Lord" and "All Things Must Pass." This feat would "continue to elude his former bandmates throughout 1971." "So, for the first few years after the Fab For's breakup, only George Harrison scored a simultaneous number one single and album."
**World's Favorite Ex-Beatle (1971)** : For the first few years after The Fab Four's breakup, George Harrison was "the world's favorite ex-Beatle" due to his chart success. "Harrison was now the world's favorite ex-beetle, and his divine smash song topped charts around the world from Canada to New Zealand."
**Concert for Bangladesh (1971)** : Harrison spent the latter half of 1971 organizing this event, which was described as "a seminal event in the history of cause supporting music." It spawned another triple LP, a live album that won the Grammy for Album of the Year, and Harrison proudly performed his "still-beloved, still faithful number one hit" (My Sweet Lord) at the concert and on the album. "Harrison spent the latter half of 71 organizing the concert for Bangladesh, a seminal event in the history of cause supporting music."
