# artist - Glass Animals

## genres
Alt-Pop

## songs
**Heat Waves (2022) - Billboard's No. 1 Song of 2022** : In the first week of December 2022, Billboard announced that Heat Waves was the top Hot 100 single of 2022, beating Harry Styles' As It Was. Heat Waves spent only five weeks at No. 1, one third as long as As It Was. It won on longevity: it was already in the top 10 in the first week of the chart year and stayed there for nearly five months before As It Was even appeared. Billboard did not make a subjective call here; the result came from the chart data. "Heat waves was on the chart practically all year."

## curiosities
**Four-Man Band from Oxford** : Glass Animals are a four-man alt-pop band from Oxford, England. Their year-end win could start a hit-making streak in America or be the start and end of their run as American hitmakers. "this four-man alt-pop band from Oxford, England."
**Year-End Win Decided by Extra Chart Weeks** : Glass Animals had many more weeks of chart data than Harry Styles during the 2022 chart year, and those extra weeks gave them the year-end title. "All those extra weeks of chart data for Glass Animals easily trumped styles in their year and hot 100 match."

# artist - Harry Styles

## albums
**Harry's House (2022) - Album Featuring As It Was** : As It Was was the lead single from Harry's House. "the lead single from the Harry's House album topped America's flagship pop chart for an amazing 15 weeks."

## songs
**As It Was (2022) - 15 Weeks at No. 1, Yet Not Year-End No. 1** : As It Was topped the Hot 100 for 15 weeks. That was the longest run at No. 1 of any song in 2022 and one of the four longest runs at the top for any single ever. Rolling Stone, The Guardian, NME, Entertainment Weekly and Consequence of Sound all named it one of the 10 best singles of the year. On Billboard's year-end Hot 100 it still lost to Glass Animals' Heat Waves, which had been on the chart nearly five months before As It Was showed up. "Not only the longest run at No.1 for any song this year, but one of the four longest runs on top for any single ever."

# artist - Sam the Sham and the Pharaohs

## songs
**Wooly Bully (1965) - Top Song of 1965 Despite Peaking at No. 2** : Billboard named Wooly Bully the top song of 1965. It never reached No. 1 and peaked in the runner-up slot in the summer of 1965. It rode the chart for 18 weeks, at a time when big hits often cycled on and off the chart in as little as 10 weeks. Its 18 weeks beat the 14 weeks of the Rolling Stones' Satisfaction, even though four of Satisfaction's weeks were at No. 1. It was the first song in Hot 100 history to top the year-end chart after peaking at No. 2, and it would not be the last. "Wolley Bulley was the first hit in Hot 100 History to best all comers on the year end chart despite peaking in the runner-up slot."

# label - Billboard

## curiosities
**How the Hot 100 Formula Works** : Since its founding in 1958, the Hot 100 has used its own formula that averages record sales and radio airplay. For more than a decade it has also included streamed tracks. The formula is complex enough that the year-end result can be genuinely suspenseful. "the Hot 100 has always had a bespoke formula, averaging record sales and radio airplay."
**Upset Rate - 26 of 65 Years** : In the 65 years Billboard has named a top Hot 100 song of the year, the song with the most weeks at No. 1 lost the year-end crown 26 times, an upset about 40% of the time. "26 out of 65 times, a song other than the one with most weeks at number one has taken the crown."
**Guideline 1 - Longevity Beats Peak** : Total weeks on the chart matter more than weeks at No. 1, as Wooly Bully over Satisfaction and Blinding Lights over The Box both show. "the first three rules for year and billboard chart placement are longevity, longevity, longevity."
**Guideline 2 - The Chart Year and the "Last Christmas Factor"** : Billboard announces its year-end charts in early December, so its chart year runs from early December through late November. Songs and albums that are strong during the previous holiday season get a big advantage on the next year's year-end list. "its chart year typically runs from early December through late November."
**Guideline 3 - Murky Methodology in the Analog Era** : Before computerization, year-end charts were compiled by hand. That led to fudged results, such as 1966 having two official No. 1s, and to "ghosted" hits that were left off entirely. "the charts, including the year end charts, were compiled quite literally by hand."
**SoundScan Era Improvements** : Since the charts were computerized in the SoundScan era beginning in 1991, Billboard totals all sales, streaming and radio data over each 52-week period and ranks every hit that charted during that time. Hits that straddle two years now commonly appear on both years' lists. "Billboard's data integrity has improved dramatically over the years."
**Guideline 4 - Superstardom Doesn't Help** : Madonna, the Rolling Stones, Donna Summer, Diana Ross and the Supremes, Michael Jackson, Janet Jackson, the Notorious B.I.G. and Eminem all have multiple Hot 100 toppers but no year-end Hot 100 No. 1. Their fans spread their support across several songs by the same artist. "Fans are spreading their love around to multiple songs by that hitmaker."
**Year-End Billboard 200 Album Chart** : Billboard publishes year-end lists for nearly everything it ranks. Past year-end No. 1 albums include The Monkees' More of the Monkees (1967), Fleetwood Mac's Rumours (1977) and Bon Jovi's Slippery When Wet (1987). For much of its history the album chart was driven only by sales; it now combines sales, tracks and streams. "The year end Billboard 200 album chart is also widely followed."

# curiosity

**1965 - Year of Classics, Won by a No. 2 Hit** : 1965 had a huge run of No. 1s: Satisfaction, a string of Beatles hits, three Supremes chart-toppers, No. 1s by the Temptations and the Four Tops, and multi-week No. 1s by the Righteous Brothers and Sonny & Cher. Billboard's December 1965 year-end issue still ranked none of them first; the top song was Wooly Bully, which peaked at No. 2. "not a single one of these songs ranked as the top song of the year."
**Casey Kasem's American Top 40 Year-End Countdown** : American Top 40 aired year-end specials of the 100 biggest records, such as the 1983 survey that ended with the Police's Every Breath You Take. "this is American Top 40 Special Survey of the 100 biggest records in the USA during 1983."
**Sean Ross's Lost Hits Project** : Radio analyst Sean Ross has identified dozens of "ghosted hits" that never received a year-end ranking, including some that topped the Hot 100. "has identified dozens of so-called ghosted hits that never got a year end ranking at all."
**Year-End No. 1s by One-Hit Wonders** : Year-end No. 1s have gone both to lasting acts and to one-off hitmakers. Domenico Modugno, Los del Río and Daniel Powter are among the acts with year-end No. 1s who are compared with long-running hitmakers such as the Beatles, Blondie and the Black Eyed Peas. "Or are Glass Animals faded to go the way of Dominico Moduño, Loz Del Rio, or Daniel Powder?"
