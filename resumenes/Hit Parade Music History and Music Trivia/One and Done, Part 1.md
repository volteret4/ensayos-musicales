Synth-pop

## member of

## songs
**Tainted Love (1982)** : This song, a cover of a Gloria Jones single, peaked at number eight in the summer of 1982. "Soft Cell's Tainted Love peaked at number eight in the summer of 1982, and it was a huge sleeper hit, spending 43 weeks on the Hot 100."
**What? (1982)** : This was Soft Cell's next US single, which bubbled under the Hot 100, peaking at number 101 in the fall of 1982. It fell off Billboard's bubbling under chart even before "Tainted Love" departed the Hot 100. "Bubbled under the Hot 100, peaking at number 101 in the fall of 82."

## curiosities
**US Chart Absence After "What?"** : After their single "What?" flopped, Soft Cell never again appeared on the US singles charts. "After What? Flopped, Soft Cell never returned to the US single's charts again."
**Pure One-Hit Wonder Status** : Soft Cell is categorized as a "pure one-hit wonder" because they appeared on the Billboard Hot 100 only once with their hit "Tainted Love" and never charted again, making them part of a relatively short list of artists on VH1's countdown that fit this strict definition. "Gary newman and his fellow British synth pop act soft sell or what I like to call pure one hit wonders each of them hit the hot 100 just one time then never again."

## songs

"Los Del Rio even charted briefly with a seasonal version called Macarena Christmas which mixed the Latin dance jam with tolling bells and holiday hymns from joy to the world to jingle bells."

## curiosities

This unique situation cemented their status as a "de facto one hit wonder" and highlighted America's significant obsession with the dance in 1996. "Los Del Rio are a de facto one hit wonder who three pitted with the same song proving if nothing else that America was just really obsessed with doing the Macarena in 1996."

# curiosity

**Origin of the Term "One-Hit Wonder"** : The phrase "one hit wonder" was not originally coined to describe musical artists. It dates back to the early 20th century in baseball, where it referred to a pitcher who limited the opposing team to a single base hit in a game. "The phrase one hit wonder according to language website the phrase finder was not even coined to describe musical artists and it once connoted success it dates to the early 20th century in baseball a one hit wonder was a picture who held the opposing team to a single base hit."
**Early Musical Usage of "One-Hit Wonder"** : By the 1970s, a Winnipeg newspaper reporter used the term "one hit wonder" in a story about the Swedish band ABBA, noting that after winning the 1974 Eurovision Song Contest with "Waterloo," they "escaped the curse of being a one hit wonder." By the 1980s, Billboard magazine was also using the term; in a 1981 edition, a radio researcher applied it to Gary Numan whose breakthrough single "Cars" was only a year old. "By the 80s billboard magazine was using the term in a 1981 edition of the magazine a radio researcher used the term one hit wonder to refer to an artist whose breakthrough single was at the time only a little over a year old."
**VH1's "100 Greatest One Hit Wonders" Countdown (2002)** : VH1, a music and pop culture TV channel, compiled several countdowns of "greatest one hit wonders." Their first multi-decade countdown was released in 2002, hosted by William Shatner, and covered songs from the 1960s to the early 2000s, including tracks like Question Mark and the Mysterians' "96 Tears" and the Baha Men's "Who Let the Dogs Out." "VH1's first one hit wonder countdown covered multiple decades and it dates all the way back to 2002 it was hosted by none other than William Schappen."
**Discrepancies in VH1's One-Hit Wonder Lists** : VH1's one-hit wonder countdowns often included artists that most chart fanatics would not consider true one-hit wonders. For example, if a strict definition of a "pure one-hit wonder" (an artist appearing only once on the Hot 100) were applied, 75% of the artists on VH1's 2002 list would be disqualified. "These countdowns were filled with artists that most chart fanatics would not consider one hit wonders."
**"Co-tails Hit" Phenomenon** : A "co-tails hit" is a follow-up single that rides the chart on the "afterburn" of a much bigger preceding hit, often culturally perceived as a one-hit wonder despite charting again. Michael Sembello's "Automatic Man" (number 34) after "Maniac" (number one) is an example. "Automatic man was what I call a co-tails hit riding the chart on the afterburn of its much bigger predecessor maniac."
