## albums
**The Fame (2008)** : Gaga's debut album, named after her ambition and Andy Warhol's concept of ordinary people transforming into stars. The album showcased her as a shapeshifter, hedging her bets by recording in a variety of pop modes to cover all commercial bases, including a hip-hop track with Flow Rida, California alt-pop emulating Gwen Stefani, and disco throwbacks for club audiences. It landed on the album chart in November 2008 and eventually peaked at number two on the Billboard album chart, staying in the top 10 for most of 2009.
"On her album The Fame, named not only for Stephanie Germanada's ambition, but also for Andy Warhol's idea that ordinary people can remodel themselves into stars, Gaga proved a quick study and a shapeshifter."

**The Fame Monster (2009)** : A follow-up mini-album released by Interscope, consisting of eight new songs. It was conceived as a brand extension that could be packaged with "The Fame" or sold independently, a common industry practice in the late 2000s and early 2010s to bolster compact disc sales. Despite being an industry "ploy" and longer than most Beatles LPs at over 34 minutes, it became Gaga's most acclaimed and eclectic album, earning a Grammy nomination for Album of the Year on its own. It spawned multiple top 10 hits and solidified Gaga's status as a major pop artist, influencing the sound and rhythm of radio by mainstreaming EDM.
"Here was the twist, this mini album by Lady Gaga, which, by the way, at more than 34 minutes, was as long or longer than most Beatles LP's, wound up being Gaga's most acclaimed, most eclectic album."

**Born This Way (2011)** : Gaga's second full-length album, released in May 2011. It debuted at number one on the album chart and sold 1.1 million copies in its first week, a rare sales achievement reminiscent of an earlier music business era. This impressive sales figure was aided by Amazon pricing the digital download at 99 cents, with over 400,000 Americans purchasing it at that rate, though even without this "fire sale," it would have outsold other albums by a significant margin. The album featured some of Gaga's most rock-oriented music, allowing her to explore influences from artists like David Bowie and Bruce Springsteen.
"Born This Way not only debuted on the album chart at number one in May of 2011, it sold 1.1 million copies in its first week, a rare sales level, more typical of the music business of a decade earlier."

**Duets II (2011)** : A chart-topping American songbook album by Tony Bennett, to which Lady Gaga contributed a track. This project, which featured 17 singing partners including legends like Aretha Franklin, Natalie Cole, and Willie Nelson, included Gaga's duet on "The Lady Is a Tramp." Critics highlighted the strength of Gaga's vocals and her clear rapport with Bennett, a collaboration that, at the time, was not widely recognized as a pivotal moment for her future career direction.
"Tony Bennett tapped Gaga as one of his 17 singing partners on duets, too, his second straight collection of American songbook collaborations."

**Artpop (2013)** : Lady Gaga's third album, announced in early 2013 and launched with a massive promotional campaign in the fall of that year, including an "art-rave" album launch party at the Brooklyn Navy Yard where Gaga appeared in a flying dress. Despite rumors of tens of millions of dollars spent on its launch, the album debuted at number one but sold less than a quarter of "Born This Way"'s first-week sales. Critics had mixed reactions, often finding the music overly familiar and less compelling than previous works, leading to industry speculation about financial losses, although it eventually achieved platinum status through streaming.
"When the Art Pop album arrived in November, it debuted at number 1, but with less than one fourth as much in sales as Born This Way had generated in its first week in 2011."

## songs
**Just Dance (2008)** : Lady Gaga's debut single, released in early spring 2008. The electro-dance pop track, seen as party music, was an anomaly on 2008 radio, which was dominated by Southern rap from artists like Lil Wayne, Flow Rida, and T.I. It slowly climbed the charts, reaching number two on Billboard's Club Play chart in the summer, debuting on the Hot 100 two months later, and finally hitting number one on the Hot 100 in January 2009, nine months after its release. Critics were charmed by its catchiness but also bemused by its celebration of inebriation, with lyrics about getting "blackout drunk." The song's intro follows a hip-hop tradition by audibly branding its producers and labels: "Red One," "Convict," and "Gaga."
"Released in the early spring of 2008, just dance grew slowly."

**Poker Face (2008)** : The second single from "The Fame," released in 2008. This "irresistibly robotic" track became the top-selling global single of 2009, selling nearly 10 million copies worldwide. In the US, it has sold over 7 million digital downloads, a feat also achieved by "Just Dance," making Gaga the only artist with two of the top 20 digital singles of all time. It topped the Hot 100 in April 2009, securing Gaga's second consecutive number one hit and setting the stage for a year of chart dominance. The song is described as being about Gaga's inscrutability and "the impossibility of leading her face."
"The irresistibly robotic poker face became the top-selling global single of the year, shifting nearly 10 million copies in 2009 worldwide."

**LoveGame (2009)** : A "Doom Dance Jam" that became Lady Gaga's third smash single, reaching the top five in the summer of 2009.
"By the summer, she had a third smash when the Doom Dance Jam Love Game reached the top five."

**Paparazzi (2009)** : An "airy, dream-like" single from "The Fame" that peaked at number six in the fall of 2009. Critics praised it as Gaga's most nuanced single to date, interpreting it as a meditation on the yearning for fame and the personal cost it exacts in the era of reality television.
"Critics praised paparazzi as Gaga's most nuanced single yet, a meditation on the yearning for what Gaga called the fame and the price it exacted in the age of the reality store."

**Bad Romance (2009)** : The lead single from "The Fame Monster," described as Gaga's most iconic single. It features a pounding beat, a soaring chorus, and "irresistible nonsense words" likened to "Wap Babalula." The song reached number one in over 20 countries but peaked at number two on the Hot 100, held back by chart-toppers from Jay-Z and Kesha. Its music video, featuring Gaga's "Little Monsters" and homages to Stanley Kubrick's "A Clockwork Orange" and Michael Jackson's "Thriller," won Video of the Year at the 2010 MTV Video Music Awards.
"She called it The Fame Monster, and she let it off with her most iconic single of all time, with a pounding beat, a soaring chorus, and irresistible nonsense words that belong alongside a Wap Babalula in the Pantheon of Rock and Roll gibberish, bad romance reached number one in more than 20 countries."

**Telephone (2010)** : A collaborative single with Beyoncé that became a top 10 hit, spun off from "The Fame Monster." The accompanying music video was a nine-minute "mini-movie" depicting Gaga and Beyoncé dancing in and breaking out of a women's prison, notable for its extensive product placements, including Virgin Mobile phones and Miracle Whip and Wonder Bread.
"Within weeks, it spun off another top 10 hit with telephone, a collaborative single with fellow diva Beyoncé."

**Alejandro (2010)** : A Europop-infused track from "The Fame Monster" that reached the top 10 by the summer of 2010. It drew influences from Abba and Ace of Base, further demonstrating the album's depth while maintaining its characteristic dance beat.
"By the summer, Gaga was back in the top 10 with the Europop infused, Abba and Ace of Base homage, Alejandro, further establishing the depth of the album, but maintaining the beat."

**Speechless (2009)** : A track from "The Fame Monster" that Lady Gaga performed live with Sir Elton John at the 2010 Grammy Awards, showcasing her live performance capabilities.
"On the 2010 Grammys, Gaga reinforced her live chops by performing the Fame Monster track Speechless, live with Sir Elton John."

**Born This Way (2011)** : The lead single and title track from Gaga's second full-length album. It debuted at number one on the Hot 100 in February 2011 and remained there for a month and a half. The song resonated with audiences for its message of self-empowerment, LGBTQ+ rights, and the embrace of difference, themes that would later appear in the opening scenes of "A Star Is Born." Musically, it was an homage to earlier dance and disco, particularly Madonna, leading some critics to accuse it of pilfering Madonna's 1989 hit "Express Yourself," while others defended it as a pastiche borrowing motifs from songs like Patrick Hernandez's "Born to Be Alive."
"Born this way was both the lead single and title track of Gaga's second full length album."

**Judas (2011)** : The second single from the "Born This Way" album, described as a blend of a straightforward pop banger and "lurching cutting-edge electrohouse." Despite its "radio unfriendly refrain," the song managed to reach the top 10.
"Despite its radio unfriendly refrain, Judas hit the top 10 in."

**Edge of Glory (2011)** : A song from the "Born This Way" album featuring legendary E Street Band saxophonist Clarence Clemens, providing a vintage 80s-style saxophone solo. Clemens also filmed the video for the song with Gaga in June 2011, just six days before his death at age 69. The single peaked at number three on the Hot 100 that summer, serving as a final chart homage to Clemens.
"On the edge of glory, she even teamed with legendary Bruce Springsteen and the East Street band Saxophoneist Clarence Clemens for a vintage 80s style saxola."

**Yoü and I (2011)** : A hit song that Lady Gaga performed at the 2011 MTV Video Music Awards while in character as her male alter ego, Joe Calderone, and backed by guitarist Brian May of Queen.
"Still in drag, Gaga performed her hit song, and I, backed by guitarist Brian May of Queen."

**The Lady Is a Tramp (2011)** : A track from Tony Bennett's "Duets II" album where Lady Gaga collaborated as one of his 17 singing partners. Critics highlighted the strength of Gaga's vocals and her obvious rapport with the jazz legend on this American Songbook standard. The duet's significance to Gaga's future career path was not apparent at the time.
"But his pairing with Gaga on The Lady is a tramp was for many the stand-at."

**Applause (2013)** : The first single from the "Artpop" album, released in August 2013. Lady Gaga encouraged her fans via social media to buy, request, and stream the song repeatedly on Spotify to boost its chart performance. It peaked at number four on the Hot 100, a respectable showing, but was overshadowed by bigger hits from artists like Katy Perry, Miley Cyrus, and Lorde later that year.
"When the first single, A Plaws, landed in August, Gaga encouraged her army of fans on social media to not only buy and request the song, but to leave it playing on repeat on Spotify, hoping to boost it up the charts."

**Do What U Want (2013)** : A song from the "Artpop" album featuring R. Kelly, which became a number 13 hit in late 2013. Lady Gaga later expressed regret for the collaboration and removed the track from her album after further revelations of R. Kelly's alleged sexual misconduct.
"Do what you want was a number 13 hit in late 2013, but Gaga would later come to regret the song."

**Dope (2013)** : A "simplest, most heart-rending" ballad from the "Artpop" album. Lady Gaga performed it at a late 2013 YouTube Music Awards live event, where she appeared to be crying with a shaking voice, making the self-bearing lyrics, "I need you more than Dope," particularly poignant. The performance was widely praised and streamed enough on YouTube for the song to briefly reach the Billboard Top 10.
"At a late 2013 YouTube Music Awards live event, Gaga performed the album's simplest, most heart-rending song, a ballad called Dope."

## curiosities
**Birth Name and Stage Name Origin** : Lady Gaga was born Stephanie Joanne Angelina Germanotta. She adopted her stage name, Lady Gaga, in homage to an old hit song by the band Queen.
"When the woman born Stephanie Joanne Angelina Germanada was ready to dance."

**Early Acting Aspirations and Education** : Growing up on New York's Upper West Side, Germanotta initially aspired to be an actress, with music serving as a backup plan. She studied at the Lee Strasberg Theatre and Film Institute and was admitted to New York University's musical theatre conservatory and theatre company but dropped out after only a year due to frustration with fruitless auditions. This experience mirrored the "right nose" theme in "A Star Is Born."
"Growing up on New York's Upper West Side, Germanada had first wanted to be an actress."

**Decision to Go Solo and Early Career** : After becoming frustrated with the entertainment system and unsuccessful auditions, Germanotta decided to strike out on her own in the mid-2000s. She worked three jobs to pay her rent, made her own music, and recorded in her apartment. During this period, she toured the Lower East Side downtown scene, performing in underground burlesque shows with performance artist Lady Starlight and writing songs like "Beautiful, Dirty Rich" that glamorized her Bohemian lifestyle.
"So I decided to go off on my own, pay my own rent, work three jobs, make my own music, and record in my apartment."

**Versatile Songwriting and Influences** : Early in her career, Gaga demonstrated versatility as a songwriter, capable of working in modes ranging from heavy metal to cabaret and piano ballads. Her idols included John Lennon, Yoko Ono, and Andy Warhol, and her musical and fashion influences spanned David Bowie, Led Zeppelin, Madonna, and Grace Jones.
"Fortunately, Gaga was a capable and versatile songwriter in a range of modes, from heavy metal to cabaret and piano ball."

**Discovery and Initial Label Deal** : Lady Gaga's talent was recognized by several key figures, including singer-songwriter and talent scout Wendy Starland, who discovered her rock band. Starland then introduced Gaga to producer-songwriter Rob Fusari, known for co-writing number one hits for Will Smith and Destiny's Child. In 2006, music executive L.A. Reed signed Gaga to Island Def Jam, then a leading label group, but dropped her three months later due to uncertainty about how to market her.
"celebrated music executive L.A. Reed signed Gaga in 2006 to Island Def Jam, then the leading label group in the industry, and then dropped her three months later, unsure of what to do with her."

**Second Label Deal and Akon's Role** : A year after being dropped by Island Def Jam, Gaga was introduced by Streamline president Vincent Herbert to Jimmy Iovine of Interscope Records, who ultimately signed her. Her breakthrough was significantly aided by Akon, a Senegalese-American R&B singer and a leading crossover pop and hip-hop artist of the mid-to-late 2000s. Akon, impressed by a reference vocal from Gaga, insisted on signing her to Con Live, his vanity label with Interscope, and was involved in her first single, "Just Dance," contributing to its production and bringing in Colby O'Donis.
"Acon was one of the leading singers of crossover pop and hip-hop of the mid to late Auts."

**Reinvention and Pivot to Dance Pop** : Lady Gaga's initial success with "Just Dance" came after years of striving and reinventing herself. She deliberately pivoted from a "stripped down rock" style, characteristic of her early Stephanie Germanada Band days, to choreographed dance pop to achieve mainstream success. This shift was a strategic move to fit into the music industry of the late 2000s, which was heavily dominated by hip-hop and where pure dance music struggled on Top 40 radio.
"Lady Gaga had finally topped the charts after years of striving and years of reinventing herself."

**Persona and Authenticity** : Gaga's persona is described as a creation and a facade, yet also authentic, reflecting her eclectic pop influences, drive, and ambition. This aspect of artifice, authenticity, and agency was central to her early "star is born moment." The question of whether there was a "real" Lady Gaga was often debated, with the perspective that she "faked it so real she was beyond fake," a paraphrase of Courtney Love.
"If I may paraphrase Courtney Love, Gaga faked it so real she was beyond fake."

**The House of Gaga** : Weeks after the release of her debut album, "The Fame," Lady Gaga launched her own creative brand, "The House of Gaga." Modeled after Andy Warhol's Factory, this team was responsible for conceptualizing and devising her looks for music videos, as well as costumes and sets for her first global tour.
"Within weeks of the release of her album, she launched her own creative brand, The House of Gaga, modeled after Andy Warhol's factory."

**Meat Dress at the VMAs (2010)** : Lady Gaga wore a dress made entirely of raw meat to the 2010 MTV Video Music Awards to collect her "Moonman" statue. She stated that the dress was a protest against the US government's "Don't Ask, Don't Tell" policy regarding gays in the military.
"Gaga showed up at the VMAs to collect her Moonman statue wearing a dress made entirely of raw meat, which she called a statement in protest of the US government's Don't Ask, Don't Tell policy against gays in the military."

**Impact on Radio and EDM** : The success of "The Fame" and "The Fame Monster" cemented Lady Gaga's status as a pop queen and was credited with changing the sound and rhythm of radio. Disk jockeys and club officials acknowledged that she brought dance music back to the mainstream and played a significant role in the mainstreaming of Electronic Dance Music (EDM) by reintroducing the "four on the floor" beat to American radio.
"one thing you gotta remember about Lady Gaga, she put four on the floor back on American radio."

**Joe Calderone Alter Ego at VMAs (2011)** : At the 2011 MTV Video Music Awards, Lady Gaga appeared in drag as a "crotch-grabbing, foul-mouthed male alter ego" named Joe Calderone to give a speech. While in this persona, she performed her hit song "Yoü and I" with Queen guitarist Brian May. This performance highlighted her engagement with identity politics and her willingness to push boundaries.
"At the 2011 MTV VMAs, she even showed up to give a speech as a crotch-grabbing, foul-mouthed male alter ego named Joe Calderon."

**The Born This Way Ball Tour (2012-2013)** : Lady Gaga embarked on a year-long global concert tour, "The Born This Way Ball," visiting many parts of the world for the first time. Had it been completed, it would have ranked among the top 15 highest-grossing tours of all time. However, the tour was cut short due to Gaga's health issues.
"In 2012 and 2013, Gaga embarked on a year-long concert tour she dubbed The Born This Way Ball, visiting parts of the world she had never toured before."

**Health Issues and Tour Cancellation** : Due to relentless activity and onstage dance moves, Lady Gaga developed a labral tear in her right hip. This led to the cancellation of the last 21 dates of "The Born This Way Ball" tour, followed by hip surgery. She was later diagnosed with fibromyalgia and began using opioids for chronic pain management.
"After months of pain from her onstage dance moves, Gaga discovered she had developed a labral tear in her right hip."

**"Artpop" Promotional Campaign and Financials** : The launch of "Artpop" in 2013 involved an elaborate promotional campaign, rumored to have cost Interscope Records "tens of millions of dollars." This included an "art-rave" album launch party at the Brooklyn Navy Yard, featuring Gaga in a flying dress. Despite these efforts, industry rumors suggested the label would write off losses from the album, although it eventually went platinum due to streams.
"Rumors spread throughout the industry that the label had spent tens of millions of dollars just to launch the album."

**Emotional Performance of "Dope"** : At a YouTube Music Awards live event in late 2013, Gaga performed the song "Dope" from her "Artpop" album. The performance, streamed widely and briefly pushing the song into the Billboard Top 10, was noted for Gaga's visible emotion, with her appearing to cry and her voice shaking, making the poignant lyrics "I need you more than Dope" even more impactful, leading to fan concern over the toll her career was taking.
"At a late 2013 YouTube Music Awards live event, Gaga performed the album's simplest, most heart-rending song, a ballad called Dope."

**Pivot Towards Respectability and Acting (Mid-2010s)** : Halfway through the 2010s, Lady Gaga seemed to pivot away from her art-pop spectacle, seeking a "new kind of respectability." This apparent about-face led to her first official frontline movie role in "A Star Is Born" (2018), where she showcased her skills as both a singer and actress, earning Grammy and Oscar nominations in 2019. This transition posed questions about whether the persona of an acclaimed actress was merely another "costume change" in her career.
"And then, halfway through the decade, she seemed to pivot the other way, seeking a new kind of respectability."

# curiosity

**"A Star Is Born" Franchise History and Themes** : The "A Star Is Born" franchise is Hollywood's ultimate "metamovie," reflecting the lead actress's real-life career trajectory. The first version, released in 1937, starred silent era film mega-star Janet Gaynor, who needed the film for a comeback after falling off in the mid-1930s; she won the first Best Actress Academy Award in 1927. This version codified the dramatic scene where the rising female star's award acceptance is interrupted by her drunken, declining male spouse. Judy Garland's 1954 version turned it into a musical, depicting her character, Esther Blodgett, as a power vocalist dreaming of a music and movie career, even envisioning her record hitting number one on the "Hit Parade." Both Gaynor and Garland received Oscar nominations for their roles. Barbara Streisand's 1976 version, despite critical dismissal, was the third highest-grossing film of the year. This iteration was entirely focused on the music business, not the movie business, with the lead character, Esther Hoffman, winning a Grammy, not an Oscar. It was also the first version to grapple with questions of rock and roll legitimacy in the rock era, with Streisand's character seeking the "swagger" and "cultural authority" of a rock star, even if her own music was a unique blend of show tunes, diva balladry, and rock. Bradley Cooper's 2018 remake specifically chose to adapt the 1976 version, focusing most intensely on questions of musical and artistic legitimacy, with Cooper's character, Jackson Maine, expressing disdain for Ali's (Lady Gaga) turn towards highly produced dance pop.
"It's because A Star's Boy is Hollywood's ultimate metamovie, a reflection of the lead actress's own career in real life"

**Music Critic Debate: Rockism vs. Pop-timism** : Discussions among music critics about Bradley Cooper's 2018 "A Star Is Born" have been heated, particularly concerning the long-standing debate between "rockism" and "pop-timism." Some critics, like Karen Gans and John Perelis of the New York Times Popcast, argued the movie was fundamentally "pro rock" or even "rockist," implying that it suggested pop music is meaningless and lacks authenticity compared to rock, which is portrayed as enduring and emotionally true. Conversely, critic Karen Tongson on Maximum Fun's Pop Rocket contended that pop ultimately triumphs in the film, viewing the final orchestral ballad as reaching the "upper echelon of pop," rather than a revalorization of rock.
"Discussions of a star is born among music critics specifically. Those who have spent the last two decades debating concepts like rockism and poptumism. The debate among these critics has been heated."

**Mainstreaming of the "Four on the Floor" Beat** : Lady Gaga is credited with effectively bringing the "four on the floor" beat back to American radio. Prior to her breakthrough, this rhythmic pattern, foundational to dance music, was largely absent from mainstream pop music. Her chart-topping hits helped to re-popularize this beat, significantly influencing the sound and rhythm of radio and contributing to the mainstreaming of Electronic Dance Music (EDM).
"one thing you gotta remember about Lady Gaga, she put four on the floor back on American radio."

**Compact Disc Industry Practice (Late 2000s/Early 2010s)** : The practice of releasing "mini albums" or EPs/LPs as brand extensions to existing full-length albums was common in the late 2000s and early 2010s. This strategy, exemplified by Lady Gaga's "The Fame Monster," was a "last-ditch effort" by the music industry to prop up the compact disc format a decade after its peak, with critics often viewing it as a commercial ploy rather than an artistic necessity.
