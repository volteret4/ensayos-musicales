## albums

## songs
**Can't Buy Me Love (1964)** : This single debuted all the way up at number 27 and was on Capitol Records, serving as the official follow-up to "I Want to Hold Your Hand." It was also the lead-off single to the movie "A Hard Day's Night," which the Beatles were filming at the time. The song achieved 2.1 million global retail pre-orders, surpassing even Adele's 2015 blockbuster "Hello" in its opening week sales. It made an improbable leap from number 27 to number 1 in its second week, setting a new chart record and becoming the Beatles' third straight number 1 single without interruption from other artists. "Can't Buy Me Love was preceded by global retail pre-orders of 2.1 million copies."
**Twist and Shout (1964)** : This song was a cover of the Isley Brothers' classic, released by VJ Records on its Tali subsidiary label as a single in America. It had originally been the last track on the Beatles' debut album, "Please Please Me," recorded with John Lennon fighting a cold, and was never issued as a single in England. Despite being an album cut, it was beloved and performed for the Queen at the Royal Command Performance in November 1963. Its US release during Beatlemania allowed it to become a smash hit, and it later re-charted in 1986, peaking at number 23 due to its prominent placement in the movie "Ferris Bueller's Day Off," making it the only Beatles song to enjoy two full top 40 chart runs in America. "In England, Twist and Shout was never issued as a single."
**She Loves You (1963)** : This exuberant record defined the group for many during the first wave of Beatlemania with its three-part harmonies from John, Paul, and George, its "yeah, yeah, yeah" chorus, and high-pitched "ooohs." Capitol Records initially refused to release it, and due to VJ Records' financial troubles, EMI's Trans-Global team had to beg smaller regional label Swan Records to license and release the single for practically no money. It eventually rose to number 3 on the Hot 100 on April 4, 1964, and had been number 1 the prior week, succeeding "I Want to Hold Your Hand" at the top. "She loves you an exuberant record that for many who remember the first wave of Beatle Mania, utterly defined the group in the public imagination."
**I Want to Hold Your Hand (1963)** : This smash hit was originally scheduled for a January 1964 release by Capitol Records, but was rush-released two days after Christmas 1963, a month ahead of schedule, due to a Washington DC teenager's request on local radio station WWDC. It debuted on the Billboard Hot 100 at number 45 for the week ending January 18, 1964, and hurtled to number 1 in just two weeks. It held the top slot for seven weeks before being replaced by "She Loves You." Its B-side, "I Saw Her Standing There," received so much airplay that it charted on its own. "Early radio airplay kicked off by WWDC forced capital records to rush release the 45 RPM vinyl single to market."
**Please Please Me (1963)** : Originally penned by John Lennon in a slow, stately, Roy Orbison-like style, this song was sped up at producer George Martin's request, becoming the group's first major hit in their homeland. It topped both the New Musical Express and Melody Maker charts and reached number 2 on the official Record Retailer chart in Britain. It was the first Beatles single issued in America, released by the Chicago independent label VJ Records in February 1963, though the band's name was misspelled "Beattles" on the label. It charted at number 5 on the Hot 100 on April 4, 1964. "Please please me, a song John Lennon originally wrote in a slow, stately, Roy Orbison mode before speeding it up at George Martin's behest became the group's first major hit in their homeland."
**Love Me Do (1962)** : The Beatles' debut single in England, recorded with producer George Martin in September 1962. John Lennon adapted its harmonica intro from Delbert McClinton, who played with Bruce Channel. It decently reached number 17 on the UK charts, boosted partly by manager Brian Epstein buying extra stock. Despite EMI's efforts to license it to various American labels in 1962, it was initially rejected. It finally appeared on the US chart week ending April 11, 1964, more than 18 months after its recording, in the version featuring Ringo Starr on tambourine and session drummer Andy White on the full drum kit. "Recorded with producer George Martin in September 1962, Love Me Do did decently for a debut single, reaching number 17 on the UK charts."
**From Me to You (1963)** : This song, specifically written by Lennon and McCartney to make their teen followers swoon, was a "classic bit of fan service." Released by VJ Records in the US, it appeared on Billboard's "Bubbling Under the Hot 100" chart, peaking at number 116 in August 1963, missing the main Hot 100. American artist Del Shannon's cover of the song actually reached number 77 two months earlier. Despite being the Beatles' second US single, American disc jockeys largely ignored it. "From Me to You holds a particular distinction. It was the first Beatles song to appear on a Billboard chart."
**I Saw Her Standing There (1963)** : This irresistible song was the B-side of the "I Want to Hold Your Hand" 45 single. It received so much radio airplay in the US that it began charting on its own, debuting at number 68 for the week ending February 8, 1964. "I saw her standing there was on capital, but it wasn't actually a new release."
**My Bonnie (1961) - Tony Sheridan & The Beat Brothers** : This cover of a traditional Scottish folk song was recorded by the Beatles as a backing group for Hamburg, Germany-based rock and roll singer Tony Sheridan in 1961, before they landed their own recording contract. After the Beatles' explosion in America, the MGM label dug this single out of their vaults and released it, capitalizing on anything with the Beatles' name on it, even with Sheridan's German-accented English vocals. "After the Beatles' explosion in America, the MGM label dug the single out of their vaults and threw it on the market."
**Roll Over Beethoven (1963)** : A cover of Chuck Berry's song, featuring George Harrison on lead vocal, this track charted in the US as an import. It had been released in November 1963 on the Capitol of Canada label, whose executives were more optimistic about the Beatles' prospects than their American counterparts. This Canadian release found its way into US record shops and charted on the Hot 100, demonstrating the intense demand during the height of Beatlemania. "Roll Over Beethoven charted as an import."

## curiosities
**Liverpool Origin and Early Rejections** : The Beatles, a quartet of "Liver Puddlyans," were looked down upon by Londoners as provincial and notoriously turned down by a half-dozen British record companies, including EMI (initially) and Decca. Decca's A&R head, Dick Row, famously dismissed them with the line, "groups with guitars are on the way out," after an audition on New Year's Day 1962. "Notoriously, the Beatles were turned down by a half dozen British record companies, including EMI, parent company of the label that eventually wound up signing them."
**Signing with Parlophone** : The Beatles eventually signed with the then-lowly Parlophone Records, a subsidiary of EMI, in June 1962. This label, led by producer George Martin, at the time made most of its profits from comedy recordings by artists like Peter Sellers, underscoring the initial underestimation of the band's musical potential. "So when the Beatles finally signed to a UK label in June of 1962, it was with the then-lowly parlorphone records, a subsidiary of EMI, led by producer George Martin, that at the time made most of its profits from comedy recordings by the likes of Peter Sellers."
**US Labels' Resistance** : Despite EMI owning America's Capitol Records and offering them first refusal on British repertoire, Capitol consistently refused to issue Beatles records in the US, considering British music unviable. This resistance forced EMI to try to lease their records through an subsidiary, Trans-Global Music, to other labels like Atlantic, Columbia, RCA, and Mercury, all of whom passed. Capitol's post Dave Dexter even proclaimed the Beatles "Stone Cold Dead in the US marketplace." "The record men in Hollywood virtually never took that option."
**VJ Records' Early Release and Missteps** : Chicago independent label VJ Records, run by African American husband and wife team Vivian Carter and James Bracken, became the first to issue a Beatles single in America, "Please Please Me," in February 1963. They initially misspelled "Beatles" as "Beattles" on the label. VJ's ability to promote the band was hampered by a royalty dispute with The Four Seasons and a gambling debt incurred by its president, which decimated the company's 1963 operating budget. "No unknown were the Beatles in America at this time that, on the label of this first VJ single of Please Please Me, the band's name was misspelled with two T's in Beatles."
**Manager Brian Epstein's Struggle in America** : Brian Epstein, the Beatles' manager, faced significant indifference and dismissal in the United States, even as late as November 1963 when the band was achieving record-breaking sales in the UK. He found no one willing to attend a reception for him, and the West Coast Capitol label issued yet another refusal to meet with him, highlighting the immense difficulty in breaking the band in the American market. "Beatles' manager Brian Epstein, showing up in the States as late as November 63 and finding no one willing to attend a reception for him, even as the Beatles were racking up record breaking sales in the UK."
**EMI's Mandate to Capitol Records** : The managing director of EMI, Len Wood, was forced to personally fly to New York and, for the first time ever in the EMI-Capitol relationship, command Capitol's president to issue the Beatles' next single, "I Want to Hold Your Hand," regardless of their reluctance. Even after this command, Capitol planned to press only 5,000 copies and scheduled its release for a full month after Christmas. "The managing director of EMI, Len Wood, having to personally fly over to New York and for the first time ever in the EMI capital relationship, command capital's president to issue the Beatles next single like it or not."
**Marsha Albert's Pivotal Radio Request** : A Washington DC teenager named Marsha Albert played a critical role in breaking the Beatles in America. After catching a televised news report about the band in mid-December 1963, she called her local radio station, WWDC, to request their brand new single, "I Want to Hold Your Hand," which Capitol was not planning to issue until January 1964. The station obtained an import copy and even allowed Miss Albert to introduce the song live on air, kicking off early radio airplay that forced Capitol to rush release the single. "Even before the legendary Ed Sullivan, it was a Washington DC teenager named Marsha Albert, who caught a televised news report about the Beatles and, in mid-December, 1963, called her local radio station, WWDC, in our nation's capital, to request that they play something by the band."
**Chart Dominance and Multi-Label Success (April 4, 1964)** : For the week ending April 4, 1964, the Beatles achieved the unprecedented feat of locking down the entire top five positions on Billboard's Hot 100, a record that has yet to be repeated 53 years later. This was possible because their commanding hits were on four different labels: Swan ("She Loves You"), Capitol ("I Want to Hold Your Hand"), Tali ("Twist and Shout"), and VJ ("Please Please Me"). "They locked down the entire top five, 53 years later, that feat has yet to be repeated."
**Successors at Number One** : For the week ending March 21, 1964, "I Want to Hold Your Hand" gave way to "She Loves You" at number one, making the Beatles the first act to succeed themselves at number one on a Billboard chart since Elvis Presley did it in 1956 with "Don't Be Cruel" and "Love Me Tender." "The Beatles were the first act to succeed themselves at number one on a Billboard chart, since Elvis Presley had done it in 1956, with his back-to-back smashers don't be cruel and love me tender."
**Most Songs on Hot 100 Record** : For the week ending April 11, 1964, the Beatles set a new mark for the most songs by a single act on the Hot 100, with 14 charting hits. This record, which they held for over 40 years, was a "fluke" made possible by so many labels simultaneously flooding the market with their 45s. Four of these 14 hits were on Capitol, while the other ten were on various imprints including Tali, VJ, Swan, and Capitol of Canada. "The Beatles set a new mark for most songs by a single act on the hot 100, a record they would hold for more than 40 years."

# label - Capitol Records

This stubbornness meant that EMI's managing director had to personally command Capitol to release "I Want to Hold Your Hand." "But a more complete version of this tale would chronicle just how stubborn capital records EMI's American subsidiary was about the band."
**Rush Release of "I Want to Hold Your Hand"** : Forced by unexpected early radio airplay ignited by a Washington DC teenager, Capitol Records had to rush release the 45 RPM vinyl single of "I Want to Hold Your Hand" two days after Christmas, 1963, a month ahead of their planned schedule. This rapid deployment was crucial in allowing Americans to finally experience England's hottest group. "Early radio airplay kicked off by WWDC forced capital records to rush release the 45 RPM vinyl single to market."
**1986 Rerelease of "Twist and Shout"** : While The Beatles' 1964 chart run of "Twist and Shout" was on the small Tali label, its 1986 chart run, spurred by its prominence in "Ferris Bueller's Day Off," was released on Capitol. This marked a shift in label for the song's second Top 40 appearance. "But the 86 run was on capital."

# label - VJ Records

## curiosities
**Founding and Early Success** : VJ Records was a Chicago independent label founded and run by the African American husband and wife team of Vivian Carter and James Bracken. The "V" and "J" in the label's name represented their initials. VJ established its reputation with smash R&B records, such as Gene Chandler's "Duke of Earl," and scored hits in 1962 with the Jersey Boy Doo-wop group The Four Seasons. "Founded and run by the African American husband and wife team of Vivian Carter and James Bracken, the V and J namesakes of the label, VJ made its bones with smash R&B records, like Jean Chandler's Duke of Earl."
**First US Label for The Beatles** : VJ Records made history by becoming the first label to issue a Beatles single in America, releasing "Please Please Me" in February 1963. This occurred after Atlantic Records and other major US labels passed on the opportunity. The Beatles' name was even misspelled "Beattles" on this first single's label, indicating their unknown status in the US at the time. "Think of it. The first label to issue a Beatles single in America was a black-owned imprint based on Chicago's south side."
**Financial Troubles and Mismanagement** : In 1963, VJ Records experienced significant financial difficulties, including a royalty dispute with The Four Seasons who sought a better deal, and a gambling debt incurred by VJ's president in Las Vegas, which "decimated the company's 1963 operating budget." These issues severely hindered VJ's ability to effectively promote and distribute Beatles singles, causing the label to miss opportunities despite holding the rights. "VJ was distracted by a royalty dispute with the Four Seasons, who were angling to leave the label for a better deal."
**Exploiting Loopholes During Beatlemania** : As Beatlemania exploded in America, VJ Records, despite its earlier financial woes, aggressively exploited "every possible loophole in their agreement with EMI" to release as much Beatles product as possible. This included issuing "Twist and Shout," an album cut in England, as a single on their newly founded Tali subsidiary, and bringing older singles like "From Me to You" to the Hot 100. "VJ was finding every possible loophole in their agreement with EMI to release as much Fab Four product as possible."

# label - EMI

## curiosities

This entity was tasked with leasing EMI's British repertoire across the United States to any label willing to distribute it, which eventually led to deals with VJ and Swan Records for Beatles releases. "In 1962, frustrated with capital, EMI had set up a subsidiary, trans-global music, to lease its records across the United States, to whatever label was willing to distribute them."

**Infamous Rejection of The Beatles** : Decca famously turned down The Beatles after an audition in a London studio on New Year's Day 1962. Their A&R head, Dick Row, dismissed the band with the now immortal line, "groups with guitars are on the way out." This decision became one of the most infamous blunders in music history, later satirized in "The Ruttles" TV movie. # curiosity

**Most Famous Week in Pop Chart History (April 4, 1964)** : The week ending April 4, 1964, is renowned as the most famous week in pop chart history because The Beatles achieved unprecedented dominance, locking down the entire top five positions on Billboard's iconic Hot 100. This feat had not been repeated 53 years later. "It's been called the most famous week in pop chart history."
**Misjudgment of British Music in America (Early 1960s)** : In the early 1960s, the US recording industry largely considered England a "musical backwater," showing little interest in British music even before the rock era. British artists were typically relegated to the lower rungs of US charts, a perception reinforced by the "oddities" of the only two British chart-toppers in 1962 (Mr. Acker Bilk and The Tornados). This prejudice explained why America was "not predisposed to care about a group of scouselads" like The Beatles. "To the US recording industry, England was a musical backwater."
**Dick Row's Infamous Quote** : Dick Row, A&R head at Decca Records, delivered one of music history's most notorious rejections to The Beatles on New Year's Day 1962, stating, "groups with guitars are on the way out." This quote, often repeated, became so infamous that Monty Python's Eric Idle and Saturday Night Live's Dan Aykroyd riffed on it in the satirical TV movie "The Ruttles." "Dick Row, the man who turned down the signing of a lifetime, dismissed the lads with the now immortal line, groups with guitars are on the way out."
**The Beatles as Inventors of the Self-Contained Rock Band** : The Beatles are credited with inventing "the self-contained rock band as we now know it." This aspect of their legacy signifies their pivotal role in shaping the modern identity and structure of rock groups, where members not only perform but also write their own material. "The inventors of the self-contained rock band as we now know it."
**Beatlemania as an Organic Phenomenon (Post-JFK Assassination)** : The story of The Beatles' American breakthrough is often framed as the US public organically "catching Beatlemania from England like a virus." This spread occurred at a time when America's "defenses were down" in the wake of President John F. Kennedy's assassination in November 1963, contributing to a collective need for cultural uplift. "It is a poetic tale of the US public organically catching beetle mania from England like a virus at a time when our defenses were down in the wake of President John F. Kennedy's assassination in November 1963."
**Billboard Hot 100 Methodology (1964)** : In 1964, Billboard's Hot 100 was a hybrid chart combining sales and airplay of singles, and its "pre-computerized methodology" made it "impossible for songs to debut at number 1" and difficult to reach the top in less than a month or two. This context highlights the extraordinary achievement of The Beatles' "Can't Buy Me Love," which shot to number 1 in its second week. "At a time when Billboard's pre-computerized methodology made it impossible for songs to debut at number 1, and difficult to get there in less than a month or two, can't buy me love did the improbable."
**Biggest Leap to Number One Record Broken** : The Beatles' record for the biggest leap to number one, set by "Can't Buy Me Love" jumping from number 27 to the top, has been shattered multiple times. American Idol winner Kelly Clarkson broke it twice: first with "A Moment Like This" (from #52 to #1 in 2002) and again with "My Life Would Suck Without You" (from #97 to #1 in 2009). "For example, The Biggest Leap to Number One, when Can't Buy Me Love shot from number 27 to the top, has been beaten several times."
**Most Songs on Hot 100 Record Broken (21st Century)** : The Beatles' record of 14 simultaneous Hot 100 hits, held for over 40 years since April 1964, has been broken multiple times in the 21st century. This change is attributed to the "technological quirk" of streaming music, which allows artists like Drake, Ed Sheeran, and The Weeknd to chart virtually every song on a new album in a single week. "The record of 14 simultaneous hot 100 hits has also been shattered multiple times in the 21st century."
**"The Billboard Book of Number One Hits"** : Chart historian Fred Bronson's classic reference book dedicates a page to "Can't Buy Me Love," detailing the chart records set by the song, including biggest leap to number one, most consecutive number ones, and greatest monopoly of the Hot 100. Some of these records have since been shattered. "In his classic reference book, The Billboard Book of Number One hits, Chart historian Fred Bronson devotes the page on Can't Buy Me Love to running down all of the chart records that song set."
**"Ferris Bueller's Day Off" and "Twist and Shout" (1986)** : The 1986 John Hughes movie "Ferris Bueller's Day Off" featured a prominent placement of The Beatles' "Twist and Shout." This led to the song charting on the Hot 100 again, peaking at number 23 in September 1986, giving it a second Top 40 run in America. "Generation Xers who remember the 80s as fondly as I do, will recall the prominent placement of the Beatles' Twist and Shout in the 1986 John Hughes movie Ferris Bueller's Day Off."
