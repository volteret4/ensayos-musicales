# artist - James Brown

## songs
**Get Up (I Feel Like Being a) Sex Machine (1970) - The Most Famous "Bridge" Reference** : A 1970 smash in which James Brown makes what is probably the most famous reference to the bridge of a song ever recorded. Mid-song he calls out to his band to take the listener "to the bridge", the contrasting middle section of a song that moves it from verse and chorus to somewhere new. "He literally takes you to The Bridge."

# artist - Madonna

## albums
**Ray of Light - Electronic Reinvention and Grammy Triumph** : Madonna returned from a long hiatus and chose to become an electronic artist at a time when electronic pop was barely established. On paper the album should have been a strange curio in her catalog. Instead it became one of her most critically acclaimed albums and won her first ever competitive mainline Grammy. It also served as a comeback after she was, in effect, censured for the first time in her career in the early 1990s, following her storied 1980s, although she kept scoring hits throughout that decade. "it shouldn't have worked."

## curiosities
**Early-'90s Backlash** : After her storied 1980s, Madonna was, in effect, censured for the first time in her life and career in the early 1990s. She never really went away and kept scoring hits all decade, but Ray of Light functioned as a comeback. "Madonna was in effect censured for the first time in her life and career early in the 90s."

## songs
**Always (1995) - Final Top Five Hit and Longest Hot 100 Run** : Bon Jovi's final top five hit was a ballad that reached No. 4 on the Billboard Hot 100, peaking in early 1995. It spent a stunning 32 weeks on the Hot 100, more than any other Bon Jovi song, and is described as an "improbable smash." "Always spent a stunning 32 weeks on the Hot 100 peaking in early 1995."

# artist - Def Leppard

## genres
Hair Metal
New Wave of British Heavy Metal
Heavy Metal

## curiosities
**New Wave of British Heavy Metal Origins** : Def Leppard came up with a group of bands labeled the New Wave of British Heavy Metal, alongside Judas Priest and Iron Maiden. When the term was coined, "new wave" did not refer to synthesizers or new wave fashion. It simply meant the next wave of metal bands. "they didn't mean new wave like synthesizers and Kaja Gugu hair or anything like that."
**Meticulous Mutt Lange Productions** : Def Leppard's 1980s hits were produced by Mutt Lange and are described as heavily produced, meticulous and synthy records rather than down-and-dirty rock and roll. The comparison made is to a British synth-pop new wave band that happened to play heavy metal. "Those Deaf Leopard hits from the 80s are produced within an inch of their lives."

## songs
**Hysteria - Compared to The Cure** : Critic Rob Sheffield once compared Def Leppard's "Hysteria" to The Cure. A similar comparison is made to Orchestral Manoeuvres in the Dark, suggesting the band could pass for a British new wave act that decided to become a hair metal band. "I believe Rob Schuffield once compared their song, Historia to the Cure."

# artist - Aretha Franklin

## labels
Columbia
Atlantic

## songs
**I Never Loved a Man (The Way I Love You) - Atlantic-Era Classic** : One of the songs recorded after Franklin moved to Atlantic in roughly 1966–67, now considered legendary. "I recorded all the songs that we now consider legendary, like I never loved to man the way I love you."
**Respect - Atlantic-Era Classic** : Recorded during Franklin's Atlantic period after her largely hitless Columbia years. It is now considered one of her legendary songs. "and respect and chain of fools and all those classics."
**Chain of Fools - Atlantic-Era Classic** : Another of the Atlantic-era recordings now considered among her legendary classics. "chain of fools and all those classics."

## curiosities
**Rock Hall Induction in 1987 - Among the First Three Women** : Aretha Franklin was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 1987. She was one of the first three women or female acts inducted, alongside Diana Ross and the Supremes (1988) and Bessie Smith (1989). "Aretha Franklin in 1987 and Bestie Smith in 1989."
**Columbia Years and the Move to Atlantic** : Franklin recorded for Columbia in the early 1960s and had almost no hits, just one minor hit. For about five years she recorded several more albums that did little. She was then picked up by Atlantic around 1966–67, where she recorded her legendary songs. Because the Hall of Fame's 25-year eligibility rule counts from a first recording, her early Columbia start made her eligible earlier than her Atlantic fame might suggest. "Aritha Franklin recorded for the Columbia label in the early 60s and had almost no hits, like one minor hit."

## curiosities
**Brooklyn VMAs and the Thicke–Cyrus Performance** : The 2013 MTV Video Music Awards were held in Brooklyn, New York. # curiosity

**Despacito's YouTube Record** : "Despacito" set a record by becoming the first video on YouTube to surpass three billion global views, and it later passed five billion. Five years earlier, PSY's "Gangnam Style" had been the first to pass one billion. "the song Despacito set a record when it became the first video on YouTube to surpass three billion global views."
**Rock and Roll Hall of Fame Gender Gap** : Of 323 inductees into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, only 45 are women, just 13.9%. Inductions began in 1986. "Only 45 are women. That's just 13.9%."
**Rock Hall 25-Year Eligibility Rule** : Artists are not eligible for the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame until 25 years after their first recording. This explains why the Supremes (first recordings around 1963–64) and Aretha Franklin (early-'60s Columbia recordings) were inducted so early. "you aren't eligible to be inducted until 25 years after your first recording."
**The Bridge in Song Structure** : In popular music, the bridge is a transitional section in the middle of a song that contrasts with the verse and chorus and moves the song from one point to another. "It's the section that contrasts with the verse and the chorus and gets you from point A to point B."
