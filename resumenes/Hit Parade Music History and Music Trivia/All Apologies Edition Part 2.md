## albums
**Dookie (1994) - Major-Label Debut** : Green Day had spent nearly half a decade in the Bay Area underground punk scene, releasing catchy, thrashy albums on the small Berkeley label Lookout Records. They then signed to Warner Music and issued Dookie in 1994, proving pop punk's commercial potential almost immediately. "they proved pop punk's commercial potential almost immediately."

## songs
**Longview (1994) - Ode to Suburban Ennui** : The bass-heavy, fiendishly catchy song about suburban ennui topped the Modern Rock chart in June 1994. It won the band a slot on that year's Lollapalooza tour and at Woodstock 94. "The bass heavy, fiendishly catchy, long-view, and ode to suburban on Wii, topped Billboard's Modern Rock Chart in June of 94."
**Basket Case (1994) - Post-Woodstock No. 1** : The week after their muddy, mayhem-filled Woodstock 94 set, Green Day returned to the top of the Modern Rock chart with this caffeinated, classically punk song, which was even catchier than "Longview." It stayed at number one for more than a month. "Basket Case stayed at number one for more than a month."
**When I Come Around (1995) - Top 40 Embrace** : The crunchy song put Green Day back on top of the rock charts in early 1995. It also made the top 10 on Billboard's radio songs chart, drawing about as much pop airplay as current hits by Boyz II Men, Sheryl Crow and TLC. "When I come around, made the top 10 on Billboard's radio songs chart."

# genre - Alternative Rock

## curiosities
**Parallel-Universe Top 40** : By 1994, alt rock was functioning as America's parallel-universe Top 40. Bands like Stone Temple Pilots, Counting Crows, Gin Blossoms and Collective Soul were in heavy rotation on both alternative and pop stations. Top 40 stations like New York's Z100 or Houston's KRBE played Mariah Carey and Boyz II Men back to back with Pearl Jam or Soundgarden. "Alternative Rock was a parallel universe top 40."
**Radio Programmers: "Modern Rock Is Pop"** : Billboard quoted program directors who saw alt rock as in sync with the pop charts. A KRBE programmer said "a lot of what's termed alternative music now is just pop with an edge," and an Atlanta program director said "modern rock is pop. It's just new pop." "modern rock is pop. It's just new pop."
**Pop for Misfits** : In 1994–95, listeners could flip between Top 40 (TLC, Ace of Base) and alt rock stations (Veruca Salt, Urge Overkill, Liz Phair). Functionally it was all accessible, youthful, sometimes danceable pop music. "Alt Rock was now just pop for misfits."
**The "Fauxternative" Late 90s** : In the late 90s, pop struck back. Top 40 moved toward millennial teen pop, and on rock radio some alt bands were essentially pop acts in tattooed, gelled drag. It was the era of the alt-rock one-hit wonder: bands such as the Verve Pipe, Eve 6, Harvey Danger and Semisonic each scored a chart-topping Modern Rock hit and a gold or platinum album, then filled used-CD bins for years. "the late 90s saw a parade, a flash in the pan bands."
**Masculinization of Alt Radio** : After the 1996–97 peak of Alanis Morissette and Lilith Fair, the Modern Rock chart became much more male-dominated, through pogoing ska punk, grimacing post-grunge, nu metal and rap rock. "proved to be a last hurrah for women on alternative rock radio."
**21st-Century Alternative Format** : Male bands from Cage the Elephant to the Black Keys still top the chart most weeks. The format has become as entrenched in the 21st century as AOR was in the late 20th. More recently, quirky pop from Lana Del Rey, Paramore, Elle King and Billie Eilish has topped the chart. "These bands are the backbone of the alternative radio format."

# genre - Post-Grunge

## curiosities
**Post-Cobain Takeover** : After Cobain's death in 1994, a wave of post-grunge took over the Modern Rock chart and radio. Bands channeled R.E.M.'s jangle through Nirvana's brooding, watering grunge down into jangle pop. "a wave of post Grunge is about to take over Billboard's modern rock chart."
**Frosted-Tips Era** : By 1997–98, fratty, punchy, slick post-grunge from Smash Mouth, Sugar Ray and Third Eye Blind flooded the charts. "It was a good time to be a bro with frosted tips, a soul patch, and a quirky voice."

# curiosity

**Modern Rock Chart Expanded to 40 Positions (1994)** : In September 1994, six years after launching the Modern Rock chart, Billboard expanded it from 30 to 40 positions. The move acknowledged that a genre born at college stations and a handful of commercial alternative stations had become the hottest format on radio. "Billboard expanded the chart from 30 to 40 positions."
**Chart Renamings** : Billboard renamed the Modern Rock chart "Alternative Songs" in 2009, and later "Alternative Airplay," its current name. "Billboard had renamed alternative songs in 2009."
**First Modern Rock No. 1 in 1988** : The chart launched in 1988 with Siouxsie and the Banshees at number one. "launched in 1988 with Suzy Su at number one."
