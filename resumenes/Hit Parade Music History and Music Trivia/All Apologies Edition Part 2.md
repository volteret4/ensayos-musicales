# genre - Alternative Rock

## curiosities
**Diversification and Expansion (Pre-Cobain Death)** : Even before Kurt Cobain's passing, alternative rock was diversifying and becoming quirkier, with chart-toppers like Blind Melon's "No Rain" and Beck's "Loser" demonstrating how far "alternative nation" had expanded. "Even before Kurt Cobain passed, alternative rock was diversifying and getting quirkier."
**America's Parallel Universe Top 40 (1994)** : By 1994, with the expansion of Billboard's Modern Rock chart from 30 to 40 positions, alternative rock was explicitly acknowledged as "America's parallel universe top 40," with bands like Stone Temple Pilots and Counting Crows receiving heavy rotation on both alternative and pop radio. "Alternative Rock was a parallel universe top 40."
**"Pop with an Edge" / "New Pop" (1994)** : In 1994, Billboard stories quoted radio program directors who saw alternative music as congruent with the mainstream "hit parade." One programmer stated, "a lot of what's termed alternative music now is just pop with an edge," while another plainly said, "modern rock is pop. It's just new pop." "a lot of what's termed alternative music now is just pop with an edge, a K-R-B-E programmer said."
**Pop for Misfits (Mid-90s)** : By the mid-90s, alternative rock was functionally considered pop music—accessible, youthful, and sometimes even danceable. It became "pop for misfits," catering to listeners craving pop hooks but finding them on alt-rock stations. "Functionally, this was all pop music, accessible, youthful, sometimes even danceable. Alt Rock was now just pop for misfits."
**Faux-Turnitive Period (Late 90s)** : By the late 90s, alternative rock entered its "faux-turnitive period" where pop music "struck back." Some so-called alt rock bands were essentially pop acts in "tattooed and gelled drag," and it was arguably the last time these video-friendly bands could credibly call themselves alternative. "By the late 90s, alternative rock was entering what I call its faux-turnitive period."
**Shift to Millennial Teen Pop (Late 90s)** : By the late 90s, Top 40 radio began "scurrying away from alternative rock" towards "millennial teen pop," indicating a divergence between mainstream pop and the evolving alternative sound. "By the late 90s pop struck back, and not just on top 40 radio, which went scurrying away from alternative rock toward millennial teen pop."
**"Bro-ification" of Radio (Late 90s)** : The late 90s saw alternative radio become much more "dude-centric" with a wave of "pogoing ska punk and grimacing post-grunge" taking over, marking a shift away from the earlier successes of women in the genre. "The modern rock chart was about to get much more dude-centric as a wave of pogoing, and the sky punk and grimacing post-grunge took over."
**End of Distinctive Identity (End of 90s)** : By the end of the 90s, the rise of bands like Creed signified the end of alternative rock's distinctive identity, losing its role as a place for either the "weirdo wit of the 80s Goth bands" or the "progressive ideals Nirvana and Pearl Jam espoused in the early 90s." "What the rise of bands like Creed signified by the end of the 90s was the end of alternative rock's distinctive identity."
**21st Century Entrenchment** : In the 21st century, the alternative radio format has become as entrenched as AOR (Album-Oriented Rock) radio was in the late 20th century, with male bands like Cage the Elephant and The Black Keys forming its backbone. "These bands are the backbone of the alternative radio format, which has become as entrenched in the 21st century as AOR radio was in the late 20th century."
