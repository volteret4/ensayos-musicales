# artist - Bing Crosby

## genres
Traditional Pop
Crooner
Christmas Music

## songs
**Swinging on a Star (1944) - Year's Longest-Running Number One** : Spent 10 cumulative weeks at number one on Billboard's pop singles charts (best-selling retail records and most-played jukebox records) in 1944, the most of any chart-topper that year. It became a standard largely because of its prominent place in *Going My Way*, the Crosby-starring film that won Best Picture for 1944. At the same Oscar ceremony the song won Best Original Song, which probably helped Crosby win Best Actor that night. Despite all this, it is not considered his signature hit. "It's spent a total of 10 cumulative weeks at number one."
**White Christmas (1942) - Best-Selling Single of All Time** : Irving Berlin wrote it for Crosby's 1942 film *Holiday Inn*, and it won the Oscar for Best Song. The original 1942 recording has reportedly sold more than 50 million copies worldwide. According to Guinness World Records, counting Crosby's later re-recordings, including the 1947 version most heard on radio today, it has sold upwards of 100 million copies, more than any other recording. More than four decades after his death, it defines Crosby, though he recorded dozens of non-Christmas smash hits. "This is the best selling single period of all time."

## curiosities
**Defined by a Holiday Song Despite a Career of Hits** : Swinging on a Star won an Oscar and topped the charts, which for most musicians would make it their definitive song, the way Theme from Shaft, My Heart Will Go On and Lose Yourself define Isaac Hayes, Celine Dion and Eminem. For Crosby, White Christmas eclipses everything else. "More than four decades after his passing, White Christmas defines Bing Crosby."

"Brenda Lee is the only woman to achieve that dual enshrinement by the way."

## songs
**Sweet Lorraine (1940) - King Cole Trio Jazz Hit** : When Cole emerged as a recording artist around 1940, he was widely respected in the jazz world. Jazz fans loved hits like this one as much for his piano playing as for his vocals. "beloved by jazz heads as much for Nat Cole's sterling piano playing as for his vocals."
**Get Your Kicks on Route 66 (1940s) - Jazz-R&B Standard** : Through the mid-1940s, the trio showed Cole's knack for translating authentic jazz playing for mainstream audiences, as on this jazzy R&B standard. "Cole had a knack for translating authentic jazz playing from our mainstream audiences."
**The Christmas Song (1946) - Career-Changing Smash** : Written by jazz and pop crooner Mel Tormé and recorded by Cole and his trio in 1946, with strings added to a Cole recording for the first time. Also known as "Merry Christmas to You" or "Chestnuts Roasting on an Open Fire," it reached number three on both Billboard's pop and R&B charts and returned year after year. It changed the direction Cole pursued for most of his career. He re-recorded it multiple times, with arrangers like Nelson Riddle and on increasingly elaborate orchestral arrangements. Today it is far and away one of his most-played recordings on radio and Spotify. "It was not only a smash it changed the direction that King Cole would pursue for most of the rest of his career."
**Nature Boy and Too Young - Orchestral Pop Shift** : After The Christmas Song, Cole moved toward vocal showcases with orchestral backing, playing down his jazz roots on tracks like these. "Nat King Cole shifted toward vocal showcases built on orchestral backing down playing his jazz roots."

## curiosities
**Began Singing Only Because Audiences Encouraged Him** : As a young man, Cole was more interested in playing piano than singing. He started singing because audiences encouraged him. "Cole only began singing due to audience encouragement as a young man he was more interested in playing."
**Jazz Purists vs. Later Generations** : Jazz aficionados still revere Cole's 1930s and '40s jazz trio records and disdain his pop records. For generations born in the late 20th and early 21st centuries, he is known mainly for The Christmas Song. The song keeps his name alive but skews his place in music history. "it does skew his place in the musical Firmament."

# artist - Gene Autry

## genres
Country and Western
Country
Christmas Music

## songs
**Deep in the Heart of Texas (1942) - Pre-Country-Chart Crossover** : Before Billboard had a country chart, Autry was scoring national crossover hits like this 1942 recording. "Even before Billboard had a country chart, Autrey was scoring national crossover hits."
**At Mail Call Today (1945) - Eight-Week Country Number One** : A military-themed song about a soldier separated from his sweetheart, and an eight-week number one in 1945. It was part of 18 straight country top 10s through 1948, after Billboard's country chart launched in 1944. Singing about the military was deliberate: there was a war on, and Autry had a masculine singing-cowboy image to protect on radio and film. "Singing about the military was done by design."
**Here Comes Santa Claus (1947) - Self-Penned Holiday Entry** : Autry co-wrote and recorded it in 1947, his first move into holiday music. It was a top five country hit in 1948 and is on Billboard's Holiday 100 in 2021. "Autrey had first started edging into holiday music with Here Comes Santa Claus."
**Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer (1949) - Career-Redefining Number One** : Written by Johnny Marks, who, like Irving Berlin, was Jewish, to accompany a promotional holiday children's book from the department store Montgomery Ward. Marks later called it "one of the worst songs ever written." Autry disliked it too and rejected it at first, even as a B-side, until his wife convinced him it was sweet and coaxed him into recording it. During the 1949–1950 holiday season it hit number one on both the country and pop charts, and Autry was primarily a Christmas singer from then on. In 2021 it was on the Hot 100 for the fourth Christmas in a row. "Autrey's career already at its height was culturally rebooted."
**Up On the House Top - Holiday 100 Perennial** : Along with Rudolph and Here Comes Santa Claus, Autry's version is on Billboard's Holiday 100 in 2021. "Autrey's versions of these songs are all on Billboard's holiday 100 chart this very week in 2021."

## curiosities
**Singing Cowboy Persona** : The self-styled singing cowboy helped bring country and western to the masses as early as the 1930s. His persona was a masculine archetype on radio and film, which may be why he resisted Rudolph. "His singing cowboy persona was a masculine archetype, both on the radio and on the silver screen."

# artist - Vince Guaraldi

## member of
Vince Guaraldi Trio

## genres

## instruments

## albums
**Vince Guaraldi Trio (1956) - Self-Titled Album** : Included the trio's version of the standard The Lady's in Love with You. The trio formed in 1955. "He formed the Vince Guraldy trio in 1955."
**Bossa Nova/Samba Interpretations LP (1962) - Jobim-Era Album** : Recorded in 1962, when samba and bossa nova were at their crossover peak in the Girl from Ipanema era. It interpreted selections from composers like Antônio Carlos Jobim and produced the hit Cast Your Fate to the Wind. "the Vince Guraldy trio recorded an entire LP interpreting selections from the likes of Antonio Carlos Jobim."
**A Charlie Brown Christmas (1965) - Peanuts TV Soundtrack** : Two television producers approached Guaraldi in 1965 to score an animated holiday special based on Charles M. Schulz's Peanuts characters. The special was sponsored by Coca-Cola, used real child voice actors, explored how the holidays can be deeply sad, and established the half-hour animated special as a TV norm. The trio's jazzy versions of O Tannenbaum and The Little Drummer Boy set a new template for ambient holiday music. The LP never charted during Guaraldi's lifetime. In 2021 it reached a new Billboard 200 peak of number 9 and was number 4 on Billboard's vinyl albums chart, behind new LPs by Adele and Taylor Swift. "the LP never charted while Guraldy was alive."
**The Eclectic Vince Guaraldi (1968) - Vocal Debut** : Introduced his jazzy singing voice, which he later reused in Peanuts TV music such as the Snoopy anthem Joe Cool. "His 1968 album, The Ecclectic Vince Guraldy, introduced his jazzy singing voice."

## songs
**Cast Your Fate to the Wind (1963) - Top 40 Pop Hit** : A Latin-flavored Guaraldi original that peaked at number 22 in early 1963, higher than Dave Brubeck's number 25 pop hit Take Five in 1961. It showed Guaraldi could connect with pop audiences after rock and roll had replaced jazz as America's main youth music. "Vince Guraldy had proved that he had the soul of a jazz bow but an ear for pop."
**Linus and Lucy (1965) - Signature Instrumental** : Named for the Peanuts brother and sister. Not specifically Christmas-themed, but it became both a holiday perennial and his signature song. It recurred in later specials like It's the Great Pumpkin, Charlie Brown, A Charlie Brown Thanksgiving and It's the Easter Beagle, Charlie Brown, but remains most tied to Christmas. It was in the top 30 on Billboard's Holiday 100 at the time of recording. "one instrumental and original by Guraldy that was not specifically Christmasy became both a holiday perennial and his signature song."
**Christmas Time Is Here (1965) - Holiday 100 Perennial** : Returns to Billboard's Holiday 100 every year along with Linus and Lucy and O Tannenbaum. "come back year after year."
**Joe Cool - Snoopy Anthem** : Peanuts TV music that reused the singing voice introduced on The Eclectic Vince Guaraldi. "The Snoopy the Dog Anthem Joe Cool."

## curiosities
**Opened for Art Tatum** : A serious jazz player inspired by Oscar Peterson and Bill Evans. Around the turn of the 1950s he warmed up crowds for Art Tatum. "At one point around the turn of the 50s, he was even warming up crowds for art Tatum."
**Early Death at 47** : Guaraldi died of a heart attack in 1976 at 47. He was satisfied with his children's-TV-infused recording career. "When Vince Guraldy died from a heart attack in 1976, sadly he was only 47."
**No Longer Considered Kids' Music** : Even with real children singing on his tracks, Guaraldi's holiday music is no longer seen as children's music. "Guraldy's music, thanks to the holidays, is no longer really considered kiddie music."

Composer Henry Mancini's original was the chart hit, peaking at number 11 on the Hot 100, and won Mancini both the Oscar for Best Original Song and the Grammy for Record of the Year in 1962. **It's the Most Wonderful Time of the Year (1963) - Streaming-Era Hit** : A new song on the 1963 Christmas album. It dominated radio and Spotify for the six Christmases before 2021, doing a little better each year on the Holiday 100 and Hot 100. In the 2020 holiday season it reached a new Hot 100 peak of number five. In 2021 it was back in the top 10 and still rising. "Last year during the 2020 holiday season, it reached a new hot 100 peak of number five."
**Do You Hear What I Hear? (1965)** : From the 1965 LP Merry Christmas; number 78 on Billboard's Holiday 100 the week of recording. "which by the way is number 78 on Billboard's Holiday 100 this week."

## albums
**A Christmas Gift for You from Phil Spector (1963) - Wall of Sound Holiday Classic** : A seasonal compilation showcasing Spector's Wall of Sound. Love was arguably the album's star and the only vocalist credited as a solo singer. She led three songs: Winter Wonderland, White Christmas (made famous by Bing Crosby), and the original Christmas (Baby Please Come Home). "The only vocalist on the compilation credited as a solo singer was Darleen Love."

## songs
**He's a Rebel (1962) - Number One Credited to The Crystals** : Spector wanted his version out before a competing one by Vikki Carr. The Crystals, a separate girl group in his roster, couldn't reach the studio in time, so Spector recorded The Blossoms with Love on lead and printed The Crystals' name on the single. It was her first time singing on a Spector recording and spent two weeks at number one on the Hot 100 in November 1962. "simply printed the crystals name on the He's a Rebel single."
**He's Sure the Boy I Love (1963) - Second Faux-Crystals Hit** : Another single credited to The Crystals with Love and The Blossoms singing. It reached number 11 in early 1963. Afterward, Love confronted Spector and demanded to sing under her own name. "Darleen confronted the producer and demanded that she be able to sing under her own name."
**Zip-a-Dee-Doo-Dah - Bob B. Soxx & the Blue Jeans** : Between the two Crystals hits, Love and The Blossoms had another hit under a different Spector act, Bob B. Soxx & the Blue Jeans. Their R&B arrangement of the Disney standard reached number 9. "Darleen Love and The Blossoms scored another hit with another Specter act."
**Christmas (Baby Please Come Home) (1963) - Signature Song** : Written by Ellie Greenwich and Jeff Barry with Spector, it was the signature song of the Spector Christmas LP. One track from the album was released as a single in 1963, but none charted on Billboard. For decades, long after Love left the controlling Spector, it was her calling card. It was covered by artists from U2 to Cher. In 2020, per MRC Data, it had over 14,000 terrestrial radio plays nationwide and over 92 million streams, 17 times the modern radio spins and 52 times the streams of He's a Rebel. "But no one could take Christmas away from Darleen Love."

## curiosities
**Joined The Blossoms in 1957** : Darlene Wright joined The Blossoms in 1957 and quickly raised their level. They recorded singles for several labels and did session work, including backing Sam Cooke. In 1962 they were invited to sing on a Phil Spector session, and Spector later renamed her Darlene Love. "He's the one who would eventually rename Darleen Wright, Darleen Love."
**28 Years on Letterman (1986–2014)** : Every holiday season for 28 years, Love sang Christmas (Baby Please Come Home) on David Letterman's late-night show, on both NBC and CBS, backed by Paul Shaffer's band. "For 28 years, from 1986 to 2014, every holiday season, like clockwork, Love would appear on David Letterman's late-night talk show."
**Rock and Roll Hall of Fame Induction (2011)** : Inducted in 2011, possibly thanks to the song. Presenter Bette Midler suggested as much, saying Love sang "lead and backgrounds for Phil Specter with and without credit." "It's safe to say also that Christmas isn't officially here until Darleen Wales Christmas baby, please come home."

# artist - Donny Hathaway

## genres
Soul
R&B
Christmas Music

## instruments

## albums
**Donny Hathaway Live (1972) - Instant Classic** : Captured his stage power. Writer Emily Lordi later said Hathaway "could bring the drama, pain and joy of soul music to an audience like nobody else." It peaked at number 18 on the pop album chart and quickly went gold, remarkable for a soul singer with few crossover hits. "Donnie Hathaway Live piqued at number 18 on the pop album chart and quickly went gold."

## songs
**A Song for You (1971) - Leon Russell Cover** : One of his first definitive versions of a classic song, Leon Russell's often-covered composition. "Among the first was his 1971 take on Leon Russell's oft-covered composition, A Song For You."
**The Ghetto (1970) - Solo R&B Peak** : Reached number 23 R&B. As a solo artist he never reached the pop top 40, and on the R&B chart he only got into the low-to-mid 20s. "Solo, Donnie never cracked the pop top 40."
**You've Got a Friend (1971) - First Crossover Hit with Roberta Flack** : Their duet of Carole King's song was his first crossover hit, reaching number 8 R&B and number 29 pop in the summer of 1971, when James Taylor also had a hit with it. "Flack's and Hathaway's duet version of Carol King's You've Got A Friend was Hathaway's first crossover hit."
**Where Is the Love (1972) - Flack & Hathaway Number One R&B** : A breezy easy-listening duet that reached number 5 pop and number 1 R&B. "Flack and Hathaway climbed even higher with the breezy, easy listener where is the love."
**This Christmas (1970) - Half-Century Climb to Pop Hit** : Recorded and co-written in 1970 with Nadine McKinnor as a deliberate attempt to create the ultimate Black Christmas song, rooted in soul and '70s R&B. Writing it was one of his proudest achievements. Producer and percussionist Ric Powell recalled Hathaway "knew what he wanted to do musically and the impact he wanted to make." It features sleigh bells, a brass breakdown, an impassioned vocal and a soul piano bridge. In his lifetime it only reached Billboard's seasonal Christmas singles chart, spending one week at number 11 in 1972. The Whispers' 1979 tribute interpolated its melody. It was reissued in 1991 on an Atco Records Soul Christmas CD, became a radio recurrent, and gave its title to the 2007 film *This Christmas* (Loretta Devine, Delroy Lindo, Idris Elba, Regina King), with a Chris Brown cover on the soundtrack. In the mid-2010s, covers by Train and Seal topped the AC chart. In 2020 the original debuted on the Hot 100 at number 39, fueled by nearly 11,000 radio spins and 63 million streams. "it took literally half a century for Donny Hathaway's sole perennial to become a pop hit."
**The Closer I Get to You (1978) - Final Flack Duet Smash** : Number 2 pop and number 1 R&B in 1978. It was the last time Hathaway heard one of his songs spread across the airwaves. "the duo scored one more huge crossover hit with the closer I get to you."

## curiosities
**Howard University Friendship with Roberta Flack** : Roberta Flack was his college classmate at Howard University, and their voices blended well. "Roberta Flack, who happened to be a college classmate of Donnie's from their days at Howard University."
**Death in 1979** : In early 1979, after a psychotic episode during a studio session, Hathaway took his own life in New York City. His death devastated Roberta Flack and the R&B community. "The loss devastated Roberta Flack as well as the entire R&B community."
