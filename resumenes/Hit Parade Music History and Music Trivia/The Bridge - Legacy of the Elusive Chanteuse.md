## albums
**Butterfly (1997) - Vocal Style Shift** : Around the release of this album, Mariah Carey's singing style began to change, introducing a more whispery, sensuous, and cooling vocal approach. This shift may have been necessitated by vocal nodules, though her voice is noted as holding up well despite 30 years of performing. "around 1997 with butterfly she started singing differently she started introducing the more whispery sort of thing."
**Glitter (2001) - Film and Album Failure** : This film and its accompanying album were described as a "super big budget" disaster and a "failed event in culture." The commercial failure of the album and its single "Loverboy" contributed to a mental health breakdown for Carey, who had never experienced a flop before as a "golden child" overachiever in pop music. It took her several years to get her career back on track after this period. "I really do think that like the failure of lover boy as well as just the album probably compounded her mental health at the time because she had never flopped before."
**E=MC² (2008) - Consistent Album** : Described by Rich Jesuyak as Mariah Carey's most consistent album. "equals MC squared I think is her most consistent album."
**Emotions (1991) - Second Album** : Her second album, where she gained more creative control. The record adopted a stronger R&B and disco sound. The title track, "Emotions," is noted as an homage to The Emotions' song "Best of My Love." "She was able to have a little bit more creative control in that record. It was more R&B more disco."
**Merry Christmas (1994) - First Christmas Album** : This was Mariah Carey's first Christmas-themed album. "there was Mary Christmas."
**Merry Christmas II You (2010) - Second Christmas Album** : This was Mariah Carey's second Christmas-themed album. "then Mary Christmas to you."
**The Elusive Chanteuse (2014) - Album Title Example** : Cited as an example of Mariah Carey's clever album titling. "any woman who names a record the elusive shantou's."
**Memoirs of an Imperfect Angel (2009) - Album Title Example** : Cited as an example of Mariah Carey's clever album titling. "something funny for the title of memoirs of an imperfect angel."

## songs
**The Roof (1997)** : Track from her 1997 album *Butterfly*. Mariah Carey built this song using Mob Deep's rap classic "Shookwans Part 2" and later invited Mob Deep to rap on a 1998 remix. This song represented a shift in her career, moving from earlier pop-centric work to more overt R&B and Hip-Hop. "Carey not only built this song out of the Mob Deep, rap classic Shookwans Part 2."
**All I Want For Christmas Is You (1994)** : The song first appeared on the Billboard Hot 100 in 2000 and eventually rose to number one in 2019, primarily due to changing technologies and chart formulation. Its appearance in the 2003 movie *Love Actually* (where it was covered by a child, not Mariah herself) had little direct impact on its chart performance, despite Mariah's original being a sizable digital seller in 2003 (the year iTunes Music Store opened and Billboard started tracking digital music). It recently set a unique chart record by plummeting from number one to completely off the Hot 100 chart, the first single in history to do so. "But honestly the story of All I Want for Christmas is you predates Love Actually. It first appeared on the hot 100 as early as the year 2000."
**Touch My Body (2008)** : The lead single from the album E=MC², known for its distinctive title. The song exemplifies the twinkling, bass-heavy style, and "plunky piano" sound characteristic of producers The-Dream and Tricky Stewart. Its vocal tone is noted as different from her other hits, featuring a playful, Madonna-like quality. "I think touch my body is just the epitome of that track."
**Vision of Love (1990)** : Mariah Carey's debut single. When it was first released, Rich Jesuyak, as a kid, initially mistook her unique vocal tone for Whitney Houston, who had a similarly unique tone. This song was one of her first five consecutive number-one singles. "I remember hearing vision of love and thinking it was Whitney Houston."
**Emotions (1991) - Single** : The first single from her second album, which also went to number one. This extended her record-breaking streak of consecutive number-one singles to five. "second album first single emotions number one as well that's a streak of five which I believe was the record at the time of them still is."
**I Don't Wanna Cry (1991)** : One of Mariah Carey's five consecutive number-one hits at the start of her career. "I don't want to cry."
**Love Takes Time (1990)** : One of Mariah Carey's five consecutive number-one hits at the start of her career. "love takes time."
**Someday (1991)** : One of Mariah Carey's five consecutive number-one hits at the start of her career. "someday."
**Can't Let Go (1992)** : This song peaked at number two on the charts in January 1992, ending Mariah Carey's unprecedented streak of five consecutive number-one singles. It featured a whispering vocal style and was noted for its R&B quiet storm aesthetic. "when it peaked at number two in January 1992 it ended carry streak of five number one."
**GTFO (2018)** : A song by Mariah Carey that was produced by 40 (Noah James Shebib). "40 produced GTFO."
**A No No (2018)** : Mariah Carey expressed a desire to collaborate with Cardi B and Little Kim on this song, which was originally a Little Kim track. However, Cardi B declined the invitation. "this song you guys were just playing a no no which was originally little Kim song."

## curiosities
**Early Career Misidentification (1990)** : As a child, Rich Jesuyak initially mistook Mariah Carey's debut single "Vision of Love" for a new Whitney Houston song due to the distinct and unique vocal tones of both artists. "I remember hearing vision of love and thinking it was Whitney Houston."
**Fanbase Obsession with Chart Records** : Mariah Carey's fanbase, known as "lambs," is described as being exceptionally vigilant and protective about her Billboard chart statistics and records, displaying an enthusiasm comparable to sports fans with their statistics. "The number one's the chart statistics as much as she sometimes pretends like it doesn't matter or she doesn't know how many number one she has she clearly does it's just part of her."
**Golden Child Status and Breakdown (Early 2000s)** : Mariah Carey was considered a "golden child" and an "overachiever" in pop music, consistently earning "straight A's" in her career. Her mental health was significantly impacted by the failure of her film and album *Glitter* and the single "Loverboy," as she had never experienced a flop before. "I really do think that like the failure of lover boy as well as just the album probably compounded her mental health at the time because she had never flopped before."
**Vocal Quality Evolution** : Mariah Carey's vocal quality in 2020 is noted as different from 30 years prior, a natural change after decades of performing, possibly linked to discussions of vocal nodules. Despite this, her voice is considered to have held up remarkably well. Rich Jesuyak expressed a preference for her more soulful singing style in later years. "it's very clear to me that in 2020 where I carried voice is not what it was 30 years ago I think it's held up pretty well considering."
**Billboard Hot 100 Chart Record (January)** : In January of the current year (2020), "All I Want for Christmas Is You" plummeted from number one on the Hot 100 to entirely off the chart, a unique and unprecedented event in the history of the Hot 100. "it just plummeted from number one on the hot 100 to off the chart the first single in hot 100 history to go from number one to not on the chart at all."
**Proximity to Beatles' #1 Record** : Mariah Carey has accumulated 19 number one hits, placing her just one shy of The Beatles' record of 20 number one hits. Achieving a 20th number one would require a new song, not a re-charting of an old one. "The Beatles have 20 number one hits Mariah has now crept one song closer she's at 19 number one hits."
**Future Collaborations and Christmas Album** : Mariah Carey has expressed interest in collaborating with artists like Cardi B and Little Kim (specifically on "A No No," which was originally Kim's song, though Cardi B declined). There have also been "rumblings" about her working with Drake. It is speculated that her next album is highly likely to be her third Christmas record. "I'm incredibly doubtful that she gets another number one."

## curiosities
**Lamb Status**: Rich Jesuyak identifies as a "lamb," a designation for Mariah Carey fans, indicating he fits the criteria for this fan group. "I would say I'm a lamb."
**Music Tastes (Late 1990s)** : Rich Jesuyak went through a phase in college in the late 1990s where he declared he no longer liked pop music. "then I kind of like in college in the late 90s just was like I don't like pop music anymore."
**Glitter Interest (Early 2000s)** : He became interested in Mariah Carey again when her film *Glitter* was coming out, drawn to it as a potential "disaster" or "failed event in culture," similar to his interest in the film *Showgirls*. "it wasn't until glitter was coming out that I was like oh I should like look at I should pay attention her because this looks like a disaster."
**Research on Mariah's Breakdown**: Rich Jesuyak reviewed old stories from "the post and daily news" regarding Mariah Carey's mental health breakdown, finding them conflicting but concluding that the commercial failure of her single "Loverboy" and the *Glitter* album compounded her mental health issues at the time. "I actually went through all of those old stories as they appeared in the post and daily news and they were sort of conflicting."
**Vocal Preference**: He expressed a personal preference for Mariah Carey's more soulful singing style in 2020 over her earlier vocal performances, even while acknowledging the astounding nature of her early vocal acrobatics. "I would actually rather hear her sing now than then even though I go back and look at some of those early performances and just I'm totally astounded."
**Fan Fandom Views**: Rich Jesuyak critiques a modern convention of fandom where pop stars are seen as incapable of flaw, stating that "flaws are what make a pop star fun" and that appreciating an artist "beyond its intentions" is part of the enjoyment. "to me the flaws are what make a pop star fun I don't want a perfect package I want somebody who's going to mess up sometimes you know I want somebody who's ridiculous."
**Mariah Carey Career Outlook**: He expressed skepticism about Mariah Carey achieving another number one hit, especially a new one, but speculates she will attempt it through collaborations. He believes her next album will likely be her third Christmas record. "I'm incredibly doubtful that she gets another number one."

# curiosity

**Love Actually Movie Impact on Christmas Music (2003)** : The movie *Love Actually*, released in 2003, was a sizable theatrical hit ($60 million in the US) but not an American blockbuster, becoming a sleeper hit primarily through home video and cable television. The film featured a cover version of "All I Want for Christmas Is You" sung by a child, not Mariah Carey's original. There is little direct evidence that the movie significantly boosted the song's chart performance at the time of its release, despite the original becoming a sizable digital seller in 2003. "There's really very little direct evidence that the appearance of the song in Love Actually did much to help all I want for Christmas is you on the charts."
**Apple's iTunes Music Store Launch (2003)** : Apple's iTunes Music Store opened in 2003, marking a significant shift in digital music distribution. In the same year, Billboard began tracking digital music, reflecting the increasing importance of digital sales and streaming in chart methodologies. "the year that Love Actually came out was the first year Apple's iTunes Music Store opened and Billboard was just starting to track digital music."
**Billboard Chart Methodology Changes**: Changes in how Billboard formulated its charts, alongside new technologies, played a crucial role in Mariah Carey's "All I Want for Christmas Is You" reaching number one in 2019. These technological and methodological shifts also contributed to the resurgence of Christmas and novelty hits on the charts. "largely about changing technologies and changes in the way the charts have been formulated."
**Wama Gettun Game**: A game where participants attempt to go from Thanksgiving to Christmas without hearing the song "Last Christmas" by Wham!. "The way Wama Gettun works is you try to get from Thanksgiving to Christmas without once hearing the song Last Christmas."
**Modern Fandom Critique**: A critique of contemporary fan culture, particularly on platforms like Twitter, where some fans are excessively protective of their favorite artists, perceiving them as flawless. The argument is that "flaws are what make a pop star fun" and that appreciating an artist's imperfections or deviations from intention adds to the enjoyment. "this modern convention of fandom that I don't understand where it's like I have to take this person so seriously yes they are incapable of flaw and to me the flaws are what make a pop star fun."
**Saturday Night Fever Soundtrack Impact**: Being featured on the soundtrack for the movie *Saturday Night Fever* was a significant way for artists, such as Kool & The Gang and Tavares, to achieve substantial financial success. Rick Dees's management, however, made a "mistake" by not allowing "Disco Duck" on the soundtrack, potentially missing out on massive sales. "Being featured on a movie soundtrack is one way to make a fortune."
**Peter Dinklage and Erica Schmidt Joint Interview**: Actor Peter Dinklage (known for Game of Thrones and The Station Agent) and his wife Erica Schmidt (a director, screenwriter, and playwright) are scheduled to have their first-ever joint interview at the Tribeca Festival. They will discuss making art separately and collaboratively, as well as how they have built their life around that. "It's their first joint interview they've done ever together and we are so glad at that sex and money to be back at the Tribeca festival."
