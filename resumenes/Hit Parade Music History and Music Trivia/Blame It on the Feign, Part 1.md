## albums
**All Or Nothing (1988) - European Release** : This album was released in November 1988 on the German label Hansa, with UK distribution handled by the acid house label Cool Tempo. It was filled with tracks that emulated the sound of the duo's smash first single, "Girl You Know It's True," and performed less successfully in Europe than its American counterpart. "A full Millie Vanille album titled All Or Nothing was released in November on the German label Hansa."
**Girl You Know It's True (1989) - American Release** : This American version of Millie Vanille's debut album was titled after its lead single and debuted on the charts in late March 1989. Within a month, it soared into the top 20 and achieved gold status, later going platinum. The album eventually reached number one on the album chart the same week "Girl, I'm Gonna Miss You" topped the Hot 100, outperforming the European "All or Nothing" album in Europe. Arista Records, under Clive Davis, insisted on swapping out some tracks for this release, including covers of The Isley Brothers' "It's Your Thing" and a song by Diane Warren. "The American version of the Millie Vanille album would be titled after its lead single Girl You Know It's True."

## songs
**Girl You Know It's True (1989) - Debut US Single** : Originally written by the Baltimore DJ crew Newmarks in 1986, Frank Farian transformed this minor club hit into a smash by adding the "Ashley's Roach clip" beat and session vocalists Charles Shaw, John Davis, and Brad Howe. Millie Vanille's version broke in Europe first in 1988, reaching number one in Germany for six weeks and number three in the UK. The Arista single debuted on the Hot 100 in January 1989, peaking at number two in early April, blocked by The Bangles and Roxette. It notably appeared on Billboard's first Hot Rap Singles chart in March 1989 and peaked at number three on the R&B chart, establishing itself as a "legit crossover smash." "This is Girl You Know It's True, The debut global smash by Millie Vanille."
**Baby Don't Forget My Number (1989)** : This second single from Millie Vanille was a "near carbon copy" of "Girl You Know It's True," featuring the exact same Ashley's Roach clip beat on the chorus and the same rap verses/sung chorus formula. It quickly entered the top 10 on both the R&B and pop charts within two months, leading to the "Girl You Know It's True" album going platinum. For the week ending July 1st, 1989, it became Millie Vanille's first chart-topper in America on the Hot 100. "Baby, don't forget my number flew into the top 10 on both the R&B and pop charts in just two months."
**Girl, I'm Gonna Miss You (1989)** : A ballad written by Frank Farian's team, this song was issued as a single by the American label Arista as a strategic "sonic changeup" after two uptempo hits. It was the Hot 100's highest new entry in the first week of August 1989 and quickly rose, becoming Millie Vanille's second straight number one and third straight top two hit by mid-September. The accompanying black-and-white video featured Rob and Fab brooding in muscle t-shirts. "By mid-September, Millie Vanille had its second straight number one and third straight top two hit."
**Blame It On The Rain (1989)** : This single reached number one on the Hot 100 on the week ending November 25, 1989. It marked the third straight chart-topper for Millie Vanille, the fourth of their five top-five hits, and affirmed their status as one of the top pop acts of the year. The song was also the second consecutive number one for its celebrated, Oscar-nominated songwriter, Diane Warren. "The week ending November 25, 1989, when Blame It On The Rain by Millie Vanille reached number one on the Hot 100."
**It's Your Thing (1969)** : An R&B classic by The Isley Brothers from 1969, this song was recorded by Millie Vanille as a cover at the insistence of Clive Davis for the American version of their album, "Girl You Know It's True." Davis believed Americans would recognize it well. "Davis proposed that Millie Vanille record an R&B classic Americans would know well. The Isley Brothers 1969 hit It's Your Thing."

## curiosities
**Musical Fraud Infamy** : Millie Vanille is now famously equated with musical fraud in the public's mind because Rob Pilatus and Fabrice Morvan did not sing or rap on any of their credited hits. This despite the fact that the German musical project was incredibly successful globally, especially in America. "But of course, as is now infamous, Palatus and Morvon weren't singing or rapping on any of the hits credited to Millie Vanille, a name that is now equated in the public's mind with musical fraud."
**1989 Chart Dominance** : In 1989, Millie Vanille achieved more number one hits than any other act on the charts, surpassing Bon Jovi, Bobby Brown, Janet Jackson, or Madonna. This demonstrated the immense popularity of their "German musical project," regardless of who actually vocalized on the records. "No act on the charts of that year, not Bon Jovi, Bobby Brown, Janet Jackson or Madonna had more number one hits than Millie Vanille."
**US Sales Dominance** : The United States purchased more Millie Vanille recordings than any other country in the world, indicating an extraordinary level of success in the American market. "We bought more Millie Vanille recordings than any country in the world."
**Exploiting Rap Crossover Moment** : Millie Vanille capitalized on a "rap driven metamorphosis" of the charts in the late 1980s, skillfully blending underground hip hop, club music, and even 60s influences into their pop sound. They took "full advantage of this early crossover moment." "The team behind Millie Vanille took full advantage of this early crossover moment."
**Widespread Fraud and Embrace** : The story of Millie Vanille is characterized by both theft and fraud, yet it was a deception that was embraced by the entire country and much of the world until it became unmanageable. "Yes, the story of Millie Vanille is one of both theft and fraud, but it was a fraud the whole country and much of the world embraced until it all got just a little out of control."
**Grammy Win** : Millie Vanille famously won a Grammy Award, which was later revoked due to the lip-syncing scandal. Their popularity at the time, despite the later controversy, reflected the direction of pop music at the turn of the 1990s. "And the winner is... What? Millie Vanille!"
**Collaboration with Oscar-nominated Songwriter** : An unmentioned "hit generating Oscar-nominated songwriter," later revealed to be Diane Warren, contributed Millie Vanille's "most enduring single," "Blame It On The Rain." This demonstrated how seriously the group was taken by industry professionals, even legitimate media and executives. "Seriously enough that a certain hit generating Oscar-nominated songwriter would give Millie Vanille their most enduring single."
**First Subject of VH1's Behind the Music (1997)** : Millie Vanille was infamously the subject of the very first episode of VH1's "Tell All Biography series Behind the Music" in 1997. The episode primarily focused on Rob and Fab's naive entry into their deal with Frank Farian and the devastating impact of the scandal on their lives, including elements of sex and drugs. "infamously they were the subject of the first ever episode of VH1's Tell All Biography series Behind the Music way back in 1997."
**Frank Farian's Secret Vocalists** : For their debut hit "Girl You Know It's True," Frank Farian recorded the track with rapper Charles Shaw and session singers John Davis and Brad Howe. He deliberately kept these middle-aged vocalists from being the public image of the project, instead choosing the younger, more visually appealing Rob Pilatus and Fabrice Morvan as the faces. "but the crew he disassembled to record the diddy, including rapper Charles Shaw and session singers John Davis and Brad Howe, all of them approaching middle age didn't strike Farion as hip enough to be the image of his new rap pop project."
**Rob & Fab's "Devil's Pact"** : German-born Robert Pilatus and French-born Fabrice Morvan, models and dancers who met in Munich in 1988, were eager for fame and signed what they later described as a "devil's pact" with Frank Farian to be the visual representatives, or "faces," of Millie Vanille. They were chosen for their good looks, athleticism, and energetic performance style, rather than their limited musical skill. "That eagerness led Rob and Fab to sign what they later called a devil's pact with Frank Farion to be the faces of Millie Vanille."
**Rob & Fab's Limited Musical Experience** : Although Rob Pilatus and Fabrice Morvan had some session work experience and Pilatus even backed Germany's Eurovision entry in 1987, most accounts agree neither had significant musical skill when auditioning for Farian. Fabrice Morvan was generally considered to have slightly more talent than Pilatus. "And virtually all accounts agree neither Rob Norfab had much musical skill when they auditioned for Farion."
**Origin of the Name "Millie Vanille"** : There are multiple, conflicting stories regarding the origin of the duo's name. The group's publicity materials falsely claimed it meant "positive energy in Turkish." Other suggested origins include a defunct disco in Berlin, a riff on the pop band Scritti Politti, or a nickname Frank Farian gave his office assistant. The latter is presented as a likely possibility. "The most repeated story originally claimed in the acts publicity materials was that Millie Vanille means positive energy in Turkish, total hogwash."
**Video Performance and Lip-Dubbing** : In their videos, Rob and Fab expertly performed their parts, striking poses, running in place, and swinging their dreadlocks. Fabrice Morvan lip-dubbed Charles Shaw's rapid-fire rap, while Rob Pilatus mimed the vocals of John Davis and Brad Howe. A backing band, including a drummer "just barely approximating" the Ashley's Roach clip sample, also mimed along. "Fab takes the harder vocal part, lip-dubbing Charles Shaw's rapid-fire rap, and Rob sings the Davis and How-Milities when he's not leaping, kicking, or executing a split."
**Clive Davis's Influence** : Arista Records founder Clive Davis, known for his "ears" and ability to spot a hit, signed Millie Vanille for American distribution. He was instrumental in shaping their US success, insisting on track changes for the American album version and using his "promotion machine" to make them more successful in America than anywhere else globally. "Clive Davis was having a good late 80s. Arraster Records, the label Davis had founded in the mid 70s, was commanding the American hip with a spate of danceable diva pop from the likes of Taylor Dane, Expos A, and of course Clive's flagship artist Whitney Houston."
**Frank Farian's Agreement to Changes for Secrecy** : Frank Farian readily agreed to Clive Davis's demands for track changes on the American album. He did so primarily out of desperation to keep the Millie Vanille lip-syncing secret from Arista executives who might have discovered the truth if they had visited Germany to oversee the recording process. "So long as Davis wouldn't send any of his Arraster executives out to Germany to watch Frank Farion work, Frank desperate to keep his Millie Vanille secret readily agreed."
**Mainstreaming Rap for Pop Fans** : Despite the dubious nature of their act, Millie Vanille played an undeniable role in making rap music palatable and accessible to mainstream pop audiences in the late 1980s. Their success on both pop and rap charts demonstrated their broad appeal. "Millie Vanille played an undeniable role, however dubious, in mainstreaming rap for pop fans in the late 80s."
**Greater American Success than Global** : By mid-September 1989, Millie Vanille had become officially more successful in America than anywhere else in the world, largely attributed to Clive Davis's promotional efforts. Their album "Girl You Know It's True" also reached number one on the US album chart, surpassing the performance of their European "All or Nothing" album. "Something odd had now happened to Millie Vanille. They were officially more successful in America than anywhere else in the world."
**Superstar Level Opportunities** : Following their consecutive number ones and album chart success in the US, Millie Vanille began to receive superstar-level opportunities. This included an invitation by MTV to join the inaugural Club MTV package tour alongside major acts like Tone Loc, Information Society, and Paula Abdul. "Millie Vanille started receiving superstar level opportunities."

## curiosities
**Birth Name and Early Career** : Born Frans Reuter in Kern, Germany, Farian initially changed his name to Frankie Ferrien. He worked as a cook before pursuing music, attempting to release records under Frankie Ferrien, including covers of US and UK hits, but found no success until the mid-1970s. "The man born Frans Reuter in Kern, Germany had already changed his name to Frankie Ferrien. The then 25-year-old had quit his job as a cook and was trying to release records under that name, including covers of US and UK hits."
**Creator of Boney M and Millie Vanille** : Farian was the mastermind producer, musician, and pop impresario behind both the 70s Euro Disco act Boney M and the 80s Dance Pop duo Millie Vanille. His vision and methods, including the use of uncredited vocalists and lip-syncing frontmen, characterized both projects. "Producer, musician and pop in Prossario Frank Farrion had scored a string of hits in the 70s with his Euro Disco act, Boni M."
**Continued Career Despite Fraud** : Despite being the mastermind behind "pop music's most famous fraud" (Millie Vanille), Farian was not run out of the music business. Instead, he continued to produce and co-write with Dance Pop acts for decades, with his productions still being heard on older Dance Pop radio stations and streaming mixes into the 2020s. "Quite the contrary, Frank Ferrien kept producing and co-writing with Dance Pop acts for decades."
**"Irrepressible Hustler" Persona** : Farian is described as an "irrepressible hustler on the make," driven by a "hunger for hits by any means necessary." This approach made him well-suited for the music business and characterized his career from early "poaching" of songs to his later, more famous projects. "In his day, Ferrien was like the title character of What Makes Sammy Run, an irrepressible hustler on the make, and What Made Franky Run was a hunger for hits by any means necessary, which made him a good fit for the music business."
**First "Poached" Song** : Farian's first hit song that he "poached" was an interpolation of Prince Buster's 1964 Jamaican Ska/Rocksteady single "Al Capone." This track, which was a hit in England, served as the basis for Farian's mid-70s single "Baby Do You Want A Bump." "This is essentially the first song Frank Ferrien poached and turned into a hit for himself."
**Diversification in the 1980s** : Although the 1980s were initially a lean period for Farian hit-wise, he diversified his collaborations. This included working with members of Toto and other UK bands to form Far Corporation, and producing American singer Meat Loaf. These experiments ultimately led to his next breakthrough with Millie Vanille. "For Farion, even as the 80s was a follow period hit wise, these experiments with new collaborators did eventually point him in the direction of his next breakthrough."
**Discovery of "Girl You Know It's True"** : In 1987, German DJ acquaintances tipped Farian off to an American import 12-inch single by Newmarks titled "Girl You Know It's True." Farian immediately recognized its potential as a smash, leading him to record his own version that would become Millie Vanille's debut global hit. "To Ferrian, it sounded like a smash in the making."
**Royalty Earnings in the 2020s** : Farian continues to earn royalties into the 2020s from his older hits, notably Boney M's "Rasputin." A 2021 remix by British DJ Majestic made the top ten of Billboard's dance and electronic songs chart and its global XUS chart, ensuring continued income for Farian. "So that's more royalties flowing in the direction of Frank Ferrien, even into the 2020s."

## songs
**Baby Do You Want A Bump (mid-70s)** : This single was the first released under the Boney M name, created by Frank Farian as a "made-up group." It became a hit in Holland and Belgium, prompting Farian to recruit actual performers to form a "real group" for touring and further recording. "This single was called Baby Do You Want A Bump, and this time for an artist's name, Frankie Ferrien decided to hide behind a made-up group that he called Boney M."
**Daddy Cool (1976)** : As the group's second single, "Daddy Cool" became a hit across Europe in 1976. The maniacal disco dancing of frontman Bobby Farrell in early TV appearances, encouraged by Farian, foreshadowed the performance style Millie Vanille would adopt a decade later. "Daddy Cool, the group's second single became a hit across Europe in 1976."
**Sunny (Bobby Hebb cover)** : This Euro-Disco cover of Bobby Hebb's Soul Jazz standard was the first Boney M single to crack the UK top three during their remarkable streak of hits. It showcased the group's ability to transform existing material into a successful disco format. "a Euro-Disco cover of Bobby Hebs' Soul Jazz Standard, Sunny, which became the first Bony M single to crack the UK top three."
**Ma Baker** : This Boney M hit featured a distinctive "mama ma hook" that was later borrowed by Lady Gaga for her hit song "Poker Face" decades later. It was part of Boney M's epic streak of nine straight UK top ten hits. "Ma Baker, whose mama ma hook, was decades later barrowed by Lady Gaga for her hit poker face."
**Rivers of Babylon** : This single achieved massive success, selling over two million copies in England alone and ranking as one of the UK's seven best-selling singles of all time. Despite its popularity in Europe, it was Boney M's only entry into the American Top 40, peaking at number 30, and marked their last US hit. "Rivers of Babylon, which sold over two million copies in England alone, and still ranks as one of the UK's seven best-selling singles of all time."
**Mary's Boy Child (1978) - UK Christmas Number One** : Boney M's cover of this Calypso Christmas classic, previously made famous by Harry Belafonte in the 1950s, became Britain's official Christmas number one of 1978. It sold an astonishing 1.6 million copies in just four weeks during that UK holiday season, ranking among the three best-selling holiday hits in British history. "Bony M's cover of the Calypso Christmas Classic, made famous in the 50s by Harry Belafonte, was Britain's official Christmas number one of 1978."
**Gotta Go Home (1979)** : This single just missed the top 10 in England in late 1979 and marked the beginning of the end of Boney M's days as major hitmakers, coinciding with the fizzling of disco on the charts. "When Gotta Go Home just missed the top 10 in England in late 1979. Bony M's days as hitmakers were largely over."
**Happy Song (1984)** : A Boney M comeback single from 1984, this track featured Bobby Farrell performing a "unganely rap break." This rap vocal by Farrell, a deviation from his usual miming, tonally foreshadowed the sound that Frank Farian would later develop with Millie Vanille. It was a top 10 hit only in Germany. "On a 1984 Bony M comeback single called Happy Song, Farrell performed an unganely rap break, which again, tonally foreshadowed the sound of Millie Vanille."
**Dreadlock Holiday (1985 Remake)** : In 1985, Boney M, with diminishing returns, recorded an 80s-sounding remake of Ten C.C.'s 1978 Cod Reggae hit, "Dreadlock Holiday." This remake was largely a flop. Frank Farian later arranged a remix in 1987, adding Charles Shaw for a rap vocal. "It recorded a remake of Dreadlock Holiday, a 1978 Cod Reggae hit by the White British Rock Band, Ten C.C. Boni M's remake sounded like a product of its time, very 80s, and it was pretty much a flop."
**Rasputin (1978)** : This 1978 single was a major hit across Europe, reaching number one in Germany and Austria and number two in the UK, though it never charted on the Hot 100 in America. Named after the 19th-century Russian mystic Grigoriy Rasputin, the song features mythical stories of his powers, a tempo suggesting a Russian Prizadka dance, and a rhythm guitar hook played on balalaikas, despite no actual Russian involvement in its creation by Germans and an Austrian, with Jamaican British co-lead vocals. It has experienced a resurgence, becoming the subject of a global TikTok challenge in 2021, and a 2021 remix by British DJ Majestic made the top ten of Billboard's dance and electronic songs chart. "Rasputin, or perhaps I should say Ra Ra Rasputin, given the way the word is sung, was a hit across Europe back in 1978, including number one in Germany and Austria and number two in the UK."

## curiosities
**Formation as a "Real Group"** : After the single "Baby Do You Want A Bump" became a hit in Holland and Belgium, Frank Farian transformed Boney M from a made-up group into a "real group." He recruited West Indian vocalists: three British women (Liz Mitchell, Marcia Barrett, Maisie Williams) and Bobby Farrell from Aruba, all people of color. "After the single was a hit in Holland and Belgium. Farion turned Bony M into a real group."
**Lip-syncing and Uncredited Vocals** : The "slippery" aspect of Boney M's lineup was that only Liz Mitchell and Marcia Barrett actually sang on the recordings, alongside Frank Farian himself, who never appeared on stage. Frontman Bobby Farrell mimed Farian's distinctive "very tutonic German accented vocals," and Maisie Williams, a former model, also lip-synced on stage. "What was more slippery was only two of these members, Liz Mitchell and Marsha Barrett actually sang on Bony M recordings."
**Remarkable European Hit Streak** : Boney M, a "Euro-British Caribbean formula," achieved a remarkable streak of hits. They scored numerous number one hits in Frank Farian's native Germany and were "amazingly popular" in the United Kingdom, achieving nine straight UK top ten hits, which was a "special delight" for Farian. "Daddy Cool kicked off a remarkable streak of hits for Bony M."
**Limited US Success** : In stark contrast to their immense popularity in Europe and the UK, Boney M found limited success in the United States, cracking the American Top 40 only once with "Rivers of Babylon" (at number 30), which was also their last US hit. Their sound was perhaps "a little too exotic for US tastes," an irony given Frank Farian's later success in America with Millie Vanille. "Bony M might have been just a little too exotic for US tastes. They cracked the American top 40 only once."
**Foreshadowing Millie Vanille's Performance Style** : Observing Bobby Farrell's "maniacally" disco dancing in Boney M's early TV appearances, encouraged by Frank Farian to flail his limbs, revealed the "seeds" of what Millie Vanille's energetic, almost manic, performance style would become over a decade later. "if you watch Farrell in Bony M's early TV appearances, disco dancing maniacally, encouraged by Farion to flail his limbs all about, you can see the seeds of what Millie Vanille would become more than a decade later."
**"Mary's Boy Child" Sales Record** : Boney M's 1978 cover of "Mary's Boy Child" sold 1.6 million copies in just four weeks during the UK holiday season. This extraordinary sales figure cemented its place among the three best-selling holiday hits in British history, alongside Band-Aid's "Do They Know It's Christmas" and Wham!'s "Last Christmas." "In fact, during that UK holiday season, Mary's Boy Child sold 1.6 million copies in just four weeks. It still ranks among the three best-selling holiday hits in British history."

# instrument - Drums

**"Ashley's Roach Clip" Drum Break** : The drum break from The Soul Searchers' 1974 instrumental "Ashley's Roach clip" became a "formative beat in rap history." It was sampled on dozens of hip hop classics by Golden Age rappers and was a key element in hits by Millie Vanille, used prominently in songs like "Girl You Know It's True" and "Baby Don't Forget My Number." "The Ashley's Roach clip drum break is a formative beat in rap history."

# curiosity

**Musical Fraud and Public Perception** : The name "Millie Vanille" became infamous, equated in the public's mind with musical fraud due to the revelation that the duo Rob Pilatus and Fabrice Morvan did not sing or rap on their hits. This scandal, despite the group's immense popularity at the time, cemented their legacy as a cautionary tale in pop music history. "a name that is now equated in the public's mind with musical fraud."
**"Made-Up Groups" Becoming "Real Groups" in Pop History** : The practice of releasing singles under a phony band name that later becomes an actual group is a well-established, if somewhat shady, tactic in rock and pop history. Examples include Kerb Alpert and the Tijuana Brass, The Archies, and Steam, all of whom transitioned from fictional or studio-only entities to performing and recording bands after achieving hit records. "Rock and Pop history is littered with examples of made-up groups having to become real groups after a hit record."
**TikTok Challenge Phenomenon** : Songs that were not major hits in their heyday can experience unexpected resurgences through platforms like TikTok. Boney M's 1978 single "Rasputin," which never cracked the Hot 100, became the subject of a global TikTok challenge in 2021, leading to a remix by British DJ Majestic charting globally. "Although Rasputin never cracked the Hot 100, just this year it was the subject of a TikTok challenge, both in America and globally."
**Billboard's First Hot Rap Singles Chart (March 1989)** : In March 1989, Billboard launched its first-ever Hot Rap Singles chart, which was based on sales reports from retailers. This new chart included both black-owned retailers and contributed to the magazine's R&B chart, signaling the increasing commercial significance and genre-specific tracking of rap music. "Billboard launched its first ever Hot Rap singles chart based on sales reports from retailers of their top selling rap singles."
