## albums
**All or Nothing (1988) - European Debut Album** : The full Milli Vanilli album was released in November 1988 on the German label Hansa. In the UK it was picked up for distribution by the acid house label Cool Tempo. It was filled with tracks that emulated the sound of the smash first single, and this is when America started to take notice. Its European performance was eventually surpassed by the American version, which reached number one in the US. "All or nothing was filled with tracks that emulated the sound of the Smash First Synod."
**Girl You Know It's True (1989) - US Debut Album on Arista** : The American version of the album, retitled after its lead single, was reshaped by Clive Davis, who swapped out tracks. He had the group record the Isley Brothers' 1969 hit "It's Your Thing" and passed Farian's team a Diane Warren song. The album debuted on the charts in late March 1989, entered the top 20 and went gold within a month, then went top 10 and platinum on the strength of the second single. It reached number one on the album chart the same week "Girl I'm Gonna Miss You" topped the Hot 100, a better result than All or Nothing achieved anywhere in Europe. "The girl you know it's true album also reached number one on the album chart."

## songs
**Girl You Know It's True (1988) - Debut Global Smash** : Frank Farian's remake of the 1986 club track by Baltimore DJ crew Numarx. He took it into the studio with his favorite session musicians and laid the Soul Searchers' "Ashley's Roachclip" drum break on top. The real performers were rapper Charles Shaw and session singers John Davis and Brad Howell. It was number one in Germany for six weeks in August and September 1988 and reached number three in the UK before year's end. On Arista it debuted on the Hot 100 at number 83 in the first week of January 1989, cracked the top 10 in its 10th week and the top five a week later. It peaked at number two in early April 1989, the same peak Tone Lōc had reached two months earlier. It was blocked by a chart-topping Bangles ballad and by Roxette, who leapt to number one the following week. It also appeared in the first week of Billboard's new Hot Rap Singles chart and peaked at number three on the R&B chart. "Farion had transformed Girl You Know It's True from catchy to catnip."
**Girl You Know It's True (1988) - Music Video** : The video that catapulted the act to fame shows Rob and Fab in big jackets, biker shorts and boots, striking poses, running in place and swinging their dreadlocks, a move that looked cool in slow motion. A band mimes behind them, including a drummer barely approximating the Ashley's Roachclip sample. Fab lip-syncs Charles Shaw's rapid-fire rap, and Rob mimes the Davis and Howell melodies when he isn't leaping, kicking or doing a split. Both give meaningful stares to the camera when singing "I love you." "It was silly, but not much sillier than most of what was on MTV at the time."
**Baby Don't Forget My Number (1989) - First US Number One** : Arista released this as the second single to push the album to platinum. It was a near carbon copy of the first single, using the exact same Ashley's Roachclip beat on the chorus and the same formula of rapped verses and a sung chorus. It reached the top 10 on both the R&B and pop charts in just two months. It was Milli Vanilli's first US chart-topper for the week ending July 1, 1989. "They dropped a second single that was a near carbon copy of the first one."
**Girl I'm Gonna Miss You (1989) - Ballad Change-Up** : Farian's team had written the ballad under the title "I'm Gonna Miss You" but had never tried it as a single anywhere in the world. Arista wanted to test whether the group could survive a sonic change-up and added "Girl" to the title. The video was shot in black and white, with Rob and Fab in tight muscle t-shirts brooding at the camera. It was the Hot 100's highest new entry in the first week of August 1989. By mid-September it was the group's second straight number one and third straight top-two hit. "Girl, I'm gonna miss you was an easy smash."
**Blame It on the Rain (1989) - Diane Warren Composition** : Clive Davis passed this song, written by Diane Warren, to Farian's team. It reached number one on the Hot 100 for the week ending November 25, 1989. It was the third straight chart-topper for Milli Vanilli and the fourth of their five top-five hits. It was also Warren's second consecutive number one, and it became the group's most enduring single. "Seriously enough that a certain hit generating Oscar-nominated songwriter would give Millie Vanille their most enduring single."
**It's Your Thing - Isley Brothers Cover** : Clive Davis proposed that Milli Vanilli record this 1969 Isley Brothers R&B classic that Americans would know well, and Farian's team gladly obliged for the US album. "Davis proposed that Millie Vanille record an R&B classic Americans would know well."

## curiosities
**Musical Fraud - Neither Rob Nor Fab Sang** : Rob Pilatus and Fab Morvan presented themselves as Milli Vanilli on MTV, in concert and at the Grammy Awards. Neither sang nor rapped on any of the hits credited to the act, and the name is now equated in the public's mind with musical fraud. "Palatus and Morvon weren't singing or rapping on any of the hits credited to Millie Vanille."
**Most Number Ones of 1989** : No act on the 1989 charts had more number one hits than Milli Vanilli, including Bon Jovi, Bobby Brown, Janet Jackson and Madonna. Paula Abdul was the only artist that year to score as many number ones. America bought more Milli Vanilli recordings than any country in the world. "No act on the charts of that year, not Bon Jovi, Bobby Brown, Janet Jackson or Madonna had more number one hits than Millie Vanille."
**More Successful in America Than Anywhere Else** : By September 1989 the group was officially more successful in the US than anywhere else, since they had not scored a second number one in any other country. This is attributed to Clive Davis's promotion machine, and it was ironic given Boney M's US failure. "They were officially more successful in America than anywhere else in the world."
**Rob and Fab - Origins and Devil's Pact** : German-born Robert Pilatus and French-born Fabrice Morvan were models and dancers who met in Munich in 1988. They bonded over their shared status as people of color in the German club scene and their desire for fame. That eagerness led them to sign what they later called a "devil's pact" with Frank Farian. They were a pretty, athletic, exotic and energetic combination, but they hardly looked like rappers or R&B singers. "That eagerness led Rob and Fab to sign what they later called a devil's pact with Frank Farion to be the faces of Millie Vanille."
**Musical Ability of Rob and Fab** : Both had tried session work. Most accounts claim Fab had the greater musical talent, and virtually all accounts agree neither had much musical skill when they auditioned for Farian. Like Bobby Farrell miming Farian's Boney M vocals in 1976, they looked convincing. "Virtually all accounts agree neither Rob Norfab had much musical skill when they auditioned for Farion."
**Origin of the Name Milli Vanilli** : The act's publicity materials claimed the name meant "positive energy" in Turkish, which is false. Other folk etymologies include a defunct Berlin disco, a riff on the band Scritti Politti, and a nickname Farian gave his office assistant. "Millie Vanille means positive energy in Turkish, total hogwash."
**Charted on the First Billboard Hot Rap Singles Chart** : In March 1989, one month before "Girl You Know It's True" peaked, Billboard launched its first Hot Rap Singles chart, based on retailer sales reports. Milli Vanilli appeared in its very first week at number five, between "Hidden Play" at number four and Rob Base and DJ E-Z Rock at number six. The reporting panel included Black-owned retailers who also reported to the R&B chart. "Girl You Know It's True was a legit crossover smash."
**Role in Mainstreaming Rap** : Milli Vanilli played an undeniable, if dubious, role in mainstreaming rap for pop fans in the late 1980s. They arrived when the charts were undergoing a rap-driven metamorphosis and pilfered from 1960s music as well as 1980s underground hip hop and club music. Like Tone Lōc's "Wild Thing," they helped make rap palatable to Middle America. "Millie Vanille played an undeniable role, however dubious, in mainstreaming rap for pop fans in the late 80s."
**Five Top Five Hits** : The group scored five top-five hits in the US. "Blame It on the Rain" was the fourth of these and their third straight chart-topper. "It was the third straight chart-topper by the duo group project and the fourth of their five top five hits."
**Superstar Lifestyle** : Rob and Fab loved their superstar status: arriving in limos, partying with scores of women and making fans scream. The Club MTV tour should have been a high point, but it was also the site of a now-infamous incident. "They loved their superstar status, arriving in limos, partying with scores of women, making fans scream."
**Grammy Win** : Milli Vanilli won a Grammy, one of the prizes they "shouldn't have" won. "Millie Vanille weren't through scoring hits or winning prizes they shouldn't have."
**Subject of First Behind the Music Episode (1997)** : Milli Vanilli were the subject of the first-ever episode of VH1's Behind the Music in 1997. It was a classic tale about the price of fame, including elements of sex and drugs. "Infamously they were the subject of the first ever episode of VH1's Tell All Biography series Behind the Music way back in 1997."

## curiosities
**Early Life as Franz Reuther** : He was born Franz Reuther in Kirn, Germany, and was a young fan of British and American pop. By age 25 he had changed his name to Frankie Farian and quit his job as a cook. He tried releasing records under that name, including covers of US and UK hits, but nothing clicked until the mid-1970s. "The then 25-year-old had quit his job as a cook and was trying to release records under that name."
**First Poached Hit - Prince Buster's Al Capone** : His first major borrowing was interpolating Prince Buster's 1964 hit "Al Capone" into "Baby Do You Wanna Bump." He released it under a made-up group name, Boney M. "This is essentially the first song Frank Ferrien poached and turned into a hit for himself. It wouldn't be the last."
**Sang Boney M's Male Vocals** : Farian sang on Boney M recordings alongside Liz Mitchell and Marcia Barrett but never appeared on stage. His "very Teutonic" German-accented vocals were mimed by frontman Bobby Farrell. "Farion's very tutonic German accented vocals were mined by frontman Bobby Farrell."
**What Makes Sammy Run Hustler** : He was compared to the title character of What Makes Sammy Run, an irrepressible hustler on the make driven by a hunger for hits by any means necessary. He is described as a hitmaker with nine lives, like a cat. "What Made Franky Run was a hunger for hits by any means necessary, which made him a good fit for the music business."
**Survived the Milli Vanilli Scandal** : He was not run out of the business and kept producing and co-writing for dance pop acts for decades. His productions still turn up on older dance pop radio stations such as New York's WKTU and on vintage dance mixes on Spotify. "Quite the contrary, Frank Ferrien kept producing and co-writing with Dance Pop acts for decades."
**Rasputin Royalties in the 2020s** : The TikTok revival and the 2021 Majestic remix of "Rasputin" sent more royalties his way into the 2020s. "So that's more royalties flowing in the direction of Frank Ferrien, even into the 2020s."
**Producing Meat Loaf** : In the mid-1980s he produced Meat Loaf's 1986 LP Blind Before I Stop, which sold poorly. "Around the same time, Farion was also producing American singer Meatloaf. Yes, that Meatloaf."
**Dreadlock Holiday Remix and Discovering Charles Shaw** : Farian never gave up on an idea. In 1987 he arranged a remix of Boney M's 1985 "Dreadlock Holiday" remake and brought in Charles Shaw to add a rap, and he liked the sound of Shaw's voice. "Because Frank Ferrian would never give up on an idea, by 1987, he arranged a remix of the Dreadlock remake."
**DJ Tip-Off on Numarx** : Two German DJs who were professional acquaintances of Farian tipped him off to hot club records. They passed him the American import 12-inch of Numarx's "Girl You Know It's True," which he heard as a smash in the making. "To Ferrian, it sounded like a smash in the making."
**Needing Faces for Milli Vanilli** : He felt the actual performers, all approaching middle age, weren't hip enough to be the image of his rap pop project. He repeated what he had done with Boney M and Bobby Farrell. "He had the song, it was recorded, and already in the camp he just needed the faces."
**Deal with Clive Davis to Protect the Secret** : Farian readily agreed to Davis's track changes on the condition that no Arista executives would come to Germany to watch him work. "Frank desperate to keep his Millie Vanille secret readily agreed."

## songs
**Baby Do You Wanna Bump (mid-1970s) - First Single** : Frank Farian interpolated Prince Buster's "Al Capone" into this single and credited it to a made-up group, Boney M. After it became a hit in Holland and Belgium, Farian turned Boney M into a real group. "This time for an artist's name, Frankie Ferrien decided to hide behind a made-up group that he called Boney M."
**Daddy Cool (1976) - Breakthrough** : The group's second single was a hit across Europe in 1976 and launched a remarkable streak. Early TV appearances show Bobby Farrell disco dancing maniacally, encouraged by Farian to flail his limbs, which foreshadowed Milli Vanilli. "Daddy Cool kicked off a remarkable streak of hits for Bony M."
**Sunny - Bobby Hebb Cover** : A Euro-disco cover of Bobby Hebb's soul-jazz standard. It was the first Boney M single to crack the UK top three. "This epic streak included a Euro-Disco cover of Bobby Hebs' Soul Jazz Standard, Sunny."
**Ma Baker** : Its "ma-ma-ma" hook was borrowed decades later by Lady Gaga for "Poker Face." "Ma Baker, whose mama ma hook, was decades later barrowed by Lady Gaga for her hit poker face."
**Rivers of Babylon (1978) - UK Best-Seller and Only US Top 40 Hit** : It sold over two million copies in England alone and still ranks among the UK's seven best-selling singles of all time. It was the group's only US top 40 hit, reaching number 30 as counted down by Casey Kasem. "Rivers of Babylon, which sold over two million copies in England alone, and still ranks as one of the UK's seven best-selling singles of all time."
**Mary's Boy Child (1978) - UK Christmas Number One** : A cover of the calypso Christmas classic made famous by Harry Belafonte in the 1950s. It was Britain's official Christmas number one of 1978 and sold 1.6 million copies in just four weeks. It still ranks among the three best-selling holiday hits in British history, alongside Band Aid's "Do They Know It's Christmas" and Wham!'s "Last Christmas." "During that UK holiday season, Mary's Boy Child sold 1.6 million copies in just four weeks."
**Rasputin (1978) - Euro Hit Revived on TikTok** : Named after the Russian mystic Grigori Rasputin, the song tells mythical, likely apocryphal stories of his healing powers and romantic prowess, including romancing the Tsarina. Its tempo suggests a Russian prisyadka (squat dance) cadence, and its rhythm guitar hook is played on balalaikas. Yet no Russians were involved: it was written by two Germans and an Austrian and sung by two Jamaican-British women. It was number one in Germany and Austria and number two in the UK. It never cracked the Hot 100, but it became a TikTok challenge in the US and globally and featured in the video game Just Dance 2. A 2021 remix by British DJ Majestic made the top ten of Billboard's Dance/Electronic Songs chart and its Global Excl. US chart. "Virtually everything about this song is kind of phony, and pretty irresistible."
**Gotta Go Home (1979) - End of the Hit Streak** : It just missed the UK top 10 in late 1979 as disco fizzled, and it marked the end of Boney M's days as hitmakers. "Bony M's days as hitmakers were largely over."
**Happy Song (1984) - Comeback Single with Bobby Farrell Rap** : Farian let Bobby Farrell vocalize for real, performing an ungainly rap break that tonally foreshadowed Milli Vanilli. It was only a top 10 hit in Germany. "Farrell performed an unganely rap break, which again, tonally foreshadowed the sound of Millie Vanille."
**Dreadlock Holiday (1985) - 10cc Remake** : Boney M covered 10cc's 1978 reggae hit. The remake sounded very 1980s and was pretty much a flop. A 1987 remix added a rap by Charles Shaw. "Boni M's remake sounded like a product of its time, very 80s, and it was pretty much a flop."

## curiosities
**Lineup and Lip-Syncing** : Farian recruited West Indian vocalists, all people of color: three British women (Liz Mitchell, Marcia Barrett, Maizie Williams) and Bobby Farrell, originally from Aruba. Only Mitchell and Barrett actually sang on the recordings, alongside Farian. Farrell and Maizie Williams, a former model, lip-synced on stage. "Only two of these members, Liz Mitchell and Marsha Barrett actually sang on Bony M recordings."
**Nine Straight UK Top Ten Hits** : Boney M scored a string of number ones in Germany and was hugely popular across Europe. In the UK they scored nine straight top ten hits, a special delight for Farian, who grew up idolizing the British charts. "Frank Farion's brainchild scored nine straight UK top ten hits."
**Too Exotic for the US** : They cracked the American top 40 only once, with "Rivers of Babylon." This was ironic given Milli Vanilli's later US dominance. "Bony M might have been just a little too exotic for US tastes."

# curiosity

**Made-Up Groups That Became Real** : By the mid-1970s, releasing singles under a phony band name that later became a real band was an established, if slightly shady, tactic. Examples include Herb Alpert & the Tijuana Brass, the Archies and Steam. "Rock and Pop history is littered with examples of made-up groups having to become real groups after a hit record."
**Rap's Late-80s Crossover Moment** : Some late-80s rap hits sounded authentic to the streets, while others dabbled in rap within what were really pop songs. Even harder-edged rap was moving toward dance. "Some of the hit acts wrapping in the late 80s sounded authentic to the streets. While others dabbled in rap, in songs that were really more pop."
**Billboard Hot Rap Singles Launch (March 1989)** : Billboard launched its first rap singles chart based on retailer sales reports.
