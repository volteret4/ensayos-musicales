## albums
**Back Home Again (1974)** : John Denver was winding up the singles from this album in early 1975. The studio version of "Thank God I'm a Country Boy" was a deep cut on this LP. "As John Denver was winding up the singles from his back home again album."
**An Evening with John Denver (1975) - Concert Album** : This live album kicked off John Denver's most imperial year, generating a number one single, "Thank God I'm a Country Boy," a relative rarity for live songs to top the Hot 100. Its release made "Thank God I'm a Country Boy" a natural for single release due to its rousing live track nature. "John Denver's most imperial year kicked off with a concert album, an evening with John Denver."
**Wind Song (1975) - Ninth Studio Album** : This album marked John Denver's Imperial Peak in 1975, topping both the pop and country LPs charts in just three weeks. It generated his least remembered number one song, "I'm Sorry," and its B-side, "Calypso." "John Denver's Imperial Peak came later in 1975 when he dropped his ninth studio album Wind Song, the most John Denver album title ever."
**I Want to Live (1977)** : This LP was John Denver's first post-"Oh, God" album and coincided with President Jimmy Carter appointing Denver to the President's Commission on World Hunger. Denver offered the title track as the Commission's theme song, but the album underperformed on charts, indicating his earnest balladry was becoming ill-suited for the late 70s. "His first post-O God LP in 1977, I Want to Live, was a case in point."
**Greatest Hits Volume 2 (1977)** : John Denver issued this greatest hits album in an attempt to match his prior bestseller. It featured a re-recorded single, "My Sweet Lady," which scraped the bottom rounds of the American Top 40, becoming Denver's last Top 40 pop hit for over four years. "Denver also tried issuing another greatest hits album in 1977 to match his prior bestseller."
**A Christmas Together (1979) - Soundtrack Album with The Muppets** : This soundtrack accompanied John Denver's first television special with Jim Henson's Muppets and became a platinum-selling album. It sold better than Denver's own previous two LPs, marking a beloved career pivot for him. "Its Platinum Soundtrack album sold better than Denver's own previous two LPs."

## songs
**Thank God I'm a Country Boy (1975)** : This was John Denver's only number one hit that he didn't write himself, penned by John Martin Summers, a multi-instrumentalist in Denver's backing band. The song was originally a deep cut on "Back Home Again" but became a natural single release from the live album "An Evening with John Denver" due to its catchy hoedown cadence and fiddle-playing lyrics. In 1975, it shot to number one on both the pop and country charts in a couple of months, unlike "Take Me Home Country Roads" which took much longer to chart lower. "Was John Denver's only number one hit he didn't write himself?"
**Take Me Home Country Roads (1971)** : This song was John Denver's second hit to feature the word "country" in the title, following "Thank God I'm a Country Boy." In 1971, it took nearly five months to climb to number two on the Hot 100 and peaked at a lowly number 50 on the country chart, in stark contrast to "Thank God I'm a Country Boy" in 1975. In 2014, the West Virginia legislature approved a resolution naming it one of its State Songs, making Denver one of only two people to have written two State Songs. "Whereas in 1975, country boy shot to number one on both charts in just a couple of months."
**I'm Sorry (1975)** : A pleasant, serviceable ballad about the end of a relationship from John Denver's "Wind Song" album, this song flew to number one on the Hot 100 in seven weeks and topped the country chart six weeks later, driven by Denver's career momentum. Tom Bryan called it his "least memorable number one single" and "most wispily generic," leading pop audiences to dump it in favor of its B-side, "Calypso." "I'm sorry was a pleasant serviceable John Denver Ballad about the end of a relationship."
**Calypso (1975)** : This song was the B-side to John Denver's "I'm Sorry" and became so popular that Billboard flipped the entry on their charts, listing it as the A-side after "I'm Sorry" fell from number one. It is a tribute to Denver's friend, undersea explorer Jacques Cousteau, named after Cousteau's ship the Calypso, and is described as a much livelier song that "the show stopping musical number from a Disney cartoon that does not exist." "Calypso was a tribute to Denver's friend undersea explorer Jacques Cousteau, named after Cousteau's ship the Calypso."
**Fly Away (1975) - with Olivia Newton-John** : Released as a single just before Christmas in 1975 from the "Wind Song" album, this song featured Olivia Newton-John on uncredited backing vocals, a common practice at the time. It topped the easy listening chart, unofficially Olivia Newton-John's seventh number one and officially Denver's sixth, and peaked just outside the top 10 on both the pop and country charts. "Olivia Newton John backed up John Denver on his follow up hit from the wind song album Fly Away."
**How Can I Leave You Again (1977)** : This was the lead single from John Denver's 1977 album "I Want to Live." The song missed the Pop Top 40 and only reached number 22 on the Country chart, indicating that Denver's earnest balladry was becoming ill-suited to the late 70s pop landscape. "I Want to Live underperformed on the album charts and its lead single How Can I Leave You Again, Missed the Pop Top 40 and Only Reach Number 22 Country."
**My Sweet Lady (1977)** : A re-recorded single from John Denver's "Greatest Hits Volume 2" LP, this song originally written by Denver for the 1974 TV movie "Sunshine" and previously a hit for Clifty Young, barely scraped the American Top 40. It topped out at number 32 on the Hot 100 and marked Denver's last Top 40 pop hit for over four years. "It was a hit by Clifty Young, the actor and singer in 74. It's a hit again."
**Annie's Song (1974)** : This was a chart-topper in 1974, with its titular subject being John Denver's wife Annie. The song is mentioned in the context of their bitter divorce in 1982. "He and his wife Annie, the titular subject of his 1974 chart-topper Annie's song, went through a bitter divorce in 1982."
**The Gold and Beyond (1984)** : John Denver was asked to write this anthemic theme song for the Winter Olympics in Sarajevo in 1984. It received plenty of play on ABC's Olympics coverage but did not chart anywhere. "His anthemic offering, The Gold and Beyond, got plenty of play on ABC's Olympics coverage but did not chart anywhere."
**Some Days Are Diamonds, Some Days Are Stone (1981)** : This 1981 comeback hit for John Denver, which he did not write, was a secret LGBTQ anthem. Songwriter Dina K. Rose, who came out as trans in the 2010s, revealed she wrote it about her struggles with gender identity, using diamonds and stone as metaphors for her feminine and masculine sides. Denver took it to number 36 on the Hot 100 and number 10 on the Country Chart. "It was a secret LGBTQ anthem."
**Shanghai Breezes (1982)** : Inspired by John Denver's trip to China, this song reached number 31 on the Hot 100 in 1982. It topped the Adult Contemporary Chart (formerly Easy Listening) for one week, marking Denver's last number one song on any Billboard Chart. "Inspired by Denver's trip to China, 1982's Shanghai Breezes reached number 31 on the Hot 100 and for one week topped the Adult Contemporary Chart."
**Rocky Mountain High (1972)** : This song was famously banned from many radio stations as a "drug-related song." Posthumously, in 2007, a decade after Denver's death, the Colorado Senate passed a resolution to make it one of the state's official State Songs. "my song Rocky Mountain High was banned from many radio stations as a drug-related song."

## curiosities
**Parallel 70s Career with Olivia Newton-John** : John Denver and Olivia Newton-John had parallel careers in the 70s, both pop singers who stumbled into country music and dominated the field for a couple of years. Their success was controversial, leading to rebellion from Nashville veterans, but their hits became too big to stop. "On our last episode, we compared the parallel 70s careers of John Denver and Olivia Newton John."
**Imperial Phase in the 1970s** : In 1975, John Denver was at the cusp of an "imperial phase," characterized by immense commercial success. His imperial peak came later in 1975 with the album "Wind Song." "John Denver's Imperial Peak came later in 1975 when he dropped his ninth studio album Wind Song."
**John Denver's Rocky Mountain Christmas (1975) - TV Special** : During the 1975 holiday season, John Denver invited Olivia Newton-John onto his TV special, fulfilling a long-ago promise. The stage set depicted them singing in a forest inside a snow globe, and the special was a ratings blockbuster. "John Denver made good on his long ago promise to Olivia Newton John by inviting her on his TV special, John Denver's Rocky Mountain Christmas."
**Vanity Record Label - Wind Song Records (established by 1976)** : John Denver's "Golden Touch" extended to his new vanity record label, Wind Song. The first act he signed was the Starland Vocal Band, his old friends Bill Danoff and Taffy Nivert from Fat City, whose number one hit "Afternoon Delight" was powered by Denver's label and promotional prowess. "Denver's Golden Touch extended to his new vanity record label, also called Wind Song."
**Political Advocacy (late 1970s - 1990s)** : John Denver maintained his celebrity through political advocacy, serving on President Jimmy Carter's Commission on World Hunger and supporting various environmental causes. He spoke out for the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge in the 90s and played a benefit in Colorado in 1992 against an anti-gay ballot measure. "In addition to his political advocacy on the Hunger Campaign and various environmental causes."
**Grammy Awards Host (1978-1985)** : Starting in 1978, John Denver was tapped to host the Grammy Awards, proving to be an "affable, broadly agreeable master of ceremonies." He hosted the Grammys six times between 1978 and 1985, tying with Trevor Noah and behind only Andy Williams who hosted seven times. "John Denver wound up hosting the Grammy's Six Times between 1978 and 1985."
**Film Role in "Oh God" (1977)** : In 1977, John Denver took on his first major film role as an actor, starring alongside George Burns in the comic fantasy "Oh God," directed by Carl Reiner. He played an ordinary supermarket manager chosen as God's modern-day messenger. The film was a critical success and the ninth highest-grossing film of 1977, with Denver's persona giving moviegoers a rooting interest, though the film contained no music. "John Denver took the role solely as an actor."
**Rejection from USA for Africa's "We Are the World" (1985)** : In 1985, John Denver's offer to participate in the "We Are the World" charity recording was rejected by the organizers despite his longstanding commitment to charity work. Industry professionals felt his "irrelevant greybeard image" would hurt the song's credibility, a snub Denver later said "broke his heart." "The organizers of USA for Africa's We Are the World rejected Denver's offer to participate in the blockbuster charity recording."
**Testimony at PMRC Hearings (1985)** : John Denver earned "cred and maybe even some coolness" by volunteering to testify before Congress as part of the Parents Music Resource Center (PMRC) hearings, a music policing effort led by Tipper Gore. Sitting alongside Frank Zappa and Dee Snider, Denver delivered eloquent testimony drawing on his own experience with censorship, such as "Rocky Mountain High" being banned. "At the PMRC hearings, Denver sat alongside Hipster Rock God Frank Zappa and Heavy Metal Frontman, D Snyder of Twisted Sister."
**International Tours (1980s-1990s)** : John Denver toured communist countries that other Western artists were not visiting, including Russia in 1985 (two years before Billy Joel) and again in 1987 for a Chernobyl benefit. He also toured China in 1992, where he even sang in Chinese. "He also toured communist countries, other Western artists were not visiting."
**1994 Memoir** : John Denver's 1994 memoir candidly discussed past drug use, marital infidelities, and a history of domestic violence, revealing a darker side to his public persona. "His 1994 memoir candidly spoke of past drug use, his marital infidelities, and his history of domestic violence."
**Death (October 12, 1997)** : John Denver died at age 53 on the afternoon of October 12, 1997, when his light home-built aircraft crashed into Monterey Bay near Pacific Grove, California. He had taken up flying planes and received his pilot's license, a hobby that ultimately ended his life. "On the afternoon of October 12, 1997, John Denver died when his light home-built aircraft crashed into Monterey Bay near Pacific Grove, California."
**Posthumous State Song Recognition** : Two of John Denver's compositions were posthumously named State Songs. In 2007, "Rocky Mountain High" became one of Colorado's official State Songs. In 2014, "Take Me Home Country Roads" was named one of West Virginia's State Songs. This made Denver only the second person, alongside Stephen Foster, to have written two State Songs. "Posturiously, two of his compositions were named State Songs."

## albums
**Have You Never Been Mellow (1975)** : Olivia Newton-John's next LP, released in early 1975, was an "instant smash," topping both the pop and country album charts in under a month. It generated the exceedingly laid-back single of the same name. "It was another near instant smash, topping both the pop and country album charts in under a month."
**Grease: The Original Soundtrack from the Motion Picture (1978)** : This double LP soundtrack was a blockbuster, spending 12 weeks on top of the album chart and becoming 1978's second best-seller behind the "Saturday Night Fever" soundtrack. It featured hits mostly sung or co-sung by Newton-John, including "Hopelessly Devoted to You," "Summer Nights" (with John Travolta), and "You're the One That I Want" (with John Travolta), successfully rebooting her career. "In all, the double LP soundtrack spent 12 weeks on top of the album chart, making it 1978's second best seller behind only the Saturday Night Fever soundtrack."
**Totally Hot (1978)** : Released less than five months after "Grease," this album marked Olivia Newton-John's comeback with a new look (dressed in head-to-toe leather) and sound. Its title made her intentions plain for a rock-oriented, sultry disco sound. It rose to number seven on the album chart and became her first platinum-certified LP. "She called it Totally Hot."
**Physical (1981)** : Olivia Newton-John's first solo studio album of the 1980s was led by her biggest hit of all, the title track "Physical." The album itself went double platinum and spawned the top five follow-up hit "Make a Move On Me." "As Physical, the album went double platinum, it spun off a top five follow-up hit."
**Xanadu: Original Motion Picture Soundtrack (1980)** : Despite the film being a critical and box office flop, this soundtrack reached number four on the album chart and went double platinum. It featured hits from both Newton-John (such as "Magic" and "Suddenly") and Electric Light Orchestra, transitioning Newton-John into the sound of '80s synth-pop. "The Xanadu soundtrack reached number four on the album chart and went double platinum."
**Soul Kiss (1985)** : Olivia Newton-John's first new studio album in four years featured provocative imagery but achieved modest chart success. The LP topped out at number 29 and went gold, not platinum, and its single "Soul Kiss" scraped the top 20, marking a decline in her hit-making streak. "The LP topped out at number 29 and went gold, not platinum."
**The Rumor (1988)** : Released after a hiatus for the birth of her daughter, this LP was named after a single written for Olivia Newton-John by Elton John and Bernie Taupin. The album flopped, with the single peaking below the top 40 at number 62, and marked the last time Newton-John actively courted a mainstream pop audience. "The Rumor album flopped."
**Warm and Tender (1989) - Children's Lullabies** : This album of children's lullabies reunited Olivia Newton-John with longtime collaborator John Farar. It generated an Adult Contemporary hit with her cover of the Burt Bacharach and Hal David classic "Reach Out for Me." "By 1989, she released an album of children's lullabies called Warm and Tender."
**Gaia, One Woman's Journey (1994)** : This album was entirely written and produced by Olivia Newton-John herself, chronicling her breast cancer ordeal. Fortunately, by the time of its release, her cancer was in remission. "Her 1994 album, Gaia, One Woman's Journey, written and produced entirely by Olivia herself, chronicled her breast cancer ordeal."
**Back With a Heart (1998)** : This album showed Olivia Newton-John had not forgotten her country audience, cracking the country albums top 10 and updating her sound for the 90s country era. It also featured an R&B-flavored remake of her 1974 chart-topper "I Honestly Love You." "Newton-John also never forgot her country audience, her 1998 album Back With a Heart, cracked the country albums top 10 and updated her sound for the 90s country era."

The film was the top-grossing movie of 1978, making $132 million. "The film adaptation of the Broadway musical Grease wasn't just a hit, it was a blockbuster."

**Breast Cancer Diagnosis and Advocacy (1992 onwards)** : Olivia Newton-John received a breast cancer diagnosis in 1992.
