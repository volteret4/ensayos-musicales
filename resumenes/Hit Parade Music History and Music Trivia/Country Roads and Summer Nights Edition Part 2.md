## albums
**Back Home Again (1974) - Home of the Studio "Country Boy"** : The studio version of "Thank God I'm a Country Boy" first appeared as a deep cut on this LP. Denver was still winding up the singles from it in early 1975, when Olivia Newton-John released "Have You Never Been Mellow." "The studio version of the song was a deep cut on Denver's back home again LP."
**An Evening with John Denver (1975) - Live Album That Produced a #1** : This concert album opened Denver's most commercially dominant year. It generated a number one single, the live "Thank God I'm a Country Boy," which is a relative rarity because only a handful of live songs have ever topped the Hot 100. "even that LP generated a number one single, a relative rarity, only a handful of live songs have ever topped the Hot 100."
**Windsong (1975) - Ninth Studio Album and Commercial Peak** : Denver's ninth studio album topped both the pop and country LP charts in just three weeks. It produced "I'm Sorry," his least remembered number one, and its B-side "Calypso," which overtook the A-side on radio. It also contained "Fly Away," which featured Olivia Newton-John on backing vocals. "Denver's Imperial Peak came later in 1975 when he dropped his ninth studio album Wind Song."
**I Want to Live (1977) - First Post-"Oh, God!" LP** : The album came out as President Jimmy Carter appointed Denver to the President's Commission on World Hunger, and Denver offered the title track as the Commission's theme song. It underperformed on the album charts. Its lead single "How Can I Leave You Again" missed the pop Top 40 and reached only number 22 country, because Denver's earnest balladry seemed ill-suited to the late 70s amid disco and New Wave. "I Want to Live underperformed on the album charts."
**Greatest Hits Volume 2 (1977) - Follow-Up Compilation** : Denver released this second greatest hits album to match his earlier bestseller. Its re-recorded single "My Sweet Lady" only scraped the bottom rungs of the American Top 40. "Denver also tried issuing another greatest hits album in 1977 to match his prior bestseller."
**A Christmas Together (1979) - Muppets Collaboration Soundtrack** : This was the soundtrack to Denver's first television special with Jim Henson's Muppets, and the special was a hit TV show. The soundtrack went platinum and sold better than Denver's own previous two LPs. "Its Platinum Soundtrack album sold better than Denver's own previous two LPs."

## songs
**Take Me Home, Country Roads (1971)** : Bill Danoff and Taffy Nivert, Denver's old friends from Fat City, co-wrote the song. In 1971 it took nearly five months to climb to number two on the Hot 100 and peaked at a lowly number 50 on the country chart. In 2014 the West Virginia legislature approved a resolution naming it one of the state's official state songs. Olivia Newton-John kept performing it until she stopped touring in 2017. "In 1971, country roads took nearly five months to climb to number two on the hot 100."
**Rocky Mountain High - Banned and Later a State Song** : Many radio stations banned the song as drug-related, and Denver cited this in his 1985 PMRC testimony as his own experience of censorship. In 2007, a decade after his death, the Colorado Senate passed a resolution making it one of the state's official state songs. "my song Rocky Mountain High was banned from many radio stations as a drug-related song."
**Annie's Song (1974)** : This 1974 chart-topper was written about Denver's wife Annie, whom he divorced bitterly in 1982. "He and his wife Annie, the titular subject of his 1974 chart-topper Annie's song, went through a bitter divorce in 1982."
**Thank God I'm a Country Boy (1975)** : This was Denver's only number one hit that he did not write himself. John Martin Sommers, a multi-instrumentalist in Denver's backing band, wrote it on a drive from Aspen to Los Angeles. Denver saw Sommers play it in a club one night and asked to record it. It has a clapping beat, a hoedown cadence and a fiddle, and its lyrics mention playing the fiddle. It was a deep cut on Back Home Again, but the live version from An Evening with John Denver was released as a single and reached number one on both the pop and country charts in just a couple of months. It was Denver's second hit with "country" in the title, after "Take Me Home, Country Roads." "Was John Denver's only number one hit he didn't write himself?"
**I'm Sorry (1975)** : This ballad about the end of a relationship, from Windsong, reached number one on the Hot 100 in just seven weeks. Six weeks later it also topped the country chart, driven entirely by Denver's career momentum. Stereogum's number ones columnist Tom Breihan called it Denver's least memorable number one and "the most wispily generic." Pop audiences dropped it while it was still charting in favor of its B-side, "Calypso." "It flew to number one on the hot 100 in just seven weeks."
**Calypso (1975)** : The song is a tribute to Denver's friend, the undersea explorer Jacques Cousteau, and is named after Cousteau's ship, the Calypso. It was the B-side of "I'm Sorry." Two weeks after "I'm Sorry" fell from number one, "Calypso" was getting so much more airplay that Billboard flipped the chart entry and listed it as the A-side. Denver yodels on the chorus. Tom Breihan called it "the show stopping musical number from a Disney cartoon that does not exist." "Billboard flipped the entry on their charts and listed Calypso as the A side."
**Fly Away (1975) - With Olivia Newton-John** : This follow-up from Windsong was Denver's first recorded collaboration with Olivia Newton-John, who sang backing vocals. She was not formally credited as a featured artist, which was common practice at the time. Released just before Christmas 1975, it topped the Easy Listening chart, officially Denver's sixth number one there and unofficially Newton-John's seventh. It peaked just outside the top 10 on both the pop and country charts. "Her vocals were hard to miss."
**I Want to Live (1977)** : Denver offered the title track as the theme song for the President's Commission on World Hunger after Jimmy Carter appointed him to it. "Denver offered the I Want to Live title track as the Commission's theme song."
**How Can I Leave You Again (1977)** : The lead single from I Want to Live missed the pop Top 40 and reached only number 22 country. "its lead single How Can I Leave You Again, Missed the Pop Top 40 and Only Reach Number 22 Country."
**My Sweet Lady (1977) - Last Top 40 Pop Hit for Four Years** : Denver originally wrote the song for the TV movie "Sunshine," and actor-singer Cliff DeYoung had a hit with it in 1974. Denver's re-recording from Greatest Hits Volume 2 debuted at number 39 on Casey Kasem's American Top 40 and peaked at number 32 on the Hot 100. It was his last Top 40 pop hit for over four years. "it would be John Denver's last Top 40 pop hit for over four years."
**Some Days Are Diamonds (Some Days Are Stone) (1981) - Secret LGBTQ Anthem** : This 1981 comeback hit was not written by Denver. Its songwriter, Dina K. Rose, came out as trans in the 2010s and revealed she wrote it about her struggles with gender identity, with diamonds and stone as metaphors for her feminine and masculine sides. Denver could not have known this in 1981, when the song reached number 36 on the Hot 100 and number 10 country. Lyric: "Now the face that I see in my mirror, more and more is a stranger to me." "It was a secret LGBTQ anthem."
**Shanghai Breezes (1982) - Final Top 40 Hit and Last #1** : Denver's trip to China inspired the song, which has a bit of a new age feel. It reached number 31 on the Hot 100 and topped the Adult Contemporary chart (newly renamed from Easy Listening) for one week. It was his last number one on any Billboard chart. "It was John Denver's last number one song on any Billboard Chart."
**The Gold and Beyond (1984) - Sarajevo Winter Olympics Theme** : Denver wrote this anthem when asked for a theme song for the 1984 Winter Olympics in Sarajevo. It got plenty of play on ABC's Olympics coverage but did not chart anywhere. "His anthemic offering, The Gold and Beyond, got plenty of play on ABC's Olympics coverage but did not chart anywhere."

## curiosities
**John Denver's Rocky Mountain Christmas (1975) - TV Special With Olivia** : During the 1975 holiday season, Denver kept a long-ago promise to Olivia Newton-John by inviting her onto his TV special. The set showed the two of them singing in a forest inside a snow globe. The special was a TV ratings blockbuster. "John Denver made good on his long ago promise to Olivia Newton John by inviting her on his TV special."
**Windsong Records - Launching the Starland Vocal Band** : Denver's vanity label was also named Windsong. The first act he signed was his old friends from Fat City, Bill Danoff and Taffy Nivert, who had formed the Starland Vocal Band with another couple. Backed by Denver's label and promotion, the band had a number one with "Afternoon Delight" in summer 1976. "The first act Denver signed to his label were his old friends from Fat City who'd co-written his hit Take Me Home Country Roads."
**Oh, God! (1977) - Acting Role Opposite George Burns** : Denver co-starred with George Burns in this 1977 comic fantasy, directed by Carl Reiner and written by Larry Gelbart. The 81-year-old Burns played God, who picks an ordinary supermarket manager, played by the 33-year-old Denver, as his modern-day messenger. Gene Siskel and Roger Ebert both praised it, and it was the ninth highest-grossing film of 1977, the year of Star Wars. Denver took the role purely as an actor: the film was not a musical and had no soundtrack, so it helped his public persona more than his pop career. "John Denver took the role solely as an actor."
**Earlier TV Acting** : Before Oh, God!, Denver had acted in a couple of TV series. "even Denver had acted in a couple of TV series."
**President's Commission on World Hunger** : President Jimmy Carter appointed Denver to the Commission in 1977. "President Jimmy Carter appointing Denver to the President's Commission on World Hunger."
**Grammy Awards Host - Six Times (1978-1985)** : Starting in 1978 Denver hosted the Grammy Awards, and he proved an affable master of ceremonies. He hosted six times between 1978 and 1985, tying Trevor Noah and trailing only Andy Williams, who hosted seven times. "John Denver wound up hosting the Grammy's Six Times between 1978 and 1985."
**Muppets Partnership (1979)** : In 1979 Denver teamed up with Jim Henson's Muppets for the TV special "A Christmas Together," which became a hit. "maybe Denver's most beloved career pivot came in 1979 when he teamed up with Jim Henson's Muppets."
**Image Makeover in the Early 80s** : Denver dropped his trademark wire-frame glasses and floppy pageboy haircut. Unlike Olivia Newton-John, he did not go fully MTV, and his sound stayed largely the same. "Finally ditching his trademark wireframe glasses and floppy page boy haircut."
**Divorce From Annie (1982)** : Denver and his wife Annie went through a bitter divorce in 1982. "went through a bitter divorce in 1982."
**Rejected From We Are the World (1985)** : The organizers of USA for Africa's "We Are the World" turned down Denver's offer to take part, despite his long record of charity work. Some industry professionals thought his image would hurt the song's credibility, yet Dan Aykroyd was invited to sing. Denver later said the snub broke his heart, especially since he was still hosting the Grammys at the time. "Denver later said the snub broke his heart."
**PMRC Senate Testimony (1985)** : Denver volunteered to testify at the Parents Music Resource Center hearings, the music-policing effort led by Tipper Gore, wife of Senator Al Gore. He sat alongside Frank Zappa and Dee Snider of Twisted Sister. He argued that a government-enforced rating system would approach censorship, citing the radio bans on "Rocky Mountain High" as a drug song. "Mr. Chairman, this would approach censorship."
**Activism and Advocacy** : Denver was especially active on nuclear disarmament, hunger relief and the environment. He spoke out to protect the Arctic National Wildlife Refuge and in the 90s supported the Clinton administration's efforts to limit drilling there. In 1992 he played a benefit in Colorado against a ballot measure that would have stopped municipalities from passing anti-discrimination protections for LGBTQ people. "Denver was especially active on nuclear disarmament, hunger relief, and environmental causes."
**Touring Communist Countries** : Denver toured countries other Western artists were not visiting. He played Russia in 1985, two years before Billy Joel, and again in 1987 for a Chernobyl benefit. He toured China in 1992 and even sang in Chinese. "He also toured communist countries, other Western artists were not visiting."
**1994 Memoir** : Denver's memoir candidly described his past drug use, his marital infidelities and his history of domestic violence. "His 1994 memoir candidly spoke of past drug use, his marital infidelities, and his history of domestic violence."
**Death in Plane Crash (October 12, 1997)** : Denver took up flying and got his pilot's license to be closer to the mountain skies he loved. On the afternoon of October 12, 1997, his light home-built aircraft crashed into Monterey Bay near Pacific Grove, California, killing him at age 53. "That hobby ultimately ended his life."
**Two Official State Songs - Joining Stephen Foster** : Colorado named "Rocky Mountain High" a state song in 2007, and West Virginia named "Take Me Home, Country Roads" a state song in 2014. Denver became only the second person, after Stephen Foster, to have written two state songs. "John Denver became only the second person, along with father of American music Stephen Foster, to have written two State Songs."

## albums
**Have You Never Been Mellow (1975) - Double Chart-Topper** : Released in early 1975, the album topped both the pop and country album charts in under a month. "topping both the pop and country album charts in under a month."
**Grease Soundtrack (1978) - Double LP Blockbuster** : The soundtrack was packed with hits, most of them sung or co-sung by Newton-John. It spent 12 weeks at number one and was 1978's second best-selling album, behind only the Saturday Night Fever soundtrack. "the double LP soundtrack spent 12 weeks on top of the album chart."
**Totally Hot (1978) - Post-Grease Reinvention** : Newton-John released it less than five months after Grease opened. On the cover she wears head-to-toe leather, much like Sandy's final scene in Grease, and the album brought a new rock and disco sound. It reached number seven, became her first platinum-certified LP, and also made the top ten of the country albums chart. "And became her first platinum certified LP."
**Xanadu Soundtrack (1980) - Hit Despite the Flop** : Shared with Electric Light Orchestra, the soundtrack reached number four on the album chart and went double platinum, so none of the film's failure stuck to her. It also moved Newton-John toward 80s synth-pop. "Thanks to the soundtrack, none of Xanadu's stink stuck to Olivia Newton-John."
**Physical (1981) - First Solo Studio Album of the 80s** : The album went double platinum and produced "Physical," her biggest hit, and the top five follow-up "Make a Move on Me." "As Physical, the album went double platinum, it spun off a top five follow-up hit."
**Two of a Kind Soundtrack (1983) - Platinum Despite the Film** : The film had modest box office and poor reviews, but the soundtrack went platinum and produced two 1984 hits, "Twist of Fate" and "Livin' in Desperate Times." "the soundtrack went platinum and generated two 1984 hits for Olivia."
**Soul Kiss (1985) - Return to Sensual Image** : Her first new studio album in four years used provocative imagery but performed only moderately. It peaked at number 29 and went gold rather than platinum, which marked the end of her hit-making streak. "The LP topped out at number 29 and went gold, not platinum."
**The Rumour (1988) - Last Mainstream Pop Push** : Newton-John released it after taking time off for the birth of her daughter Chloe in 1986. It was named for a single written for her by Elton John and Bernie Taupin. The album flopped, and it was the last time she actively pursued a mainstream pop audience. "It was the last time Newton-John actively courted a mainstream pop audience."
**Warm and Tender (1989) - Children's Lullabies** : This album of lullabies reunited her with John Farrar and produced an AC hit with her cover of Burt Bacharach and Hal David's "Reach Out for Me." "By 1989, she released an album of children's lullabies called Warm and Tender."
**Gaia: One Woman's Journey (1994) - Breast Cancer Chronicle** : Olivia wrote and produced the whole album herself, and it tells the story of her breast cancer ordeal. Her cancer was in remission by then. "written and produced entirely by Olivia herself, chronicled her breast cancer ordeal."
**Back With a Heart (1998) - Return to Country** : The album reached the top 10 of the country albums chart and updated her sound for 90s country. "her 1998 album Back With a Heart, cracked the country albums top 10."

## songs
**Have You Never Been Mellow (1975)** : The laid-back single topped the Hot 100 and, despite having very little country twang, reached number three on the country singles chart. It is cited as a sign of her commercial invincibility at the time. "despite containing very little twang, it made it all the way up to number three."
**Please Mr. Please (1975)** : The follow-up reached number three pop and number five country, with lyrics about roadhouse jukeboxes and cowboys. "Shot to Number Three Pop, Number Five Country."
**Let It Shine (1976)** : It reached the country top five but barely made the pop top 30, a sign that her country image was starting to pigeonhole her. "made the country top five but barely scraped the top 30 on the pop side."
**Don't Stop Believin' (1976)** : After seven top ten country hits between 1973 and 1976, this single fell short of the country top ten. It is unrelated to the later Journey hit. "no relation to the later journey hit."
**Making a Good Thing Better (1977)** : The single reached only number 87 pop and did not chart country at all, which told her team something had to change. "Team Olivia knew something had to change."
**Hopelessly Devoted to You (1978)** : John Farrar wrote it for the Grease film as a showcase for her, and it was not in the original Broadway show. It reached number three on the Hot 100 and number 20 country. A week after her death in 2022 it led a dozen of her songs back onto Billboard's digital sales chart, as fans' favorite song to mourn her. "A new song, not in the original Broadway show, written by Olivia's longtime collaborator John Farar as a showcase for her."
**Summer Nights (1978) - With John Travolta** : The Grease duet reached number five on the Hot 100. "a duet between Travolta and Newton-John, reached number five on the Hot 100."
**You're the One That I Want (1978) - With John Travolta** : John Farrar wrote this closing duet for the film, and it reached number one on the Hot 100. It follows the scene where Sandy appears in skin-tight pants and a bouncy perm. "went all the way to number one on the Hot 100."
**A Little More Love (1978)** : The first Totally Hot single was written by John Farrar and sounded unlike her earlier hits: rock-oriented with sultry disco touches. Lyrics include "No is a word I can't say" and "Where did my innocence go?" It reached number three on the Hot 100 by February 1979. "Rock-oriented with sultry disco overtones."
**Deeper Than the Night (1979)** : The follow-up leaned a bit toward New Wave rock and reached number 11. "a number 11 hit, even leaned a bit toward New Wave Rock."
**Dancin' 'Round and 'Round (1979) - Last Country Top 40** : Her last twangy single, a farewell to her country audience, reached number 29 on the Hot Country Singles chart. It was the final top 40 country hit of her career. "It would be the last top 40 country hit of Olivia Newton-John's career."
**Magic (1980)** : The Xanadu lead single topped the Hot 100 for four weeks in August 1980 and kept her sultry post-Grease image. Billboard ranked it the third biggest hit of the year. "according to Billboard, magic wound up the third biggest hit of the year."
**Xanadu (1980) - With Electric Light Orchestra** : The ELO title song, with Olivia on lead vocals, reached number eight on the Hot 100. "That reached number eight on the Hot 100."
**Suddenly (1980) - With Cliff Richard** : This disco ballad duet with her old friend Cliff Richard reached number 20. "Their disco ballad suddenly reached number twenty."
**Physical (1981) - Biggest Hit** : Steve Kipner, her longtime friend, co-wrote it, and it tapped into the 1981 fitness craze, the year of Jane Fonda's Workout book and video. It was originally meant for a male rock singer like Rod Stewart, so it was full of innuendo. Newton-John kept the lyrics but then worried they were too risqué. By then radio DJs already had it in heavy rotation. In January 1982 it tied Debby Boone's rock-era record of 10 weeks at number one, a record otherwise beaten only by Elvis Presley's "Hound Dog"/"Don't Be Cruel," which had 11 weeks before the Hot 100 existed. "For the 10th consecutive week, the most popular song in the land is Physical by Olivia Newton-John."
**Make a Move on Me (1982)** : The racy follow-up from Physical reached the top five. "The irresistibly racy-bop make a move on me."
**Heart Attack (1982)** : A new-wave song recorded new for a greatest hits album, it reached number three. "The New Wave Bop Heart Attack, Climbed to number three."
**Twist of Fate (1983)** : This theme song from Two of a Kind reached number five in 1984. "Twist of Fate climbed all the way to number five."
**Livin' in Desperate Times (1984)** : The second Two of a Kind hit reached number 31. "a follow-up living in desperate times reached number 31."
**Soul Kiss (1985)** : The title single barely made the top 20 on both the Hot 100 and adult contemporary charts. "the Soul Kiss single scraped the top 20 on both the Hot 100 and adult contemporary charts."
**The Rumour (1988) - Written by Elton John & Bernie Taupin** : Elton John and Bernie Taupin wrote it for her. It was catchy but sounded more like Elton than Olivia, and it peaked at number 62. "It was catchy but sounded more like Elton than Olivia."
**Reach Out for Me (1989)** : Her cover of the Burt Bacharach/Hal David classic, from Warm and Tender, was an AC hit. "generated an AC hit with her cover of the Bert Bakerak Hal David Classic Reach Out for Me."
**I Honestly Love You (1998 Remake) - With Babyface** : She remade her 1974 chart-topper as an R&B slow jam, with backing vocals by Babyface. It reached number 67 on the Hot 100. "Reimagined as a slow jam, the remake featured backing vocals by R&B singer-songwriter Babyface."
**Physical (2010 Glee Remake) - With Jane Lynch** : On the TV series Glee she guested on an EDM-style remake of "Physical" with Jane Lynch. It reached number 89 and was her last Hot 100 hit. "Newton-John's last hot 100 hit came in 2010."
**Take Me Home, Country Roads - Live Staple** : She was still performing her friend John Denver's song when she stopped touring in 2017. "she was still performing her friend John Denver's most cherished hit song."

## curiosities
**Toomorrow (1970) - Early Film Flop** : Before her stardom she appeared in this sci-fi musical, described as disastrous. "Newton John's appearance in the disastrous sci-fi musical Tomorrow in 1970."
**Unofficial Seventh Easy Listening #1 via "Fly Away"** : Her uncredited backing vocals on John Denver's "Fly Away" unofficially gave her a seventh number one on the Easy Listening chart. "unofficially making it Newton John's seventh number one on that chart."
**Grease (1978) - Career-Rebooting Blockbuster** : Grease was the top-grossing film of 1978, earning $132 million. John Travolta reportedly lobbied for her to play Sandy. Sandy's backstory was rewritten to make her an Australian exchange student so Newton-John could keep her natural accent, and she did all her own singing. Sandy's final transformation reshaped her public image and foreshadowed her own career pivot. "Legend has it John Travolta himself lobbied for Olivia Newton-John to get the role of Sandy."
**Country-to-Pop Pivot Compared to Taylor Swift** : Like Taylor Swift on Red three decades later, Newton-John gave her country fans one last twangy single on a transitional album. Her younger fans in the 80s and beyond never knew she had been a country star. "Olivia's younger fans in the 1980s and beyond would never register she was ever a country star."
**Xanadu (1980) - Flop Film, Hit Soundtrack** : She played Kira, a magical spirit who enchants a struggling illustrator named Sonny. It was Gene Kelly's final film, and she sang and danced with him. The film had elaborate sets, special effects and dozens of roller-skating extras but no comprehensible plot. It opened in late summer 1980, barely earned back its budget, made no profit and was critically reviled. "What it didn't have was a comprehensible plot."
**Two of a Kind (1983) - Travolta Reunion** : This comic fantasy reunited her with John Travolta. It did modest box office and got reviews nearly as bad as Xanadu's. Returning to Travolta and her sensual image again helped end her hit streak. "What finally ended Newton-John's hit-making streak was going repeatedly to the same well."
**Birth of Daughter Chloe (1986)** : She took time off to give birth to her daughter Chloe in 1986. "After taking a time out to give birth to her daughter Chloe in 1986."
**Breast Cancer Advocacy** : She was diagnosed with breast cancer in 1992 and became a breast cancer research advocate and spokesperson for a breast self-examination product called Livkit. "She became a breast cancer research advocate and spokesperson for a breast self examination product called Livkit."
**Death (August 8, 2022)** : In May 2017 she announced that her breast cancer had returned, spread to her back and reached stage 4. She lived five more years and died on August 8, 2022 at her home in the Santa Ynez Valley, aged 73. Obituaries focused on Grease and "Physical" and mentioned her country career mostly in passing. "died on August 8, 2022 at her home in the Santa Inez Valley."

# curiosity

**Live Songs at #1 on the Hot 100 Are Rare** : Only a handful of live recordings have ever topped the Hot 100, which made Denver's live "Thank God I'm a Country Boy" notable. "only a handful of live songs have ever topped the Hot 100."
**Uncredited Featured Artists in the 70s** : It was common practice at the time not to formally credit guest vocalists as featured artists, as with Newton-John on "Fly Away." "This was common practice at the time."
**Easy Listening Renamed Adult Contemporary** : By 1982 Billboard's Easy Listening chart had been renamed Adult Contemporary. "for one week topped the Adult Contemporary Chart. Now finally renamed from Easy Listening."
**Rock-Era Weeks-at-#1 Records Through 1982** : Through 1981 only two rock-era singles had spent 10 or more weeks at number one: Elvis Presley's double-sided "Hound Dog"/"Don't Be Cruel" with 11 weeks in 1956, before the Hot 100 existed, and Debby Boone's "You Light Up My Life" with 10 weeks in 1977. Olivia Newton-John's "Physical" tied Boone in January 1982, as Casey Kasem announced on American Top 40. "only two rock-era singles had ever spent 10 weeks or more at number one."
**Most Frequent Grammy Hosts** : Andy Williams hosted the Grammys seven times, the record. John Denver and Trevor Noah are tied behind him with six each. "behind only Andy Williams, who hosted seven times."
**We Are the World (1985) Exclusion Controversy** : USA for Africa's organizers rejected John Denver's offer to join the charity recording over concerns about his image, while inviting Dan Aykroyd. "Dan Acroid was invited to sing on We Are the World. Go figure."
**PMRC Hearings (1985)** : The Parents Music Resource Center hearings, led by Tipper Gore, considered whether the government should enforce rating labels on records with explicit sex and graphic violence. John Denver, Frank Zappa and Dee Snider of Twisted Sister testified. "The infamous music policing effort spearheaded by Senator Al Gore's wife, Tipper Gore."
**Pop Stars Turning to Film** : Pop stars at career turning points, from Elvis Presley to Whitney Houston to Ariana Grande, have often turned to movies, as Denver and Newton-John both did in 1977. "So, what have countless pop stars at a career inflection point from Elvis Presley to Whitney Houston to Ariana Grande, done to shake things up. Why movies, of course."
**Writers of Two Official State Songs** : Only two people have written two official US state songs: Stephen Foster and John Denver. "John Denver became only the second person, along with father of American music Stephen Foster, to have written two State Songs."
