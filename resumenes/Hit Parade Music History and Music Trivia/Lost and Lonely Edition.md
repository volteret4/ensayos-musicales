## albums
**Pornography (1982) - The Darkest Album Yet** : Preceded by the single "Charlotte Sometimes," Pornography was the Cure's darkest album up to that point. It made the UK top 10 in 1982 as goth music was becoming more commercial. Its gloom pushed Robert Smith toward his first major stylistic turn, the pop single "Let's Go to Bed." Seven years later, Disintegration would be described as his darkest set of songs since Pornography. "the cure's unremittingly dark pornography made the top 10 as well in 1982."
**The Head on the Door (1985) - Pop Melodies Meet Gloom** : The album balanced the band's pop melodies with Robert Smith's gloomiest lyrics. It dominated college radio and reached number 59 on the Billboard album chart, higher than any Cure studio album before it. It produced "In Between Days" and "Close to Me." "split the difference between the band's pop melodies and Robert Smith's gloomiest lyrics."
**Standing on a Beach (1986) - First Singles Collection Goes Gold** : The Cure's first singles collection peaked at only number 48 on the Billboard album chart. In early 1987, nearly a year after its release, it quietly went gold in America. This showed the band's US fanbase was growing even without a top 40 hit. "the Cures' secret army of US fans was growing."
**Kiss Me, Kiss Me, Kiss Me (1987) - Ambitious Double LP** : Released three months after Standing on a Beach went gold, this double LP was the band's most ambitious album to that point. It held 18 new songs, all written by Robert Smith, which showed his playful sense of humor was intact. It produced "Why Can't I Be You" and "Just Like Heaven," the singles that finally broke the Cure on the Hot 100. "18 new songs all penned by Robert Smith"
**Disintegration (1989) - Biggest-Selling Studio Album** : Released in May 1989, the album swung back to heavy, dark goth rock after the Cure's mid-80s pop turn. Smith wrote his darkest songs since Pornography, yet they sounded stadium-sized. It became the Cure's biggest-selling and most acclaimed studio album, eventually selling more than 3 million copies worldwide. It was certified platinum in America the same week "Love Song" peaked at number 2, and it spent six months in the Billboard album chart's top 30, longer than contemporary LPs by Prince and the Rolling Stones. It kept producing modern rock hits into 1990. "Robert Smith wrote his darkest set of songs since 1982's pornography, and yet the songs sounded massive, majestic, stadium-size."
**Wish (1992) - Highest-Charting Album** : Disintegration remains the band's top seller, but Wish charted higher, peaking at number 2. This was partly thanks to the more accurate SoundScan chart technology of the 1990s. It produced the top 20 pop hit "Friday I'm in Love." Over the following two decades, the Cure released another half-dozen albums that reached the Billboard top 40. "thanks in part to more accurate sound scan chart technology in the 1990s."

## songs
**Killing an Arab (1978) - Camus Adaptation** : This early single set lyrics adapted from Albert Camus's philosophical novel The Stranger. Robert Smith took pains to explain that its provocative title was a literary reference. It helped establish the Cure's propulsive, enigmatic sound, which matched Joy Division in minor-key moodiness. "was a literary reference, not a racist manifesto."
**Boys Don't Cry (1979) - Defining the Lyrical Outlook** : An early form of punk pop. On this song, more than on "Killing an Arab," Robert Smith began defining the Cure's lyrical outlook: passion shrouded in remorse and regret, sung in a morose voice even on romantic songs. Despite its catchiness, this kind of dirge-like post-punk was not chart music in the late 70s, even in England. "He conveyed passion by shrouding it in expressions of remorse, regret, and self-recrimination."
**A Forest (1980) - First UK Top 40 Hit** : The brooding, rumbling single that finally put the Cure in the UK top 40 in 1980. By then Robert Smith had decided that full-on gothic rock was the core of the band's sound, though his pop instincts kept even the darkest singles swooning and majestic. "Robert Smith had determined that full on gothic rock was the core of the cure's sound."
**Charlotte Sometimes (1981) - Prelude to Pornography** : A dreamlike, desperate 1981 single that came before the album Pornography. It reached the middle of the British charts. "it scraped the middle rungs of the British charts."
**Let's Go to Bed (1982) - First Major Stylistic Turn** : A sharp turn into straight-ahead three-minute synth pop, the most cheerful Robert Smith had made. Fiction Records warned it might alienate goth fans, but Smith felt he needed it for his own sanity after Pornography. He kept his droll, doomy persona in lines like "well I don't care if you don't, and I don't want it if you don't," inside a cheeky dance song about sex. It reached only number 44 in the UK in late 1982. In America it was a smash on new wave stations like KROQ, WLIR and WFNX, which did not report to the Hot 100, and it quietly began the Cure's US breakthrough. "Let's Go To Bed was also the most cheerful synth pop Robert Smith had ever produced."
**The Love Cats (1983) - Playful Top 15 Hit** : A playful pop single that combined the Cure's dark, droll image with lighter lyrics. It reached the UK top 15. In America it was among the most played songs on new wave radio in 1983. "The Walk reached the top 10, Love Cats the Top 15."
**The Walk (1983) - Danceable UK Top 10** : A more danceable Cure single that reached the UK top 10. It was among the most played songs on American new wave stations in 1983, when the Cure and New Order were both moving closer to dance music. "The Walk reached the top 10, Love Cats the Top 15."
**In Between Days (1985) - Love Song About Feeling Too Old** : Lead single from The Head on the Door, a soaring love song about feeling too old to fall in love. It topped alternative radio playlists. It spent a single week at number 99, the Cure's only real Hot 100 appearance before 1987. "a soaring love song about feeling too old to fall in love"
**Close to Me (1985) - Most Infectious Dance Song** : Released soon after "In Between Days," this was the Cure's most infectious dance song to that point. Within a month of its release, Siouxsie and the Banshees released "Cities in Dust." "the percolating, minimalist and moody close to me."
**Why Can't I Be You (1987) - Breaking the Logjam** : Before this song, the Cure had almost no Hot 100 hits: two singles had bubbled under, and "In Between Days" had spent one week at number 99. This horn-driven, R&B-flavored, slightly lewd single peaked at number 54 in summer 1987, setting up the band's top 40 breakthrough. "The horn inflected, R&B flavored, and slightly lewd single"
**Just Like Heaven (1987) - First US Top 40 Hit** : Robert Smith's most acclaimed composition, named by Rolling Stone and Pitchfork as one of the greatest songs of the 1980s. It builds up one instrument at a time, has no lyrics for nearly a minute, and uses its title only once, at the very end. It reached number 40 on the Hot 100 in the first week of January 1988, just barely the Cure's first American top 40 hit. "has no lyrics for nearly a minute, and deploys its title only once at the very end"
**Fascination Street (1989) - Seven Weeks at Modern Rock #1** : The lead single for Disintegration, a muscular goth rock anthem that spent seven weeks at number 1 on the Modern Rock chart. It was the second-biggest Modern Rock hit of 1989. It was knocked from the top spot by Love and Rockets' "So Alive," one set of Bauhaus-linked goth veterans replacing another. "a muscular goth rock anthem that spent seven weeks atop the Modern Rock chart."
**Love Song (1989) - Number 2 on the Hot 100** : Perhaps Robert Smith's most vulnerable composition, which he called "an open show of emotion." It was the Hot 100's highest new entry the week of August 12, 1989, debuting at number 58. It entered the top 40 two weeks later, the top 20 a month after that, and the top 10 the following week. Three weeks later, in the week ending October 21, 1989, it peaked at number 2 behind Janet Jackson's "Miss You Much." That marked a new pop peak for gothic British alternative rock in America. "Love Song was perhaps Robert Smith's most vulnerable composition."
**Lullaby (1989) - Disintegration Single** : One of several Disintegration singles that kept charting on modern rock into 1990. "the Dreamlike Creepy Spider Fantasia Lullaby"
**Pictures of You (1989) - Slow-Burning Sleeper Hit** : A later Disintegration single that became a slow-burning hit, extending the album's run into 1990. "the slow-burning sleeper hit Pictures of You."
**Friday I'm in Love (1992) - Top 20 Pop Hit** : The top 20 pop hit from Wish, the Cure's highest-charting album. "generated the Deathless Top 20 Pop Hit Friday I'm in Love."

## curiosities
**From Malice to Easy Cure to The Cure** : Robert Smith, a young guitarist and songwriter from Crawley, played in bands with schoolmates from the early 70s. In 1976, inspired by the Sex Pistols and by early post-punks like Siouxsie Sioux, he joined a group called Malice, later renamed the Easy Cure. Smith later shortened the name to The Cure. "Smith shortened the band's name from Easy Cure to The Cure"
**A Reluctant Singer** : The self-effacing but quietly assertive Smith never meant to sing. He took the job after a string of vocalists left the group. He later told Musician magazine about his voice. "I hated my voice, but I didn't hate it more than I hated everyone else's voice."
**The Goth Look** : Around 1981–82, as the Cure joined the wave of bands fully embracing the goth persona, Smith adopted white makeup, dark lipstick, eyeliner and spiky hair. He befriended Siouxsie Sioux, who wore a similar spiky-haired goth look. "Robert Smith changed his look to a mask of white makeup, dark lipstick, and eyeliner, and a shock of spiky, spidery hair."
**Pop for Sanity** : Seven years before Disintegration, Smith moved away from the darkest goth rock with frothy singles like "Let's Go to Bed" and "The Love Cats," for both the band's career and his own mental health. With Disintegration the pendulum swung back. "with the album Decentigration, the pendulum swung back."
**Revolving Lineup** : Members came and went, including bassist Simon Gallup and guitarist Porl Thompson, each of whom left and rejoined more than once. Robert Smith remained the fixture and leader. "Robert Smith remained the fixture, the leader and the guiding force"
**1989 Stadium Tour with Love and Rockets** : In summer 1989 the Cure toured with Love and Rockets and played New Jersey's Giants Stadium, matching Depeche Mode's Rose Bowl show. The Pixies were also on the bill. "That summer, The Cure and Love and Rockets would tour together, even playing New Jersey's giant stadium"
**Rock and Roll Hall of Fame Induction (2019)** : After multiple nominations, the Cure were inducted in 2019, the same year as Janet Jackson, whose "Miss You Much" kept "Love Song" from number 1 thirty years earlier. Self-proclaimed Cure acolyte Trent Reznor of Nine Inch Nails inducted them. Smith stood on stage in front of nine current and former members of the band, by then more than 40 years old, and the band played a five-song set of obscurities and hits. "Thank you for the vote for us. It's a very nice surprise to be inducted into the Rock Hall."
**Influence on Emo and Goth Fashion** : The Cure, along with Siouxsie Sioux and Bauhaus, largely invented the look later sold at Hot Topic. Mid-2000s emo bands such as New Jersey's My Chemical Romance took visual and sonic inspiration from the Cure. "took both visual and sonic inspiration from the cure."

## songs
**Love Will Tear Us Apart (1980) - First UK Top 40 Hit of the Scene** : The first of these post-punk bands' songs to become a UK top 40 hit, reaching number 13 in summer 1980. It also reached the lower rungs of the American dance charts. It became a blueprint for modern and alternative rock in the following decade. Because singer Ian Curtis had died before it peaked, it became an elegy for Joy Division itself. "It was a down-tempo dance track for depressives, a gloriously sad song."

## curiosities
**Formed After the Sex Pistols Gig** : A quartet of Manchester natives who formed after seeing the Sex Pistols' June 1976 show at the Lesser Free Trade Hall. At first, in 1977, their clipped punk energy sounded like most bands formed in the Pistols' wake. "They formed after seeing that sex pistols gig in 1976."
**Origin of the Band Name** : The subversive, satirical name came from the Nazis' name for their sexual slavery department. "Joy Division's subversive satirical band name was taken from the Nazis' name for their sexual slavery department."
**Bass as Lead and the Baritone Voice** : Partly inspired by Siouxsie and the Banshees, Joy Division's sound grew more distinctive and spookier. Bassist Peter Hook played his bass as if it were a lead guitar, and Ian Curtis sang in a foreboding baritone. Both became core elements of post-punk and goth rock. "intensive bass and gloomy vocals would become core elements of post punk and Goth Rock."
**Death of Ian Curtis (1980)** : In spring 1980, days before Joy Division was to begin a US tour that could have made him a star, the clinically depressed singer Ian Curtis died by suicide in his flat in Cheshire. The remaining members carried on, with guitarist Bernard Sumner as vocalist, and by 1981 they had become New Order. "Curtis was 23 years of age."

## albums
**Power, Corruption & Lies (1983) - Rock Meets Electronics** : Released in 1983 by the renamed former Joy Division, this album blended rock instruments with electronic rhythms. It was anchored by the single "Blue Monday." "a seminal blend of rock instruments and electronic rhythms."
**Low-Life (1985) - Cracking the Billboard Album Chart** : This album entered the Billboard album chart a few months before the Pretty in Pink soundtrack, as New Order was starting to break in the US. "Their low-life album had cracked the Billboard album chart just a few months earlier."
**Substance (1987) - Compilation** : A 1987 compilation that featured "Bizarre Love Triangle." Its bonus track "True Faith" gave New Order their first American top 40 hit. "it was a bonus track on the Substance collection that gave New Order its first ever American top 40 hit."
**Technique (1989) - Recorded in Ibiza** : New Order spent several months of 1988 on Ibiza, absorbing the growing acid house and techno scenes, and recorded their most experimental, club-oriented album. As Manchester became the center of rave culture ("Madchester"), the album brought a version of that scene to suburban America. "recording what became their most experimental club-oriented album."
**Republic (1993) - Highest-Charting Album** : The band's only album of the 1990s. Members often disagreed over how far to move from rock toward dance music. This Euro dance-friendly album arrived when grunge dominated American charts, yet it charted higher than any previous New Order album and produced their biggest US hit, "Regret." "charted higher than any previous new order album"

## songs
**Blue Monday (1983) - Best-Selling 12-Inch Ever** : The electronic dance single at the center of Power, Corruption & Lies. It sold 3 million copies as a 12-inch single and is reportedly the best-selling 12-inch of all time. "remains reportedly the biggest selling 12-inch of all time."
**Confusion / The Perfect Kiss - US Dance Hits** : Along with "Blue Monday," these were among New Order's run of top 20 US dance singles before "Shellshock." "New Order's fourth straight top 20 US Dance Single"
**Shellshock (1986) - Pretty in Pink Debut** : This hybrid of alt-rock and club music first appeared on the Pretty in Pink soundtrack and reached number 14 on Billboard's club chart. It was New Order's fourth straight top 20 US dance single, after "Blue Monday," "Confusion," and "The Perfect Kiss." "New Order's latest hybrid of alt-rock and club music"
**Bizarre Love Triangle (1986) - Sleeper Hit** : Pairs relentless dance beats with romantically confused, quietly melancholy lyrics. It was a top 5 US club song in 1986, a highlight of Substance (1987), and later a pop radio staple. It reached the Hot 100 in 1995, nearly ten years after release. It remains New Order's most played radio song despite never reaching the top 40 in the US or UK. "remains New Order's most played radio song, despite never reaching the top 40 in either the US or the UK."
**True Faith (1987) - First US Top 40 Hit** : A doomy dance anthem with allusions to drug addiction and the line "a childhood I lost replaced by fear." A bonus track on Substance, it peaked at number 32 in December 1987. As with Depeche Mode, going darker worked for New Order. "a doomy dance anthem with allusions to drug addiction"
**Fine Time (1989) - Modern Rock Top 3** : The first single from Technique. It entered the Modern Rock chart in January 1989 and rose into the top 3, and reached number 2 in US dance clubs, New Order's biggest US hit up to then. "remarkable for such a cutting-edge track far from the rudiments of rock."
**Round & Round (1989) - Club Chart #1** : The follow-up to "Fine Time" did even better. "rising to number one on the club play chart and number six at modern rock."
**Regret (1993) - Biggest US Hit** : From Republic, New Order's biggest US hit. "A number one Modern Rock and number 28 pop hit."

## curiosities
**Members at the Sex Pistols' 1976 Gig** : Future members of New Order were at the Sex Pistols' June 1976 show at Manchester's Lesser Free Trade Hall. "New Order and the Smiths, were present at the pistols legendary June 1976 show"

## albums
**Kaleidoscope (1980) - UK Top 10** : One of two consecutive Siouxsie and the Banshees albums to reach the UK top 10, in 1980 and 1981. "Goth music was gradually becoming more commercial."
**Juju (1981) - UK Top 10** : The second of the two consecutive UK top 10 albums. Even with faster tempos, the Banshees' records became more ghoulishly goth. "Two straight Susie albums, kaleidoscope and juju, went top 10 in the UK in 1980 and 81"

## songs
**Spellbound (1981) - UK #22** : A faster, increasingly goth Banshees single. "Spellbound reached number 22 in the UK in 1981."
**Cities in Dust (1985) - Biggest US Dance Hit to Date** : Released within a month of the Cure's "Close to Me," this was regarded as the Banshees' most irresistible single. It kept Siouxsie's edgy goth persona over a skittering club beat. It reached the UK top 20 and number 17 on the US club play chart. "Susie's biggest American dance single to date."
**Peek-a-Boo (1988) - First Ever Modern Rock #1** : The number 1 song in the first week of Billboard's Modern Rock Tracks chart, launched in September 1988. "the number one song was by a post-punk and goth rock veteran"

## curiosities
**Anticipating Goth** : The London band was fronted by Susan Janet Ballion, known as Siouxsie Sioux. From the start they had punk's energy plus tribal drumming and sinister vocals. Pitchfork later wrote that without Siouxsie Sioux, goth might never have taken root. They inspired both Joy Division and Robert Smith. "layered in tribal drumming and sinister vocals that would anticipate the sound of Gothic rock."
**Biggest US Hit in 1991** : The band scored their biggest American hit in 1991, during the early-90s Modern Rock era. "who scored their biggest hit in America in 1991"

## songs
**Bela Lugosi's Dead (1979) - Goth Blueprint** : Released in late summer 1979, this single defined goth rock for many and served as a template for the goth subculture. Four years later, just before breaking up, the band performed it in the 1983 vampire film The Hunger. "would serve as a skeletal blueprint for the entire Goth subculture."

**Spin-Off: Love and Rockets** : After Bauhaus broke up, three former members formed Love and Rockets in 1985. "Even the former members of Bauhaus were beginning to find favor on the US airwaves."

**No New Tale to Tell (1987) - Album Rock Crossover** : In late 1987 this anthemic, fist-pumping song was played on American rock stations alongside Bruce Springsteen and Aerosmith. It crossed over to peak at number 3 on the Hot 100 in August 1989, higher than any band of its generation had reached on the US pop charts, including Depeche Mode, Siouxsie, New Order and the Cure. "a slinky homage to the sound of 70s glam rockers T. Rex."

## albums
**Speak & Spell (1981) - Debut** : The only Depeche Mode album with Vince Clarke, who left afterward. "He would leave Depeche Mode after only one album, 1981's Speak and Spell"
**Some Great Reward (1984) - Martin Gore Takes Charge** : By this album Martin Gore had become the band's leader, writing about everything from romantic betrayal to the existence of God, and about consensual sadomasochism on "Master and Servant." It contained "People Are People." "Martin Gore had come into his own as Depeche Mode's leader"
**Black Celebration (1986) - Darker Turn** : Starting with this album, Gore gave singer Dave Gahan lyrics that were both sensual and ominous, over music with an industrial sound. "the music had an industrial throng."
**Music for the Masses (1987) - First US Top 40 Album** : By 1987 the band had dropped most of their earlier technopop sound, which made them bigger than ever. The title was accurate, since it was their first US top 40 album (peaking at number 35), and ironic, given its dark, slow songs. "music for the masses meant digital dirges with hooks."
**101 (1989) - Live Album and Pennebaker Film** : The soundtrack to D.A. Pennebaker's film of the end of the Music for the Masses tour. Its 101st show was at the Rose Bowl in Pasadena, California, in 1988. Film and album both came out in spring 1989, and the album peaked just outside the top 40 in April. Some critics had mocked the band's live shows, wrongly believing their music depended on pre-programmed synthesizers. "But no one was laughing at the sounds of roaring crowds on Depeche Mode 101."
**Violator (1990) - Biggest Album** : Anticipation built by "Personal Jesus" made Violator the band's biggest-selling, most hit-filled and most acclaimed album. It debuted higher than any previous Depeche Mode album except Music for the Masses had peaked, then rose into the top 10, higher than the Cure's Disintegration. "the biggest selling, most hit generating, and most widely acclaimed DePesh Mode album ever."
**Songs of Faith and Devotion (1993) - Guitar Rock Turn** : The follow-up to Violator topped the chart. Pushed by lead singer Dave Gahan, now wearing leather, the band moved further into guitar rock. "went even deeper into Guitar Rock territory"

## songs
**Just Can't Get Enough (1981) - First UK Top 10** : Written by then-leader Vince Clarke in a bouncy new wave style, with Dave Gahan's Teutonic croon. It reached number 8 in the UK and number 26 on Billboard's club play chart. It was the band's most cheerful pop hit and the last single Clarke wrote for them. "Just can't get enough was a number 8 UK hit in 1981"
**Get the Balance Right (1983) - Early Gore Single** : An upbeat-sounding Martin Gore single that paired dance beats with cynical lyrics. "throbbing dance beats were paired with cynical lyrics."
**Master and Servant (1984) - Song About Consensual S&M** : A Martin Gore song about consensual sadomasochism, from Some Great Reward. "on the unnervingly catchy, master and servant"
**People Are People (1984) - First US Top 40 Hit** : From Some Great Reward, Martin Gore's protest against racism and war took over a year to break in America. It crossed over to top 40 radio and peaked at number 13 in August 1985. Casey Kasem counted it down on American Top 40. The crossover proved a fluke, and it was their last US pop hit for nearly five years. "the first American hit for the English band with a French name."
**Never Let Me Down Again (1987) - Apocalyptic Anthem** : A track from Music for the Masses. "sounded like high school anthems for the end of days."
**Personal Jesus (1989) - Guitar-Led Single** : Not the first song Gore wrote on guitar, but the first Depeche Mode single with guitar as its main instrument. Producer Flood (Mark Ellis), just off Nine Inch Nails' Pretty Hate Machine, helped turn it into a kind of industrial roots music. It changed how the public saw Depeche Mode, establishing them even for synth-skeptical rock purists as a rock band. It peaked at number 3 on Modern Rock in October 1989 and number 28 on the Hot 100 in March 1990. "It was the twang of the Old West, crossed with just a touch of blasphemy."
**Enjoy the Silence (1990) - Only Pop Top 10** : Depeche Mode's first Modern Rock number 1, in April 1990, and their only pop top 10 hit, peaking at number 8. "Depeche Mode were finally American radio deities."
**Policy of Truth (1990) - Third Violator Single** : Martin Gore's reimagining of the theme of "I Heard It Through the Grapevine." Number 1 at Modern Rock and number 15 on the Hot 100. "essentially an alt-rock version of R&B's I heard it through the grapevine"
**I Feel You (1993) - Modern Rock #1** : The Modern Rock number 1 that showed the band's move into guitar rock on Songs of Faith and Devotion. "as exemplified by the Modern Rock number one hit"

## curiosities
**Name from a French Magazine** : The Basildon band went through several lineups in the late 70s after punk before dropping most traditional instruments for synthesizers. Unlike the goth bands, they began cheerful and grew darker. "Named for a French magazine whose title roughly translates to Fast Fashion."
**KROQ Staples in 1984** : By 1984 the band were staples of US college and new wave radio, with four songs in LA's KROQ top 100 for the year. "By 1984, Depeche Mode had become college and new wave radio staples in America."
**Rose Bowl Sellout (1988)** : The band sold out the Rose Bowl, the 101st show of the Music for the Masses tour. D.A. Pennebaker, who had pioneered music documentaries with Bob Dylan's Don't Look Back, Monterey Pop, and David Bowie's Ziggy Stardust and the Spiders from Mars, filmed it. "The film captured the extreme devotion of fans traveling cross-country to see the show"
**Rock Hall Ballot** : Depeche Mode had been on the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame ballot several times and were nominated again in 2019. "Depeche Mode have made the Rock Hall ballot multiple times"

**The Queen Is Dead (1986) - Most Acclaimed Album** : Released in June 1986, weeks after the Pretty in Pink soundtrack peaked. It stayed on the Billboard album chart for the rest of the year and topped many 1986 critics' lists. It had no US top 40 hits but dominated college and alternative radio. It was the last studio album released while the band was together. "It would wind up The Smiths' most acclaimed album"
**Strangeways, Here We Come (1987) - Final Studio Album** : Recorded in 1987 before Marr left and led by the MTV favorite "Girlfriend in a Coma." By its release in fall 1987, the band had split. "by the time Strange Ways arrived in the fall of 1987, The Smiths were no more."

## songs
**How Soon Is Now? (1984) - B-Side Turned Anthem** : Originally a 1984 B-side. It combines a Johnny Marr tremolo guitar riff with what may be Morrissey's most impassioned vocal. It became the Smiths' most played song on US alternative radio, ranking just outside KROQ's top 20 songs of 1984, the peak year for New Romantic synth pop. It was later covered by more than a dozen artists. "quickly became the Smiths most played song on American alternative radio"
**Please, Please, Please, Let Me Get What I Want (1984) - John Hughes Favorite** : Another 1984 B-side, a gentle, drumless ballad with mandolin by Johnny Marr. John Hughes put an instrumental version in Ferris Bueller's Day Off (1986), and the song closed the Pretty in Pink soundtrack. "was many Americans' introduction to The Smiths."
**There Is a Light That Never Goes Out (1986) - Alt-Rock Standard** : From The Queen Is Dead. "songs that would become alt-rock standards"
**The Boy with the Thorn in His Side (1986) - Alt-Rock Standard** : From The Queen Is Dead, a college and alternative radio staple. "songs that would become alt-rock standards"
**Bigmouth Strikes Again (1986) - Self-Referential Hit** : From The Queen Is Dead. Morrissey's mouth went on to cause him trouble throughout his career and helped end the band. "one of Morrissey's most self-referential hits"
**Ask / Panic (1986–87) - Final-Year Singles** : Singles from the band's last year. "they would issue a few more classic singles"
**Girlfriend in a Coma (1987) - MTV Favorite** : Lead single from Strangeways, Here We Come. "Led off by the MTV favorite Girlfriend in a coma."

## curiosities
**The British Answer to R.E.M.** : In the mid-80s, when American indie rock was led by R.E.M. and Peter Buck's jangly guitar, Johnny Marr combined 60s rock and 80s post-punk jangle with Morrissey's arch, self-lacerating lyrics. "became, in essence, the British answer to R.E.M."
**UK Hitmakers, No US Hot 100** : On Rough Trade they had 18 UK top 40 hits from 1983 to 1986. They never had a US Hot 100 hit, but were alternative radio heroes within their first year. "scoring 18 top 40 hits between 1983 and 1986"
**Feuds and Breakup** : Morrissey feuded with Marr, bassist Andy Rourke and drummer Mike Joyce, and Marr quit in 1987. They missed America's embrace of British post-punk by a year or two, leaving the question of whether they could have become US superstars. "tensions would eventually lead Mar to quit the band in 1987."
**Rock Hall Nominees, Never Inducted** : One of only three of these bands nominated for the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, along with the Cure and Depeche Mode. They have not been inducted, and the former members have feuded publicly and refused to reunite. "The former bandmates have been publicly feuding for years and loudly refused to play together again."
**Members at the Sex Pistols' 1976 Gig** : Future Smiths were at the Sex Pistols' June 1976 Lesser Free Trade Hall show. "New Order and the Smiths, were present at the pistols legendary June 1976 show"

# genre - Post-Punk

## curiosities
**Punk Plus Glam** : Post-punk owed a debt to punk and also to David Bowie and Roxy Music. "The bands of post-punk would combine punk's angularity and economy with the theatricality and moodiness of glam and art rock."
**From New Wave to Alternative** : These bands helped turn new wave into alternative. "These were the moody rockers who helped turn new wave into alternative, before grunge, before industrial, before electronic."
**Never Fully Faded** : After grunge, the UK post-punk bands kept charting into the 90s. Their arrangements influenced Blink-182's "I Miss You," and mid-2000s emo bands like My Chemical Romance borrowed their sound and style. "Their Baroque alt pop arrangements were emulated."


# genre - Gothic Rock

## curiosities
**Heavier, Gloomier Than Early-80s New Wave** : Unlike the decadent new wave of the early 80s, late-80s British rock got heavier and gloomier while still producing catchy pop. "The music got heavier, the lyrics gloomier."
**Many Names** : The subgenre has been called mope rock, goth rock and "sad bastard music." The bands differed in instruments, some guitar-based, some synth-based, some switching from one to the other. "Some call it goth rock, but not all of it is terribly gothic."


# genre - New Romantic

## curiosities
**Second British Invasion** : After MTV launched in 1981, by 1983 the Hot 100 was full of British synth pop, led by New Romantic acts like Duran Duran. Like goth, the style descended from punk and Roxy Music-style glam. "the New Romantic bands leaned on the glam side of the post punk equation."

# curiosity

**Billboard Modern Rock Tracks Chart Launch (September 1988)** : Publications like CMJ, Trouser Press and the Gavin Report had tracked college radio for years, and MTV's The Cutting Edge and 120 Minutes had shown indie videos. Billboard then launched a dedicated chart, whose first number 1 was Siouxsie and the Banshees' "Peek-a-Boo." The chart also included Big Audio Dynamite, 10,000 Maniacs, Information Society, the Sugarcubes, Patti Smith and the Psychedelic Furs. "The Billboard Modern Rock Tracks chart launched in September 1988."
**British Dominance of Modern Rock (1989–1991)** : From 1989 through 1991, UK acts held the top of Billboard's Modern Rock chart more than 60% of the time. "to a generation of American kids' alternative music was British music."
**Pretty in Pink Soundtrack (1986)** : John Hughes, after Sixteen Candles (1984, with Spandau Ballet and Kajagoogoo) and The Breakfast Club (1985, with Simple Minds' number 1 "Don't You (Forget About Me)"), put together an alt-rock mixtape soundtrack for Pretty in Pink. It was named for the Psychedelic Furs song and featured OMD, Echo & the Bunnymen, the Smiths and New Order. It reached the top 5 on the album chart in spring 1986, the most successful John Hughes soundtrack and the biggest US chart success for most of its acts. "It would do for 80s post-punk, what the Saturday Night Fever soundtrack had done for 70s disco."
**Commercial New Wave Radio** : Besides college radio, coastal stations like KROQ (Los Angeles), WLIR (Long Island) and WFNX (Boston) did not report to the Hot 100 but made these bands stars to suburban teens. "On these stations, let's go to bed was a smash."
**MTV's 120 Minutes** : MTV's Sunday-midnight showcase of underground and indie videos. "Beneath the glossy surface of the pop machine lies the underground"
**"Sad Bastard Music" in High Fidelity** : Nick Hornby coined the term in his 1995 novel High Fidelity. In the 2000 film, Jack Black's character Barry says it when hearing Belle and Sebastian. "he's just been confronted by some sullen indie rock by a Scottish band"
**Flood (Mark Ellis)** : Producer of Nine Inch Nails' Pretty Hate Machine and Depeche Mode's "Personal Jesus." "helped turn personal Jesus into a kind of industrial roots music."
**D.A. Pennebaker** : The documentary filmmaker behind Don't Look Back, Monterey Pop and Ziggy Stardust and the Spiders from Mars, who filmed Depeche Mode's 101. "In 1988, Depeche Mode signed on to be the subject of a film by legendary documentarian DA Pennebaker."
