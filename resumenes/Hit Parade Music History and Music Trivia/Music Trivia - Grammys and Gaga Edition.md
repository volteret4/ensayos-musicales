# artist - LadBaby

## songs
**We Built This City on Sausage Rolls (2018) - UK Christmas Number One 2018** : A novelty song that won the 2018 UK Christmas number one, against expectations. As late as December 18 the race was still undecided. It was a neck-and-neck contest with Ariana Grande's "Thank U, Next" and the dark horse "Sweet but Psycho" by Ava Max, and all three were within a few hundred streams and sales of each other on the British charts. The win was announced on Friday, December 21, 2018, in the final couple of days before the holiday. It belongs to the British tradition of random, left-field, cheesy or "naff" novelty songs taking the Christmas crown. "Lad Baby's, we built this city on sausage roll, had eaked out a win."

## curiosities
**Real Identity and Hometown** : LadBaby is an internet pseudonym. The man behind it was born Mark Hoyle (transcribed as "Mark E. and Hoyle") and comes from West Bridgford, Nottinghamshire. "Lad Baby is his internet pseudonym."

**Christmas Number One as a Gift for His Wife** : Hoyle set out to get a UK Christmas number one as a present for his wife, whom he calls "LadBaby Mum" online. "He wanted to give it as a gift for his wife, whom he refers to online as Lad Baby Mum."

"She just won an award at the Golden Globes, not the one she necessarily wanted to win."

## curiosities
**Only Two Album of the Year Wins in Grammy History** : Only two hip hop albums have ever won the Grammy for Album of the Year: Lauryn Hill's *The Miseducation of Lauryn Hill* (1999) and OutKast's *Speakerboxxx/The Love Below* (2004). **Penalized by Older Grammy Voters** : The typical Grammy voter, much older than average, is described as having a problem with hip hop's recording values: its reliance on samples and its relative lack of traditional instrumental chops. This tends to count against hip hop in the final vote. "The Grammy voter, picture somebody considerably older than you or me, has a problem with hip hop recording values, if you will."

# concert - Grammy Awards

## curiosities
**2019 Ceremony Date** : The 61st Grammy Awards took place on February 10, 2019. "It's coming up February 10th."

**Expansion from Five to Eight Nominees** : For the 2019 ceremony, Record, Song and Album of the Year each had eight nominees instead of the five used since the Grammys launched in the 1950s. The change was a diversity move meant to bring more country, hip hop and R&B records into each category. At least at the nomination level, it worked. "There are now eight nominees in each of these categories, record of the year, song of the year, album of the year."

**Checkered History with Hip Hop** : NARAS, the National Academy of Recording Arts and Sciences, has a checkered history with hip hop, especially in the frontline categories. Album of the Year, typically the last award of the night, has almost never gone to a hip hop record. "has a checkered history with hip hop, particularly in the front line categories."

**Diverse 2018 Nominees, Traditional Winner** : The 2018 Album of the Year nominees were praised as among the most diverse ever, including Kendrick Lamar, Jay-Z and several other hip hop albums. The prize went to Bruno Mars instead, illustrating where the Grammys tend to land. "these are among the most diverse nominees, particularly in the album of the year category that we've ever seen."

**Best New Artist: A Checkered Category** : Many Best New Artist winners faded into ignominy, from Starland Vocal Band to Milli Vanilli. Meanwhile, deserving artists such as Lady Gaga, Whitney Houston and Cardi B were excluded on technicalities, while acts with years-long recording careers were sometimes included. "The history of that award is so checkered, so many of one and then gone on to, terrible ignominy from Starland Vocal Band to Millie Vanilley."

# curiosity

**The UK Christmas Number One Novelty Tradition** : The UK Christmas number one has a long British tradition of random, left-field, cheesy or "naff" novelty songs taking the top spot. Non-holiday songs have also won, such as "Only You," "The Power of Love," "Another Brick in the Wall, Part 2" and "Don't You Want Me." "the great British tradition of random left field novelty cheesy to use a British term, NAF songs, taking the UK number one crown at Christmas."

**Margaret Thatcher's Favorite Christmas Number One** : Prime Minister Margaret Thatcher said the Flying Pickets' 1983 Christmas number one "Only You" was a favorite of hers, which mortified the socialist band. "A favorite of then prime minister Margaret Thatcher."
