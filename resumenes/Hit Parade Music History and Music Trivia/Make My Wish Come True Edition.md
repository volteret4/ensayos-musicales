## albums
**Mariah Carey (1990) - Self-Titled Debut** : Carey's debut album produced four number one singles, and she took a co-writing credit on all of them. This set her apart from Whitney Houston: she was both a singer and a songwriter. "taking a co-writing credit on virtually all of her hits including all four number ones on her self-titled debut album."
**Merry Christmas (1994) - First Holiday Album** : Around 1993, after Hero hit number one, Sony Music encouraged Carey to record a Christmas album. She was skeptical, seeing holiday LPs as something aging stars did late in their careers. The label, and especially her then-husband, Sony Music executive Tommy Mottola, convinced her it would add to her stature as a vocalist. She recorded it with frequent collaborator Walter Afanasieff, ironically in the hot summer of 1994. The CD featured hymns and pop standards, a cover of Darlene Love's Christmas (Baby Please Come Home), and a handful of originals including All I Want for Christmas Is You. "She perceived holiday LPs as the sort of thing aging stars did later in their careers."
**Merry Christmas (1994) - Sales and Perennial Status** : The CD reached music stores in October 1994 and was certified triple platinum in its first year. Carey and Sony expected it to become a regular holiday seller, like perennial LPs by Frank Sinatra, Ella Fitzgerald and Kenny G. It has since been certified sextuple platinum. "It has since been certified sex-tuple platinum."
**MTV Unplugged (1992) - Jackson 5 Cover** : During her 1990s run, when "everything Carey touched turned to platinum," she covered the Jackson 5 on the MTV franchise Unplugged. "everything Carey touched turned to platinum"
**Glitter (2001) - Film Soundtrack Flop** : Carey's 2001 movie Glitter flopped, and its soundtrack produced only one short-lived top 10 hit, Loverboy. It seemed to signal the end of her chart era. "It appeared that the chart era of Mariah Carey was over."
**The Emancipation of Mimi (2005) - Comeback Album** : This multi-platinum album yielded We Belong Together, its biggest single, and Don't Forget About Us. It drove one of the most remarkable comebacks in pop history after her early-2000s breakdown and her years in the chart wilderness. "Suddenly and unexpectedly, Mariah Carrie was a chart titan again."
**Sequel Holiday Album (2010) - Re-Recorded Christmas Song** : In 2010 Carey re-recorded All I Want for Christmas Is You for a sequel holiday album. "She re-recorded the track in both 2010 for a sequel holiday album."
**Merry Christmas Deluxe Reissue (2019) - 25th Anniversary** : This was part of the 2019 anniversary campaign marking 25 years of both the song and the album. Carey released a deluxe reissue of the Merry Christmas LP alongside a slew of other anniversary Christmas products. "In 2019 alone, Mariah Carries has released a slew of anniversary Christmas product, a deluxe reissue of the Mary Christmas LP"

## songs
**Vision of Love (1990) - Debut Single** : Carey's debut single topped both the Hot 100 and the R&B chart at the same time in summer 1990, when the 1990s were just months old. It began a streak of hits unparalleled in chart history. "The vision of love, Carey's debut single topped both the Hot 100 and R&B chart simultaneously in the summer of 1990."
**Love Takes Time (1990)** : This was the second of Carey's first five singles, all of which reached number one on the Hot 100 between summer 1990 and fall 1991. "five straight Carey songs, her first five single releases all reached number one on the Hot 100."
**Someday (1991)** : The third of her five consecutive opening number ones. "Vision of love, love takes time, someday I don't want to cry and emotions."
**I Don't Wanna Cry (1991)** : The fourth of her five consecutive opening number ones. "Vision of love, love takes time, someday I don't want to cry and emotions."
**Emotions (1991)** : The fifth straight number one. It made Carey the only artist in chart history to launch a career with five number ones, beating the Jackson 5's 1970 mark of four. "She became the only artist in chart history to launch a career with five number ones"
**Hero (1993)** : An inspirational power anthem that was number one at Christmas 1993. Around this time Sony began encouraging Carey to make a Christmas album. "a number one at Christmas of 1993."
**All I Want for Christmas Is You (1994) - Songwriting Origin** : Carey and Walter Afanasieff wrote it in the style of Phil Spector, and it sounded as though it could have come straight off his 1963 album A Christmas Gift for You. The stories about its creation are Rashomon-like, and the two writers give differing accounts of its genesis, though they still share the songwriting royalties equally. Afanasieff came up with what he called the boogie-woogie piano chords, which evoke early rock and roll and the girl group era. The melody was largely Carey's: a sturdy, familiar chord progression she reportedly came up with in under 15 minutes. She also wrote the earnest lyrics about wanting nothing more than a lover's devotion at the holidays. "a sturdy familiar chord progression that she reportedly came up with in less than 15 minutes."
**All I Want for Christmas Is You (1994) - Non-Single Release** : It was the standout track on Merry Christmas. Sony shot a video of Carey cavorting in the snow and released it in October 1994, but chose not to issue the song as a retail single. That was cold business logic: the 1990s industry was phasing out singles to push CD album sales, and holiday songs had underperformed on the Hot 100 for four decades. Not issuing a single was unusual for a chart dominator like Carey. Under the rules of the time, the song could not appear on the Hot 100. "Sony elected not to issue all I want for Christmas is you as a retail single."
**All I Want for Christmas Is You (1994-95) - Radio Songs Chart Peak** : In the 1994 and 1995 holiday seasons, the only chart the song appeared on was Billboard's radio songs chart. It peaked at number 12 there, competing with non-holiday songs by TLC, Brandy and Boyz II Men. "On that chart it peaked at number 12 impressive for a Christmas song"
**All I Want for Christmas Is You (1999-2000) - First Hot 100 Appearance** : Billboard changed its rules at the end of 1998 to allow album cuts on the chart. As a result, for one week of the 1999-2000 holiday season, the song (still not a retail single) debuted on the Hot 100 at number 83. Making the chart at all showed how beloved it was five years on, without active promotion. "made its debut on the Hot 100 at Position, Wait for it, Number 83."
**All I Want for Christmas Is You (2003-2004) - Digital Download Breakthrough** : Weeks after Billboard began tracking downloads in 2003, the song debuted on the Hot Digital Tracks chart. It rose into that chart's top 10 as the highest-ranking digital track that was not a current single, which was its first top 10 on any Billboard chart. It sold steadily in 2003 and 2004 while Carey was otherwise cold. It kept her in the public's good graces and may have contributed to her comeback. "This was the song's first appearance in the top 10 of any Billboard chart."
**All I Want for Christmas Is You (2005-2010) - Recurrent Stealth Hit** : Every year from 2005 to 2010 it returned to Billboard's digital songs chart, selling hundreds of thousands of downloads. In 2005 it topped that chart and went gold, which was only possible once iTunes sold it as a single. As a "recurrent," though, it was barred from the Hot 100. In the 90s it wasn't a retail single, in the early 2000s it charted once modestly as an album cut, and in the download era its recurrent status kept it off the big chart. "Mariah's Christmas classic was a smash hiding in plain sight."
**All I Want for Christmas Is You (2011) - First Holiday Songs Chart Number One** : In the very first week of Billboard's relaunched holiday chart in December 2011, Carey's 1994 original was number one. The chart was called Holiday Songs and later renamed the Holiday 100. "its number one song was What Else Mariah's 1994 original edition of All I Want for Christmas is You."
**All I Want for Christmas Is You (2011) - Justin Bieber Duet** : In 2011 Carey re-recorded the song with rising teen idol Justin Bieber. The version was not a big hit, peaking at number 86 on the Hot 100, and her original remained the standard. "peaking on the hot 100 at a lowly number 86 in 2011."
**All I Want for Christmas Is You (2012) - First Top 40 Hit** : In the 2012 holiday season Carey performed a special live version with Jimmy Fallon, his house band The Roots, and a classroom full of kids on classroom instruments. The performance spurred a wave of downloads and streams. After the Whitney Houston rule change the song was eligible for the Hot 100, and it re-debuted at number 29, its first time in the top 40. "In 2012, All I Want for Christmas is You was finally a top 40 hit."
**All I Want for Christmas Is You (2013-2017) - Climb Up the Hot 100** : The song debuted a little higher in 2013, joined by Nat King Cole's The Christmas Song. In 2015 it entered the top 20 alongside Brenda Lee's Rockin' Around the Christmas Tree. In Christmas week 2017 it reached the top 10, as streaming became the biggest factor on the Hot 100. "Two years after that, Christmas week of 2017, All I Want for Christmas is You broke into the top 10."
**All I Want for Christmas Is You (2018) - Number Three Peak** : The week after Christmas 2018 it was the number three song on the Hot 100. That week holiday songs filled 16 spots in the top 40. "Finally, the week after Christmas 2018, Mariah Carey had the number three song on the Hot 100."
**All I Want for Christmas Is You (2019) - Number One in 25th Anniversary Year** : In December 2019, more than two weeks before the end of the 2010s, the song became the number one song in the US. It gave Carey her 19th number one, one short of the Beatles. It is the highest-charting holiday hit since 1958, the year the Hot 100 was born, and the first holiday chart-topper since that year. Over 25 years, whenever Americans had the chance to buy, request or stream it, they did. "And Mariah Carries had her 19th number one hit, just one shot of the Beatles."
**Christmas (Baby Please Come Home) Cover (1994)** : On Merry Christmas, Carey covered Darlene Love's song, the most famous original from Phil Spector's A Christmas Gift for You. Even so, her boldest Spector homage was her own All I Want for Christmas Is You. "But Carey's boldest homage to Phil Spector wasn't her darling love cover."
**Fantasy (1995)** : This chart-topper reflected Carey's move away from pop toward R&B and hip hop. It was built on a sample of the Tom Tom Club's hit Genius of Love, and its remix featured Wu-Tang Clan rapper Ol' Dirty Bastard. "was built out of a sample of the Tom Tom Club breakbeat hit Genius of Love"
**One Sweet Day (1995) - With Boyz II Men** : The late-1995 duet with Boyz II Men spent 16 weeks at number one on the Hot 100. That record stood for 23 years, until Lil Nas X's Old Town Road spent 19 weeks on top in 2019. "spent a stunning 16 weeks at number one on the hot 100."
**Always Be My Baby (1996)** : A critically acclaimed number one from late in her 90s run. "the critically acclaimed number one always be my baby."
**Honey (1997)** : A number one collaboration with then-hot producer Sean "Puff Daddy" Combs. "a collaboration with then hot producer Sean Puff Daddy Comes on the number one hit Honey."
**When You Believe (1998) - With Whitney Houston** : A heavily hyped duet with Whitney Houston, Carey's diva contemporary and forebear, that underperformed and peaked at number 15. "underperformed on the charts, peaking at number 15."
**Heartbreaker (1999) - With Jay-Z** : One of a couple of number ones in Carey's 1999 bounce-back. It was a Jay-Z collaboration that sounded like Fantasy. "the JZ collaboration and fantasy sound alike heartbreaker."
**Loverboy (2001)** : The only hit from the Glitter soundtrack, a short-lived top 10. "its soundtrack produced only one short lived top 10 hit, Love a Boy."
**We Belong Together (2005)** : This epic torch song spent 14 weeks at number one and was Billboard's top hit of 2005. The public embraced it as a sturdy ballad and as a meta-narrative about Carey's perseverance after her breakdown. It was her 16th number one. "a turnaround in Carrie's fortunes that no one, including her, saw coming."
**Don't Forget About Us (2005)** : Her 17th number one, released three months after We Belong Together. "she followed it just three months later with her 17th, don't forget about us."
**Touch My Body (2008)** : Her 18th Hot 100 number one, which passed Elvis Presley's 17 to make her the solo artist with the most number ones in Billboard pop chart history. "That put her past Elvis as the solo artist with the most number ones in Billboard pop chart history."

## curiosities
**Signed as Sony's Answer to Whitney Houston** : Sony Music reportedly signed Carey hoping to replicate Arista Records' 1980s success with Whitney Houston. Carey quickly stepped out of Houston's shadow as a singer-songwriter with an acrobatic voice distinct from Houston's gospel-trained instrument. "Reportedly Sony Music had signed the multi-octave singer out of hopes that they would replicate competing label Arraster Records blockbuster 80s success with Power Deva Whitney Houston."
**Five-Octave Range and Whistle Tone** : The early-90s media made much of Carey's five-octave range and her so-called whistle tone. "The media made much in the early 90s of Carey's five octave range including her so-called whistle tone."
**Divorce from Tommy Mottola and Late-90s Cracks** : Her divorce from Sony executive Tommy Mottola, together with her shift toward hip hop and R&B, destabilized her hit-making. The teen pop era of Britney Spears and NSYNC was also a tangible threat to her chart reign. "Her divorce from Sony's Tommy Matola and her shift toward hip hop and R&B shook the foundations of her sturdy hit making apparatus."
**Glitter Breakdown and Cold Early 2000s** : While promoting Glitter, she suffered a highly publicized emotional breakdown. Her singles then began missing the top 40 entirely, and it took almost half a decade to recover. "she suffered a highly publicized emotional breakdown"
**Busta Rhymes Guest Spot (2003)** : Without her guest appearance on a number three hit by rapper Busta Rhymes in 2003, Carey would have gone more than three years without a top 40 hit. "Carrie would have gone more than three years without a single top 40 hit."
**Number Ones Tally Through 2008** : When her 90s run petered out, Carey had 15 number ones, the most of any female artist and third overall behind Elvis Presley (17) and the Beatles (20). Her comeback added seven more top 40 hits in the late 2000s, three of them number ones. She topped the Hot 100 18 times between 1990 and 2008. By 2009 her singles were falling short in an era of Katy Perry and Miley Cyrus. "she had amassed 15 number one singles, the most of any female artist"
**Holiday Songs Chart Dominance - 38 of 43 Weeks** : From 2011 to 2019 Billboard ran its holiday chart for five weeks a year, 43 weeks in total across nine seasons. Only five songs ever topped it: Justin Bieber's Mistletoe (one week, 2012), two Pentatonix songs (three weeks, 2013-14), Ariana Grande's Santa Tell Me (one week, 2015), and Carey's song for the other 38 weeks. "This holiday chart, it's basically the Mariah chart."
**2019 Anniversary Campaign** : For the 25th anniversary, team Mariah released a deluxe Merry Christmas reissue and a new version of the video with outtakes from the 1994 original. They also released a vintage live video from the Cathedral of St. John the Divine, with video views counting for the Hot 100. The song came out as a physical single for the first time, on CD and even cassette, with those sales counting too. Amazon Music made a mini documentary about the song. "team Mariah left nothing to chance."
**Carey on Her Wall of Sound Homage** : In the Amazon Music mini documentary, Carey described her approach. "I was trying to do my own version of the wall of sound, those background vocal parts that become almost the lead."
**Critics and the "Music Industrial Complex"** : Since her 1990 debut, critics have complained that her success seemed foreordained by the music industry. But her vocal talent came naturally and her songwriting knack for a hook is real. "her success seemed foreordained by the music industrial complex."
**Queen of Christmas** : By the end of the 2010s, with the Christmas chart renaissance fully established, the public had crowned her. "Mariah Carey was the queen of Christmas."

## albums
**A Christmas Gift for You (1963) - Philles Records Holiday Compilation** : This various-artists compilation showcased Philles acts including the Ronettes, the Crystals, Darlene Love and Bob B. Soxx, all with Spector's wall of sound production. It peaked at only number 13 on Billboard's new Christmas albums chart, and retailers considered it a flop. No tracks were issued as singles, so none charted, and the new rules made holiday songs ineligible for the Hot 100 anyway. It became one of the most influential albums in rock and holiday music history. "A Christmas gift for you changed the sound of 20th century holiday music."

# genre - Christmas Music

## curiosities
**The Seasonal Window Problem** : Hot 100 runs take a dozen weeks or more, but Christmas music has only a six-to-eight-week window. "a roughly six to eight week window from about early to mid-November through the last week of December."
**Long Sales History** : Christmas music has sold well in America since the era of Bing Crosby and Nat King Cole. Charting, however, requires weeks of cultural omnipresence. "Christmas music has long sold well in America, dating back to the era of Bing Crosby and Nat King Cole."
**80s Classics Were Uncharted Album Cuts** : Most new 1980s Christmas classics were album cuts that didn't chart. Holiday songs were rarities on the charts after Band Aid. "Most of the new 80s Christmas classics were album cuts that didn't chart."
**Adult Contemporary All-Christmas Format** : By the early 2000s, many AC radio stations switched to all-Christmas formats in November and December. Christmas songs began regularly topping the AC chart and getting a boost on the Hot 100. "many adult contemporary or AC radio stations would switch to an all Christmas music format every November and December."
**2010s Christmas Chart Renaissance** : Streaming and Spotify and Apple Music Christmas playlists put holiday songs on an even playing field. In 2017 recurrent hits by Brenda Lee, Nat King Cole, Burl Ives and Andy Williams were in the top 40. In 2018 holiday songs held 16 top 40 spots, from Gene Autry to Wham! to José Feliciano. "the addition of downloads and especially streaming to the chart gave holiday music a truly even playing field for the first time."

# curiosity

**Billboard's Inconsistent Holiday Policy** : In nearly a century, Billboard never kept one holiday music policy for more than a few years. Songs were sometimes included on the Hot 100 and sometimes not, charted and vanished, or reappeared on different charts. "had a consistent policy on holiday music that lasted more than a few years."
**Pre-Hot 100 Charts in 1957** : Before the Hot 100, holiday records charted on separate best-seller, disc-jockey and jukebox charts. "In 1957, the magazine had no hot 100 yet."
**Launch of the Hot 100 (1958)** : Launched in 1958, the Hot 100 was the first successful all-in-one chart combining sales and radio airplay. Radio acts as a tempering force, which makes holiday chart-toppers harder to achieve. "Billboard build the Hot 100 as the first successful all-in-one chart combining single sales and radio airplay."
**1963 Christmas Charts Created** : Billboard removed holiday records from its pop charts and created Christmas album and singles charts. These encouraged labels to release album cuts as 45s. "The magazine removed holiday records from its pop album and singles charts and launched a Christmas record section"
**Mid-70s Return to the Hot 100** : The first Christmas chart lasted about a decade. When holiday songs returned to the Hot 100 in the mid-70s, slow-moving radio playlists limited them. "the results were mixed at best."
**1983 Christmas Chart Reinstated** : Billboard brought back the Christmas singles chart in 1983 while allowing some superstar holiday singles onto the Hot 100. "In 1983 billboard tried reinstating the Christmas singles chart"
**UK Christmas Number One** : By 1973 the UK Christmas number one race was a phenomenon. UK charts were based only on sales, which made holiday chart-toppers easier there. "the British charts were entirely based upon sales of singles no radio"
**1998 Album Cut Rule Change** : At the end of 1998 Billboard allowed non-retail album cuts on the Hot 100 for the first time. "Billboard changed its rules at the end of 1998 to allow non-retail songs, album cuts, to appear on the Hot 100 for the first time."
**iTunes Store Opens (2003)** : Apple's store ushered in legal downloads and made album cuts such as Stairway to Heaven available individually. Billboard began tracking download sales in 2003. "it finally made it possible to acquire album cuts, songs that had never been singles in the first place, like Stairway to Heaven, for the price of 99 cents."
**Recurrent Rule** : Unless an old song was being actively re-promoted, each song got only one Hot 100 chart run, and this applied to Christmas songs. "A song was only allowed one hot 100 chart run."
**Holiday Songs / Holiday 100 Chart (2011)** : Billboard relaunched a Christmas chart in 2011 that used radio airplay and, for the first time, digital data. It included recurrents from Bing Crosby to Bobby Helms to Wham!. "They called it holiday songs. It was later expanded and renamed the holiday 100."
**Whitney Houston Rule Change (February 2012)** : After Houston's death, Billboard allowed recurrents on the Hot 100 if they made the top 50. Billboard's director of charts explained the change. "the line has blurred between the relevancy of new and older recordings."
**Spotify Data Added** : By the 2012 holiday season Billboard had added Spotify data to the charts. This revealed what Americans actually wanted to hear at the holidays. "the addition of large-scale streaming finally gave a window into the songs that Americans most wanted to consume at the holidays."
**A Very Special Christmas (1987)** : This Special Olympics charity LP produced a slew of perennial superstar tracks, including songs by U2, Run-DMC and Whitney Houston. "produced a slew of superstar tracks that became perennials"
**1958 to 2019 Gap** : Christmas 1958 was the last time a holiday song topped the Hot 100 until December 2019. "That Christmas, 61 years ago, was the last time America's flagship chart was topped by a holiday song"
