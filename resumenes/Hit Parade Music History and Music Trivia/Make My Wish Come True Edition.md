## albums
**Merry Christmas (1994)** : This album was recorded in the hot summer of 1994, a decision Mariah Carey was initially skeptical about, viewing holiday LPs as something "aging stars did later in their careers." Her then-husband, Sony Music Executive Tommy Mottola, convinced her it would burnish her stature as a vocalist. The album features her takes on hymns and pop standards, including Darlene Love's "Christmas (Baby Please Come Home)," and original songs written with Walter Afanasieff in the style of Phil Spector's "Wall of Sound." In its first year, it was certified triple platinum and has since achieved sex-tuple platinum status, becoming a perennial holiday seller like albums by Frank Sinatra, Ella Fitzgerald, and Kenny G. "Merry Christmas was certified triple platinum and like so many holiday LPs from Frank Sinatra to Ella Fitzgerald to Kenny G Carrey and her label expected the album to become a regular selling holiday perennial."
**The Emancipation of Mimi (2005)** : This multi-platinum album marked a remarkable comeback for Mariah Carey. Its biggest single, "We Belong Together," became Billboard's top hit of 2005, spending 14 weeks at number one on the Hot 100. The album was embraced by the public not just for its sturdy ballads but also for its meta-narrative about Carey's perseverance after her emotional breakdown and period in chart wilderness in the early 2000s. "The biggest single from Carrie's multi-platinum album, The Emancipation of Mimi, we belong together was embraced by the public not only as a sturdy ballad, but as a meta narrative about Carrie's perseverance after her early aughts breakdown and her years spent in the chart wilderness."

## songs
**Vision of Love (1990)** : Mariah Carey's debut single, released in the summer of 1990, simultaneously topped both the Hot 100 and R&B charts. This song marked the beginning of an "imperial phase" for Carey, who launched her career with five consecutive number-one singles, surpassing the Jackson Five's record of four. "The vision of love, Carey's debut single topped both the Hot 100 and R&B chart simultaneously in the summer of 1990."
**Love Takes Time (1990)** : This song was Mariah Carey's second consecutive number-one single on the Hot 100, part of her unprecedented streak of five straight chart-toppers to begin her career. "five straight Carey songs, her first five single releases all reached number one on the Hot 100."
**Someday (1991)** : As her third consecutive number-one hit, "Someday" further solidified Mariah Carey's historic debut, contributing to her record-setting achievement of five straight chart-topping singles. "five straight Carey songs, her first five single releases all reached number one on the Hot 100."
**I Don't Wanna Cry (1991)** : This track was Mariah Carey's fourth consecutive number-one single on the Hot 100, making her the only artist in chart history to launch a career with five number ones, beating a mark set by the Jackson Five. "five straight Carey songs, her first five single releases all reached number one on the Hot 100."
**Emotions (1991)** : As Mariah Carey's fifth consecutive number-one single, "Emotions" solidified her unprecedented entry into chart history, surpassing the Jackson Five's record for most number-one debuts. "five straight Carey songs, her first five single releases all reached number one on the Hot 100."
**Hero (1993)** : This inspirational power anthem reached number one at Christmas of 1993. It was part of Mariah Carey's continued platinum success in the 1990s. "singing inspirational power anthems like Hero, a number one at Christmas of 1993."
**All I Want for Christmas Is You (1994)** : Co-written and co-produced by Mariah Carey and Walter Afanasieff, this song was recorded in the summer of 1994. Afanasieff contributed "Boogie-Woogie Piano Chords" reminiscent of early rock and roll and the girl group era, while Carey created the indelible melody in less than 15 minutes, along with heartfelt lyrics about a lover's devotion. Sony Music released a video in October 1994 but opted not to issue it as a retail single, which was a common business strategy in the 1990s to encourage album sales. Consequently, it only appeared on Billboard's Radio Songs chart, peaking at number 12. Despite not being an active single, it debuted on the Hot 100 at number 83 in the 1999-2000 holiday season after a rule change allowing album cuts to chart. In 2003, it debuted on Billboard's Hot Digital Tracks chart, rising into the top 10 as the highest-ranking digital track not a current single, marking its first top 10 appearance on any Billboard chart. In 2019, its 25th anniversary year, it became the number one song in America, making it the highest-charting holiday hit of the 21st century and the highest since 1958. "The indelible melody was largely Carey's creation. A sturdy familiar chord progression that she reportedly came up with in less than 15 minutes."
**Fantasy (1995)** : This chart-topping song by Mariah Carey was built on a sample from the Tom Tom Club's breakbeat hit "Genius of Love." Its memorable remix featured Wu-Tang Clan rapper Ol' Dirty Bastard, signaling Carey's shift deeper into R&B and hip hop. "Her 1995 chart topper fantasy was built out of a sample of the Tom Tom Club breakbeat hit Genius of Love and its memorable remix featured Wutan Klan rapper Old Dirty Bastery."
**One Sweet Day (1995)** : A collaboration with the premiere 90s R&B vocal troupe Boyz II Men, this song spent an unprecedented 16 weeks at number one on the Hot 100. This benchmark for chart dominance remained undefeated for 23 years until Lil Nas X's "Old Town Road" in 2019. "Her late 1995 hit One Sweet Day pairing her with premiere 90s R&B vocal troupe Boys to Men spent a stunning 16 weeks at number one on the hot 100."
**Always Be My Baby (Year Unknown)** : This critically acclaimed song by Mariah Carey achieved the number one spot on the charts during her period of continued chart dominance in the late 1990s. "critically acclaimed number one always be my baby."
**Honey (1997)** : A number one hit in 1997, this song was a collaboration with then-hot producer Sean "Puff Daddy" Combs. Its success came during a period where Mariah Carey still held the key to the top of the charts. "a collaboration with then hot producer Sean Puff Daddy Comes on the number one hit Honey."
**When You Believe (1998)** : This heavily hyped duet with vocal diva contemporary Whitney Houston underperformed on the charts, peaking at number 15. It reflected the beginning of "cracks" in Carey's bulletproof chart success towards the end of the 1990s, influenced by her divorce from Tommy Mottola and her shift towards hip hop and R&B. "a heavily hyped duet with her vocal diva contemporary and forebear Whitney Houston called When You Believe, underperformed on the charts, peaking at number 15."
**Heartbreaker (1999)** : This song was a collaboration with Jay-Z and had a sound similar to Carey's earlier hit "Fantasy." It was one of a couple more number ones Carey scored in 1999, demonstrating a rebound after her previous song "When You Believe" underperformed. "scored a couple more number ones, including the JZ collaboration and fantasy sound alike heartbreaker."
**Loverboy (2001)** : The only short-lived top 10 hit from the soundtrack of Mariah Carey's 2001 movie "Glitter," which flopped. This song's limited success coincided with a period where it "appeared that the chart era of Mariah Carey was over" and she suffered a highly publicized emotional breakdown. "its soundtrack produced only one short lived top 10 hit, Love a Boy."
**We Belong Together (2005)** : This epic torch song spent 14 weeks at number one on the Hot 100 in 2005 and was Billboard's top hit of the year. It marked a remarkable comeback for Mariah Carey after a period of chart decline and emotional breakdown, becoming her 16th number-one single and the biggest from her multi-platinum album "The Emancipation of Mimi." "The epic torch song we belong together spent 14 weeks at number one on the Hot 100 in 2005."
**Don't Forget About Us (2005)** : Released just three months after "We Belong Together," this song became Mariah Carey's 17th number-one hit on the Hot 100, continuing her comeback in the mid-2000s. "followed it just three months later with her 17th, don't forget about us."
**Touch My Body (2007)** : This song became Mariah Carey's 18th Hot 100 leader two years after "Don't Forget About Us." This achievement allowed her to surpass Elvis Presley as the solo artist with the most number-one hits in Billboard pop chart history. "Touch My Body became her 18th hot 100 leader."

## curiosities
**Exceptional Chart Records** : Mariah Carey is known for setting exceptional chart records, including launching her career with five consecutive number-one singles, surpassing the Jackson Five. She later surpassed Elvis Presley for the most number-one hits as a solo artist. "Mariah Carey, who is known for setting exceptional chart records."
**Five-Octave Vocal Range** : In the early 1990s, the media frequently highlighted Mariah Carey's impressive five-octave vocal range, which included her distinctive "whistle tone." Her acrobatic voice was noted as both impressive and distinct from Whitney Houston's gospel-trained instrument. "The media made much in the early 90s of Carey's five octave range including her so-called whistle tone."
**Career Launch Record** : From the summer of 1990 through the fall of 1991, Mariah Carey achieved five straight number-one songs on the Hot 100 with her first five single releases: "Vision of Love," "Love Takes Time," "Someday," "I Don't Wanna Cry," and "Emotions." This made her the only artist in chart history to launch a career with five number ones, surpassing the Jackson Five's mark of four set in 1970. "She became the only artist in chart history to launch a career with five number ones, beating a mark set in 1970 by the Jackson Five who launched their career with four."
**Sony Music Signing and Whitney Houston Comparison** : Mariah Carey was reportedly signed by Sony Music with the hope that she could replicate the blockbuster 80s success of Whitney Houston, who was signed to competing label Arista Records. However, Carey quickly distinguished herself from Houston, not only with her unique vocal style but also by co-writing virtually all of her hits, including the four number ones from her debut album. "Reportedly Sony Music had signed the multi-octave singer out of hopes that they would replicate competing label Arraster Records blockbuster 80s success with Power Deva Whitney Houston."
**Shift to R&B and Hip Hop** : After the mid-1990s, Mariah Carey diversified her musical direction, moving away from pure pop and deeper into R&B and hip hop. This shift was exemplified by her 1995 hit "Fantasy," which sampled Tom Tom Club and featured Ol' Dirty Bastard on a memorable remix. "Mariah moved on in her record breaking career. If anything she moved away from pop and deeper into R&B and even hip hop."
**Divorce and Chart Decline** : Towards the end of the 1990s, cracks began to show in Mariah Carey's chart success, partly due to her divorce from Sony Music Executive Tommy Mottola. Her deliberate shift toward hip hop and R&B also "shook the foundations of her sturdy hit making apparatus," leading to a period of chart decline in the early 2000s. "Her divorce from Sony's Tommy Matola and her shift toward hip hop and R&B shook the foundations of her sturdy hit making apparatus."
**Glitter Movie and Soundtrack Flop (2001)** : Mariah Carey's 2001 movie "Glitter" was a commercial failure, and its accompanying soundtrack only produced one short-lived top 10 hit, "Loverboy." This period coincided with a highly publicized emotional breakdown for Carey and an "unexpectedly cold early Aughts period" where her singles often missed the top 40 entirely. "When her 2001 movie Glitter flopped and its soundtrack produced only one short lived top 10 hit, Love a Boy. It appeared that the chart era of Mariah Carey was over."
**Comeback and Chart Dominance (2000s)** : After nearly half a decade in a chart slump, Mariah Carey made a remarkable comeback in 2005. Her song "We Belong Together" spent 14 weeks at number one, becoming Billboard's top hit of the year. She went on to secure a total of seven more top 40 hits in the second half of the 2000s, including three number ones, further solidifying her place in chart history. "It would take Carrie almost half a decade to come back from her chart fall off."
**Most Number Ones for Solo Artist** : Prior to her 2005 comeback, Mariah Carey had amassed 15 number-one singles, the most for any female artist and placing her third overall behind Elvis Presley (17) and The Beatles (20). With subsequent hits like "We Belong Together," "Don't Forget About Us," and "Touch My Body," she reached 18 number ones, surpassing Elvis to become the solo artist with the most number ones in Billboard pop chart history. "That put her past Elvis as the solo artist with the most number ones in Billboard pop chart history."

# artist - Phil Spector

## labels
Phillies Records

## albums
**A Christmas Gift for You from Phil Spector (1963)** : This various artists compilation is considered one of the most influential albums in rock history and holiday music history, despite being regarded as a flop by retailers in 1963 and peaking at a modest number 13 on the Christmas album chart. It showcased acts from Spector's Phillies Records label, including The Ronettes, The Crystals, Darlene Love, and Bob B. Soxx, with all tracks featuring Spector's legendary "Wall of Sound" production style. The album significantly "changed the sound of 20th century holiday music," influencing the vocal style of standards like "Santa Claus Is Comin' to Town." None of its tracks were issued as singles in 1963, making them ineligible for Billboard charts under the new rules segregating holiday songs. "But there are few albums in rock history let alone holiday music history as influential as a Christmas gift for you from producer Phil Spector."

## curiosities
**Wall of Sound Production Style** : Phil Spector's legendary production technique was a defining feature of his 1963 album, "A Christmas Gift for You from Phil Spector." This signature style "changed the sound of 20th century holiday music," including influencing the more syncopated and soulful way songs like "Santa Claus Is Comin' to Town" were sung, a method later imitated by artists like the Jackson 5 and Bruce Springsteen. "And all of the tracks featured Spector's legendary wall of sound production style."

# curiosity

**Billboard Hot 100 - Birth and Evolution** : The Billboard Hot 100 was launched in 1958 as the "first successful all-in-one chart," combining single sales and radio airplay. These two factors remain essential to the chart's methodology, with radio serving as a "tempering force" that helps average out explosive single sales to determine enduring hits in a country as vast as America. The Hot 100 remains the benchmark for hit status 61 years later, but its launch made it harder for Christmas songs to top the pop charts. "Billboard build the Hot 100 as the first successful all-in-one chart combining single sales and radio airplay."
**Billboard's Inconsistent Holiday Music Policy** : For nearly a century, Billboard, referred to as the "Bible of the Music Industry," has never maintained a consistent policy on holiday music for more than a few years. Christmas songs were sometimes included on the Hot 100 and sometimes not, appearing on various charts, disappearing, and reappearing on different charts, or being entirely excluded, especially if they were album cuts not issued as singles. This shifting policy has historically made it challenging for holiday hits to chart effectively. "The Bible of the Music Industry has never, in nearly a century of charting hit songs and albums, had a consistent policy on holiday music that lasted more than a few years."
**Challenges of Seasonal Music on Pop Charts** : Holiday music, particularly Christmas music, struggles to fit the weekly popularity tracking of charts like the Hot 100 due to its limited six-to-eight-week impact window (early/mid-November through late December). Unlike typical hits that chart for months, seasonal music quickly loses relevance for the rest of the year. While Christmas music historically sells well, achieving chart success requires sustained cultural omnipresence across radio and streaming, which is difficult within such a short seasonal frame. "Seasonal music is just a hard fit for a pop chart that is designed to track how songs accrue their hitstacks."
**Eligibility of 45 RPM Singles** : In 1957, holiday songs from Elvis Presley's "Elvis' Christmas Album," such as "Blue Christmas" and "Santa Bring My Baby Back," were not issued as 45 RPM singles in America and were therefore ineligible for any of Billboard's singles charts. Before the Hot 100's existence, holiday records, if they charted, appeared on various specialized charts tracking sales, disc jockey play, or jukebox play. "Presley's holiday songs, like Blue Christmas and Santa Bring My Baby Back, were not issued as 45 RPM singles in America at the time, and hence were not eligible to appear on any of Billboards singles charts."
**Novelty Records in 1958** : The year 1958 saw novelty records at the height of their popularity. Kitchy singles like Bobby Helms' "Jingle Bell Rock" successfully functioned as both holiday songs and novelty hits. This trend also paved the way for other number-one hits like Sheb Wooley's "The Purple People Eater" and David Seville's "Witch Doctor," which utilized sped-up recording techniques. "Kitchy singles, like Jingle Bell Rock, worked as both holiday songs and, to some extent, novelty records, which, by the way, couldn't have been hotter in 1958."
**Sped-Up Vocal Fad (1958)** : A "wacky sped up vocal fad" gripped the charts in 1958, significantly contributing to the success of novelty hits like Sheb Wooley's "The Purple People Eater" and David Seville's "Witch Doctor." This technique, where artists sang slowly and then sped up the tape to create high-pitched, comical voices, also played a role in making The Chipmunks' "The Chipmunks Song (Christmas Don't Be Late)" a number one Christmas single that year. "And this wacky sped up vocal fad helps explain what happened to David Seville just six months later. And why there was a number one Christmas single in 1958 at all."
**Billboard's 1963 Christmas Chart Policy Change** : Starting in 1963, Billboard changed its policy on holiday music by removing holiday records from its main pop album and singles charts. Instead, it launched a dedicated "Christmas record section" with separate Christmas album and Christmas singles charts. This approach aimed to provide the music business with a clearer sense of which holiday records were performing best within the short holiday window, free from non-seasonal competition, and particularly benefited singles like The Beach Boys' "Little St. Nick." "The magazine removed holiday records from its pop album and singles charts and launched a Christmas record section with a Christmas album chart and a Christmas singles chart."
**UK Christmas Number One Competition** : By 1973, charts in England were influenced by the "relatively new phenomenon" of the UK Christmas number one competition. Unlike the American Hot 100, the British charts were "entirely based upon sales of singles" with no radio airplay component. This difference made it "comparatively easy for a blockbuster holiday song to top the UK pop chart." "in England by 1973 the charts were gripped by the relatively new phenomenon of the UK Christmas number one competition"
**Billboard's Mid-70s Chart Policy Reversal** : After approximately a decade, Billboard's first dedicated Christmas chart ended in the mid-1970s, when the magazine once again allowed holiday songs to compete on the Hot 100. However, the results were "mixed at best," as slower-moving US radio playlists continued to limit the performance of Christmas singles, preventing popular songs like Boney M.'s "Mary's Boy Child" from achieving high chart positions in America compared to the UK. "In the mid 70s when the magazine once again allowed holiday songs to compete on the hot 100 the results were mixed at best."
**Billboard's 1983 Christmas Chart Reinstatement** : In 1983, Billboard attempted to reinstate the Christmas singles chart. This move again provided a "leg up" to songs that would not typically compete on the Hot 100, such as Elmo and Patsy's novelty single "Grandma Got Run Over by a Reindeer," which crossed over from country radio to top the Billboard Christmas chart. "In 1983 billboard tried reinstating the Christmas singles chart and once again songs that would never have posed serious hot 100 competition were given a leg up."
**1980s Christmas Chart Rarities** : During the rest of the 1980s, holiday songs were largely "rarities on the charts." Most new Christmas classics from this decade were album cuts that did not chart, and often, Christmas music could only compete if it was by a dominant artist at their peak or if the song did not sound overtly Christmasy. "For the rest of the 1980s holiday songs were rarities on the charts."
**A Very Special Christmas (1987) Charity LP** : This 1987 Special Olympics charity LP "produced a slew of superstar tracks that became perennials." Notable contributions included U2's cover of Darlene Love's "Christmas (Baby Please Come Home)," Run-DMC's original "Christmas in Hollis," and Whitney Houston's gospel rendition of "Do You Hear What I Hear?." "The 1987 special Olympics charity LP a very special Christmas produced a slew of superstar tracks that became perennials"
**1990s Music Industry - Phasing Out Singles** : In the 1990s, the music industry was "gradually phasing out the single" as a retail product. This strategy aimed to encourage consumers to purchase full-length CD albums. This business logic influenced decisions like Sony's choice not to issue Mariah Carey's "All I Want for Christmas Is You" as a retail single in 1994, to drive sales of her "Merry Christmas" CD. "the music industry in the 1990s was gradually phasing out the single in an effort to encourage full length CD album purchases."
**Billboard's 1998 Rule Change - Album Cuts on Hot 100** : At the end of 1998, Billboard changed its rules to allow non-retail songs, or "album cuts," to appear on the Hot 100 for the first time. Within the first year of this change, holiday album cuts like 98 Degrees' "This Gift" made it onto the chart, and Mariah Carey's "All I Want for Christmas Is You," still an album cut, debuted on the Hot 100 at number 83 in the 1999-2000 holiday season. "Billboard changed its rules at the end of 1998 to allow non-retail songs, album cuts, to appear on the Hot 100 for the first time."
**Early 2000s AC Radio Shift to All-Christmas Format** : By the early 2000s, a significant industry trend emerged where many Adult Contemporary (AC) radio stations would switch to an "all Christmas music format" every November and December. This practice led to Christmas songs regularly topping Billboard's AC chart and receiving a boost on the Hot 100, enabling tracks that would have never been hits in earlier decades, such as Josh Groban's "O Holy Night," to gain prominence. "By the early 2000s, many adult contemporary or AC radio stations would switch to an all Christmas music format every November and December."
**iTunes Music Store and Legal Downloads (2003)** : The launch of Apple's iTunes Music Store in 2003 marked a "revolutionary" change in the music business, inaugurating the era of legal downloadable music. This platform made it possible to acquire album cuts, previously unavailable as singles, for 99 cents each (e.g., "Stairway to Heaven"). This development directly benefited songs like Mariah Carey's "All I Want for Christmas Is You," which debuted on Billboard's Hot Digital Tracks chart in 2003 and rose into the top 10 as the highest-ranking digital track that was not a current single. "the launch of Apple's iTunes music store, which inaugurated the era of legal downloadable music."

"holiday songs took up 16 spots in the top 40, from Jean Autry to WAM to Jose Feliciana, whose 1970 recording Felice Navidad was now a top 40 hit."

"the addition of downloads and especially streaming to the chart gave holiday music a truly even playing field for the first time."

"the Christmas music chart Renaissance was fully established, and the people had spoken."

# label - Billboard

## curiosities
**Relaunch of Christmas Music Chart (2011)** : In December 2011, Billboard relaunched its Christmas music chart, initially called "Holiday Songs" and later expanded to "Holiday 100," incorporating radio airplay and, for the first time in a holiday chart, digital data. "Billboard relaunched its Christmas music chart in a new improved form, employing radio airplay. And for the first time in a holiday chart, digital data."
**Holiday Chart Inclusivity (2011)** : The rebooted Holiday Songs/Holiday 100 chart, launched in 2011, distinguished itself from the Hot 100 by including both new releases and recurrent songs from various artists like Bing Crosby, Bobby Helms, and Wham!. "This rebooted holiday chart included both new and recurrent songs from Bing Crosby to Bobby Helms to WAM."
**Hot 100 Recurrent Rule (2011)** : As of 2011, the Hot 100 maintained a long-standing rule that generally did not allow old or recurrent songs to re-enter the chart unless actively re-promoted, distinguishing it from the newly relaunched Holiday Songs chart. "the hot 100, which in 2011 still did not allow old songs."
**Hot 100 Recurrent Rule Change (2012)** : Following Whitney Houston's death in February 2012, Billboard revised its Hot 100 rules to permit recurrent songs to re-enter the chart, provided they accumulated sufficient points to qualify for the top 50. "It would now allow recurrent songs to appear on the chart as long as they amassed enough points to make the top 50."
**Rationale for Chart Rule Change (2012)** : The magazine's director of charts explained the 2012 Hot 100 rule change by stating that the digital age had altered their calculations, blurring the distinction between the relevancy of new and older recordings. "the line has blurred between the relevancy of new and older recordings."
**Addition of Spotify Data (2012)** : By the 2012 holiday season, Billboard had integrated Spotify data into its chart methodologies, expanding the scope of information used to track song popularity. "Billboard had also added Spotify data to its chart."
**Transparency of Holiday Perennial Popularity (2012)** : The inclusion of digital downloads and streaming data on the Hot 100 from 2012 onwards provided chart followers with the first opportunity to accurately gauge the popularity of old holiday perennials against current hits on the same chart. "chart followers could see just how popular old holiday perennials were compared with current hits on the same chart."
**Streaming Dominance on Hot 100 (2017)** : By 2017, streaming had become the single most significant factor influencing positions on the Hot 100, with Christmas playlists on services like Spotify and Apple Music driving an unprecedented volume of holiday song streams. "Streaming was now the single biggest factor on the Hot 100."
**Holiday Songs Chart Schedule (2011-2019)** : Billboard's Holiday Songs chart is active for five weeks each year; between 2011 and 2019, this amounted to 43 weeks across nine holiday seasons. "Billboard brings back holiday songs for five weeks a year."
**Limited Number of Holiday Songs Chart Toppers (2011-2019)** : Throughout its entire 43-week history from 2011 to 2019, Billboard's Holiday Songs chart has been topped by only five different songs, highlighting its significant stability and dominance by a few key tracks. "it has only ever been topped by five songs."
**"The Mariah Chart"** : Due to Mariah Carey's overwhelming dominance, having topped the Holiday Songs chart for 38 out of its 43 weeks from 2011 to 2019, the chart is colloquially referred to as "the Mariah chart." "This holiday chart, it's basically the Mariah chart."
**Video View & Physical Sales for Charting (2019)** : For the 2019 Hot 100 charts, Billboard factored in views from new music videos and sales of the song released as a physical single on both CD and cassette, marking a first for the track's charting eligibility. "views of these new videos count for the hot 100, a new release of the song as a physical single for the first time on CD and even cassette, Billboard counts these sales for the chart too."

# curiosity

**Billboard Hot 100 Recurrent Rule (Pre-2012)** : A long-standing Billboard rule stipulated that old songs were generally allowed only one run on the Hot 100 chart, making them ineligible to return unless actively re-promoted, such as Queen's "Bohemian Rhapsody" in 1992 or The Beatles' "Twist and Shout" in 1986, which were revived by movies. "A song was only allowed one hot 100 chart run."
**Streaming's Impact on Holiday Charts (2012)** : The integration of large-scale streaming data into Billboard's charts in 2012 finally provided a clear window into which songs, particularly holiday ones, Americans most desired to consume during the holiday season. "large-scale streaming finally gave a window into the songs that Americans most wanted to consume at the holidays."
**SoundScan (1991) and Rap Music** : The implementation of SoundScan technology in 1991 revolutionized music charting by accurately tracking retail sales, thereby creating a more equitable playing field and enabling rap songs to top the Hot 100. "sound scan technology in 1991 had even the playing field and made it possible for rap songs to top the Hot 100."
**Wall of Sound Production Technique** : The "wall of sound" is a distinctive music production technique characterized by a dense, layered orchestral sound, aiming to create a powerful, immersive sonic experience where background vocals can become almost as prominent as the lead. "my own version of the wall of sound, those background vocal parts that become almost the lead."
**Hitmaking as Art and Science** : Hitmaking is understood as a complex blend of creative inspiration, widespread public affection for the music, and astute commercial strategy. "hitmaking is both an art and a science, a combination of inspiration, public affection, and raw commerce."
**Defining the Christmas Canon** : With modern chart data now fully revealing which holiday songs are most consumed by Americans through downloads and streaming, there is a newfound ability to more accurately define the comprehensive "Christmas canon" based on actual public preference. "we can better define the fullness of the Christmas canon."
