# artist - The Doobie Brothers

## members
Michael McDonald

## albums
**Minute by Minute (1979)** : This LP topped the Billboard album chart in April 1979, the same month their single "What a Fool Believes" hit No. 1. The album's success coincided with their biggest hit ever, establishing a significant presence on the charts. "That same month, their minute by minute LP topped the Billboard album chart."

## songs
**What a Fool Believes (1979)** : This song became the band's biggest hit ever as 1978 turned to 1979, hitting No. 1 in April 1979. Co-written by Michael McDonald and Kenny Loggins, it's about romantic regret and the impossibility of rekindling a dormant romance, a lyrical theme identified as core to the Yacht Rock genre. Its "Doobie Bounts" — a sprightly, syncopated chord progression often played on piano or synthesizer — became a remarkably sturdy pop song template. "The Doobies, what a fool believes, hit No. 1 in April 1979."

## curiosities
**Grammy Award Sweep (1980)** : "What a Fool Believes" swept the top song prizes at the Grammy Awards in 1980, marking a significant win for the band and for the burgeoning sound of LA Session Musicianship. This was part of a larger trend where the axis of LA Session Musicianship, not yet known as Yacht Rock, took home major Grammy Awards. "what a fool believes swept the top song prizes at the Grammys in 1980."
**Band Dissolution (1982)** : The band experienced a dissolution in 1982, leading to Michael McDonald's solo debut. This marked a transition for its prominent members into individual careers. "The track was McDonald's solo debut after the 1982 dissolution of the Dubie Brothers."

## curiosities
**Not Primarily Yacht Rock** : Despite common perception since the invention of the "Yacht Rock" moniker, Daryl Hall and John Oates are mostly not considered true Yacht Rock by the genre's creators, JD Riznar and friends. Their music is generally characterized as blue-eyed soul with diverse influences. "Hall and Oats are mostly not yacht rock."
**Yacht Rock Series Appearance (2005)** : A comedic version of Hall and Oates appeared in the original 2005 "Yacht Rock Channel 101" series, serving as "comic foils" from "the Mean Streets of Philadelphia." In this portrayal, John Oates, not Daryl Hall, was comically depicted as the duo's aggressive mastermind. "a version of Hall and Oats appear in the original 2005 yacht rock channel 101 series."
**Career Revival via Yacht Rock** : John Oates himself has acknowledged in interviews that the popularity of the "Yacht Rock" concept helped revive Hall and Oates' reputation and career, bringing renewed attention to their music. This indicates a positive impact of the genre's popularization. "John Oats himself has, in at least one interview, credited the popularity of yacht rock with helping to revive Hall and Oats reputation and career."
**West Coast Personnel Avoidance** : Their music generally does not feature West Coast session personnel, a key characteristic of authentic Yacht Rock, though they occasionally worked with L.A. producers like David Foster. This factor contributed to their music being deemed "adjacent" but not "integral" to the genre by its founders. "Their music generally contains no West Coast personnel, although Hall and Oats did work with L.A. producers like David Foster occasionally."

# artist - Christopher Cross

## genres

## labels

## albums
**Christopher Cross (1979) - Debut Album** : This self-titled debut LP, released in the closing weeks of 1979, featured a tropical green background and a pink flamingo on its cover, making it visually resemble a "Yacht Rock album." Produced by Michael Omartian, it extensively utilized "dozens of West Coast players" and led to Cross commanding the charts in 1980. "Cross's eponymous LP dropped in the closing weeks of 1979."

## songs
**Ride Like the Wind (1980) - Lead Single** : This lead single from Cross's debut album, featuring backing vocals by Michael McDonald, settled at No. 2 on the Hot 100 for four weeks by spring 1980. Its success helped establish Cross's chart dominance. "By spring, ride like the wind settled in at number two on the Hot 100 for four weeks."
**Sailing (1980) - Prototypical Yacht Rock Ballad** : This "candy-scan-do miracle" topped the Hot 100 in August 1980 and is considered an atypical but essential Yacht Rock song, serving as the genre's "prototypical ballad." Lyrically about a boat and "tranquility," critics found it "somnolent and numbing," but the public embraced it. "Sailing topped the Hot 100 in August 1980, and it made Christopher Cross the King of 1981's Grammy Awards."
**Arthur's Theme (Best That You Can Do) (1981) - Oscar-Winning Movie Theme** : This chart-topping theme from the movie *Arthur* won the 1981 Best Original Song Oscar. Despite its lyrics about New York City, it possesses a sound strongly reminiscent of Los Angeles. "Christopher Cross's chart topping theme from the movie Arthur, winner of 1981's Best Original Song Oscar, a ditty about New York City that sounds very much like LA."

## curiosities
**Grammy Award Sweep (1981)** : Christopher Cross became the "King of 1981's Grammy Awards" by sweeping the top four prizes: Record of the Year (for "Sailing"), Song of the Year (for "Sailing"), Album of the Year (for *Christopher Cross*), and Best New Artist. He was the last artist to achieve this Grammy sweep until Billy Eilish in 2020. "Cross had swept the night's top four prizes, record, song, and album of the year and best new artist."
**Direct Entry into Yacht Rock** : Unlike other artists who adapted to or helped build the Yacht Rock genre, Christopher Cross, a San Antonio, Texas guitarist and songwriter, had little history as a session player before signing with Warner Bros. in 1978. He was effectively "born career-wise into Yacht Rock." "You might say Cross was born career-wise into Yacht Rock."
**LA Session Player Utilization** : Producer Michael Omartian convinced Warner executives to let Cross record his own material but insisted on using "the cream of LA's session players." This approach ensured his debut album was filled with the characteristic sound of West Coast musicianship. "Producer Michael O'Marty, a California studio veteran who'd played with the likes of Steely Dan and Kenny Loggins, convinced Warner executives to let the easy-listening crooner record his own material, but used the cream of LA's session players."

## songs
**Rosanna (1982) - Session Rock Master Class** : The lead single from *Toto IV*, written by David Paich and named for Rosanna Arquette (Steve Porcaro's girlfriend at the time), spent a month at No. 2 on the Hot 100 in summer 1982. It was a "Session Rock Master Class" built around a half-time shuffle beat adapted by Jeff Porcaro from Bernard Purdie, featuring traded vocals by Steve Lukather and Bobby Kimball. "Rosanna, written by Toto's David Page, and named for Steve Parkero's then-girlfriend, actress Rosanna Arquette, was the lead single from Toto 4, the LA Group's biggest selling album."
**Make Believe (1982)** : The band and their label attempted to release this jazzy pop number as a follow-up to "Rosanna," but it stalled at No. 30 in the fall of 1982, failing to replicate the success of its predecessor. "The band and their label tried issuing a similarly jazzy pop number named Make Believe. But it stalled at number 30 in the fall of 82."
**Africa (1983) - Enduring Yacht Rock Song** : This "dreamy Fantasia," primarily written by David Paich, about a man's imagined view of a continent, was an "oddball track" the band almost left off *Toto IV* and initially thought unsuitable as a single. It unexpectedly reached No. 1 on the Hot 100 in early February 1983 and became "the most enduring yacht rock song of all." "The song that everyone in Toto underestimated reached number one on the Hot 100 in early February 1983."
**Human Nature (1982) - Written for Toto, Recorded by Michael Jackson** : Written by Steve Porcaro and originally intended for Toto, this song was heard by Quincy Jones, who secured it for Michael Jackson's *Thriller*. It became a No. 7 hit on the Hot 100, effectively a "Toto song with Jackson singing lead." "the song Human Nature was written by Steve Porcaro and intended for his own band before Quincy Jones heard it and asked if Michael Jackson could record it."
**The Girl Is Mine (1982) - Backing for Michael Jackson** : Toto members played backup on this "equally yachty" lead single from Michael Jackson's *Thriller*, a duet with Paul McCartney that became a No. 2 smash. This collaboration showcased their versatility and continued involvement in major pop hits. "They also played back up on the album's equally yachty lead single, the Paul McCartney duet The Girl Is Mine, a number two smash."
**Beat It (1982) - Backing for Michael Jackson** : Toto also backed this "searing rock single" from Michael Jackson's *Thriller*, notably with Steve Lukather playing the main guitar riff on the verses, while Eddie Van Halen performed the famous solo. This demonstrated their range beyond smooth pop. "And believe it or not, Toto also backed the album's searing rock single, Beat It."
**I'll Be Over You (1986) - Last Top 20 Pop Hit** : This song was Toto's last top 20 pop hit, marking a point in 1986 when the Yacht Rock movement, though unnamed, was quietly concluding its chart dominance. ## curiosities
**Grammy Award Sweep (1983)** : At the Grammy Awards, Toto won Record of the Year for "Rosanna" and Album of the Year for *Toto IV*. Drummer Jeff Porcaro, in his acceptance speech, highlighted the band's background as session veterans, implying their widespread industry connections contributed to their win. "Their earlier hit Rosanna, one record of the year, and Toto 4 took album of the year."
**Session Veteran Background** : The band was "composed entirely of session veterans," a fact Jeff Porcaro emphasized in his Grammy acceptance speech, suggesting their extensive work with many industry figures contributed to their success. This background linked them to the broader "LA Session Musicianship" axis. "In his acceptance speech, Drummer Jeff Porcaro made plain why this band, composed entirely of session veterans, had won."
**Band's Underestimation of "Africa"** : The band initially considered "Africa" a "moody, oddball track" that didn't fit *Toto IV*, almost leaving it off the album and burying it at the end of side two. They never thought it would be a single, and years later, members called it a "goofy song" they'd be "most remembered for." "A moody, oddball track that the band almost left off the album, thinking it didn't fit."
**Jeff Porcaro's Death (1992)** : Drummer Jeff Porcaro passed away in 1992, making him a non-surviving member of the band's core lineup. His contributions to the band, particularly on drums and percussion, were foundational. "drummer Jeff Porcaro, died back in 1992"
**Attempted Genre Shift (Post-Toto IV)** : Following their Grammy-winning success with *Toto IV*, the band attempted to "transform themselves into new wave synth rockers" with little success. This effort to adapt their sound post-*Thriller* did not yield the desired results. "Toto attempted to transform themselves into new wave synth rockers, with little success."
**Socially Distanced "Africa" (2020)** : In spring 2020, during the COVID-19 pandemic, founding members Steve Lukather and David Paich, along with longtime percussionist Lenny Castro, produced a socially distanced version of "Africa" from their homes. This video was offered as pure entertainment during lockdown, showcasing veteran musicians playing from their residences. "Founding Toto members Steve Lucather and David Page, along with longtime percussionist Lenny Castro, produced a socially distanced version of their most famous hit, Complete with Video, recorded from their homes."

# genre - Yacht Rock

## curiosities
**Definition and Origin** : Yacht Rock is a term "invented in the Aughts" (2000s) to define "smooth, California-based music from the late 70s and early 80s." It is an "invented retroactive genre" with parameters formulated by JD Riznar and friends. "Yacht Rock, a term invented in the Aughts to define smooth, California-based music from the late 70s and early 80s."
**Archetype: The Doobie Bounts** : The "Doobie Bounts," characterized by a "sprightly syncopated chord progression, typically played on piano or synthesizer," was established by The Doobie Brothers' "What a Fool Believes" and became a remarkably sturdy pop song template that recurred on many hit records. "FULL also established a Yacht Rock archetype, the Doobie Bounts."
**Prototypical Ballad: "Sailing"** : Christopher Cross's "Sailing" is considered the "most atypical but essential Yacht Rock song" and the genre's "prototypical ballad." It is described as being to Yacht Rock what The Bee Gees' "How Deep Is Your Love" was to disco, showcasing a smooth song about smoothness. "Sailing is perhaps the most atypical but essential Yacht Rock song."
**Haven for African American Acts (Yacht Soul)** : As the 70s ended and disco declined, black artists were increasingly "boxed out of top 40 playlists" by radio programmers avoiding anything that "seemed too black." This made "Yacht Rock or Yacht Soul a haven for African American acts," providing vital crossover opportunities into both R&B and pop radio. "This made Yacht Rock or Yacht Soul a haven for African American acts."
**LA Session Musicianship Axis** : Much of the chart-topping Yacht Rock came from an "axis of LA Session Musicianship," with the same musicians playing on records by various artists like Steely Dan, The Doobie Brothers, and George Benson. This network was key to the genre's cohesive sound and Grammy success. "It was the third time in four years... that a song by this axis of LA Session Musicianship... took home one or more of the top Grammy Awards."
**Decline and End of Chart Dominance (Mid-1980s)** : The chart dominance of Yacht Rock began to wane after *Thriller* in late 1982, which shifted pop towards more kinetic forms. While some yacht rock players contributed to later hits, the movement's chart command quietly ended by 1986, when it still "didn't have a real life name." "The movement that still didn't have a real life name, but which had quietly, smoothly commanded the charts for the better part of a decade was over."
**Enduring Influence and Revival (2000s onwards)** : Despite its decline, Yacht Rock "never really went away," with later artists like Warren G sampling classics like Michael McDonald's "I Keep Forgettin'." In the 2000s, the "Yacht Rock video series" on the web sparked a revival driven by "kitsch, nostalgia, and eventually admiration," giving a boost to original performers. "No one's more amazed than I think myself and my contemporaries are that we're still working."
**Metaphor for Cultural Reinterpretation** : In a literal sense, the song "Africa" is described as a metaphor for Yacht Rock itself: "black culture reinterpreted by white musicians." This highlights a significant aspect of the genre's cultural dynamic. "In one rather literal sense, the song is a metaphor for yacht rock itself, black culture reinterpreted by white musicians."
