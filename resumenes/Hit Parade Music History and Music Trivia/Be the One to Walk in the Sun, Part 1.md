## albums
**True Colors (1986) - Sophomore Album** : This was Cyndi Lauper's second studio LP, released in the closing months of 1986. A lot was riding on this album as it followed a multi-platinum debut, and it featured guest vocalists such as The Bangles on its lead-off track "Change of Heart" and Amy Mann. The title track, "True Colors," was in its second week at number 1 on the Hot 100 on November 1, 1986. "The album was True Colors, the second studio LP by New York singer, songwriter, and fashion iconoclab Cindy Lopper."
**She's So Unusual (1983) - Debut Solo Album** : Released in the fall of 1983, this album steadily climbed the Billboard LP chart through early 1984, peaking at number four. By early 1985, it was certified quadruple platinum. The album's title and branding, including the Annie Leibovitz cover photograph of Lauper dancing barefoot in a red prom dress in Brooklyn's Coney Island, were expertly managed to match its content and Cyndi's "unusual" image. "Released in the fall of 1983, she's so unusual climbed the Billboard LP chart steadily through early 1984, while girls just want to have fun scale the hot 100 fueled by heavy play on MTV."

## songs
**Change of Heart (1986)** : This was the lead-off track from Cyndi Lauper's "True Colors" album. It featured backing vocals by the fast-rising Los Angeles all-female band, The Bangles. "On background vocals, the LP's lead-off track, Change of Heart, featured the fast-rising Los Angeles All Female For some, The Bangles."
**Blue Christmas** : Cyndi Lauper performed this standard at a New Year's gig in a dive in Passaic, New Jersey, shortly after meeting Dave Wolfe. Her "tipsy performance" of the song did not deter Wolfe, leading her to not only continue dating him but also to hire him as her manager. "When her tipsy performance of the standard Blue Christmas didn't scare Wolfe off, she not only kept dating him, she hired him as her manager."
**Money Changes Everything (1983)** : A cover of a song by the band The Brains, this track led off Lauper's "She's So Unusual" album. Cyndi felt the band was playing in a "dated Dylanesque style" and instructed guitarist Eric Bazilian and producer Rick Chertoff to listen to The Clash's "London Calling" to achieve a tough guitar sound. "Cindy felt the band was playing in a very dated Dillonesque style, so she told guitarist Eric Bazzillion and producer Rick Chertoff to go and listen to the clash."
**When You Were Mine (1983)** : This is a transformative cover of Prince's "new waviest song" from his 1980 "Dirty Mind" LP, which had only ever seen release as a B-side. Cyndi's version found the ache in Prince's jam, showcasing her ability to interpret and reinvent material. "Cindy transformed that song too, finding the ache in Prince's jam."
**Girls Just Want to Have Fun (1983)** : Originally a "quirky, horny, and kind of sexist" demo by Robert Hazard written from a male perspective, Cyndi Lauper and producer Rick Chertoff transformed it into an empowering anthem. Lauper asked guitarist Eric Bazilian to shift his "white reggae style" playing toward "Motown-esque riffs," changed the song's key to be more female-friendly, incorporated a Buddy Holly-inspired hiccup after "fun," and had Ellie Greenwich arrange the backing vocals as a Shangri-Las-like chant. The music video, co-starring her mother and diverse friends, contributed to its iconic status, and the single peaked at number two on the pop chart by March 1984. "Rick Chertoff was convinced girls just want to have fun could be transformed by Cindy Lopper into something empowering."
**Time After Time (1983)** : Co-written with Rob Hyman of The Hooters, this song became Cyndi Lauper's second single from "She's So Unusual" and went all the way to number one, establishing her as a capable songwriter despite initial discouragement from her producer. The title was inspired by a 1979 sci-fi film starring Malcolm McDowell and Mary Steenburgen. It has since become a new standard, covered by jazz legend Miles Davis (as early as 1985), acoustic jazz duo Tuck and Patti (1988), and 90s pop and R&B singer InnoJay (1998), who took it back to number six on the Hot 100. "Cindy Lopper and Rob Hyman hadn't just written a hit. They'd created a new standard."
**She Bop (1984)** : Co-written by Lauper and Steve Lunt, this song was conceived as an unprecedented track about female masturbation, designed to "sail over the heads of young kids" and receive radio play, despite referencing "Blue Boy" magazine and lines like "They Say All Better Stop or Algar Blind." It became an instant smash, rising to number three by September 1984, but was later included in the Parents Music Resource Center’s "Filthy 15" list, which annoyed Lauper as she had intentionally tried to keep it PG-rated. "Lopper and her co-writer Steve Lunt wanted Shebop to be an unprecedented song about female masturbation, one that would sail over the heads of young kids."
**All Through The Night (1984)** : This was Cyndi Lauper's fourth straight hit from "She's So Unusual" and her first single since "Girls Just Want to Have Fun" that was a cover. She remade a track by singer-songwriter Jules Shear, and the song peaked at number five on the charts. "When the song peaked at number five, Billboard reported that Cindy Lopper had become the first female artist to pull four top five hits from a single album."

## curiosities
**Early Life and Vocal Issues** : Born Cynthia Ann Stephanie Lauper in 1953 and raised in Ozone Park, Queens, she adopted the unusual spelling "C-Y-N-D-I" for her first name at a teen friend's suggestion. She left home at 17, spending her early 20s singing with various cover bands. By 1977, her potent four-octave voice was damaged due to overuse, requiring a singing coach to recover it. "Born in 1953 and raised in Ozone Park, Queens, Cindy Lauper, a teen friend encouraged her to give her first name the unusual spelling C-Y-N-D-I."
**Bankruptcy and Vintage Clothing Store Job** : Following strained relations with Polydor Records and Blue Angel's manager, Lauper declared bankruptcy to escape her contract. To pay her bills, she worked for two years as a salesgirl at Screaming Mimi's, a vintage clothing store renowned for its ahead-of-the-curve looks, which significantly influenced Lauper's distinctive personal style. "Lauper ultimately declared bankruptcy to get out from under her contract to pay the bills, Cindy wound up working at screaming memes, a vintage clothing store famed for its ahead of the curve looks and informative to Lauper's own look."
**Meeting Dave Wolfe** : In 1981, Cyndi Lauper met Dave Wolfe, a charming musician turned road manager, at a Christmas party. After they started dating and her tipsy New Year's performance of "Blue Christmas" didn't deter him, she hired him as her manager. By the end of 1982, Wolfe successfully secured Lauper a solo recording contract with Portrait Records, a subsidiary of Epic Records. "Had Cindy not attended a Christmas party in 1981 and met a fast-talking goofy but charming musician turned road manager named Dave Wolfe."
**Producer's Influence on Songwriting** : Rick Chertoff, producer of "She's So Unusual," initially viewed Lauper primarily as a song interpreter, leading to half the album consisting of covers. Lauper later expressed frustration, realizing she could have written more songs earlier in the process after "Time After Time," a song she co-wrote, became a massive hit. "The album wound up being half-covers thanks largely to producer Chertoff, who envisioned Lauper as a song interpreter more than a songwriter."
**Madonna Rivalry (Media-Fomented)** : In 1984, the media and music industry attempted to create a rivalry between Cyndi Lauper and Madonna, who debuted their solo albums mere months apart. Lauper claimed to have worn a corset as a blouse before Madonna but largely dismissed the rivalry, noting their distinct musical styles and Madonna's astute business and marketing sense, stating, "you don't fucking knock another sister ever." Lauper achieved chart milestones first, with "Girls Just Want to Have Fun" hitting the top 10 months before Madonna, and "Time After Time" reaching number one while Madonna's "Borderline" peaked at number 10. "The press always asked me about Madonna. They tried to create this big rivalry, but my feeling was, you don't fucking knock another sister ever."
**Billboard Chart Record** : In 1984, when her single "All Through The Night" peaked at number five, Cyndi Lauper became the first female artist to achieve four top five hits from a single album. She was also the first artist of any kind to accomplish this feat with a debut LP. "When the song peaked at number five, Billboard reported that Cindy Lopper had become the first female artist to pull four top five hits from a single album."
**Tour with The Bangles** : In early 1985, The Bangles were offered an opening slot on Cyndi Lauper's tour. Both acts, being signed to CBS labels, "took a shine to each other" during this synergistic pairing. "That by early 85, the bangles were given an opening slot on Cindy Loppers tour, where the LA group and the New York singer took a shine to each other."

**I'm Gonna Be Strong** : A slow dance cover of Jean Pitney's song, this was the only Blue Angel track to chart anywhere in the world, reaching number 37 in the Netherlands. Despite its success, Cyndi Lauper remained loyal to the band and declined multiple solo offers, not wanting to be pigeonholed as a balladeer. "Indeed, the only Blue Angel song to break on any chart anywhere in the world was their slow dance cover of Jean Pitney's I'm Gonna Be Strong, which went to number 37 in the Netherlands."

## curiosities
**Formation and Sound** : Blue Angel formed in 1978 when saxophonist John Tury met Cynthia Ann Stephanie Lauper at a Greenwich Village nightclub. The band cultivated a new wave retro rockabilly sound, incorporating kitsch and Lauper's vintage clothes, influenced by New York rock. While not punk, they primarily played uptown clubs and Great Guildersleeves rather than the legendary CBGB. "Blue Angel was finally the band to win Lauper a recording contract at the fairly advanced age of 26. She, John Tury, and the other band members settled on a kind of new wave retro rockability sound, which they showcased on the band's one and only self-titled album on Polydor Records in 1980."
**Cyndi Lauper's Loyalty** : Cyndi Lauper demonstrated strong loyalty to John Tury and her Blue Angel bandmates by repeatedly declining offers to leave the group and pursue a solo career. She also resisted being typecast as a balladeer, even though her torch songs were the most commercially successful for the band. "Cindy turned down multiple offers to ditch the band and go solo. She was loyal to John and the Blue Angel bandmates, and she didn't want to be pigeonholed as a baladier."
**Band's Dissolution** : The band experienced deteriorating relations with both Polydor Records and their manager. Ultimately, Lauper declared bankruptcy to extricate herself from her contract, which effectively led to the end of Blue Angel. "Relations soured with both Polydor Records and the band's manager, and Lauper ultimately declared bankruptcy to get out from under her contract to pay the bills, Cindy wound up working at screaming memes, a vintage clothing store famed for its ahead of the curve looks and informative to Lauper's own look."

## curiosities
**Formation and Early Influences** : In 1981, sisters Vicki Peterson (guitar) and Debbie Peterson (drums) met Susanna Hoffs in a garage Hoffs had converted into an apartment and practice space in Los Angeles. Born around 1959-1960, they shared a love for AM Radio Pop music, British Invasion, and power pop, and discovered their voices had "natural instantaneous harmony." "In 1981, the Peterson sisters, guitarist Vicki Peterson and drummer Debbie Peterson of Northridge, California met Brentwood native Susanna Hoffs."
**Band Name Evolution** : After briefly including bassist Annette Zlinsky and trying out several names, the group settled on "The Bangs" by 1982, inspired by a hairstyle reference in a 1965 fashion magazine. By the end of 1982, they tweaked the name to "The Bangles" due to another East Coast band already using "The Bangs." "After bringing in a bassist named Annette Zlinsky and trying out several band names, by 1982 they had settled on the bangs after the hairstyle inspired by a reference in a 1965 fashion magazine article and that name wasn't the only thing retro about the bangs."
**Paisley Underground Scene** : The Bangles were a prominent part of the "Paisley Underground," an LA scene of similarly retro bands. This group included Susanna Hoffs' boyfriend's band Rain Parade, Michael Quercio's The Three O'Clock (originally The Salvation Army), and Steve Wynn's The Dream Syndicate. Critic Chris O'Leary characterized these acts as "translators of the dead 60s into the fledgling 80s who treated it with more love than irony." "Together these bands came to be known as the Paisley Underground. A name Kursio from the three o'clock tossed off in an interview that stuck."
**MTV and American Bandstand Appearances** : The Bangles' manager, Miles Copeland, swiftly elevated their profile by securing them an opening slot on a tour with New Wave Titans The English Beat and a guest appearance on MTV's first indie music show, The Cutting Edge. During this appearance, Susanna Hoffs articulated their "60s gone 80s ethos." By 1983, with only an independent EP released, they also appeared on Dick Clark's American Bandstand. "And a guest slot on MTV's first Indy music show The Cutting Edge, where Susanna Huffs described their whole 60s gone 80s ethos."
**Bassist Change (Annette Zlinsky to Mickie Steel)** : Around 1983, bassist Annette Zlinsky departed the group because her voice "never quite harmonized as well" with the other members. She was replaced by Mickie Steel (then known as Michael Steel), a seasoned bassist with experience in the first version of The Runaways and over a dozen other LA bands, known for playing her instrument like a lead. Critic Chris O'Leary likened Steel's arrival to Ringo joining The Beatles, stating, "She elevated them." "It was around this time that bassist Annette Zalinsky's whose voice never quite harmonized as well with the Peterson sisters and Hoffs left the group for another band."
**Influence of The Go-Go's** : The industry perceived Columbia Records' signing of The Bangles as an attempt to get their "own version of the Go-Go's," as The Go-Go's were nearing their breakup. Critic Chris O'Leary noted that despite both being all-female rock bands, they weren't rivals, but radio stations maintained "strict if tacit limits" on airplay for female artists, suggesting if one "girl rock band was over, there was more room on playlists for that other girl rock band." "One DJ was later quoted in Billboard saying of the bangles quote, we need stuff like this. We haven't had a girl group since The Go-Go's, unquote."
**Tour with Cyndi Lauper** : In early 1985, The Bangles were given an opening slot on Cyndi Lauper's tour. The two groups, both signed to CBS labels, developed a mutual fondness for each other during this synergistic pairing. "That by early 85, the bangles were given an opening slot on Cindy Loppers tour, where the LA group and the New York singer took a shine to each other."

**Berklee College of Music Dropout** : Amy Mann left Virginia to enroll in Boston's Berklee College of Music in 1978 but dropped out after only a year and a half. "Amy Mann escaped Virginia by enrolling in Boston's Berkeley College of Music in 1978, but she dropped out after only a year and a half and dove into her Boston music scene."

## curiosities
**Early Formation and Induction** : The all-female quintet, comprising Charlotte Caffey, Kathy Valentine, Belinda Carlisle, Gina Schock, and Jane Wiedlin, began recording together as The Go-Go's as early as 1980. They were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2021, performing "We Got The Beat" live at the ceremony. "Charlotte, Kathy, Belinda, Carlyle, Gina Schock, Kathy Valentine, and Jane Weedlin, first recorded together as the Go Go's as early as 1980."
**Rock Hall Recognition (Long Overdue)** : Their 2021 induction into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame was described as "a long time coming," highlighting the institution's historically "poor" track record with women. Their inclusion was notable as half of the 2021 class consisted of living women, a "sadly a first for the hall." "This recognition was a long time coming. Charlotte, Kathy, Belinda, Carlyle, Gina Schock, Kathy Valentine, and Jane Weedlin, first recorded together as the Go Go's as early as 1980."
**Seminal Influence** : The Go-Go's were considered "seminal in the development of rock" for women and for performers of any gender. Despite their significant impact and chart success, it took an extended period for them to be nominated for, let alone inducted into, the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame. "They were seminal in the development of rock, for women, and for performers of any gender."
**Influence on Other Artists** : The Go-Go's served as an influence for both The Bangles, a near-contemporaneous all-female group, and Cyndi Lauper, a new wave fellow traveler. However, Lauper and The Bangles developed out of distinctive scenes separate from the LA punk scene that spawned The Go-Go's. "The Go Go's, they were an influence on both the bangles, a near-contemporaneous all-female group, and Cindy Lauper, a new wave-feller traveler."

## curiosities
**Contribution to Cyndi Lauper's Album** : Rick Chertoff, who produced Cyndi Lauper's debut album "She's So Unusual" and was also working on The Hooters' debut, incorporated elements of their sound into Lauper's record. Keyboardist Rob Hyman and guitarist Eric Bazilian formed the "backbone" of Lauper's studio band for the album. "Chertoff, at the time, was working on the debut album by an up-and-coming Philadelphia band called The Hooters. **Songwriting Collaboration with Cyndi Lauper** : Keyboardist Rob Hyman contributed the phrase "a suitcase of memories" to Cyndi Lauper, which she used as a foundation to build the song "Time After Time."
