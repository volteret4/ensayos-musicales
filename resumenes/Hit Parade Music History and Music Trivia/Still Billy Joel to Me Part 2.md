## albums
**An Innocent Man (1983)** : This album, released when Joel was 34, became his most hit-packed LP and improbably made him one of the top stars of the early MTV era. Conceived as an antidote to its predecessor "The Nylon Curtain," it was ebullient, accessible, and explicitly retro-themed, with Joel in a new romance with supermodel Christy Brinkley. The songs "poured out of him" as he openly rewrote musical tropes of his youth, showcasing a talent for evoking specific song modes while writing original melodies. By 1985, it was quadruple platinum and had the biggest hit ratio of any of Joel's albums, with more than half its songs becoming top 40 hits, yet it never got past number four on the album chart due to Michael Jackson's "Thriller." "An Innocent Man was an antidote to the nylon curtain, ebulliant, where its predecessor had been dower, blatantly accessible, where its predecessor had been thorny and experimental."
**Greatest Hits, Volume 1 & Volume 2 (1985)** : Billy Joel took advantage of his peak popularity in 1985 to release this two-record set, which went on to become one of the best-selling albums of all time. The collection included two new tracks, one of which ("You're Only Human (Second Wind)") became a top 10 hit. "Joel picked 1985 to issue his first greatest hits album, a two record set that went on to become one of the best-selling albums of all time."
**The Bridge (1986)** : Released after a longer lapse between albums, "The Bridge" reflected Joel's content approach to nearing 40 and his continued refusal to settle on one sound. The album was described as a hodgepodge with a pervading Broadway showtunes vibe, featuring tracks like "Running on Ice" and "Big Man on Mulberry Street." Its biggest hit, "A Matter of Trust," was a rare guitar-centric song for Joel. "Start to finish, The Bridge was a hodgepodge."
**Storm Front (1989)** : This album was released during a period of professional turmoil for Joel, who had tired of his long-time producer Phil Ramone and faced a bitter lawsuit over fiscal malfeasance by his former manager and brother-in-law Frank Weber. Against expectations, it became Joel's first album chart number one since "Glass Houses" in 1980, largely thanks to "We Didn't Start the Fire." Mick Jones of Foreigner produced the album after Joel considered Eddie Van Halen. "Stormfront, his first LP in more than a dozen years not to be produced by Phil Ramone."
**River of Dreams (1993)** : Billy Joel's final studio album of pop material, released in 1993, debuted at number one on the album chart, making it Joel's first LP to open at the top at age 44. Its title track was an undeniable hit, influenced by African music and New York-style rock and roll, akin to Paul Simon's "Graceland" and The Tokens' "The Lion Sleeps Tonight." "At age 44 Billy Joel had his first ever LP open on top of the charts."
**Fantasies and Delusions (2001)** : This album marked Billy Joel's first and, to date, only fully composed classical album, self-deprecatingly titled and performed by pianist Richard Young-Key Jew. It comprised "A2D style piano pieces" that Joel had been developing for years, similar to the original instrumental version of his song "Lullaby." "he issued his first and to date only fully composed classical album, the self-deprecatingly titled Fantasies and Delusions."

## songs
**Tell Her About It (1983)** : The first single from "An Innocent Man," this song was an admitted homage to Motown, specifically the recordings of The Supremes, though its melody and lyrics were original. It exuded Motown flavor, even in its lyrics about an experienced elder advising a younger man on romance. The song debuted on the Hot 100 in July 1983 and reached number one by September, marking the second time in three years Joel topped the charts with a song that didn't sound like typical Billy Joel, lacking prominent piano or troubadourish lyrics. "Joel has openly admitted this song was an homage to Motown."
**Uptown Girl (1983)** : This follow-up hit from "An Innocent Man" reached number three on the Hot 100 in November 1983, holding the position for five weeks and staying on the chart longer than "Tell Her About It." It is Joel's most played radio hit to this day and his only number one in England. Joel openly admitted it was a retro homage to the falsetto-singing doo-wop group of his youth, The Four Seasons. "To this day, Uptown Girl is Billy Joel's most played radio hit."
**An Innocent Man (1984)** : The title track from Joel's 1983 album, this song became a number 10 hit in February 1984. Musically, it was an interpolation of the moody, impassioned R&B style characteristic of artists like The Drifters and Ben E. King. "Was an interpolation of the moody, impassioned R&B of artists like the Drifters and Ben E. King."
**The Longest Time (1984)** : The fourth single from "An Innocent Man," this song was pure acapella doo-wop, relying solely on human voices for instrumentation. Remarkably, despite this stark sound, it reached number 14 on the Hot 100 in May 1984, during the peak of synth-pop, paying respects to doo-wop Hall of Famers Frankie Lymon and the Teenagers. "The Longest Time, was pure acapella duop."
**Leave a Tender Moment Alone (1984)** : This fifth single from "An Innocent Man" was a yearning mid-tempo ballad featuring prominent harmonica, reaching number 27 in August 1984. While fans and Joel initially cited influences like The Rascals and Smokey Robinson, Joel later revealed the melody was deliberately based on the Bert Bacharach/Hal David composition "What the World Needs Now," sung by Jackie De Shannon and Dionne Warwick. "Leave a Tender Moment Alone, was a yearning mid-tempo ballad with prominent harmonica."
**Keeping the Faith (1985)** : The improbable sixth top 40 hit from "An Innocent Man," this wistful, up-tempo pop track was almost not released as a single, but it put a cap on the album's era, reaching number 18 in March 1985, 19 months post-album release. Its "lurching guitar groove" was inspired by Betty Wright's 1972 R&B jam "Clean Up Woman." "Keeping the Faith put a button on the Innocent Man era, reaching number 18 in March of 1985, 19 months after the album's release."
**You're Only Human (Second Wind) (1985)** : One of two new tracks included on "Greatest Hits, Volume 1 & Volume 2," this "synth pop meets Duwap track" became a top 10 hit. Despite being described as "rather dated," its theme was heartfelt: a plea to teenagers contemplating suicide, inspired by Joel's own battles with depression and suicidal ideation. Proceeds from the song went to the National Committee for Youth Suicide Prevention. "The rather dated, synth pop meets Duwap track, your only human second wing."
**Modern Woman (1986)** : The lead single from "The Bridge," this "synth pop," "fifth driven, bouncy hit" reached number 10 on the Hot 100. Joel himself later denigrated the song, which was featured in the film "Ruthless People" and notable for its "Broadway showtunes vibe." "Modern Woman, a number 10 hit on the Hot 100, was taken from the Bett Middler Danny DeVito film comedy Ruthless People."
**A Matter of Trust (1986)** : Widely considered "The Bridge's best hit," this song reached number 10 in October 1986. It was a rare composition for Joel that was written primarily for the guitar, with Joel famously abandoning his piano to play an electric guitar in both its music video and live concerts. "A Matter of Trust, was a rare song Joel wrote primarily for the guitar."
**This Is The Time (1986)** : A number 18 hit from "The Bridge," this song is described as a traditional, wistful Billy Joel keyboard ballad. Joel overtly echoed the "somber beach at Winter Vib" of Don Henley's 1984 hit "The Boys of Summer" in its composition. "Joel overtly echoed the somber beach at Winter Vib of Don Henley's prior 1984 hit The Boys of Summer."
**Back in the U.S.S.R., Live in Leningrad (1987)** : This was Billy Joel's live cover of The Beatles' song, released as a single from the live album recorded during his 1987 Soviet concerts. It holds significance as the first single Joel ever issued that was written by someone other than himself. "His cover of The Beatles Back in the USSR, Live in Leningrad, was the first single he'd ever issued written by someone other than himself."
**We Didn't Start the Fire (1989)** : This "stream of consciousness history lesson" led off Joel's "Storm Front" album and became his third and last career number one, topping the Hot 100 in December 1989. Joel claimed he wrote it as a rejoinder to a Gen-Xer who thought nothing happened in the 1950s, and as a way for the high school dropout to fulfill a dream of being a history professor. Musically, Joel claimed it was inspired by the rise of Hip Hop. The song features guitars, synthesizers, drums, congas, and timbales, with the piano notably not the primary instrument. "we didn't start the fire, became Billy Joel's third and last career number one, topping the hot 100 in December 1989."
**I Go to Extremes (1990)** : The second single from "Storm Front," this track was the album's second top ten hit on the album rock chart and also a top ten hit on the Hot 100, peaking at number six. "I go to extremes... was its second top ten hit on the album rock chart and another top ten hit on the Hot 100, peaking at number six."
**And So It Goes (1990)** : This "Heart Breaking Ballad" was the third top 40 pop hit from "Storm Front," reaching number 37 on the Hot 100 and number five at Adult Contemporary Radio. Joel wrote the song back in 1983, basing it on an old English folk ballad. "The Heart Breaking Ballad and So It Goes, a song Joel wrote back in 1983 and based on an old English folk ballad."
**Shameless (1989)** : Buried as an album cut on "Storm Front," this "bluesy, twangy saloon ballad about abject infatuation" was considered the album's most uncharacteristic song, with Joel having no intention of releasing it as a single. However, Garth Brooks covered it in 1991, turning it into a country mega-hit that topped Billboard's Hot Country Songs chart for two weeks. Joel's original version subsequently became a minor hit on the adult contemporary chart. "Joel had no intention of releasing Shameless as a single but in 1991 rising country megastar Garth Brooks heard Shameless for what it was, a country song waiting to happen."
**Traveling Prayer** : Billy Joel had previously written and recorded songs with a country leaning, and this track in particular was notably covered by country artists. "the aforementioned Traveling Prayer was covered by country artists."
**The Ballad of Billy the Kid** : A deep cut from Billy Joel's earlier work, this song utilized Western cowboy tropes, demonstrating his prior explorations of country-influenced sounds. "another piano man deep cut, the ballad of Billy the Kid, played with western cowboy troops."
**The River of Dreams (1993)** : The irresistible lead single and title track from Joel's 1993 album, it reached number three on the Hot 100 in October 1993. Critics noted its allusions to Paul Simon's "Graceland" and African music fused with New York rock and roll, also invoking the doo-wop and R&B vocals of The Tokens' "The Lion Sleeps Tonight." Despite these influences, the song remained distinctly Billy Joel music. "Joel's latest single reached number three on the hot 100 in October of 1993 and that wasn't the most incredible chart feat."
**Lullaby (Good Night My Angel) (1993)** : This was the last single issued from "River of Dreams" and hinted at Joel's future musical direction. Originally an instrumental classical piece, Joel added lyrics to it as a literal lullaby for his daughter, Alexa Rae Joel. It was a modest hit, making the top 20 on the adult contemporary chart and number 77 on the Hot 100, but it served as a quiet coda to Joel's pop career as he decided to stop recording pop albums. "Lullaby became a literal Lullaby for his daughter Alexa Rae Joel one night after he was trying to reassure the girl before bed."
**This Night** : An album cut from "An Innocent Man," this track showcased Joel's earlier infusion of classical melodies into his pop songs, specifically borrowing the second movement of Ludwig von Beethoven's "Pathetique Sonata," which Joel credited in the LP's liner notes. "One track on an innocent man, the album cut this night borrowed the second movement of Ludwig von Beethoven's Pateteek Sonata."
**To Make You Feel My Love (1997)** : Written by Bob Dylan, Billy Joel was the first artist to record this song in 1997 for a greatest hits album, before it was later released by Dylan, Garth Brooks, and Adele. Joel's version was a modest hit, peaking halfway up the Hot 100 and reaching the top 10 on the adult contemporary chart. "Joel was actually the first to record this oft-covered track."
**Just the Way You Are (1977)** : A mega-hit from Joel's breakthrough 1977 album "The Stranger," this song is often offered as a choice alongside "Vienna" at his Madison Square Garden residency concerts, where fans frequently choose "Vienna" instead. "The first choice would be a song called Just the Way You Are."
**Vienna (1977)** : From Joel's 1977 album "The Stranger," "Vienna" was never released as a single and was not a chart hit, yet it is consistently chosen by fans over the mega-hit "Just the Way You Are" at his Madison Square Garden shows. Joel claims he wrote it for his German-born father, Helmut Joel, incorporating elements of Kurt Weill and German Cabaret, complete with an accordion solo on the record. "Joel has long claimed he wrote Vienna for his German-born father, Helmut Joel."

## curiosities
**"Piano Man" Moniker and Pastiche Style** : While known as the "Piano Man," Joel was also a "pastiche man," borrowing styles and melodic tropes from contemporary artists and hitmakers of his youth, demonstrating a talent for evoking specific song modes while writing original melodies. His three number one hits ("It's Still Rock and Roll to Me," "Tell Her About It," and "We Didn't Start the Fire") notably did not feature the piano as the primary instrument. "Yes, he was the piano man, but really Joel was the pastiche man, borrowing styles and melodic tropes, sometimes down to the same beat from both contemporary artists and hitmakers from his youth."
**Christie Brinkley Romance** : Billy Joel's new romance with supermodel Christie Brinkley, who would become his second wife, contributed to his positive mood during the creation of "An Innocent Man." "For starters, he was in a new romance, dating his future second wife, Supermodel Christy Brinkley."
**Ed Sullivan Show Homage** : The music video for "Tell Her About It" featured Billy Joel playing a singer performing on an imagined "Ed Sullivan show," embracing a kitschy retro aesthetic. "in the song's smash music video Joel plays a singer performing on the Ed Sullivan show."
**Kristi Brinkley's Music Video Cameo** : Billy Joel's then-girlfriend, Kristi Brinkley, made a cameo in the music video for "Uptown Girl," contributing to its MTV sensation status. "Thanks to a cameo by Joel's then girlfriend, Kristi Brinkley."
**Betty Wright "Clean Up Woman" Easter Egg** : The music video for Billy Joel's "Keeping the Faith" contains an Easter egg around 45 seconds into the clip; set in an imagined musical courtroom, Joel is seen spinning a vinyl 45 of Betty Wright's "Clean Up Woman," subtly revealing the inspiration for his song's guitar groove. "Billy sits at the defense table, spinning a vinyl single on his finger, and what 45 is it? A copy of Betty writes Clean Up Woman."
**Battles with Depression and Suicidal Ideation** : Billy Joel wrote the song "You're Only Human (Second Wind)" as a heartfelt plea to teenagers contemplating suicide, drawing from his own personal experiences battling depression and suicidal ideation. "a plea to teenagers contemplating suicide, from a songwriter who had battled depression and suicidal ideation himself."
**Broadway Showtunes Influence** : Much of Billy Joel's 1986 album "The Bridge" was pervaded by a "Broadway showtunes vibe," with songs like "Modern Woman" and "Big Man on Mulberry Street" displaying this characteristic. A decade and a half later, choreographer Twyla Tharp would create a jukebox musical, "Moving Out," based entirely on Joel's music. "If anything was interesting about Modern Woman, it was its Broadway showtunes vibe."
**Glasnost Cultural Outreach** : By 1986-1987, Billy Joel was a well-established cultural figure who represented America in the Soviet Union as part of Russian President Mikhail Gorbachev's cultural outreach effort known as Glasnost. "And represent America in the then Soviet Union, as part of Russian President Mikhail Gorbachev's cultural outreach effort known as Glasnost."
**First Western Rock Star in Soviet Union** : In the summer of 1987, Billy Joel made history as the first Western rock star to play in the Soviet Union under communism, performing a series of three concerts in Moscow and three in Leningrad. "He was the first Western rock star to play in the Soviet Union under communism."
**Lawsuit against Frank Weber** : Prior to his "Storm Front" album, Billy Joel experienced professional turmoil, including growing tired of producer Phil Ramone and uncovering fiscal malfeasance by his manager and former brother-in-law, Frank Weber, which led to a bitter lawsuit. "he uncovered fiscal malfeasance by his manager and former brother-in-law Frank Weber, leading to a bitter lawsuit."
**"We Didn't Start the Fire" Origin Story** : The origin of "We Didn't Start the Fire" is an oft-told, possibly apocryphal, anecdote where Joel claims he wrote it as a rejoinder to a Gen-Xer (sometimes identified as Sean O'Neal Lennon's 21-year-old friend) who believed nothing significant happened in the 1950s, prompting Joel to list historical events. He also claimed it was a mental exercise for a high school dropout's dream of being a history professor. "Joel claimed he wrote the song as a rejoinder to a gen-exer who, in a conversation with Boomer Billy, claimed that nothing much had happened in the 50s."
**Hip-Hop Influence on "We Didn't Start the Fire"** : Billy Joel claimed that the musical "bars" he used in "We Didn't Start the Fire" were inspired by the rise of Hip Hop, which was entering its "golden period" in 1988 and 1989. Even his most negative critics agreed with this comparison. "Musically, Joel has also claimed in certain interviews that the bars he's dropping in the song were inspired by the rise of Hip Hop, which in 1988 and 1989 was entering its golden period."
**"Old Man Yells at Cloud" Irony** : Despite being written by a baby boomer "ranting at a younger person" about historical ignorance, "We Didn't Start the Fire" became beloved by generations of young people who memorized its history lesson lyrics, mirroring "The Simpsons" "old man yells at cloud" trope. "It is quite literally to paraphrase the Simpsons, the old man yells at cloud of pop hits."
**Garth Brooks Covers "Shameless"** : In 1991, country megastar Garth Brooks recognized Billy Joel's album cut "Shameless" from "Storm Front" as a country song, recorded it Nashville-style for his album "Ropin' the Wind," and turned it into a two-week country chart-topper, also boosting Joel's original to a minor adult contemporary hit. "in 1991 rising country megastar Garth Brooks heard Shameless for what it was, a country song waiting to happen."
**Central Park Collaboration with Garth Brooks** : At Garth Brooks' heavily hyped 1997 Central Park concert, fellow New Yorker Billy Joel joined him on stage to perform "Shameless," the song Joel wrote and Brooks famously covered. "at Garth's heavily hyped 1997 concert in Central Park, famed New Yorker Billy Joel joined him on stage to perform the song that Joel had written and Brooks had made things."
**Musical Retirement from Pop** : After "River of Dreams," Joel made a conscious decision to stop writing and recording pop albums, feeling he had "said all I wanted to say with lyrics" and was wary of being a hitmaker into middle age, thus concluding his pop career with a final chart-topping album. "He would never write or record another pop album again."
**"Lullaby" for Daughter Alexa Rae** : Billy Joel initially wrote "Lullaby (Good Night My Angel)" as a standalone classical instrumental piece, but later added lyrics to turn it into a literal lullaby to reassure his daughter, Alexa Rae Joel, before bed. "Lullaby became a literal Lullaby for his daughter Alexa Rae Joel one night after he was trying to reassure the girl before bed."
**Feud with Elton John** : A series of blockbuster tours with Elton John in the 1990s and 2000s ended by 2010 due to a feud between the "piano men" over Elton's public remarks about Joel's health and sobriety following his rehab stints. "a feud between the piano men over Elton's impressions of Joel's health and sobriety after his rehab stints. He brought those tours to an end by 2010."
**Kennedy Center Honors** : In 2013, Billy Joel received the prestigious Kennedy Center Honors from President Obama, marking a significant renaissance in his public regard and solidifying his status as a cultural icon. "He received the Kennedy Center Honors from President Obama in 2013."
**Madison Square Garden Residency (2014-Present)** : In December 2013, Joel began an open-ended monthly residency at New York's Madison Square Garden, which he stated would continue as long as demand for tickets existed. By February 2020, just before the nationwide touring shutdown, he had played a record 73rd consecutive monthly show, often interspersing these with one-off stadium concerts. "Joel began a so-called residency at New York's Madison Square Garden, a series of monthly concerts at the arena that he says will continue as long as there is demand for tickets."
**Audience Polling at MSG Concerts** : During his Madison Square Garden residency, Joel charmingly polls the audience on which song they'd prefer to hear, often presenting a choice between a mega-hit like "Just the Way You Are" and a deep cut like "Vienna." Fans consistently choose "Vienna." "he will also take a moment to pull the audience on what song they would prefer to hear."
**"Vienna" Inspired by Father** : Billy Joel has long stated that he wrote the song "Vienna" for his German-born father, Helmut Joel. The composition incorporates elements of Kurt Weill and German Cabaret, reflecting its familial and cultural inspirations, and notably includes an accordion solo on the record. "Joel has long claimed he wrote Vienna for his German-born father, Helmut Joel."
