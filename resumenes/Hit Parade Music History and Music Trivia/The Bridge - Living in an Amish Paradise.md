## curiosities
**Fandom and Generational Discovery** : There's a common observation that people tend to discover Weird Al and consider the album released when they were around 12 years old to be his best, reflecting a strong sense of personal nostalgia. "every critics favorite Weird Al album, the best Weird Al album, is whatever was released when they were 12 years old."
**Collaborative Relationship with Nathan Rabin** : Weird Al personally reached out to Nathan Rabin via Twitter about eight years prior to 2012, choosing him to write his story, which resulted in the book `Weird Al The Book`. He also fact-checked and copy-edited Rabin's later book, `The Weird Accordion to Al`, due to being "mortified" by Rabin's grammar. "Al wrote that of all the writers in the world he had chosen me to tell his story."
**Career Evolution by Decade** : Weird Al's thematic obsessions have evolved significantly over the decades. In the 1980s, his main themes were television and food, as television was a dominant cultural force. In the 1990s, as he matured, his work became more calculating and opportunistic, focusing on movies like "Forrest Gump" and "Jurassic Park." By the 2000s, with the rise of the internet and YouTube supplanting MTV, his obsession shifted to computers, technology, and the internet, exploring how it makes lives "better and worse and a whole lot more ridiculous." "as culture has changed, Al has changed with it."
**Synthesis of Novelty Music Pioneers** : Al learned from earlier novelty artists like Spike Jones, Allan Sherman, Tom Lehrer, and Dr. Demento. He adopted the best aspects of their work and avoided their mistakes, such as Allan Sherman's carelessness with his career. He is described as a "super beast," or the "perfect novelty artist," having transcended the label itself. "So what he did was he kind of took all of these different influences and just kind of created this sort of super beast, you know, sort of like the perfect novel, T.R. to Sniper."
**Rare Non-Hit Parody** : Weird Al has only ever parodied a non-hit song once in his career, which was Mick Jagger's solo track "Ruthless People." This highlights Al's usual strategy of choosing widely popular songs for his parodies. "he's wholly really ever-paradied a non-hit once. And that was a song, Ruthless People, by Mick Jagger."
**Frank Zappa's Influence** : Al's work is seen to carry a similar "sense of outrageousness, button-pushing provocation" reminiscent of Frank Zappa. "Frank Zappa, I think there's that sense of outrageousness, button-pushing provocation."
**The Simpsons Guest Appearance** : Weird Al made at least one guest voice appearance on "The Simpsons," where it was revealed that Homer Simpson was a fan who sent Al his own terrible, food-related parody song ideas like "Living La Pita, Loka" or "another one by Stichress." This gag underscores the difficulty of crafting genuinely witty parody. "Did you ever get the parody songs I sent you? Yes. Which one was better? Living La Pita, Loka, or another one by Stichress? They were pretty much the same home."
**Strategic Career Management** : Weird Al is known for his incredible care, craft, and meticulousness in managing his career. He has a proven "formula for crafting albums" that allows him to remain relevant, popular, and funny, even in a field where most novelty acts are ephemeral. "Al has exited for with incredible care and craft and meticulousness."
**The "Mathimen Con hay and Dei Stinc and Fused" Observation** : Weird Al is noted for being in a unique position where he, as a 60-year-old artist, parodies pop stars who remain young, creating a dynamic where "he gets out and they stay the same age." This means he continually has to engage with new, potentially "obnoxious" pop songs to find parody angles. "he's like Mathimen Con hay and Dei Stinc and Fused, you know, where he gets out and they stay the same age."
**Rabin's Guilt Over Al's Grammar Correction** : Nathan Rabin humorously expresses guilt over the extensive time Weird Al spent correcting the grammar in his book `The Weird Accordion to Al`. Rabin fears that his "word crimes" might have prevented Al from recording his next masterpiece by diverting his creative energy to editing instead. "my fear is that he was going to take that time and like go to the woods and record his masterpiece. But instead, I'm going to fix this morons so yeah, I hope that I singlehandedly did not prevent him from releasing a comeback album."
