## songs
**Got a Job (1958) - The Miracles' Debut Answer Record** : The Miracles' first single, released in 1958 on the New York label End Records. A teenage Smokey Robinson and his group made it as a quickie answer record to The Silhouettes' chart-topper "Get a Job," interpolating that hit to ride its coattails. It got some airplay in Detroit but was a national flop, and it earned so little money that Robinson encouraged Berry Gordy to start his own label, which led directly to the birth of Tamla, Motown's first label. "Released on New York label end records, the miracles first single scored some airplay in Detroit, but was otherwise a national flop."
**Bad Girl (1959) - First Miracles Single on the Billboard Charts** : Produced by Berry Gordy and co-released by Motown and Chess Records, "Bad Girl" was the first Miracles single to reach the Billboard charts. It only reached No. 93 on the Hot 100 in October 1959, but it showed that The Miracles and Motown were gaining traction and set the group up for their breakthrough. "It only reached number 93 on the Hot 100 in October of 1959, but it indicated that the miracles and Motown were gaining traction."
**Way Over There - Dry Run for Shop Around** : Once Gordy saw that Robinson's band was poised for a hit, he began tinkering with their singles to get them onto the charts. He released "Way Over There" twice: first in an early arrangement without strings, and then in a final single version that got some regional airplay. In hindsight it reads as a dry run for "Shop Around." "Gordy released the single twice, first in an early arrangement without strings, and then in the final single version that scored some regional airplay."
**Shop Around (1960) - Motown's First Million-Seller, No. 2 on the Hot 100** : The breakthrough hit for the Detroit quintet and for Motown, released on Tamla, Berry Gordy's first Detroit imprint. It was Motown's first million-selling single and the first smash written by William "Smokey" Robinson Jr., co-written with Gordy. The lyric is about a mother's improbable advice to her son not to settle down too soon. The original "Detroit version" was bluesy, a bit sluggish and dominated by a thumping tambourine, with an eager but slightly hoarse Robinson vocal. It sold and got early airplay, but after only a couple of weeks on sale in fall 1960 Gordy decided the record should be peppier, with big sonics, a heavier bass line and brushed drums. He ordered Robinson and The Miracles into a studio at 3 a.m., and they rearranged the track overnight. The new version debuted on the Hot 100 just before Christmas 1960, topped the R&B chart in January 1961, and peaked at No. 2 on the Hot 100 in the week ending February 20, 1961. Lawrence Welk's "Calcutta," in its second and final week at No. 1, kept it from becoming the first pop No. 1 for both Robinson and Motown. "He ordered Robinson and the miracles into a studio at 3 in the morning and they completely rearranged the track overnight into a future smash."
**You've Really Got a Hold on Me - No. 8 Hit** : One of the 1960s Miracles recordings that most critics rank above "Shop Around." It peaked at No. 8. "including the number 8 hit you've really got a hold on me."
**Going to a Go-Go (1966) - Namesake of The Go-Go's** : This 1966 Miracles hit inspired The Go-Go's' name. The Go-Go's considered recording a cover of it. Charlotte Caffey spent a day listening to it, and its opening beat inspired her to write "We Got the Beat." "Caffey said she was inspired after spending the day listening to the miracle's 1966 hit Going to a Go-Go, a song that had inspired the Go-Go's name."
**I Second That Emotion - No. 4 Hit** : Another 1960s Miracles single that critics rate above "Shop Around." It peaked at No. 4. "The number 4 hit I second that emotion."
**The Tracks of My Tears - No. 16 Hit** : Described as perhaps The Miracles' all-time greatest recording, though it only reached No. 16 on the Hot 100. "perhaps their all-time greatest, the number 16 hit The Tracks of My Tears."
**The Tears of a Clown (1970) - First Miracles No. 1** : First recorded in 1967 and not originally issued as a single. A teenage Stevie Wonder co-wrote it and Robinson finished it. A British Motown executive pulled it from obscurity, and it topped the charts in both the UK and the US in late 1970. It was the only No. 1 pop hit of Smokey Robinson's career, either with The Miracles or solo, and it came almost a decade after "Shop Around" just missed the top spot. "The song, co-written by a teenage Stevie Wonder and finished by Robinson, was plucked from obscurity by a British Motown executive."
**Love Machine (1976) - Post-Smokey Disco No. 1** : Robinson left in 1971 and the group carried on as a quartet. Five years later they had a disco-driven No. 1 hit with "Love Machine," their last chart-topper. "After Robinson left in 1971, the group continued as a quartet, and five years later they scored a disco-driven number 1 hit called Love Machine."

## curiosities
**Originally Called The Matadors - First Act Signed to Tamla** : The Miracles were originally named The Matadors. They were the first act signed to Gordy's Tamla label, and Gordy was working with them even before he had a label, when he was still a song salesman and publisher releasing singles through other companies. "The miracles, originally known as the Matadors, were the first acts signed to Gordy's Tamilar label."
**A Decade-Long Wait for No. 1** : Just missing No. 1 in 1961 meant The Miracles waited the rest of the decade to top the Hot 100. They did not reach No. 1 until late 1970, with "The Tears of a Clown." "it took them all decade to finally top the charts."

## albums
**Beauty and the Beat (1981) - Debut Album, Slow Climb to No. 1** : The Go-Go's debut album included "Lust to Love," "Our Lips Are Sealed" and "We Got the Beat." "Our Lips Are Sealed" pushed it into the top 20 of the album chart, and "We Got the Beat" took it to No. 1 in its 32nd week, one of the slowest climbs to No. 1 in album chart history. It went double platinum. In spring 1982 it was No. 1 on the album chart while Joan Jett's album sat at No. 2. "the beauty and the beat album had risen to the top in its 30 second week, one of the slowest climbs to number 1 in album chart history."

## songs
**Lust to Love - Early Demo** : Co-written by lead guitarist Charlotte Caffey and rhythm guitarist Jane Wiedlin, it ended up on "Beauty and the Beat." The early demo shows the band's ties to the LA punk scene and to the sound that was becoming new wave. "That's a demo version of Lust to Love, a song co-written by lead guitarist Charlotte Caffey and Riven guitarist Jane Leedlin."
**Our Lips Are Sealed (1981) - Sleeper Hit Built From a Terry Hall Letter** : The lead single, written by Jane Wiedlin, who set music to a letter from her paramour Terry Hall of The Specials. It only reached No. 20 on the Hot 100 in December 1981 but stayed on the chart for 30 weeks, pushing the album into the top 20. It was an early MTV hit. "Our Lips are sealed only reached number 20 on the Hot 100 in December 1981, but it was a sleeper hit, spending a remarkable 30 weeks on the chart."
**We Got the Beat (1980/1982) - Biggest Hit, Three Weeks at No. 2** : Written by Charlotte Caffey after a day of listening to The Miracles' "Going to a Go-Go," whose opening beat inspired it. It sounded more like punk than Smokey Robinson but was meant for dancing from the start. The original 1980 version came out as a single on the UK pub/punk label Stiff Records. It earned the band underground credibility in the UK and became a US dance hit as an import, reaching No. 35 on Billboard's Club Play Chart in summer 1980. IRS later sent MTV a live performance clip that got heavy rotation. The song entered the top 10 by early March 1982 and peaked at No. 2 for three weeks in April 1982 (week ending April 10, 1982), held back by Joan Jett's "I Love Rock 'n' Roll." "Already a hit in clubs and on MTV, We Got the Beat became a chart inevitability."
**Vacation (1982) - Final Top 10 Hit** : The immediate 1982 follow-up to "We Got the Beat" and the band's only other top 10 hit. "The Go-Go's followed We Got the Beat with just one more top ten hit. Their immediate 1982 follow-up vacation."

## curiosities
**Punk Credibility Questioned** : The band came out of the late-70s LA punk scene, but its punk credentials were doubted from the start. People called the material either too sloppy or too poppy, and the band's ambition stood out at a time when careerism in punk was uncool. "Depending on whom you talk to, the lady's material either seemed too sloppy or too poppy."
**Wanted a Major, Signed to IRS** : The band wanted a major-label deal and was reportedly disappointed to end up on IRS Records, because majors didn't know how to promote an all-female rock band. IRS would later sign R.E.M. "they wound up on IRS records, the soon-to-be legendary independent label that would later sign REM."
**MTV Breakthrough** : The launch of MTV helped establish the band. IRS made sure to supply the channel with clips of the telegenic group. "IRS records made sure to service the music video channel with clips of the Telegenic group."
**Spring 1982 - Go-Go's and Joan Jett Hold the Top Two** : For three weeks in spring 1982, Joan Jett and The Go-Go's held the top two spots on the Hot 100, two punk-adjacent LA veterans leading both the singles and album charts. In the 35 years after, women have led the Hot 100 many times, but never a pair of female-led rock bands like this. "never a pair of female lead rock bands like this."
**Short Career and Breakup by 1984** : After several more top 40 hits, the band had broken up by 1984. Carlisle and Wiedlin both went solo. "The Go-Go's career was a firework. It burned bright for a very short period."
**Influence on Female Rock Acts** : They opened radio in the 1980s to female rock acts coming from punk and new wave, from The Bangles to Cyndi Lauper. With Jett, they cleared space in the mainstream for women in rock before 90s riot grrrl. "They made the radio safe in the 1980s for female rock acts emerging from the punk and new wave scenes."

## songs
**A Moment Like This (2002) - Debut No. 1 and Coronation Song** : Clarkson's first single, a power ballad, topped the Hot 100 in October 2002. She sang it live on TV within seconds of winning season one of American Idol in summer 2002. Co-finalist Justin Guarini would have released it if he had won. Swedish songwriter Jörgen Elofsson, part of Max Martin's team, co-wrote it. Its success mainly showed that Idol was a phenomenon, not yet that Clarkson would become a star. "The song shot to number one, proving mostly that American Idol was a pop culture phenomenon, but not necessarily that the Winsome Clarkson was destined for stardom."
**Since U Been Gone (2004) - No. 2 Hit and Pop's Trojan Horse** : Max Martin and Dr. Luke wrote it as a pop rewrite of the Yeah Yeah Yeahs' "Maps," borrowing its chugging guitar and G major chord, so the opening seconds sound like straight indie rock. It was first offered to Pink, who passed. Hilary Duff tried it but couldn't hit the high notes. Then it went to Clarkson, whose rasp, soulful trills and power vocals it used fully. Rock radio considered it too pop, but critics praised its eclecticism. It reached No. 2 in the week ending April 9, 2005, behind 50 Cent's "Candy Shop," and stayed on the Hot 100 twice as long as "Candy Shop." It showed Clarkson was more than a talent-show novelty, became the turning point back toward pure pop on the Hot 100, and remains the most consistent song in her live sets. "Since you've been gone, was pop's Trojan Horse, smuggling indie rock production elements into a big pop hit."

## curiosities
**First American Idol Winner** : The Fort Worth, Texas singer won the first season of American Idol in summer 2002, a show that became probably the highest-rated US TV show of the decade. Unlike the other acts in this story, her career started with a No. 1. "Kelly Clarkson's career actually began with a chart-topper."
**Later No. 1s in 2009 and 2012** : Clarkson kept having hits over the next decade and returned to No. 1 in 2009 and again in 2012. "She even managed to return to the number one spot, both in 2009. And again in 2012."

## curiosities
**Cheiron Studio, Stockholm** : Since the late 1990s Martin has led a Swedish collective of producers and songwriters, including Jörgen Elofsson and Karl Johan Schuster (Shellback). In the 90s it was based at the Stockholm studio Cheiron, whose sound drove late-90s boy band and teen pop and the TRL era. From 1997 to 2000 the team wrote more than a dozen US top 40 hits for Celine Dion, Robyn, the Backstreet Boys and NSYNC. "From 1997 to 2000, Martin and his stable of Scandinavian pop scientists wrote more than a dozen US top 40 hits."
**Out of Favor After 2001 and Later Comeback** : As America went into recession in 2001 and teen pop faded, Martin's team fell out of favor at US radio. After "Since U Been Gone," they reworked their sound for millennial pop with hits for Katy Perry and Pink, and brought Britney Spears back to No. 1. "Max Martin and his stable of Stockholm studio craftsman who had retooled their sound for a new age of Millennial Pop."
