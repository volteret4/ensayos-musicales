# genre - Yacht Rock
  ## genres
  Smooth music
  Pop
  Soft Rock
  Crossover music
  Yacht Soul
  ## curiosities
  **Origin and Definition (1976-1984)** : Yacht Rock, a term coined by a 2005 Web TV series, describes a genre of "really smooth music" that dominated radio airwaves from approximately 1976 to 1984. It offered a definitive name to a type of music that critics and listeners had struggled to classify, replacing terms like jazz rock, Revlon Rock, West Coast, Soft Rock, or Retail Rock. "From 1976 to 1984, the radio airwaves were dominated by really smooth music, also known as Yacht Rock."
  **Viral Spread and Cultural Impact** : The "Yacht Rock" web series, launched in 2005, became an early viral internet phenomenon on YouTube due to its strong storylines, characters, and cliffhanger endings, winning the Channel 101 competition. The term itself gained traction because it was evocative, Google-friendly, and provided a clear visual style (captain's hats, Hawaiian shirts) that made it accessible for tribute bands and fans to adopt. "I think the term caught on because it defined a kind of music that people had been struggling to define ever since it was being produced."
  **Evolution of Sound** : Early Yacht Rock music tended to sound more like Boz Scaggs, while later Yacht Rock, especially after 1979-1980, was heavily influenced by the sound of Michael McDonald, the "What a Fool Believes" riff, and the production of Quincy Jones and Rod Temperton. This later phase often featured the "doobie bounce" and a distinct "yacht soul strain." "Yeah, earlier, yeah, rock tends to sound more like buzz, skags than Michael McDonald, like the first couple years and to up before what a full belief comes out."
  **Misinterpretations and Visuals** : The literal interpretation of "Yacht Rock" led some to believe the music had to be about boats or seafaring, mistakenly including artists like Jimmy Buffett. The web series' portrayal of characters like Coco Goldstein, dressed in captain's hats and Hawaiian shirts, became an accessible and cheap "fashion template" for tribute bands and audiences, further popularizing the visual stereotype. "It was much easier to put on a captain's hat and listen to Brandy by looking glass and go, I'm doing Yacht Rock."
  **Artistic Value and Permission to Enjoy** : Initially dismissed by traditional rock critics who valued "rebellious spirit," "high energy," and a "raw" sound, Yacht Rock's comedic portrayal in the web series shifted perception, giving people "permission" to openly enjoy the "very well crafted music." Many who approached it ironically eventually developed a genuine appreciation for its quality. "And making a comedy show out of it not only starts to change the perception around what this music is, but it's also at the same time sort of giving people permission."
  **R&B and Jazz Roots (Yacht Soul)** : The core of Yacht Rock, particularly the "yacht soul strain," is deeply rooted in Black R&B and jazz. Many foundational "white Yacht Rock guys" like Kenny Loggins, Michael McDonald, Steely Dan, and Toto grew up listening to these genres, and the same session musicians often played on both "white pop records" and R&B records by Black artists around 1979-1980, making it true "crossover music." "First of all, all the guys, all the white Yacht Rock guys that you first think of, Kenny Loggins, Michael McDonald, Steely Dan, all growing up, listen to Black R&B, Black jazz."
  **Artist Reactions to Label** : Reactions from artists to the Yacht Rock label vary. Michael McDonald is "delighted," Kenny Loggins is "in the middle," and Boz Scaggs is "super annoyed," disliking the pigeonholing but still performing. Steely Dan fans particularly object to the label due to its perceived kitsch. "On a scale from Lugins to McDonald. Rate how annoyed to delighted the Yacht Rockers are by the genre that you guys invented for them."
  **Book Project by Creators** : The creators of the Yacht Rock series and podcast, JD Riznar, Dave Lyons, and Hollywood Steve Huey, are writing an "ultimate, all-in-all book on Yacht Rock" with Little Brown UK. Their ongoing research reinforces their theory about the genre, tracing its narrative, the careers of its artists, and how they shaped the sound. "So now we're writing like the ultimate, like, and I'll be all book on Yacht Rock."

# genre - Yacht Soul
  ## curiosities

Invented by a fictional character named Gene Yachtsky, it's described as "completely arbitrary, but it's not," providing a serious yet ironic study of the genre's boundaries. "On the podcast they also created the Yacht Skiscale, two scrupulously and scientifically rate weather songs are Yacht or Yacht."

This approach helped lay the groundwork for a broader appreciation of diverse music, or the "pop-demist movement." "And their whole philosophy from the beginning is we're going to cover all music."

"We get story ideas by looking at liner notes and see who played on what."
