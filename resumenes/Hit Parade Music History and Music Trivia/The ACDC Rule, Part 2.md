# artist - ACDC
## curiosities
**ACDC Rule Originators** : The band ACDC is the namesake for the "ACDC rule," a concept explaining why follow-up albums often open bigger or chart higher than their more beloved predecessors. This is because their first ever number one LP was not as well-remembered or strong-selling as its predecessor, *Back in Black*. "I named it after ACDC, a band whose first ever number 1 LP was not as well-remembered or strong-selling as its predecessor back in black."

# artist - Herb Alpert and the Tijuana Brass
## genres
Instrumental Pop
## albums
**Wipped Cream, and Other Delights (1965)** : This album, released in 1965, gained significant popularity due to its famous LP jacket featuring a seemingly nude model buried in whipped cream up to her chest, which contributed to its sales of millions of copies. Its success served as a foundation for the band's subsequent chart-topping albums. "The album's sexy cover of a seemingly nude model buried in Wipped Cream up to her chest helped move millions of copies of Alperts LP."
**What Now My Love** : This album was one of four chart-topping LPs released by Herb Alpert and the Tijuana Brass between 1966 and 1968, all of which benefited from the success of their earlier hit, *Wipped Cream and Other Delights*. Notably, *What Now My Love* spent one more week at number one than *Wipped Cream and Other Delights*. "One of these later Alperts LP's, what now my love, actually spent one more week at number one, then Wipped Cream and Other Delights did."
## songs
**Spanish Flea** : This hit instrumental bop by Herb Alpert was later adopted as the memorable theme music for the popular television show *The Dating Game*. Its catchiness contributed to the band's widespread recognition. "Alperts hit instrumental bops like Spanish Flea, later the theme to TV's The Dating Game, didn't hurt."
## curiosities
**ACDC Rule Application** : Herb Alpert and the Tijuana Brass broadly fit the parameters of the ACDC rule because, despite later albums achieving number one status and even longer chart runs, none are as well-remembered as their breakthrough album, *Wipped Cream and Other Delights*. "So, Herb Alperts does broadly fit the parameters of the ACDC role."

# artist - Jimi Hendrix
## albums
**Are You Experienced? (1967) - Debut Album** : Released in 1967, this LP coincided with Jimi Hendrix's searing performance at the Monterey Pop Festival, which transformed him into a cultural phenomenon. It took several months to crack Billboard's top ten and over a year to reach its eventual peak of number five in the fall of 1968. Despite not hitting number one, it is his most acclaimed and best-selling album, certified quadruple platinum, outselling his two follow-ups by approximately four times. "Are you experienced took several months to crack Billboard's top ten, and more than a year to reach its eventual peak of number five in the fall of 1968?"
**Axis: Bold as Love (Early 1968)** : This album was released in early 1968, following *Are You Experienced?*, and achieved a peak position of number three on the charts. "By that time, Hendrix had also released two other albums, Axis Bold is Love, a number three hit in early 1968."
**Electric Ladyland (1968) - Only Number One Album** : Released in 1968, this album was the one that finally brought Jimi Hendrix to the top of the chart, making it his only number one LP. It notably featured his only top 40 hit, a cover of Bob Dylan's "All Along The Watchtower." "By that time, Hendrix had also released two other albums, Axis Bold is Love, a number three hit in early 1968, and the album that would finally bring him to the top of the chart, Electric Ladyland."
## songs
**All Along The Watchtower (1968)** : This song is Jimi Hendrix's cover of a Bob Dylan track, featured on his album *Electric Ladyland*, and became his only top 40 hit. "It included Hendrix's only top 40 hit, his cover of Bob Dylan's All Along The Way."
## concerts
**Monterey Pop Festival** : Jimi Hendrix's electrifying performance at this 1967 festival was a pivotal moment, instrumental in making him a cultural phenomenon and boosting the initial success of his debut album, *Are You Experienced?* "The 1967 LP that came out just as his searing performance at the Monterey Pop Festival made him a cultural phenomenon."
## curiosities
**ACDC Rule Application** : Jimi Hendrix's career provides a "pretty good example" of the ACDC rule; while his debut *Are You Experienced?* is his best-seller and most acclaimed, its sustained success and long chart run arguably set the stage for *Electric Ladyland* to become his only chart-topping album. "Arguably, over its long run on the charts, Are you experienced set up Electric Ladyland for its eventual chart-topping success? A pretty good example of the ACDC rule in effect."

# artist - Carole King
## albums
**Tapestry (1971) - Breakthrough Album** : This album, released in the spring of 1971, was a massive success, spending 15 weeks at number one and yielding multiple hit songs, including the chart-topping double-A-sided single "It's Too Late" and "I Feel the Earth Move." It earned a Grammy for Album of the Year and achieved U.S. sales of 10 million copies, making it America's biggest selling album of all time for much of the 1970s until surpassed by the *Saturday Night Fever Soundtrack*. Despite its eventual triumph, King was initially unproven as a performer, and the album debuted at number 79 on Billboard, which even misspelled her name, taking about a dozen weeks to finally reach number one. "If you know anything about Carol Keen, surely you know her album Tapestry was a smash. 15 weeks at number one, multiple hit songs, including a chart-topping double-a-sided single, It's Too Late, and I Feel the Earth Move, the Grammy for album of the Year, and eventual sales in the U.S. alone of 10 million copies."
**Music (1971) - Instant Blockbuster** : This album was recorded in the late summer of 1971 while *Tapestry* was still enjoying its long run at the top, using the same studio, producer, and many of the same musicians, and featuring a similar "homey domestic vibe" on its cover. Released on the last day of November 1971, it debuted on the chart just a week and a half later, immediately shooting into the top ten and reaching number one for the week ending January 1, 1972, driven by blockbuster sales just before Christmas of 1971. Reportedly, *Music* sold an astounding 1.3 million copies on its first day, making it her second number one album that year, with "Sweet Seasons" as its only single and an eventual number nine hit. "What is less well-remembered decades later is that Carol Keen scored two number one albums in 1971, and the second one opened much, much bigger than Tapestry."
## songs
**It's Too Late (1971)** : This song formed one half of a chart-topping double-A-sided single, prominently featured on Carole King's smash album *Tapestry*. "multiple hit songs, including a chart-topping double-a-sided single, It's Too Late, and I Feel the Earth Move."
**I Feel the Earth Move (1971)** : This song was the other half of a chart-topping double-A-sided single, also featured on Carole King's monumental album *Tapestry*. "multiple hit songs, including a chart-topping double-a-sided single, It's Too Late, and I Feel the Earth Move."
**Sweet Seasons (1971)** : This was the sole single released from Carole King's 1971 album *Music*, achieving success as an eventual number nine hit. "This song, Sweet Seasons, was its only single, and eventual number nine hit."
## curiosities
**ACDC Rule Application** : Carole King's career exemplifies an additional dimension of the ACDC rule, where a slow-building chart-topper like *Tapestry* can set the stage for an explosive sequel like *Music*, which debuted with unprecedented sales of 1.3 million copies on its first day. "This is an additional dimension to my ACDC rule. Number one albums, even by the same superstar, are not created equal. A slow-building chart-topper might lead to an explosive sequel."
**Billboard Name Misspelling** : Upon the release of *Tapestry* in 1971, when Carole King was still unproven as a performer, Billboard misspelled her first name as "Carol" (lacking its closing 'E') in its chart debut at number 79. "Billboard misspelled King's name, Carol lacked its closing E."

# artist - Jethro Tull
## members

## genres

## albums
**Aqualung (Fourth studio album)** : This album marked Jethro Tull's breakthrough in America, becoming their fourth studio LP and their first to crack the US Top 10, peaking at number seven in about a month. Its title track became an album-oriented rock radio standard, famous for its memorable guitar riff. *Aqualung* remains Jethro Tull's top global seller and is certified triple platinum in America, building on a US following cultivated through touring and two prior LPs that reached the Top 20. "Jethru Tull finally cracked the US top 10 with their fourth studio album, Aquila, whose title track became an album-oriented rock radio standard thanks to one very memorable guitar riff."
**Thick as a Brick (1972) - Concept Album** : This 1972 LP was innovatively conceived and recorded as a single 44-minute piece of music, titled simply "Thick as a Brick," and divided into two parts to fit the sides of a vinyl LP, with no individually titled songs. It fully embraced progressive rock rather than conventional album rock, and in an "inside joke," frontman Ian Anderson claimed to have co-written it with a fictitious eight-year-old schoolboy named Gerald Bostock. Despite this "affected anti-commercialism," and thanks to the fanbase built by *Aqualung*, the album hit number one on the album chart in just three weeks. "Thick as a brick, Jethru Tull's 1972 LP contains no individually titled songs. It was conceived and recorded as one 44-minute piece of music, titled Simply Thick as a Brick, and broken into two parts to accommodate the sides of a vinyl LP."
**A Passion Play (1973) - Concept Album** : Following the successful "geeky gambit" of *Thick as a Brick*, this 1973 album also consisted of one long track, broken into two LP sides, and similarly topped the album chart. This solidified Jethro Tull's brand as "heady, mystical, but uncompromising Prague rockers," for which consumers rewarded them. "A passion play was also one long track, broken into two LP sides, and it two topped the album chart in 1973."
## curiosities
**Name Origin** : The band Jethro Tull is named after an 18th-century British agriculturalist who is credited with inventing the seed drill. "Jethru Tull, who invented the seed drill, broke fairly quickly in their native UK in the late 60s."
**Gerald Bostock Hoax** : For their 1972 album *Thick as a Brick*, frontman Ian Anderson created an "inside joke" by claiming to have co-written the LP with a fictitious eight-year-old schoolboy named Gerald Bostock, an act that highlighted the album's deliberately anti-commercial nature. "in a kind of inside joke, Ian Anderson claimed to have co-written the LP with a fictitious eight-year-old schoolboy named Gerald Boostock."
**Anchorman Reference** : The distinctive guitar riff from the title track of Jethro Tull's album *Aqualung* achieved popular culture recognition when it was famously incorporated into the 2004 Will Ferrell comedy *Anchorman*, during Ron Burgundy's "epic jam on Jazz flute." "If you're too young to remember Jethru Tull firsthand, but you're old enough to have seen the 2004 Will Ferrell comedy, Anchorman, it's the riff that 70s-swinger Ron Burgundy drops into the middle of his epic jam on Jazz flute."
**ACDC Rule Application** : Jethro Tull's career demonstrates the ACDC rule, as their two American number one albums, *Thick as a Brick* and *A Passion Play*, are "considerably weirder" and less frequently heard on classic rock stations than *Aqualung*, their top global seller that built their American fanbase. "But Jethru Tull have two American number one albums, and both of them are considerably weirder than Aquila."

## albums
**T for the Tillerman** : This album was Cat Stevens' first US top 10 album, reaching number eight in April 1971. Its success established his introspective folkpop style in America and prompted him to quickly record a similarly styled follow-up. "Wild World came from T for the Tillerman, Kat Stevens' first US top 10 album. It reached number 8 in April 1971."
**Teaser and the Firecat (1971) - Hit-packed Companion** : Released just six months after its predecessor *T for the Tillerman* peaked, this album featured a whimsical cartoon-like cover and gentle folkpop melodies, consciously designed to look and sound like a companion album. It was packed with hits, including "Peace Train" (a number 7 hit) and "Morning Has Broken" (a number 6 hit, an adaptation of an early 20th-century Christian hymn), helping it reach number 2 on Billboard's album chart in December 1971. This album is certified triple platinum. "Peace Train came from teaser and the firecat, released just six months after its predecessor peaked. Like T for the Tillerman, teaser and the firecat had a whimsical cartoon-like cover and gentle folkpop melodies."
**Catch Bull at Four (1972) - Only Number One Album** : Released in November 1972, this album topped the chart, becoming Cat Stevens' only number one LP, despite its material being described as pleasant but "not as memorable" as its predecessors. It was named after one of Buddhism's ten bulls of Zen and featured Asian imagery on its LP. Its piano-based song "Sitting" stalled at number 16 on the Hot 100, and another single, "Can't Keep It In," did not chart at all. By 1972, Cat Stevens had reportedly run out of his backlog of folk pop material and wrote these songs "from the road." It ultimately sold about one-third as much as *T for the Tillerman* and *Teaser and the Firecat*. "But Katz Stevens was clearly on an unstoppable hot streak. Katz Bull at 4 topped the album chart anyway, his only No. 1 album in November 1972."

Katz Bull at 4, The Chart Topper, has sold about one third as much."

**The ACDC Rule - Album Sales Phenomenon** : This rule describes a phenomenon where a highly successful, often slow-growing, predecessor album drives massive first-week sales for its follow-up, even if the follow-up's long-term sales or hit singles are less significant. It often means a slower-growing predecessor ends up being the better overall seller despite the successor's explosive debut. The name stems from a specific sales pattern observed where about half of the prior album's buyers would show up in the first week to buy the next. "This was especially ACDC rule like in as much as about half as many people who bought M&M's prior disc showed up in the first week to buy the second CD."
**ACDC Rule's Persistence into the Digital Era** : Even as the music industry transitioned into the digital era, the "ACDC rule" continued to influence the opening performance and sales patterns of major albums. "Even into the digital era, the ACDC rule was shaping the way major albums opened."
**Decline of the ACDC Rule in the 2020s** : Instances of the "ACDC rule" became rarer in the 2020s because the rule is driven by traditional album sales, whereas the Billboard 200 album chart is now predominantly influenced by streaming, leading to the disappearance of slow-growing sleeper hit albums. "instances of the ACDC rule are becoming more rare."
