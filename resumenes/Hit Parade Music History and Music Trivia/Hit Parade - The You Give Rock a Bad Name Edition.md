## albums
**Bon Jovi (1984) - Self-Titled Debut** : The band's self-titled debut album entered the Billboard album chart in February 1984. Its lead single "Runaway" emerged slowly and reached the Top 40 in April 1984. After that the band would not crack the upper reaches of the Hot 100 for another two and a half years. "The self-titled Bonjovie album debuted on the Billboard album chart in February 1984."
**7800° Fahrenheit (1985) - Second Album** : By 1985 Bon Jovi had released their second album, and none of its singles connected at Top 40 radio. The title refers to the supposed melting point of rock. The first single, "Only Lonely," peaked at No. 54 in spring 1985. The pop charts were dominated by Madonna, Wham! and Tears for Fears at the time, and radio programmers were not interested in the band. "that temperature is the supposed melting point for rock."
**Slippery When Wet (1986) - Third Album and Commercial Breakthrough** : Songwriter Desmond Child shaped the album's sound. Its final 10-song lineup was chosen by surveying New York-area teens about which songs they liked best from a pool of dozens. The band dropped the original cover, a photo of a buxom woman in a wet yellow T-shirt, and replaced it with the album's title written with a finger on a wet black garbage bag. It first topped the album chart in October 1986, a couple of weeks before "You Give Love a Bad Name" topped the Hot 100. Billboard named it the top album of 1987, beating new releases that year by Michael Jackson, Whitney Houston, U2 and Bruce Springsteen. It was certified eight times platinum by the end of 1987 and reached 12 million US sales by the mid-90s. "Slippery beat them all, and was certified Octopple Platinum by the end of 1987."
**New Jersey (1988) - Fourth Album** : The follow-up to Slippery When Wet reached No. 1 in its second week of release and spun off Top 10 hits. It was certified five times platinum by mid-1989. Even so, Guns N' Roses' Appetite for Destruction and Def Leppard's Hysteria both outsold it by mid-1989. "New Jersey, Bon Jovi's fourth album, reached number one on the charts in its second week of release"
**Keep the Faith (1992) - Fifth Album and First Post-Grunge Test** : Released in fall 1992, this was the band's first test in the post-Nirvana rock landscape. It debuted and peaked at No. 5, a respectable result but a big drop from the chart-topping success of their previous two albums. It came out the same month Madonna's Erotica broke her streak of No. 1 albums. The ballad "Bed of Roses" revived it, and after two years it went double platinum. "It was a rough month for 80s megastars trying to keep up with the 90s."
**This House Is Not for Sale (2016) - 2018 Chart-Topping Re-Entry** : In 2018 Bon Jovi launched another leg of their tour and offered this 2016 album as a free download to fans buying concert tickets. Redeemed downloads counted toward the Billboard charts. In early March, as the leg began, more than 120,000 fans redeemed the coupon, and the album re-entered the Billboard 200 at No. 1. "A shady but perfectly legal tactic for scoring a chart topping album in the late tens."

## songs
**Runaway (1984) - Debut Single** : Jon Bon Jovi first recorded the song in 1982 with a completely different, unnamed group of musicians he called the All-Star Review. A New York Top 40 station played it as a single in 1983, which led Mercury Records to sign him. The song took about 18 months to become a hit. It reached the national Top 40 in April 1984 but stayed there only one week. Casey Kasem counted it down on American Top 40 and struggled to pronounce Richie Sambora's surname. "Runaway took about 18 months to become a hit."
**Only Lonely (1985) - Lead Single from 7800° Fahrenheit** : This was the first single from the band's second album. It peaked at No. 54 in spring 1985. "the album's first single, Only Lonely, peaked at number 54 in the spring of 1985."
**You Give Love a Bad Name (1986) - First No. 1 Hit** : The song hit No. 1 on the Hot 100 for the week ending November 29, 1986, and stayed there one week. It marked a shift in how metal-adjacent rock was received on the charts and radio, and signalled that late-80s pop would move toward guitars. Desmond Child co-wrote it as a "ruthlessly effective radio smash" that opens straight on the chorus, anticipating the streaming-era habit of putting the hook first. A year later Belinda Carlisle's "Heaven Is a Place on Earth" copied its structure, and Max Martin's boy-band hits later echoed its explosive opening. "didn't waste a second of your time waiting for the hook."
**Livin' on a Prayer (1987) - Talk Box Anthem** : This Desmond Child composition entered the Hot 100 just two weeks after "Bad Name" left No. 1. Child, Jon Bon Jovi and Richie Sambora co-wrote it as a Springsteen-lite blue-collar anthem. It revived the talk box, the effect Peter Frampton made famous in 1976. It reached No. 1 in February 1987 and, unlike "Bad Name," stayed on top for four weeks. "opens with what is now probably the most famous talk box hook in pop history."
**I'll Be There for You (1989) - Ballad Chart-Topper** : Harder bands like Metallica and Skid Row were pushing metal in a more aggressive direction at the time, and this ballad sounded close to easy listening by comparison. It still topped the Hot 100 in spring 1989. "might as well have been easy listening, but the song topped the Hot 100 in the spring of 1989 anyway."
**Keep the Faith (1992) - Underperforming Title Track** : The title track of the fifth album was the band's first single of the grunge era. It peaked at only No. 29 on the Hot 100. "It did not go well."
**Bed of Roses (1993) - Comeback Power Ballad** : After "Keep the Faith" flopped, the band regrouped with this love song. It took them back into the Top 10 by March 1993. "By March of 1993, Bon Jovi had managed to sneak back into the top 10 with the Gushy Love Song, Bed of Roses."
**Always (1994) - Longest-Charting Hit** : This mega-ballad peaked at No. 4 on the Hot 100 in January 1995, when every other 80s metal band had fallen off the radio. It spent 32 weeks on the Hot 100, longer than any of their 80s hits, and was their last Top 5 hit. The band's staying power was such that the cartoon metalhead Beavis defended them to his friend. "Always, Bon Jovi's last top 5 hit spent a stunning 32 weeks on the Hot 100, longer than any of their 80s hits."
**It's My Life (2000) - Max Martin Collaboration** : The band recorded this song with Max Martin at the turn of the millennium. It peaked at only No. 33 on the Hot 100 but became a lasting hit, appearing regularly in their live sets and ranking as their most-viewed song of the YouTube era. Richie Sambora plays talk box on it as a homage to "Livin' on a Prayer." In the early 2000s a fan mashed it up with the Backstreet Boys' 1999 Max Martin hit "Larger Than Life" because the two songs are structurally so similar. "It's My Life has proven a long-lasting hit for Bon Jovi, regularly making their live set lists and ranking as the group's most viewed hit of the YouTube era."

## curiosities
**Named After Its Leader, Van Halen Style** : Band manager Doc McGhee suggested naming the group after its leader, as Van Halen had done. Jon's Italian surname Bongiovi became the band name Bon Jovi. "Band manager Don McGee recommended naming the new group Van Halen style after its leader"
**2018 Rock and Roll Hall of Fame Induction** : Bon Jovi were inducted in the 2018 class alongside Sister Rosetta Tharpe, the Moody Blues, Nina Simone, Dire Straits and the Cars. They were the most controversial of that year's nominees, but they had outgrossed all the other inductees, possibly even combined once live tour grosses are counted. "the most controversial of this year's nominees"
**Breakthrough With Coattails** : Van Halen's "Jump" had no followers, but Bon Jovi's 1986–87 breakthrough opened Top 40 radio to pop metal in general. Europe, Cinderella, Poison, Mötley Crüe, Whitesnake and later 40-something rockers such as Ozzy Osbourne, Robert Plant, Kiss and Alice Cooper all benefited. "what distinguished Bon Jovi's 86-87 breakthrough was it had coattails."
**Redefining the Sound of Pop** : By the end of 1987 the Bon Jovi formula had spread into pure pop. Examples include Belinda Carlisle's "Heaven Is a Place on Earth" and power-ballad drama in songs by Cher and dance diva Taylor Dayne. "Bon Jovi's music had redefined the middle of the road."
**Staying Afloat on Ballads in the 90s** : Like Madonna in the first half of the 90s, Bon Jovi survived the grunge era by focusing on ballads. "Bon Jovi stayed afloat by focusing on ballads."
**Chart Longevity After 1992** : The band never reached the singles Top 10 again after the mid-90s. In the 25 years after Keep the Faith, however, they scored nine more Top 10 albums, and their last four albums all debuted at No. 1. "Their last 4 discs have all actually debuted on the charts at number 1."
**Four Decades as a Chart Force** : Bon Jovi have dominated the Billboard charts for most of the 32 years since 1986. They survived the change in tastes in the 1990s and are well into their fourth decade as chart-toppers. "they have proved resilient, irrepressible, unkillable."

**Critical Dismissal vs. Commercial Success** : Edgier bands like Guns N' Roses and Metallica made it easier for critics to embrace metal and dismiss Bon Jovi. Jon could ignore that criticism while New Jersey was going five times platinum. "the public, not the critics, were going to have to determine on their own that hair metal was terminally uncool."

## albums
**Van Halen (1978) - Self-Titled Debut / "Van Halen I"** : The 1978 debut by the four-man Pasadena band is credited with starting the hair metal 80s in earnest, and it shaped Bon Jovi and 80s rock generally. On it Eddie Van Halen essentially invented a whole genre of post-blues, hammer-on, fast, pyrotechnic metal guitar. The album entered the Billboard album chart the same week its lead single entered the Top 40, in March 1978. "Van Halen would be a formative text for not only Bon Jovi but all of 80s Rock in more ways than one."
**1984 (1984) - MCMLXXXIV** : The album's title appears on its cover in Roman numerals. It opened with "Jump," which gave metal its first No. 1 single. Little else on the album followed the synth-pop approach, and none of its three follow-up singles reached the pop Top 10. "None of its three follow-up singles cracked the pop top 10."

## songs
**You Really Got Me (1978) - Kinks Cover and Lead Single** : This remake of the Kinks' 1964 No. 7 hit was on the Hot 100 about a month and a half before the album came out. It entered the Top 40 in March 1978 and showcased vocalist David Lee Roth. It peaked at only No. 36 and lasted three weeks in the Top 40, a lower ceiling than Kiss or Led Zeppelin reached. "You really got me peaked at number 36 in March of 1978 and it was in and out of the top 40 in just three weeks."
**Jump (1984) - First Metal No. 1** : The song opens with a large synthesizer hook. In early 1984, when MTV and Top 40 radio were at peak synthesizer, leading with a synth-driven song was a smart move. It reached the Top 40 in two weeks and No. 1 in seven, then spent five weeks on top. It helped bring hard rock to female listeners and established hair metal as hard rock for everybody. However, no other metal single reached the Top 10 for the rest of 1984, and it remained the only metal No. 1 of the first half of the decade. "Van Halen's breakthrough hit had essentially no coattails."

## curiosities
**Named After the Van Halen Brothers** : The band took its name from guitarist Eddie and drummer Alex Van Halen. Unlike Jon Bon Jovi, Eddie was not the singer but the lead guitarist, one of the most influential in rock history. "Eddie wasn't a singer. He was the band's lead guitarist and one of the most groundbreaking in Rock history."
**Eddie's Beat It Solo** : In late 1982 Eddie played a guitar solo on Michael Jackson's R&B-rock hybrid "Beat It." The song hit No. 1 on both the Hot 100 and the Hot Black Singles chart in spring 1983, which prepared pop radio for a Van Halen chart-topper. "Beated, primed pop radio for Van Halen to score a chart-topper of their own."
**On Hiatus by Late 1985** : By late 1985 Van Halen were on hiatus. "or even the On Hyatus Van Halen."

## albums
**High 'n' Dry (1981) - First Album With Mutt Lange** : Mutt Lange began working with the Sheffield band in 1981, a few months after Back in Black, starting with this album. "Mutt began working with Leopard on 1981's High and Dry"
**Pyromania (1983) - MTV Breakthrough** : This was the band's third album and their second with Mutt Lange, and it made them MTV celebrities. It peaked at No. 2 on the Billboard album chart, held off only by Michael Jackson's Thriller. Had it reached No. 1, it would have been the first heavy metal album to top the US charts, a distinction Quiet Riot claimed instead. None of its three singles, "Photograph," "Rock of Ages" and "Foolin'," reached the Top 10, but by 1984 it was six times platinum. "If not for Michael Jackson's Mega Blockbuster thriller, Death Leopard would have topped the album chart."
**Hysteria (1987) - Long-Delayed Follow-Up** : This follow-up to Pyromania arrived in August 1987, after drummer Rick Allen's accident delayed it. It was built over three years of meticulous sessions with Mutt Lange and had originally been planned for 1986. It took most of a year to break big, then produced six Top 40 hits and sold more than 12 million copies in the US alone, outselling Bon Jovi's New Jersey by mid-1989. "hysteria would go on to spawn six top 40 hits and sell more than 12 million copies in the U.S. alone."

## songs
**Photograph (1983) - Pyromania Lead Single** : This pop-metal single reached No. 12 on the Hot 100 in May 1983, while Pyromania peaked at No. 2 on the album chart. "The album's lead-off single, The Pop Metal Classic, Photograph, reached number 12 on the Hot 100 in May of 1983."
**Pour Some Sugar on Me / Love Bites / Armageddon It (1987) - Hysteria Hits** : These Hysteria hits might have broken hair metal on pop radio if the album had come out in 1986 as originally planned. "maybe pour some sugar on me, or love bites, or Armageddon it would have been the song to break hair metal on pop radio."

## curiosities
**Union Jack Merchandise** : The band's frequent use of the Union Jack on merchandise is a leftover of the New Wave of British Heavy Metal movement, which also included Judas Priest and Iron Maiden. Def Leppard were its biggest-selling band. "it's a vestige of that movement, encompassing such metal bands as Judas Priest and Iron Maiden."
**Rick Allen Loses His Arm** : Drummer Rick Allen lost his arm in a car accident on New Year's 1984, which delayed the recording and release of the fourth album. As a result the band was absent from the Top 40 for about four years and missed hair metal's pop breakthrough. "drummer Rick Allen lost his arm in a car accident on New Year's 1984."
**A Great 2018** : In winter 2018 the band reissued all their classic albums on streaming services, and several returned to the Billboard charts. That summer they toured on a double bill with Journey. "Over the winter, the band re-issued all of their classic albums on streaming services, which prompted several of those albums to reappear on the Billboard charts."

# genre - Heavy Metal

## curiosities
**Undefinable Genre Boundaries** : Metal's boundaries are policed more zealously than almost any other genre's. Artists once considered core metal get downgraded to hard rock, arena rock or glam, and songs once seen as almost satanic now play on classic rock or adult contemporary radio. Thirty years of subgenres such as death metal, speed metal, black metal and grindcore have moved the goalposts further. "Let's simply agree that metal's goal posts are ever shifting."
**The 70s Chart Ceiling** : For most of the two decades before 1986, hard rock and heavy metal were not considered pop chart music. The leading metal bands of the 70s could not get near the Top 40, and it is debatable whether anything called metal topped the Hot 100 before the 80s. "Hard Rock and Heavy Metal had not been considered pop chart music."
**A Young Male Audience** : Until the mid-80s metal's appeal was largely limited to young men. Billboard later reported that metal songs were starting to get onto pop radio playlists and draw requests from female listeners, including songs by Scorpions ("Rock You Like a Hurricane"), Ratt ("Round and Round") and Twisted Sister. "the appeal of metal had been largely confined to young men."
**Five of the Top Six Albums** : In June 1987 five of the top six albums in America were metal. "five of the top six albums in America were metal."

# genre - Hair Metal

## curiosities
**Born With Van Halen in 1978** : The hair metal 80s began in earnest in 1978 with Van Halen's debut, in the same way Donna Summer's "I Feel Love" (1977) began electro-dance and techno-pop. "The Hair Metal 80s kicked off in earnest in 1978 with this album by a four man band from Pasadena, California."
**The 1984–86 Interzone** : From mid-1984 to late 1986, metal videos with flashy solos, leather-clad performers and scantily clad women filled hours of MTV each day. On radio, however, the songs were only medium-sized hits. "This two-year period on the pop charts, from mid 1984 to late 1986, would be an odd interzone for Hard Rock and Heavy Metal."
**Second Wave Pushes Harder** : Metallica and Guns N' Roses led a second wave that pushed the limits of how hard a commercially successful act could sound. "Metallica were the leading edge of a second wave of hair metal"
**Did Grunge Kill Hair Metal?** : The argument that Kurt Cobain and grunge killed hair metal is described as both too simple to be true and too persuasive to ignore. Metal bands had long cited punk influences, from the MC5 to the Sex Pistols and the Ramones. Metal acts that were mellow enough or tough enough kept charting after Nevermind. "It's both too simple to be true and too persuasive to ignore."
**Gone From Radio by 1995** : By January 1995 every 80s metal band except Bon Jovi had dropped off the radio. "a time when every other 80s metal band had cycled off the radio."
