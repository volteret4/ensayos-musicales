## albums
**Whitney Houston (1985) - Self-Titled Debut Album** : Released on Valentine's Day, 1985, it included the Teddy Pendergrass duet "Hold Me," which had appeared the year before. Pop crossover came slowly. Arista spent most of the first half of 1985 working "You Give Good Love" to Top 40 radio. By the end of 1985 the album was the year's sleeper hit, reaching the Billboard top 10 and going double platinum while still dominating the R&B chart. It reached number one on the pop album chart a couple of weeks after "How Will I Know" hit number one, in its 50th week on the chart. That was the slowest rise to number one since Fleetwood Mac's self-titled album a decade earlier. Billboard named it the top black album of 1986 and also the top album of any genre that year. Houston became the first black female artist to have the year's top album, and it was the first LP by a black woman to produce three number one pop hits. "Arista Records had pulled off one of the most painstaking artist introductions in pop history."
**Whitney (1987) - Second Album and Pre-SoundScan Number One Debut** : Released in June 1987 and set up by "I Wanna Dance with Somebody," it shocked the music business by entering the Billboard album chart at number one. Houston became the first female artist to debut at number one on the album chart and the only woman to do so before SoundScan. Only six albums debuted at number one before the 1990s, and this was the fifth. It was only Houston's second album. By comparison, Elton John's two number one debuts were his ninth and tenth albums, Songs in the Key of Life was Stevie Wonder's 18th studio album, and Bad was Michael Jackson's seventh. Houston was the youngest artist ever to do it, barely three years after she began recording. All five singles reached the top ten on both the pop and R&B charts. Four were pop number ones and none were R&B number ones. The album was certified for 6 million in sales as of 1989. "Forget crossover, this was cross cultural dominance."
**I'm Your Baby Tonight (1990) - Third Album and R&B Reconnection** : Clive Davis asked L.A. Reid and Babyface to produce tracks after their success with Bobby Brown's Don't Be Cruel. They ended up producing three tracks, including the title track, which introduced Houston's new sound for the 90s. The Japanese edition included a bonus cover of Steve Winwood's "Higher Love," recorded in 1990, which Kygo revived in 2019. "Never again would she veer too far from the preferences of African American listeners."
**The Bodyguard (1992) - Soundtrack and Best-Selling Soundtrack of All Time** : This was Houston's first feature film role, with a soundtrack she led, and it was much less of a sure thing than Bobby Brown's album at the time. The movie grossed more than $120 million in the US and more than $400 million worldwide. The album spent 20 weeks at number one. For one week just before Christmas 1992, it became the first album of the SoundScan era to sell a million copies in a single week. It became the top-selling soundtrack of all time, with estimated global sales of 45 million copies, and won the Grammy for Album of the Year. "It was also packed with hits that seamlessly fused Houston's black and white influences."
**Waiting to Exhale (1995) - Multi-Platinum Soundtrack** : The 1995 film was a female empowerment melodrama that also showcased African American actresses, with co-stars Angela Bassett, Loretta Devine and Lela Rochon. Kenneth "Babyface" Edmonds produced the soundtrack and wrote almost all of it. It placed Houston alongside leading female R&B artists of the day, including Mary J. Blige and Brandy, who also had Babyface-penned hits from the album. "Both the film and the soundtrack paired Whitney Houston with a coterie of black sisterhood."
**The Preacher's Wife (1996) - Best-Selling Gospel Album of All Time** : This was the soundtrack to the gospel-influenced 1996 film directed by Penny Marshall and starring Denzel Washington, with Houston as co-star. Houston took inspiration from her spiritual hit "Count On Me." The album has sold 6 million copies worldwide, more than Aretha Franklin's Amazing Grace. "It now ranks as the best-selling gospel album of all time, with 6 million copies sold worldwide, outselling even Aretha Franklin's Amazing Grace."
**My Love Is Your Love (1998) - Last Multi-Platinum Album** : This was Houston's first straight studio album since 1990 that was not tied to a movie, though it opened with the film tie-in "When You Believe." It was her last multi-platinum album, and it covered pop, R&B and dance music. "how cleverly it worked multiple sides of the street, genre-wise, pop, R&B, and even dance music."
**Two 2000s Albums - Late-Career Releases** : Houston released only two albums in the 2000s. Neither was her best work because her voice had been damaged by years of drug and other abuse. "thanks to a diminished voice ravaged by years of drug and other abuse."

## songs
**Home (1983) - Merv Griffin Show Performance** : Houston sang this song from the musical The Wiz on her 1983 TV debut. Up to then it had been sung only by Stephanie Mills on Broadway and Diana Ross on film, and it had never been released as a single. It was best known to serious R&B fans, so the performance connected most strongly with black audiences. "If Home was played on the radio at all by 1983, it was a black radio deep cut."
**Hold Me (1984) - Duet with Teddy Pendergrass and Recorded Debut** : This was Houston's formal recorded debut, credited as a Teddy Pendergrass duet. It was a top 5 R&B hit in summer 1984 and helped re-establish Pendergrass after his car accident and label switch. It peaked at number 46 on the Hot 100, just outside the top 40. It appeared again in 1985 on Houston's debut album. "Hold Me was almost exclusively an R&B hit."
**You Give Good Love (1985) - First Solo Single** : The first single was chosen as a deliberate signal to black audiences. It was a slow-burning mid-tempo soul song produced by multi-instrumentalist Kashif, who had his own run of black radio hits. In spring 1985 it topped Billboard's R&B chart, then called Hot Black Singles, back to back with Freddie Jackson's "Rock Me Tonight." After Arista worked it at Top 40 radio for most of the first half of 1985, it reached number three on the Hot 100 that summer, charting alongside Tears for Fears, Duran Duran and Sting. "The choice of You Give Good Love as the first single from the Whitney Houston LP was a signal to black audiences."
**Saving All My Love for You (1985) - First Pop and R&B Number One** : Veteran white songwriters Gerry Goffin and Michael Masser wrote it. It is sung from the point of view of a married man's mistress, and earlier versions had never reached their full potential. Houston's version became her first number one on both the R&B and pop charts in fall 1985. "the track took on both youthful innocence and soulful grit."
**Thinking About You (1985) - Black Radio Exclusive** : Arista promoted this up-tempo funk and dance song only to black radio while the album was producing pop hits. It reached number 10 on Hot Black Singles in the last weeks of 1985 and never made the Hot 100. "did not make the Hot 100 at all."
**How Will I Know (1986) - Crossover Fulcrum** : George Merrill and Shannon Rubicam wrote it for Janet Jackson a couple of years before her Control album, and Janet's team passed on it. Arista bought it as a straightforward pop crossover single. Arista then brought in Narada Michael Walden, who was unimpressed by the demo and asked to produce, rearrange and co-write. Merrill and Rubicam had never used an outside co-writer and pushed back at first. They eventually let Walden add a verse and change the key and tempo. Walden invited Cissy Houston to sing backing vocals, so the stacked harmonies include both mother and daughter. Its paint-splattered video, with white and black dancers, was Houston's first to get significant MTV play, and it briefly shows Aretha Franklin. It reached number one pop about a month before it hit number one R&B. In doing so it replaced her cousin Dionne Warwick's "That's What Friends Are For." It was her last R&B number one for more than four years. "it vocalized like a soul song but played like New Wave pop."
**Greatest Love of All (1986) - Biggest Hit from the Debut** : Michael Masser and Linda Creed wrote the song in 1977 for The Greatest, a biopic of Muhammad Ali. George Benson recorded it first and took it to number two R&B and number 24 pop. Houston's version spent three weeks at number one on the Hot 100 but peaked at number three on Hot Black Singles. It was her first single to chart higher on the pop chart than the R&B chart, an odd result for a song black audiences had embraced more warmly in the 70s. "the song became an athletic feat of a different variety."
**I Wanna Dance with Somebody (Who Loves Me) (1987) - Fourth Straight Pop Number One** : Arista specifically requested the song from Merrill and Rubicam, and Narada Michael Walden produced it again. Walden rearranged it but did not take a co-writing credit. Released in May 1987, it reached number one on the Hot 100 in seven weeks and became her fourth straight pop number one. It peaked at number two R&B. "I thought it was too country and western. I felt there's gotta be some way I can make it's funkier."
**Didn't We Almost Have It All (1987) - Fifth Straight Pop Number One** : Michael Masser co-wrote this torch ballad. It reached number one on the Hot 100 in late September 1987 and peaked at number two R&B. "reached number one on the Hot 100 in late September 1987."
**So Emotional (1988) - Sixth Straight Pop Number One** : Billy Steinberg and Tom Kelly, who had written pop number ones for Madonna, Cyndi Lauper and Heart, wrote this light synth-funk track. It reached number one on the Hot 100 in January 1988 and number five on Hot Black Singles. "It was Houston's sixth consecutive number one pop hit."
**Where Do Broken Hearts Go (1988) - Record Seventh Straight Number One** : Narada Michael Walden produced it, and future Broadway composer Frank Wildhorn co-wrote it. It reached number one on the Hot 100 in April 1988 as her seventh consecutive pop number one. That broke her tie with the Beatles and the Bee Gees, and she still holds the record. It peaked at number two R&B. "Whitney Houston achieved yet another chart record that she holds to this day."
**Love Will Save the Day (1988) - Fifth and Final Whitney Single** : This was a Latin-flavored dance track produced by Jellybean Benitez. It reached number five R&B and number nine on the Hot 100, making it the only single from Whitney to chart higher on R&B. "the only one to perform better on the R&B chart was the album's fifth and final single."
**One Moment in Time (1988) - Seoul Olympics Theme** : NBC asked Houston to record it for the 1988 Olympic Games in Seoul, South Korea. It reached number five on the Hot 100 but only number 22 R&B, the clearest early sign that her black audience was shrinking. "Still, to varying degrees with pop and R&B audiences, even this Olympian hymn was a hit."
**I'm Your Baby Tonight (1990) - R&B Return** : L.A. Reid and Babyface produced it. It topped both the Hot 100 and the R&B chart in the week ending December 1, 1990, and on the Hot 100 it replaced a hit by newcomer Mariah Carey. The sound recalled 60s R&B and the video evoked Diana Ross and the Supremes. It marks a turning point in her career. "found Whitney Houston getting right with the audience that brought her to the dance."
**All the Man That I Need (1991)** : This big ballad topped both the pop and R&B charts in early 1991. "another lung-busting ballad, all the man that I need, topped both the pop and R&B charts."
**The Star-Spangled Banner (1991) - Super Bowl XXV** : Houston pre-recorded it before the game. She was inspired by Marvin Gaye's unusual anthem performance and wanted a gospel feel. Musical director Rickey Minor changed the anthem from 3/4 waltz time to 4/4 by adding a beat to give her room. Its popularity was boosted by the January 1991 start of the first Persian Gulf War. Released as a single two weeks after the Super Bowl, it became the highest-charting version of the anthem in the rock era, peaking at number 20 and going double platinum. When it was reissued after 9/11 in 2001, it reached a new peak of number six. "all black music is four-four."
**Something in Common (1992) - Duet with Bobby Brown** : Houston made a cameo on Brown's album Bobby with this light romantic song. Released as a single, it reached the top 40 on the Hot 100 and the top 30 R&B. "Houston was leveraging her husband's status to burnish her own."
**I Will Always Love You (1992) - Bodyguard Megahit** : Dolly Parton wrote it and had taken it to number one on the country chart twice. Kevin Costner, Houston's Bodyguard co-star, suggested the song and came up with the a cappella opening. It mixes country, pop, gospel and soul. Released in November 1992, it reached number one on the Hot 100 in three weeks and on the R&B chart in four. It held number one R&B for 11 weeks and on the Hot 100 for a then-record 14 weeks. After her 2012 death it sold more than 350,000 downloads in one week. Under Billboard's new policy it re-entered at number 7 and rose to number 3, its highest position since 1993. "Few hits in chart history have been quite this multi-genre and multicultural at their core."
**I'm Every Woman (1993) - Chaka Khan Cover** : This Bodyguard cover of Chaka Khan's 1978 R&B hit became a top 5 hit on both pop and R&B. "It was also packed with hits that seamlessly fused Houston's black and white influences."
**I Have Nothing (1993) - Bodyguard Torch Song** : This big torch song from The Bodyguard reached the top 5 on both the pop and R&B charts. "which also made the top 5 on both charts."
**Exhale (Shoop Shoop) (1995) - Number One Debut** : The understated lead single from Waiting to Exhale debuted at number one on both the Hot 100 and R&B charts, showing how much demand had built for her return. "debuted at number one on both the Hot 100 and R&B charts"
**Count On Me (1996) - Duet with CeCe Winans** : This top 10 follow-up from the Waiting to Exhale cycle was a duet with gospel star CeCe Winans. It inspired her move into the gospel-influenced The Preacher's Wife. "Houston even scored a follow-up top 10 hit"
**I Believe in You and Me (1996)** : This single from The Preacher's Wife reached number four on both the pop and R&B charts. "It gave Houston a number four hit on both the pop and R&B charts"
**When You Believe (1998) - Duet with Mariah Carey** : This duet opened My Love Is Your Love and came from the animated biblical film The Prince of Egypt. "led off with a spiritual film tie-in single"
**Heartbreak Hotel (1998) - With Faith Evans and Kelly Price** : This sultry hit paired Houston with Faith Evans and Kelly Price, then leading-edge R&B singers. It spent seven weeks at number one R&B and reached number two on the Hot 100. "spending seven weeks atop that chart, and also reaching a potent number two on the Hot 100."
**It's Not Right But It's Okay (1999) - Two Versions** : Black radio favored the original album cut produced by Rodney Jerkins. Top 40 radio played a club remix by Thunderpuss, and the single became a top 10 hit. "Black radio stations tended toward the original"
**My Love Is Your Love (2000) - Title Track Remix** : After the remix success of "It's Not Right But It's Okay," the title track also got a club remix. It reached the top five on both the R&B and pop charts in early 2000. "got its own club remix and reached the top five on both the R&B and Pop charts in the early weeks of the year 2000."
**Million Dollar Bill (2009) - Last Hit of Her Lifetime** : Critics praised this up-tempo single, and it reached the R&B top 20. It was the last new hit of her lifetime. "the last new hit of her lifetime."
**Higher Love (1990/2019) - Posthumous Hit with Kygo** : Houston recorded this Steve Winwood cover in 1990 as a bonus track for the Japanese edition of I'm Your Baby Tonight. In 2019 Norwegian DJ Kygo remixed it as tropical house, credited as a Kygo and Houston duet. It reached number one on the dance club chart, number 29 on mainstream top 40 and number 12 on adult top 40. It was in the adult contemporary top 10, briefly made the Hot 100, and reached the top 20 in more than two dozen countries. "Her legacy secure Whitney Houston is still scoring hits from the great beyond"

## curiosities
**Born August 1963 into R&B Royalty** : Whitney Elizabeth Houston was born in August 1963 to John and Cissy Houston. That week, former Drinkard Singers member Doris Troy was in the pop and R&B top 10 with "Just One Look." Dionne Warwick was her first cousin and Aretha Franklin was her honorary aunt. "members of The Drinkard Singers were already helping to define the sound of 60s rhythm and blues."
**Merv Griffin Show TV Debut (1983)** : At 19, before she had released any recordings, Houston was introduced on The Merv Griffin Show by Griffin and by Clive Davis, the Arista president who signed her. Davis described her as elegant, sensuous, innocent and full of soul. Davis is often described as pushing her toward white-friendly pop from the start, but the early campaign actually targeted African American audiences first. "She was introduced not only by Griffin, but by the man who signed her, Arista Records President, Clive Davis."
**Complicated Relationship with R&B** : Her success with mainstream audiences complicated her standing with R&B, black audiences and the black musical tradition she came from. Members of the gospel and R&B community questioned her dedication to that tradition. "Whitney's successful crossover to a mainstream audience complicated her relationship to R&B, the African American audience, and the black musical tradition that spawned her."
**First Artist to Succeed a Blood Relative at Number One in Eight Years** : "How Will I Know" replaced her cousin Dionne Warwick's "That's What Friends Are For" at number one. Nobody had done that since the Bee Gees and Andy Gibb traded the top spot in 1978. "It was the first time an artist succeeded a blood relative at number one in eight years"
**Seven Consecutive Pop Number Ones** : From "Saving All My Love for You" to "Where Do Broken Hearts Go," she had seven straight Hot 100 number ones. That passed the Beatles (six, 1964–66) and the Bee Gees (six, 1977–79), and the record still stands. "It was her seventh consecutive number one pop hit, breaking her out of a tie with two legendary groups."
**Booed at the 1989 Soul Train Music Awards** : When she was announced as a nominee for Best R&B/Urban Contemporary Single by a Female, at the height of her fame, the audience booed. Kevin Macdonald's documentary Whitney blames a "Whitey" campaign stirred up by Reverend Al Sharpton. Some black radio programmers had also stopped playing her records. However, competitors that night, such as Anita Baker and Vanessa Williams, had also crossed over. Houston reportedly took the incident hard. "the audience at this televised program, honoring achievement in African American music, is actually booing Whitney Houston"
**Met Bobby Brown at the Soul Train Awards** : She met her future husband Bobby Brown at the April 1989 Soul Train Awards, the same night she was booed. That night Brown had the number one R&B song in America, "Every Little Step." "she met her future husband on the Soul Train Awards that same night was no coincidence."
**Two Documentaries** : Footage of the Soul Train booing features in two recent theatrical documentaries: Whitney: Can I Be Me by British director Nick Broomfield and Whitney by Scottish director Kevin Macdonald. "Versions of it feature prominently in a pair of documentaries about Houston"
**Film Career** : Her film roles were The Bodyguard (1992), Waiting to Exhale (1995) and The Preacher's Wife (1996). In Waiting to Exhale she co-starred with Angela Bassett, Loretta Devine and Lela Rochon. The Preacher's Wife was directed by Penny Marshall and starred Denzel Washington. "Her first ever role in a feature film, plus a Whitney-led soundtrack."
**Death on February 11, 2012** : She died at age 48 on February 11, 2012, one day before the 2012 Grammy Awards. That was the day she was due to appear at Clive Davis' pre-Grammy party. Her death led to widespread grief, put her hits back on radio, and led Billboard to change its Hot 100 policy on older songs. "the day she was scheduled to appear at her longtime mentor, Clive Davis' pre-Grammy party"
**Rock and Roll Hall of Fame Induction (2020)** : She became eligible in 2009, 25 years after her first recordings, without any fanfare. She was not nominated for another decade, and like Donna Summer, she was reappreciated only after her death. She was inducted in 2020 on her first and only ballot. Some critics objected that she was mainly a pop act whose work was only adjacent to rock. "some critics arguing that Houston is primarily a pop act, whose body of work is only adjacent to rock."
**Only 2020 Rock Hall Inductee with a Current Hit** : In the class with T. Rex, the Notorious B.I.G. and the Doobie Brothers, she was the only inductee with a current hit on the radio: "Higher Love" with Kygo. "She has a current hit on the radio."
**Influence on Later Vocalists** : She kept having hits after the backlash and influenced generations of singers, including Christina Aguilera and Beyoncé. "still influencing generations of vocalists from Christina Aguilera."

## curiosities
**From Drinkard Singers to Sweet Inspirations** : By 1967 the Drinkard Singers had become the Sweet Inspirations. Dionne and Dee Dee Warwick had gone solo, and Cissy Houston led the remaining quartet. "By 1967, the Drankard singers had transformed themselves into the sweet inspirations."
**Signed Thanks to Aretha** : Their backing work on Aretha Franklin's success partly led Atlantic to sign them. "That success in part led the label to sign the sweet inspirations to their own contract."
**Premier Backing Vocal Group** : They backed the Drifters, Jackie DeShannon, Aretha Franklin, Van Morrison and Dusty Springfield. "they became the premier backing vocal group of their era"
**Elvis Presley's Vocal Group** : For several years they were Elvis Presley's go-to vocal group in the studio and on tour. "the sweet inspirations became King of Rock and Roll, Elvis Presley's Go To Vocal Group, both in the studio and on the road."

Harold Melvin & the Blue Notes

## genres
R&B
Soul

## songs
**Close the Door - Only Pop Top 40** : His only pop top 40 hit, peaking at number 25. "scored only one pop top 40 hit, Closed the Door, which peaked at number 25."
**You're My Latest, Greatest Inspiration (1982)** : His 1982 R&B top 5 hit, just before his accident. "his 1982 top 5 hit, Your My Latest, Greatest Inspiration"
**Hold Me (1984) - Duet with Whitney Houston** : His comeback single and Houston's recorded debut, a top 5 R&B hit in summer 1984 that peaked at number 46 on the Hot 100. "It reinvigorated Pendergrass's status as a leading figure on black radio"

## curiosities
**R&B Legend Without Crossover** : He played sold-out concerts to mostly black audiences. He had a dozen R&B top 5 hits and nearly 30 R&B top 40 hits, with a streak running from 1977 to 1982. "Pendergrass was an R&B legend, performing sell out concerts composed largely of black listeners"
**Car Accident and Label Switch** : After his 1982 accident left him paralyzed from the chest down, he spent 1982–83 recovering and switched record labels. "a car accident that left him paraplegic from the chest down."

# artist - George Benson

## songs
**The Greatest Love of All (1977) - Original Version** : He recorded it for The Greatest, the Muhammad Ali biopic. It reached number two R&B and number 24 pop. "it was first recorded by jazz and R&B singer and guitarist George Benson who took the song to number two on the R&B chart that year."

# artist - Michael Masser

## curiosities
**Songwriter for Houston** : The Chicago-born songwriter co-wrote "Saving All My Love for You" with Gerry Goffin. He also co-wrote "Greatest Love of All" with Linda Creed and "Didn't We Almost Have It All." With Goffin he wrote "Theme from Mahogany" and "Tonight I Celebrate My Love." "It was written by a pair of veteran white male songwriters, legendary Brill Building Songwriter Jerry Goffin and Chicago-born Michael Masser."
**Greatest Love of All Origins** : He and Linda Creed wrote the song as a tribute to Muhammad Ali. "co-wrote the song to reflect the achievements and inspirational symbolism of Ali's career."

# artist - George Merrill & Shannon Rubicam

## member of
Boy Meets Girl

## songs
**How Will I Know (1986)** : They wrote it for Janet Jackson, whose team passed on it. They initially resisted Narada Michael Walden's request to co-write, because they had never worked with an outside co-writer. "was originally pinned for Janet Jackson, a couple of years before her control album."
**I Wanna Dance with Somebody (1987)** : Arista specifically requested the song from them. "It was specifically requested of them by Arista Records."
**Waiting for a Star to Fall (1988) - Rejected by Whitney** : They offered it to Houston first, but Arista turned it down as off-brand. Released a year later as a Boy Meets Girl single, it became a top five pop hit. "The song that's so poppy even Clive Davis didn't think Houston should record it."

## curiosities
**Husband-and-Wife Team** : They were married at the time and also recorded as Boy Meets Girl. "Written by the then-husband and wife pop songwriting team of George Merrill and Shannon Rubikam."

# artist - Narada Michael Walden

## curiosities
**Reworking How Will I Know** : He was unimpressed by the demo and asked to produce, rearrange and co-write. He added a verse, changed the key and tempo, and brought in Cissy Houston for backing vocals. "allowing Walden to add a new verse and change the song's key and tempo."
**Funking Up I Wanna Dance with Somebody** : He reworked the song but did not take a co-writing credit. He later produced "Where Do Broken Hearts Go." "I felt there's gotta be some way I can make it's funkier"

# artist - Bobby Brown

## member of
New Edition

## genres
R&B
New Jack Swing
Pop

## albums
**Don't Be Cruel (1988)** : Released in summer 1988, it drew on several strands of late-80s black pop. L.A. Reid and Babyface co-wrote and produced half the tracks, including every hit single except "My Prerogative." Billboard named it the number one pop and R&B album of 1989. It produced five top ten hits, all of which reached the Hot Black Singles top five, with three number ones there. It was certified six-times platinum. "he had achieved his pop crossover without alienating his black fanbase."
**Bobby (1992) - Comeback** : His first album since Don't Be Cruel was more eagerly anticipated than Houston's next project. It debuted at number two on the Billboard 200 and spawned an immediate number two pop, number one R&B hit produced by L.A. Reid and Babyface. It included the duet "Something in Common" with Houston. "Browns was the more hotly anticipated album."

## songs
**My Prerogative (1988)** : This pop and R&B number one was made with Teddy Riley, the leading producer of New Jack Swing. "Brown worked with Teddy Riley, the producer king of New Jack Swing."
**Every Little Step (1989)** : This L.A. Reid and Babyface song was number one R&B in April 1989, the night he met Houston. "Brown had the number one R&B song in America"
**Something in Common (1992) - Duet with Whitney Houston** : It reached the Hot 100 top 40 and the R&B top 30. "Whitney made her own cameo on the Bobby album"

## curiosities
**Crossover Megastar in 1989** : When he met Houston he was a major R&B-to-pop star, about as popular as she was. "Brown was an honest to goodness R&B to pop crossover Megastar."

# genre - R&B

## curiosities
**The R&B Chart as Its Own Universe** : By the 80s, artists such as Luther Vandross, Frankie Beverly and Maze, Jeffrey Osborne and Stacy Lattisaw had runs of R&B top 10s that never reached the upper Hot 100. "by the 80s, the R&B chart was its own universe with its own lineup of stars who would rarely or never cross to the pop charts."
**Glass Ceiling on Black Female Crossover** : Black female stars of the 60s and 70s had big crossover hits but struggled to sustain consistent pop chart records. "This glass ceiling on black female crossover achievement would change eventually."
**Hot Black Singles** : Billboard's R&B chart was called Hot Black Singles in 1985. "topped Billboard's R&B chart, then called Hot Black Singles in the spring of 1985."

# genre - Disco

## curiosities
**Good for Black Female Performers** : Disco boosted Diana Ross, Gloria Gaynor, Donna Summer and even Cissy Houston. "The rise of disco, too, was good for black female performers"

# curiosity

**Pre-SoundScan Number One Album Debuts** : Only six albums debuted at number one before the 1990s. They were Elton John's Captain Fantastic and Rock of the Westies (1975), Stevie Wonder's Songs in the Key of Life (1976), Bruce Springsteen's Live/1975–85 (1986), Whitney Houston's Whitney (1987) and Michael Jackson's Bad (1987). "There were exactly a half dozen number one album debuts before the 1990s."
**SoundScan Computerization** : Billboard computerized its charts with SoundScan in the early 90s, after which number one album debuts became common. "Billboard would not computerize its charts with the soundscan system until the early 90s, after which number one debuts on the album chart became commonplace."
**Billboard Recurrent Policy Change (2012)** : Houston's death led Billboard to let older songs back onto the Hot 100, since in the download and streaming era they could sell like current hits. "Houston's death prompted Billboard Magazine to change its hot 100 policy to allow old or recurrent hits to return to the chart."
**80s Black Male Crossover Explosion** : Michael Jackson, Lionel Richie (whose hits spanned R&B, pop and country) and Prince (who mixed R&B with guitar rock and got album-rock airplay) crossed over without alienating black audiences. "The 1980s would see an explosion of black male crossover from Michael Jackson to Lionel Richie to Prince."
**Crossover Dominance in 80s Culture** : The decade saw crossover dominance in several media. "Even in a decade that saw crossover dominance by Michael Jackson in music, Eddie Murphy at the movies and Bill Cosby on TV"
**2020 Rock Hall Class** : Whitney Houston was inducted alongside T. Rex, the Notorious B.I.G. and the Doobie Brothers. "from T. Rex to Biggie Smalls to the Dubie Brothers."
