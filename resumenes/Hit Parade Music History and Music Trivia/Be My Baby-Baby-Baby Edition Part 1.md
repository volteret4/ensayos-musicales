# artist - The Dixie Cups

## genres
Girl Group
Pop

## songs
**Chapel of Love (1964) - Knocked the Beatles Off No. 1** : In late May 1964, the Dixie Cups, a Black female trio from New Orleans who had moved to New York City to make it big, knocked the Beatles out of No. 1 on Billboard's Hot 100 with "Chapel of Love." They were the only American group to top the chart in the entire first half of 1964, a period when the Beatles otherwise had a stranglehold on the top spot. Like many hits by female ensembles of the period, the song was written by professional New York songwriters who set to music the desires and dreams of other young women. Lyrically it is a wish for a white wedding. "A wish for a white wedding called Chappell of Love."
**Iko Iko (1965) - First Hit Version of a New Orleans Standard** : One of a couple of mid-charting follow-ups to "Chapel of Love," this was the first ever hit version of the New Orleans standard and reached No. 20. Its energy recalled the Bobbettes' "Mr. Lee." "including the first ever hit version of the New Orleans standard Ico Ico, a number 20 hit."

## curiosities
**Only American Group at No. 1 in Early 1964** : At the height of Beatlemania, the Dixie Cups were the only American group to top the Hot 100 in the first half of 1964. "They were the only American group to top the chart in the entire first half of 1964."

**No Scrubs (1999) - Hot 100 No. 1** : In May 1999, the Atlanta trio of young Black women was finishing its run at No. 1 on the Hot 100 with "No Scrubs." # artist - The Ronettes

## members
Ronnie Spector

## genres
Girl Group
Pop
Rock and Roll

## songs
**Be My Baby (1963) - No. 2 Hot 100, No. 1 Cashbox** : In the week ending October 12, 1963, "Be My Baby" reached No. 2 on Billboard's Hot 100 and No. 1 on the Cashbox Top 100. It confirmed that America was at peak girl group. Ellie Greenwich and Jeff Barry of the Brill Building wrote it, Phil Spector produced it, and the Wrecking Crew, the legendary L.A. studio musicians, played on it. A teenage Cher sang backing vocals, and the lead was sung by Ronnie Bennett. It is considered the apex of the girl group era and a landmark for rock and roll in general. "one of the most influential records in rock and roll history."
**Be My Baby - Critical Acclaim** : Brian Wilson of the Beach Boys called it the greatest record ever produced. Hit songwriter Rick Knowles called it "ground zero for the modern pop era, a line in the sand that left everything that came before in the rear-view mirror. It was the beginning of pop music being a serious American art form." "It's the greatest record ever produced."
**Be My Baby - Lyrics About Power and Control** : The song is lyrically advanced even among girl group songs, and is as much about power and control as about romance. Music journalist Mark Spitz wrote that it reverses the usual norm. "a woman makes a play for a man while infantilizing him."
**Be My Baby - Blocked by Sugar Shack** : On the Hot 100 the song peaked at No. 2, behind the mostly forgotten No. 1 "Sugar Shack" by Jimmy Gilmer and the Fireballs. Cashbox, Billboard's competitor, used a different chart formula and ranked it No. 1. "behind the mostly forgotten number one hit sugar shack by Jimmy Gilmer and the fireballs."
**Be My Baby - Only One Ronette Sings** : Although the record is credited to the New York City trio, the only Ronette who sings on it is Veronica Bennett, born in Brooklyn, the future Ronnie Spector. Her trilling vocals are the record's signature element, especially her phrasing of "whoa, oh, oh." "her iconic phrasing of, whoa, oh, oh."
**Be My Baby - The Hal Blaine Intro** : The record opens with a thump-thump-thump beat by Wrecking Crew drummer Hal Blaine. "quite possibly the most imitated five seconds in all of pop."
**Be My Baby - Decades of Imitations** : Many artists borrowed from the record:
- Brian Wilson imitated it almost immediately on the Beach Boys' 1964 hit "Don't Worry Baby."
- The Four Seasons paid homage the same year on their No. 1 "Rag Doll."
- Billy Joel openly emulated it on his throwback hit "Say Goodbye to Hollywood," copying both Blaine's beat and Ronnie's "whoa, oh, oh."
- Meat Loaf and songwriter Jim Steinman echoed it on the 1978 hit "You Took the Words Right Out of My Mouth."
- Hair metal band Poison invoked it on their 1986 debut single "Cry Tough."
- British indie noise rockers the Jesus and Mary Chain imitated it on their most famous single, "Just Like Honey."

"Decades later, artists across rock subgenres were still imitating Be My Baby."
**Be My Baby - Eddie Money's Take Me Home Tonight (1986)** : Eddie Money's 1986 Top 5 hit did not borrow the drumbeat, but it was the most overt and affectionate homage to "Be My Baby." Money copied the chorus lyric "Be my little baby" and got Ronnie Spector herself to sing it. The appearance revived her career more than a decade after her acrimonious divorce from Phil Spector. "The appearance revived Ronnie's career, more than a decade after her acrimonious divorce from Phil Spector."
**Sleigh Ride (1963) - Christmas Streaming Staple** : The Ronettes' version, recorded for Phil Spector's holiday album *A Christmas Gift for You*, was not issued as a single. It is now heavily streamed and routinely makes the Top 10 every Christmas. "Slayride does now routinely make the Top 10 every Christmas in the streaming era."
**Baby, I Love You (1964) - Be My Baby Follow-Up** : The follow-up to "Be My Baby" reached No. 24. "The Ronets did manage a number 24 hit with their Be My Baby follow-up, Baby I Love You."
**Walking in the Rain (1964) - No. 23** : This classic reached No. 23 in 1964 and often appears alongside "Be My Baby" on greatest-songs lists. "which often joins Be My Baby on greatest songs lists"

## albums
**A Christmas Gift for You (1963) - Phil Spector Holiday Album** : The Ronettes were a star attraction on Phil Spector's late-1963 holiday album, which included their version of "Sleigh Ride." "they were a star attraction on Phil Spector's holiday album A Christmas Gift for You"

## curiosities
**Never Returned to the Top 10** : "Be My Baby" made the Ronettes legends, but it did not guarantee more hits. They never returned to the Hot 100's Top 10 while they were an active group. They represent the artistic heights of the girl group model, and also how dependent these groups were on outside songwriters, players, and especially a tyrannical producer. "In short, the Ronets were Girl Group icons, but also a cautionary tale."
**Joey Ramone's Admiration** : Ramones frontman Joey Ramone was a huge fan of the Ronettes and the Shangri-Las. "was a huge fan of the Shangri-La's and the Ronets."

# artist - Phil Spector

## genres
Pop
Rock and Roll

## curiosities
**The Wall of Sound and a Controlling Reputation** : Spector gave his productions, including the Crystals' "Then He Kissed Me," his patented Wall of Sound. He was known as both a genius and a notoriously controlling, irascible producer. He nonetheless produced wonders with his stable of girl groups. "the notoriously controlling Phil Specter, who gave the song his patented wall of sound."
**He's a Rebel Credited to the Wrong Group** : Spector wanted to get his version of the surefire hit "He's a Rebel" into stores before a competing version by singer Vikki Carr. The Crystals could not make it to the studio in time for the session, so he recorded the Blossoms, featuring Darlene Love's lead, and printed the Crystals' name on the single. It spent two weeks at No. 1 in November 1962. "And he simply printed the crystals name on the single."
**Treating Singers as Interchangeable** : Spector was mainly impressed with the Blossoms' lead singer Darlene Love. He seemed to regard the other Blossoms, and to an extent even Darlene, as interchangeable performers. "as essentially interchangeable performers."
**Studio Climate and He Hit Me** : The Crystals recorded the notorious non-hit "He Hit Me (And It Felt Like a Kiss)" with Spector. "Given the climate in Phil Spector's studio, a song like this was not beyond the pale."
**Marriage to Ronnie Spector** : The lead vocalist of "Be My Baby" would later marry Spector. The two later went through an acrimonious divorce. "And its lead vocal was by a woman who would one day marry Phil Spector."

# artist - The Shirelles

## genres
Girl Group
Pop

## songs
**Will You Love Me Tomorrow (1960) - First Girl Group No. 1** : Carole King and Gerry Goffin, a young married songwriting couple then in their early twenties, wrote it in 1960 for this quartet from Passaic, New Jersey. It deals with teen angst over a real dilemma at the dawn of the sexual revolution: whether a young girl should sleep with her boyfriend, and whether he would be there for her in the morning. It set this dilemma in a plush orchestral arrangement with one of King's best melodies. In January 1961 it became the first girl group No. 1 in Hot 100 history and kicked off the girl group era. "The first girl group number one in Hot 100 History in January 1961."
**Dedicated to the One I Love (1961) - First Hit Version** : The Shirelles had the first hit version of this song, which reached No. 3 in 1961. "including the first hit version of dedicated to the one I love, which reached number three in 1961."
**Mama Said (1961) - No. 4 Hit** : This sassy single reached No. 4 in mid-1961. "The Sassy Mama said a number four hit also in mid-61."
**Baby It's You (1962) - No. 8, Covered by the Beatles** : The Shirelles took it to No. 8 in 1962. The Beatles covered it on their 1963 debut LP *Please Please Me*. "after the Cherelles took the aforementioned baby it's you to number eight"
**Soldier Boy (1962) - Second No. 1** : The Shirelles returned to the top of the Hot 100 in May 1962 with this sweetly smitten single. "they returned to the top of the Hot 100 in May, with the sweetly smitten soldier boy."
**Boys (1960) - B-Side Covered by the Beatles** : The Beatles covered this 1960 B-side on *Please Please Me*. "their 1960 B-side Boys."

## curiosities
**Crossover Megastars Before Motown** : "Will You Love Me Tomorrow" set off a wave of female harmony groups. It also made the Shirelles crossover megastars at a time before Motown had turned Black artists into consistent pop hitmakers. Over the next three years they racked up a string of hits, many in the Top 10 and many now considered pop classics. "made the Cherelles crossover mega stars."

# genre - Girl Group

## curiosities
**Pitchfork's Definition** : A Pitchfork history of the category set three criteria for what counts as a girl group. "an act must have three or more members, all the members must be women, and they must record pop music with clear harmonies."
**Vocals First** : Girl groups are defined by vocal rather than instrumental skill, and that is not a judgment of quality. "This emphasis on vocal over instrumental prowess is not a value judgment, however."
**Greil Marcus on Girl Group Rock** : Veteran critic Greil Marcus, who covered girl groups for the *Rolling Stone Illustrated History of Rock & Roll*, wrote that the records were glorious: "music of celebration, of simple joy, of innocence, of sex, of life itself. Girl groups gave you the genuine article." "of all the genres of rock and roll, girl group rock is likely the warmest and the most affecting."
**Bridging Elvis and the Beatles** : In the early 1960s, between Elvis Presley's peak and the Beatles' arrival, girl groups dominated rock and roll, from the Crystals to the Chiffons. "rock and roll was dominated and many critics argue, saved by girl groups, from the crystals to the chiffons."
**Engine of Dance Music Innovation** : Dance music of many kinds was developed through girl groups, from the Emotions to Exposé. Boy bands refined styles that were already popular, such as the Monkees building on the Beatles or Backstreet Boys and NSYNC modeling their harmonies on Boyz II Men. Girl groups instead laid foundations for sounds that were new when they appeared. "girl groups were a foundation for sounds and styles that, when they emerged, sounded novel and fresh."
**Narrating Women's Emotions** : For women listeners, girl groups narrated deep emotions and personal freedom, even when the singers themselves were not free. Over generations their themes ran from sexual agency to body positivity. "girl groups narrated profound emotions and expressed personal freedom even when the singers were not so free themselves."
**Inspiration for Men** : Male artists kept borrowing the sound of several women in dialogue while harmonizing, from the Beatles' covers to 1970s punk rockers covering girl groups. "For male listeners, girl groups provided inspiration."
**Precursors: Boswell Sisters, Andrews Sisters, Chordettes** : The jazz-era Boswell Sisters and Andrews Sisters, and the Chordettes' 1954 No. 1 "Mr. Sandman," anticipated the girl group sound. "as did the Cordettes 1954 number one hit Mr. Sandman."
**Peak Girl Group, 1963** : "Be My Baby" reaching No. 2 in October 1963 marked the high point of the genre. "It confirmed that America was at peak girl group."
**1990 Reboot and 90s Renaissance** : By the end of the 1980s the genre needed a reboot that could speak to the hip-hop era. It began in 1990 when a new R&B quartet scored its first hits, and a flood of mostly women-of-color groups singing in R&B and gospel traditions followed. "A flurry of Girl Group's mostly women of color singing in the R&B and gospel traditions flooded the charts all over again."
**Shaping American Women's Mores** : Across generations, girl groups reflected, channeled and shaped the values of young American women. "Girl groups reflected, channeled, and even shaped the mores of contemporary American women"

# genre - Disco

## curiosities
**A Good Era for Girl Groups** : As the 1970s went on, disco favored girl groups. No. 1 hits included Silver Convention's "Fly, Robin, Fly," A Taste of Honey's "Boogie Oogie Oogie," and the Emotions' "Best of My Love" (1977). "Chart-topping highlights included the Silver Convention's Fly, Robin Fly, a Taste of Honey's Boogie-Oogie-Oogie, and the Emotions 1977 number one best of my love."

# genre - Freestyle

## curiosities
**Female Vocalists as the Genre's Lifeblood** : Freestyle, a Latin-flavored dance subgenre, was the only 1980s category to produce multiple hitmaking girl groups. Exposé led the field, followed by second-tier groups like Company B, Seduction and the Cover Girls. "female vocalists were the lifeblood of freestyle."
**Closest to the Early-60s Aesthetic** : Freestyle groups were as comfortable with dance bangers as with torch songs. Both Exposé and Sweet Sensation topped the Hot 100 with ballads. The genre was short-lived and transitional. "they came the closest in the 80s to replicating the aesthetic of the early 60s Girl Group model."

# label - Motown

## curiosities
**First Hot 100 No. 1 via Tamla** : Motown's first Hot 100 No. 1 was the Marvelettes' "Please Mr. Postman" in late 1961, on its subsidiary Tamla Records. "In late 1961, Motown scored its first ever Hot 100 number one on Motown subsidiary label Tamla Records via the Marvel Let's"
**Berry Gordy's Assembly Line** : Motown's in-house songwriting teams, including Brian Holland, produced the label's girl group hits. "Barry Gordy's soon to be legendary songwriting assembly line."

# curiosity

**Cashbox vs. Billboard Chart Formulas** : Cashbox magazine, Billboard's competitor, used a different formula for its Top 100. That is why "Be My Baby" was No. 1 on Cashbox but only No. 2 on the Hot 100. "billboard competitor Cashbox magazine ranked Be My Baby number one on its top 100 chart, which used a different formula from the hot 100."
**Ramones and Punk Love of Girl Groups** : 1970s punk rockers often imitated or directly covered girl groups. "The punk rockers of the 70s often imitated or even directly covered girl groups."
