# artist - The Dixie Cups

## genres
Pop

## songs
**Chapel of Love (1964)** : Released in late May 1964, this song was a number one hit on Billboard's Hot 100, notably knocking The Beatles out of the top spot. It was penned by professional New York songwriters and articulated the romantic desires and dreams of young women, specifically a wish for a white wedding. The Dixie Cups were the sole American group to achieve a number one hit in the entire first half of 1964. "A wish for a white wedding called Chappell of Love."
**Iko Iko (N/A)** : This song, a New Orleans standard, became The Dixie Cups' first hit version, reaching number 20 on the charts. It served as a mid-charting follow-up to their number one hit "Chapel of Love" and possessed an energy reminiscent of The Bobbettes' "Mr. Lee." "It recalled the energy of the Bobbets Mr. Lee."

## curiosities
**New Orleans Origins** : This black female trio originated from New Orleans before relocating to New York City with aspirations of achieving significant musical success. "a black female trio from New Orleans, who'd moved to New York City to make it big."

# artist - The Ronettes

## members
Ronnie Bennett (Veronica Bennett, later Ronnie Spector)
Cher (backing vocals on "Be My Baby")

## genres
Pop
Rock and Roll

## songs
**Be My Baby (1963)** : Co-written by Ellie Greenwich and Jeff Barry, and produced by Phil Spector with instrumental backing from The Wrecking Crew, this song is considered the apex of the girl group era and a landmark in rock and roll. It reached number two on Billboard's Hot 100 and number one on the Cashbox Top 100 chart in the week ending October 12, 1963, featuring lead vocals by Ronnie Bennett and backing vocals by a teenage Cher. Brian Wilson hailed it as "the greatest record ever produced," and Rick Knowles labeled it "ground zero for the modern pop Ira." Lyrically, music journalist Mark Spitz noted its deceptively advanced theme of power and control, where a woman makes a play for a man while infantilizing him. The iconic opening, featuring drummer Hal Blaine's "thump, thump, thump" beat, is one of the most imitated five seconds in pop, complemented by Ronnie's unique trilling vocals and her signature "whoa, oh, oh" phrasing. "Be My Baby is not only considered the apex of the girl group Ira, it's a landmark for rock and roll in general."
**Sleigh Ride (N/A)** : This song was included on Phil Spector's 1963 holiday album, "A Christmas Gift for You," and while not issued as a single at the time, The Ronettes' version now routinely achieves a Top 10 spot every Christmas in the streaming era. "their now much streamed version of Slayride wasn't issued as a single."
**Baby I Love You (1963)** : This track served as the follow-up to "Be My Baby," reaching number 24 on the charts in late 1963. "The Ronets did manage a number 24 hit with their Be My Baby follow-up, Baby I Love You."
**Walking in the Rain (1964)** : Often cited alongside "Be My Baby" on lists of greatest songs, this classic by The Ronettes reached number 23 in 1964. "And their classic Walking in the Rain, which often joins Be My Baby on greatest songs lists, made it to number 23 in 1964."

## curiosities
**Chart Performance Discrepancy** : Despite "Be My Baby" being highly influential and making The Ronettes legends, it peaked at number two on Billboard's Hot 100, behind "Sugar Shack" by Jimmy Gilmer and the Fireballs, though it did hit number one on the Cashbox Top 100 chart. "it is frankly a crime that Be My Baby peaked on billboards hot 100 at number two behind the mostly forgotten number one hit sugar shack by Jimmy Gilmer and the fireballs."
**Legendary Status vs. Further Hits** : "Be My Baby" alone cemented The Ronettes' legend, but they never returned to the Hot 100's Top 10 as an active group. Their holiday recording of "Sleigh Ride" only achieved Top 10 status decades later in the streaming era. "As for the Ronets, Be My Baby by itself made them legends, but it didn't guarantee them further hits."
**Reliance on External Talents** : The Ronettes epitomized the artistic peaks of the girl group model while also demonstrating how dependent such groups could become on outside songwriters, session players, and particularly, a controlling producer. "They represented the artistic heights of the Girl Group model, but also exemplified how reliant these groups could be on other songwriters, players, and most especially a tyrannical producer."
**Origin** : The group is identified as a trio from New York City, with lead singer Veronica Bennett born in Brooklyn, New York. "Though Be My Baby is credited to the New York City trio, the only Ronet who sings on it is the woman born Veronica Bennett in Brooklyn, New York, the future Ronnie Spector."

## curiosities
**Classification Debate** : Despite being an all-female band with harmonized vocals and retro influences, music historians typically do not classify The Go-Go's as a traditional "girl group." Emerging from the late 1970s LA punk scene, they played their own instruments and wrote their own songs, striving for respect as a rock band among their male peers. They preferred to be known as a rock band that happened to be all women rather than a "girl group." "But music historians generally don't classify the Go-Go's, who emerged from the late 70's LA punk scene and fought to earn the respect of their male peers as a girl group of the likes of the charelles or the Shangra-Las."
**Genre Transcendence** : Similar to The Beatles and boy bands, The Go-Go's were simultaneously part of the girl group pop genre and managed to transcend it. "In other words, the Go-Go's were not too good for girl group pop. Like the Beatles and boy bands, they were of the genre and they transcended the genre."

**Soldier Boy (1962)** : In May 1962, The Cherelles returned to the top of the Hot 100 with this sweetly smitten song. "they returned to the top of the Hot 100 in May, with the sweetly smitten soldier boy."

## songs
**He's Sure the Boy I Love (1963)** : This 1963 hit, featuring female vocals, was presented as a pop song but was fundamentally a rock and roll track. "The Crystal's 1963 hit, Then He Kissed Me, is like eavesdropping on a private story shared between girlfriends about a young woman's romantic and sexual awakening."
**He Hit Me (and It Felt like a Kiss) (N/A)** : This notorious non-hit, also recorded with Phil Spector, was intended by co-writer Carole King as an exposé of domestic violence. This was a decision by producer Phil Spector, who needed to release the song quickly and used The Crystals' name because the original group could not make it to the studio in time. "He's a rebel, was credited to the crystals, a pre-existing, totally separate girl group in Spector's stable of acts."

## songs
**He's a Rebel (1962)** : This song was recorded by The Blossoms, featuring Darleen Love's powerful lead vocals, but it was controversially credited to The Crystals by producer Phil Spector. Spector, desperate to release a version before Vicky Carr's, used The Blossoms but printed The Crystals' name on the single, which subsequently spent two weeks at number one on the Hot 100 in November 1962. ## curiosities
**Uncredited Backup and Lead Vocals** : This LA trio frequently sang backup on numerous hit records but never broke into the Top 40 under their own name. They operated under the erratic direction of producer Phil Spector, who viewed them, including lead singer Darleen Love, as essentially interchangeable performers. "This LA trio, who sang backup on dozens of hit records, not only never broke into the top 40, at least under their own name, they suffered under the whims of the renowned genius and infamously, erasible producer Phil Spector."
**Producer's Manipulation** : Phil Spector, while impressed with lead singer Darleen Love, orchestrated for The Blossoms to record "He's a Rebel" but credited it to The Crystals because the latter group could not attend the studio session in time. "Spector was mainly impressed with the Blossoms lead singer, the legendary Darleen Love. But he seemed to regard the other Blossoms, and even Darleen, to an extent, as essentially interchangeable performers."
