# artist - Steve Lawrence

## genres
Traditional Pop

## songs
**Go Away Little Girl (1963) - Lawrence's Only Hot 100 Number One** : Written by Carole King and Gerry Goffin, who were then married. Lawrence's recording topped the Hot 100 for two weeks in January 1963, a year before Beatlemania broke. Eight years later, Donny Osmond's cover made it the first song in Hot 100 history to reach number one twice. Critic Tom Breihan gives both versions a rating of one in his Stereogum series The Number Ones. "It's hurting me more each and every day that you delay."

## songs
**Go Away Little Girl (1971) - First Song to Hit Number One Twice** : In 1971 the Osmonds were breaking on the radio, and the promotional machine picked 13-year-old Donny for Tiger Beat-style fame. His squeaky-voiced re-recording of Steve Lawrence's 1963 hit topped the Hot 100 in September 1971. That made it the first song in the chart's history to reach number one twice. The week before, Paul and Linda McCartney's "Uncle Albert/Admiral Halsey" was number one, and that week Aretha Franklin's "Spanish Harlem" peaked at number two. "the simpering song became the first in the chart's history to reach number one twice."

# artist - Carole King

## curiosities
**Goffin-King Songwriting Partnership - Two Double Number Ones** : Carole King and Gerry Goffin were legendary composers and, at the time, spouses. They wrote "Go Away Little Girl" (number one for Steve Lawrence in 1963 and for Donny Osmond in 1971) and "The Loco-Motion" (number one for Little Eva in 1962 and for Grand Funk in 1974). That makes them the writers of the first two songs to top the Hot 100 twice. "it was another song penned by Jerry Goffin and Carol King."

**The Loco-Motion - Babysitter Recording** : Goffin and King wrote "The Loco-Motion" during the early-'60s instructional dance record craze that produced "The Twist." They wanted Dee Dee Sharp, who already had a dance-craze hit with "Mashed Potato Time," to sing it. After Sharp turned it down, they recorded the song with their 17-year-old babysitter, Eva Boyd, on vocals. "Carol and Jerry instead recorded the song with their 17-year-old babysitter Eva Boyk on vocals."

# label - Motown

## curiosities
**First Number One via Tamla** : The Marvelettes' "Please Mr. Postman" (December 1961) was Motown's first number one, released on subsidiary Tamla Records. "it became the Motown recording company's first number one on Motown subsidiary, Tamla Records."

**Three Versions of Grapevine** : Smokey Robinson & the Miracles, Marvin Gaye, and Gladys Knight & the Pips all recorded "I Heard It Through the Grapevine" at Motown. "a song that seemed to be great no matter who at Motown was singing it."

# curiosity

**Nine Double Number Ones - Qualifying Rules** : Nine songs have reached number one on the Hot 100 twice, by two different acts, under the same title. The pairs are "Go Away Little Girl," "The Loco-Motion," "Please Mr. Postman," "Venus," "Lean on Me," "You Keep Me Hangin' On," "When a Man Loves a Woman," "I'll Be There," and "Lady Marmalade." Both versions had to top the Hot 100 itself, not another Billboard chart, and the cover had to be a traditional, mostly word-for-word remake. Interpolations with different titles do not count. "As long as the song went to number one twice by two different acts under the same title, it counts."

**History of Cover Songs on the Charts** : Covers began as Tin Pan Alley commerce, with multiple versions of the same song charting almost simultaneously. They continued through the Beatles and Bob Dylan's singer-songwriter era, which led to others covering their songs, and into the rap era's sample-heavy interpolation, "often a cover in disguise." "the rap era and the creation of the sample heavy song interpolation, often a cover in disguise."

**Rarity Noted by Casey Kasem** : In May 1974 on American Top 40, Casey Kasem noted that a song returning to number one by a different artist had happened only once before in 19 years. "Now this has happened only one other time during the past 19 years."

**Decade-Long Gap Then '80s Flurry** : After "Please Mr. Postman" in 1975, no double number one appeared for more than a decade. Then came three in quick succession ("Venus," "Lean on Me," "You Keep Me Hangin' On"), each modernizing the rhythm of its original. "All of them modernizing the rhythm of their respective original hit."

**Fame Over Quality** : In more than half of the cases, the cover reached number one because of the act's fame rather than the cover's quality. Donny Osmond, Grand Funk, the Carpenters, Michael Bolton, and Mariah Carey were all at an imperial high point. "In more than half the cases, the cover reaching the top had to do more with the acts fame than the cover's quality."

**Tom Breihan's Ratings** : Tom Breihan's Stereogum series The Number Ones rates every Hot 100 number one. In seven of the nine pairs he rates the cover lower than the original. The exceptions are "Venus" (Shocking Blue 7, Bananarama 8) and "Go Away Little Girl" (both versions rated 1). "In every pair but two, Tom rates the cover lower than the original."

**Last Traditional Cover to Top the Hot 100** : As of 2024, the 2001 "Lady Marmalade" was the last traditional cover of any kind to reach number one. In the 23 years since, the closest was Luke Combs' "Fast Car" at number two. "In the 23 years since, the closest a straight cover came to topping the chart, was Luke Combs' aforementioned retake on Tracy Chapman's fast car."

**Kevin Costner's Suggestion** : Kevin Costner, Whitney Houston's co-star in The Bodyguard, proposed that she cover "I Will Always Love You." "a cover that her co-star Kevin Costner proposed of a song he thought she could do a good job with."
