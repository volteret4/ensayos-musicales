**Respect (1965)** : Otis Redding wrote and recorded "Respect" in Muscle Shoals Studio in 1965. As he conceived it, the song was about a man needing respect from his partner when he gets home from work. It resonated with audiences at the height of the Civil Rights Movement, going top five on Billboard's R&B chart and cracking the pop top 40 at number 35, his second single ever to do so. "Otis Redding wrote Respect and recorded it in Muscle Shoal Studio in 1965."
## curiosities
**Relinquished Ownership of "Respect"** : After hearing Aretha Franklin's cover of "Respect," Otis Redding "relinquished ownership of the song," which is significant given his original version was already great. He publicly gave credit to Aretha Franklin at the 1967 Monterey Pop Festival. "And like Trent Rezner after hearing Johnny Cash's hurt, that man relinquished ownership of the song after he heard the cover, which is saying something because his original version was pretty great to begin with."
**Monterey Pop Festival (1967) - Praise for Aretha Franklin** : At the 1967 Monterey Pop Festival, Otis Redding publicly praised Aretha Franklin's cover of "Respect" on stage, uttering one of the most respectful lines a creator has ever said about an artist who covered him: "This song is a song that a girl took away from me, a good friend of mine. # curiosity
**Hot 100 Double-Toppers - Definition** : To qualify as a double-topping number one song, both the original and its cover version must have reached number one on the Hot 100 chart, not any other Billboard chart. The cover versions must also be "traditional covers" and "mostly word-for-word remakes," explicitly excluding interpolated reboots with different song titles. "To qualify for this list, both the original song and the cover had to have reached number one on the Hot 100, not any other Billboard chart. Also, the nine cover versions, in other words, the second hit in each pair, are all traditional covers under the old-fashioned definition. And for note and mostly word-for-word remakes of the original song."
**Hot 100 Double-Toppers - First Ever (Go Away Little Girl)** : Donnie Osmond's 1971 cover of "Go Away Little Girl" became the first song in the Hot 100's history to reach number one twice, following Steve Lawrence's original in 1963. "Donnie Osmond's Go Away Little Girl was number one the week I was born, a cruel irony for a life-long chart nerd."
**Hot 100 Double-Toppers - Longest Gap Record (You Keep Me Hangin' On)** : Kim Wilde's 1987 cover of The Supremes' "You Keep Me Hangin' On" set a new record for the longest gap between an original number one and a number one remake, at 21 years. This record held for another four and a half years. "When Wilde's cover reached number one in June 1987, she reset the record for the longest gap between an original number one and a number one remake, 21 years after the supreme's hit."
**Hot 100 Double-Toppers - Last Traditional Cover to Top Chart (Lady Marmalade)** : The 2001 version of "Lady Marmalade" by Christina Aguilera, Pink, Mýa, and Lil' Kim became the last cover song to bring a hit back to number one a second time. As of 2024, it also remains the last traditional cover to top the Hot 100 at all. "When this version of Lady Marmalade reached number one in June of 2001, not only did it become the last cover song to bring a hit back to number one a second time, as of this year it remains the last traditional cover period to top the hot 100."
**Cover Song Quality vs. Artist Fame** : In more than half the cases of double number ones, the cover reaching the top had more to do with the act's fame at an "imperial high point" (e.g., Donny Osmond, Grand Funk, The Carpenters, Michael Bolton, Mariah Carey) than the cover's inherent quality. "In more than half the cases, the cover reaching the top had to do more with the acts fame than the cover's quality."
**Cover Song Quality Ratings by Tom Bryan** : According to Tom Bryan's "The Number One's" column at Stereogum, in every pair of original and cover double-toppers but two, the cover is rated lower than the original. The exceptions are "Venus" (Bananarama's cover rated slightly higher) and "Go Away Little Girl" (both versions rated equally abysmal, a one). "In every pair but two, Tom rates the cover lower than the original."
**Interpolated Reboots Not Counted as Traditional Covers** : Chart historians do not count interpolated reboots with a different song title as traditional covers for the purpose of tracking double number ones. An example is Drake's "Way Too Sexy" (2021), which heavily interpolated Right Said Fred's "I'm Too Sexy" (1992) but is not considered a cover in this context. "They don't count an interpolated reboot with a different song title as a cover."
