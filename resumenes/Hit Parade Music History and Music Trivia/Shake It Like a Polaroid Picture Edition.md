## albums
**Stankonia (2000) - Game-Changing Album** : Released in the fall of 2000, this album was described as changing the trajectory of OutKast's career and arguably their hometown. It became a significant success, selling more copies than almost any Atlanta rap album that came before it, and rather than conforming to the mainstream, OutKast made the mainstream gravitate towards their unique style. "They would call the album, released in the fall of 2000, Stankonia, and it would change the game, selling more copies than almost any Atlanta rap album that came before it."
**Early Albums - Pre-Stankonia Work** : Prior to the release of "Stankonia," OutKast had already released previous albums, a fact they later emphasized to their growing fanbase who might have discovered them through their mainstream success. "But it was not their first album. As Outcast would take pains to tell their new fans later."

## curiosities
**Transition to Mainstream Pop Stars** : In the early 2000s, OutKast evolved from leading figures in Southern Hip Hop into major American pop acts, topping the Hot 100 charts and becoming significant cultural tastemakers. They achieved this by making the mainstream adapt to their sound rather than conforming themselves. "But perhaps the most improbable thing about Outcast was that for a few years in the early 2000s, they were also hot 100-topping pop stars, America's taste makers, the arbiters of what was cool, and what's cooler than being cool?"
**Grammy Award and Self-Imposed Demise** : OutKast achieved immense chart and cultural success, earning a very rare Grammy award, a distinction that made them the last rappers to receive such an honor. This significant win, however, ironically led to the group's self-imposed breakup. "Outcast's chart and cultural success was so enormous, they even won a very rare Grammy award, the last rappers to do so, and this win effectively brought about Outcast's self-imposed demise."
**Self-Replacement at Hot 100 Number One** : In a unique chart event during the week ending February 14th, 2004, OutKast replaced their own song, "Hey Ya!", with "The Way You Move" at number one on the Billboard Hot 100. This rare feat occurred simultaneously with their accolades at the Grammys. "And that's where your Hit Parade marches today, the week ending February 14th, 2004, when Outcast replaced themselves at number one on the Hot 100."
**Beginning of the End for Partnership** : The period around February 2004, marked by OutKast's unprecedented chart success and Grammy wins, represented the culmination of a decade-long journey for the group. However, for the partnership of Big Boi and Dre, it also signaled the start of their eventual separation. "It was the culmination of a ride Outcast had begun a decade earlier, but for the partnership of Big Boy and Dre, it was the beginning of the end."
**Vision for Atlanta as Hip Hop's Center** : From the very beginning of their careers, Andre and Big Boi shared a vision for Atlanta to become "Hip Hop's center of gravity" by the 21st century, a future acknowledged by the New York Times. "The very idea that Atlanta, by the 21st century, would become, according to the New York Times, Hip Hop's center of gravity, was one Andre and Bigboy began envisioning from the start of their career."
**Impact on Hip Hop's Sound and Geography** : OutKast significantly transformed hip hop, not only expanding its sonic boundaries but also shifting its geographical center, contributing to the genre's permanent move southward. They affirmed that hip hop would become the "lingua franca of 22nd century pop." "Outcast helped change not just the sound, but the breath of hip hop, including geographically."
**Expansive Musical Vision** : OutKast possessed a broad and inclusive musical vision, perceiving hip hop as "everything music" and a "melting pot of styles." This innovative approach led to the creation of a unique musical universe that inspired subsequent spin-off acts in pop and solidified Atlanta's status as a thriving rap scene. "But Outcast had a more expansive vision. They saw that hip hop could be everything music, a melting pop of styles."
**Breakthrough on an Atlanta Label** : OutKast first achieved success and broke into the music scene through an Atlanta-based record label. This label was primarily known in the early 1990s for its focus on urban pop music. "Outcast broke on an Atlanta label, better known in the early 90s for its urban pop."
