## albums
**Ray of Light (1998) - Trippy Yet Maternal Album** : Madonna's first studio album in four years, debuting on Billboard's album chart at number two on March 21st, 1998. It was the culmination of multiple threads in her life, released when she was a few months shy of her 40th birthday. It marked a pivot to a sound that was both mature and cutting-edge, ensuring her continued relevance and hitmaking ability into the 21st century. The album was held back from the number one spot by Celine Dion's 10 million-selling *Titanic* soundtrack. "Ray of Light was the culmination of multiple threads in the life of Madonna Louise Chaconin."
**The Immaculate Collection (1990) - Greatest Hits Album** : This album included the new single "Justify My Love." "It was the most dissonant track Madonna had ever issued as a single."
**Erotica (1992) - Sexually Bold Club Culture Embrace** : Madonna's first album to require a parental advisory sticker, and the first release of her Maverick label venture. The album had a running time of 75 minutes, making it one of the longest CDs of its time, and was her loudest embrace of club culture, featuring deep house beats. It received harsh criticism and broke her streak of chart-topping studio albums, peaking at number two for a single week and leaving the top 10 in four weeks, though it did go double platinum. "Erotica was not only the most sexually bold album of Madonna's career to date, and also long, its running time clocking in at a peak CD length of 75 minutes."
**Bedtime Stories (1994) - Pivot to New Jack Swing and R&B** : This album followed *Erotica* and featured Madonna working for the first time with collaborators like TLC producer Dallas Austin and Kenneth "Babyface" Edmonds. Despite its billing, the album's biggest hit was a traditional ballad. It also included the single "Bedtime Story," which hinted at her future direction with its ambient sound. "Bedtime Stories, build at the time as a pivot toward New Jack Swing and R&B, stories found Madonna working for the first time with such collaborators as TLC producer Dallas Austin."
**Something to Remember (1995) - Ballads Collection** : This holiday season collection compiled Madonna's romantic songs, aiming to remind mainstream audiences of her ballad successes like "I'll Remember" and "Take a Bow." It featured her remake of Marvin Gaye's "I Want You," with Massive Attack co-credited as artists. "The Madonna album that I want you appeared on, called Something to Remember, was a holiday season collection of Madonna's ballads, compiled to remind mainstream audiences of her years of romantic songs following the success of I'll Remember and Take a Bow."
**Evita (1997) - Soundtrack Album** : This soundtrack for the movie *Evita* was a very strong seller, peaking at number two in the winter of 1997 and selling more than two million copies. It featured "You Must Love Me," a new song written for Madonna, which helped the movie win an Oscar for Best Original Song. "As for the charts, Ivita was a very strong selling soundtrack, peaking at number two in the winter of 1997, and selling more than two million copies."

**Early Career - New York Clubs and Post-Disco** : Madonna's career grew out of New York clubs in the early 1980s, and she established a post-Disco brand of electric dance pop. "whose career grew out of New York clubs in the early 80s, and a post-Disco brand of electric dance pop."

By the 1990s, she had also become a mogul, co-founding Maverick, a recording and media company distributed by Warner Brothers, which became much more than a vanity label and issued top rock and pop of the decade. "By the 90s, Madonna had also become a mogul, the co-founder and flagship artist of Maverick, a recording and media company distributed by Warner Brothers."
**Focus on Acting in the 90s** : By the mid-to-late 1990s, Madonna was increasingly focused on her acting career, leading to questions about her desire to remain primarily a pop star. "She was increasingly focused on her acting career."
**The Queen of Pop's Age and Relevance** : Madonna made cutting-edge electronic music viable pop a few months shy of her 40th birthday, at a time when pop was generally getting younger. This pivot ensured her continued relevance and hitmaking ability into the 21st century. "Madonna made cutting-edge electronic music viable pop, and she did it just a few months shy of her 40th birthday."
**Early Billboard Overconfidence** : Madonna entered the Billboard charts with significant overconfidence, which was quickly rewarded as she became a hitmaker almost immediately. "Madonna entered the Billboard charts overconfident. That confidence was rewarded quickly."
**Record-Breaking Top 10 Streak** : Starting with "Borderline" in 1984, Madonna achieved 17 consecutive top 10 hits, a record that still stands. Sixteen of these were actually top five hits. "In the 1980s, Madonna was a model of consistency. Starting with 1984's Borderline, she strung together 17 consecutive top 10 hits, a record that still stands."
**Post-Chart Streak Freedom** : After "O Father" ended her five-year streak of top 10 smashes in 1989, Madonna felt freed to follow her muse even more freely in the 1990s, going deeper and darker with her artistic output. "Now freed from the need to maintain a chart streak, in the 90s, Madonna would follow her muse even more freely, and go deeper and darker."
**SNM Themes in Early Work** : Madonna began inserting SNM themes into her music videos, such as "Open Your Heart," and lyrics of lighter hits like "Hanky Panky," laying groundwork for her more explicit projects in the 1990s. "She'd been leading up to this moment for a while, inserting SNM themes into such music videos as open your heart, and into the lyrics of such lighter, frothier hits as the Dick Tracy soundtrack single, Hanky Panky."
**Sex (1992) - Controversial Coffee Table Book** : Released in 1992 alongside her *Erotica* album, this coffee table book was sold in bookstores in a wrapper and often stocked behind the counter. It contained uninhibited photographs, including artistic montages, nude images, depictions of sexual fantasies, and "Madonna in flagrante delicto with other celebrities," positioning it as perhaps the highest-profile act of sex-positive feminism to date, issued at the peak of the AIDS crisis. "The latter, a book, literally sold in bookstores in a rapper, often stocked behind the counter."
**Censure and Pariah Status in the 90s** : Following the release of *Erotica* and *Sex*, Madonna was harshly criticized and "pilloried in the mainstream media," feeling truly censured for the first time in her career despite continuing to sell millions of CDs and top the charts. "But for the first time in her career, Madonna felt truly censured."
**Billboard Woman of the Year Acceptance Speech (2016)** : At a 2016 Billboard Women in Music Awards ceremony, Madonna accepted the Woman of the Year Prize and delivered a blunt acceptance speech. In it, she spoke about being called a "whore and a witch" and compared to Satan after her *Erotica* album and *Sex* book release, and realizing that women lacked the same freedoms as men. This rare moment of public vulnerability occurred more than a year before the Me Too movement. "I remember being the headline of every newspaper and magazine. And everything I read about myself was damning. I was called a whore and a witch. One headline compared Me Too Satan."
**"Paralysis" and Creative Comeback** : After the severe criticism of her early 90s work, Madonna "felt paralyzed" and it took her a while to "pull myself together and get on with my creative life, to get on with my life." "I remember feeling paralyzed. It took me a while to pull myself together and get on with my creative life, to get on with my life."
**Adult Contemporary Stateswoman Phase** : In the mid-90s, Madonna evolved into an adult contemporary stateswoman, finding her work better rewarded on the charts when it was more mainstream-friendly, despite not compromising her artistic vision. "While hip hop and R&B were taking over as the new center of pop, Madonna was evolving into an adult contemporary stateswoman."
**"Celine Dion Territory"** : The chart triumph of "Take a Bow," with its massive adult contemporary airplay, pushed Madonna "solidly into Celine Dion territory," signaling that the public preferred a less edgy Madonna during this period. "As great as this chart triumph was for Madonna, it pushed her solidly into Celine Dion territory, signaling yet again that the public liked Madonna best in this period when she was just a little less edgy."
**Profiting from Maverick Acts** : During the late 90s team pop explosion and the electronica boomlet, Madonna and her colleague Guy Oseary were "profiting handsomely" from pop-friendly versions of rock's more extreme and independent movements through Maverick acts like Candlebox and Alanis Morissette, even as the public seemed to want a less edgy Madonna herself. "It's one of the fun paradoxes of Madonna's story in the 90s. At a time when the public was signaling that they no longer wanted her on the front lines of music's cutting edge, she and her colleague Guy Ossiri were profiting handsomely on pop-friendly versions of rock's more extreme and independent movements."
**Pregnancy and Motherhood During Evita** : While filming *Evita*, Madonna became pregnant with her first child, Lourdes Maria Ciccone Leone, conceived with her then-boyfriend Carlos Leone. She gave birth in October 1996, just weeks before the film's release. "As if this wasn't enough pressure, during the making of the film, Madonna became pregnant with her first child, conceived with her then-boyfriend Carlos Leone, a dancer and trainer from Havana."
**Critical Acclaim and Awards for Evita** : Madonna earned some of the most respectful reviews of her acting career for her title role as Eva Perón in *Evita*. She won the Golden Globe for Best Actress in a Musical or Comedy and helped secure an Oscar for Best Original Song by performing "You Must Love Me" at the 1997 Academy Awards. "Madonna won some of the most respectful reviews of her acting career, and thanks in part to the new song, her work garnered the movie some actual statuettes."
