## albums
**Like a Virgin (1985) - Chart-Topping Studio Album** : One of three consecutive 1980s studio albums that spent months at No. 1 on the Billboard album chart, along with True Blue and Like a Prayer. Its title track gave Madonna a six-week run at No. 1 on the Hot 100 in 1984–85, her career high until Take a Bow. Globally it is one of her two best-selling studio albums, ahead of Ray of Light. "all spent months at number one on the Billboard album chart."
**True Blue (1986) - Best-Selling Studio Album** : Spent months at No. 1 on the Billboard album chart. Globally it is her top-selling studio album, ahead of Like a Virgin and Ray of Light. "her third best studio album overall, after True Blue and Like a Virgin."
**Like a Prayer (1989) - Last Album of the 1980s Peak** : Spent months at No. 1 on the Billboard album chart. It produced Express Yourself (No. 2), Cherish (No. 2) and Oh Father (No. 20), and set the template for her 1990s direction. Ray of Light later edged out its global sales. "the last album of her 1980s peak."
**The Immaculate Collection (1990) - Greatest Hits** : This greatest-hits album included the new single Justify My Love, the darkest and most radical single of her career to that point. "Justify was a new single, included on Madonna's greatest hits album, The Immaculate Collection."
**Erotica (1992) - First Maverick Album and First Parental Advisory Sticker** : Released alongside the Sex book as the first releases of her Maverick label/multimedia venture. At about 75 minutes it ran to the peak CD length, and it was her loudest embrace of club culture, setting radio-friendly pop over deep house beats. The album and especially the book were harshly criticized, and she was pilloried in mainstream media. Erotica broke her streak of chart-topping studio albums: it peaked at No. 2 for a single week and left the top 10 within four weeks. It still went double platinum and produced hits including Deeper and Deeper and Rain, while Bad Girl became her first single to miss the top 30. Maverick's Candlebox debut outsold it. "Madonna's first to require a parental advisory sticker."
**Bedtime Stories (1994) - New Jack Swing / R&B Pivot** : Her follow-up to Erotica was billed as a turn toward New Jack Swing and R&B, with first-time collaborators such as TLC producer Dallas Austin. Its biggest hit was the Babyface ballad Take a Bow. Other singles were Human Nature, which addressed her critics' moralism, and the Nellee Hooper/Björk-written ambient single Bedtime Story, a sign of her future direction. "build at the time as a pivot toward New Jack Swing and R&B."
**Something to Remember (1995) - Ballads Compilation** : A holiday-season collection of her ballads, compiled after the success of I'll Remember and Take a Bow to remind mainstream audiences of her romantic songs. Ironically, it also contained her trip-hop remake of Marvin Gaye's I Want You with Massive Attack. "a holiday season collection of Madonna's ballads."
**Evita (1996) - Soundtrack** : The soundtrack to Alan Parker's film of the Andrew Lloyd Webber/Tim Rice musical. It peaked at No. 2 in the winter of 1997 and sold more than two million copies, producing You Must Love Me (No. 18) and a Latin dance remix of Don't Cry for Me Argentina (No. 7). "Ivita was a very strong selling soundtrack, peaking at number two in the winter of 1997, and selling more than two million copies."
**Ray of Light (1998) - First Studio Album in Four Years** : Released March 3, 1998, this was her first studio album in four years, made with producer William Orbit just months shy of her 40th birthday. It debuted at No. 2 on the Billboard 200 in the week ending March 21, 1998, blocked from No. 1 by the 10-million-selling Celine Dion-led Titanic soundtrack. Madonna and Orbit spent four and a half months in the studio, the longest she had ever worked on an album, building tracks from live instruments such as guitar, computer-generated sounds and obscure samples. She said she found much of electronica "clinical and remote" and wanted to put emotion back into the music, even if much of it wasn't very danceable. The album combined trance and trip hop with themes of motherhood (Little Star), her mother's death (Mer Girl) and spirituality. "They wound up spending four and a half months in the studio, the longest Madonna had ever worked on an album."
**Ray of Light (1998) - Sales, Awards and Legacy** : The album went triple platinum in the U.S. by the end of 1998 and quadruple platinum by early 2000, her best-selling studio album of the 1990s. It sold 16 million copies worldwide, her third-best studio album after True Blue and Like a Virgin, edging out Like a Prayer. It won five MTV Video Music Awards plus a sixth for the Frozen video, and the Grammy for Best Pop Album at the 41st Grammys. Its singles charted higher than anything from the electronica wave, and it may have helped open the market for Cher's Believe, Fatboy Slim and Moby's Play. "Globally, the album sold 16 million copies, her third best studio album overall, after True Blue and Like a Virgin."
**Ray of Light (1998) - 20th Anniversary Critical Reappraisal** : For the 20th anniversary, Lindsay Zoladz in The Ringer called it "a record about the anxieties of existing in a female body, one of the rawest pop albums about motherhood." Rob Sheffield wrote that Madonna spends the songs celebrating her newborn daughter, mourning her long-lost mother and reckoning with her messed-up adult self. "Ray of Light is easily the most intense pop album ever made by a 39-year-old."
**Veronica Electronica - Unreleased Remix Album** : Orbit and Madonna planned this remix follow-up to Ray of Light, meant to include the unreleased 10-minute-plus full version of the title track. It never got past the planning stage. When the title leaked to the press, it became a snarky media nickname for her, a sign the media could have turned on her as in 1992. "Veronica Electronica became Madonna's new unofficial snarky nickname."
**Music (2000) - Ray of Light Follow-Up** : This album repeated, tweaked and expanded the Ray of Light formula with French producer Mirwais Ahmadzaï. It reached No. 1 on the album chart, and its title track became her most recent No. 1 on the Hot 100. "Music did Ray of Light one better, reaching number 1 on the album chart."

## songs
**Everybody (1983) - First Single** : Her debut single reached the top three on Billboard's Club Play chart by 1983. "Her first single, Everybody, reached the top three on Billboard's Club Play chart by 1983."
**Holiday (1984) - American Bandstand Performance** : When she performed it on Dick Clark's American Bandstand in 1984, it was already a top 20 hit on the Hot 100. Holiday ranks among her longest-charting hits, behind Take a Bow. "it was already a top 20 hit on the Hot 100."
**Borderline (1984) - Start of the 17-Hit Streak** : Her first top 10 hit came about five months after Holiday. It began a run of 17 consecutive top 10 hits, 16 of them top five, a record that still stands. The streak included Lucky Star, Like a Virgin, Material Girl, Open Your Heart, Like a Prayer and Cherish. "Starting with 1984's Borderline, she strung together 17 consecutive top 10 hits, a record that still stands."
**Like a Virgin (1984) - Six Weeks at No. 1** : Its six weeks at No. 1 in 1984–85 stood as her career high until Take a Bow spent seven. "her career high watermark of six weeks set by like a virgin in 1984 and 85."
**Open Your Heart (1986) - S&M Video** : One of the music videos where she inserted S&M themes before the explicit Erotica era. "inserting SNM themes into such music videos as open your heart."
**Express Yourself (1989) - Shep Pettibone Remix** : This No. 2 hit from summer 1989 became known on radio and MTV through Shep Pettibone's remix rather than the album version. The remix premiered in the lavish video, an homage to the silent sci-fi classic Metropolis directed by future Oscar nominee David Fincher. It became the accepted hit version and signaled her growing allegiance to house and club culture. "Shep Pettybone's radical rethink of Express caught on at Top 40 Radio."
**Cherish (1989) - No. 2 Hit** : A No. 2 hit from Like a Prayer and the last entry in her 17-single top 10 streak. "the number two hit Cherish."
**Oh Father (1989) - Streak-Ending Elegy** : This deeply personal ballad about her mother, who died when Madonna was a child, stalled at No. 20 and ended her five-year streak of top 10 hits. Free of the streak, she followed her muse more freely, deeper and darker, in the 1990s. "The haunting elegy to Madonna's mother, who died when she was a child, peaked at number 20."
**Vogue (1990) - Deep House No. 1** : Her second collaboration with Shep Pettibone, an original single borrowing the structure of the Express Yourself remix, became one of her most iconic No. 1 hits. "The Deep House Classic Vogue."
**Hanky Panky (1990) - Dick Tracy Single** : A lighter, frothier soundtrack single that still carried S&M lyrical themes. "the Dick Tracy soundtrack single, Hanky Panky."
**Justify My Love (1990) - Darkest Single to Date** : Released in late fall 1990 as a new track on The Immaculate Collection, it was her most dissonant single, a spoken-word "sex rap" with lascivious lyrics such as "You Put This In Me," though she never curses. William Orbit remixed it as progressive house, which did well in clubs but got little radio play. At a time when even No. 1 singles might not go gold, it went platinum, partly thanks to Jean-Baptiste Mondino's black-and-white video. That video was so explicit MTV banned it, so Madonna released it as the first-ever video single on VHS. Strong sales of the cassingle, CD single and 12-inch took the song to No. 1. "It was the darkest and most radical single of Madonna's career to date."
**This Used to Be My Playground (1992) - A League of Their Own** : This stately ballad from the film she co-starred in was her only No. 1 of 1992 and her last for nearly three years. "she scored her only number one hit of that year and her last for nearly three years."
**Erotica (1992) - Orbit Remix** : The title track was the second Madonna single William Orbit remixed, after Justify My Love. "his third Madonna single after remixing the songs Justify My Love and Erotica."
**Deeper and Deeper (1992) - Club Hit** : A club-centric top 10 hit from Erotica. "the top 10 club-centric classic, Deeper and Deeper."
**Bad Girl (1993) - First Single to Miss the Top 30** : This lurid saloon ballad from Erotica was her first single since the start of her career to miss the top 30. "became her first single to miss the top 30 since the start of her career."
**Rain (1993) - Righting the Ship** : This ballad peaked at No. 14 in September 1993, nearly a year after Erotica came out, and restored her chart footing. That same week Janet Jackson's sexually frank If sat in the top five, suggesting other pop acts could be more ribald than Madonna. "Rain peaked at number 14 in September 1993, nearly a year after the album was released."
**I'll Remember (1994) - With Honors Single** : A pulsating, nostalgic one-off ballad from the Brendan Fraser/Joe Pesci film With Honors. It was her biggest hit of 1994, reaching No. 2 in May and going gold, even though the Maverick soundtrack flopped. The maxi single included remixes from several producers, including William Orbit. "Madonna's With Honor single reached number two in May of 1994 and went gold."
**Human Nature (1995) - Answering Critics** : A Bedtime Stories single in which she addressed her critics' moralism directly. "she directly addressed her critics' moralism."
**Take a Bow (1994) - Longest-Charting Hit** : This ballad, co-written and co-produced with Kenneth "Babyface" Edmonds, is the longest-charting hit of her career. It spent 30 weeks on the Hot 100, 27 in the top 40, and seven weeks at No. 1 in early 1995, beating Like a Virgin's six. It also spent nine weeks at No. 1 on the adult contemporary chart, pushing her into Celine Dion territory. "take a bow wound up being the longest charting hit of Madonna's career."
**Bedtime Story (1995) - Trip-Hop Experiment** : The third single from Bedtime Stories was produced by Nellee Hooper and co-written by Hooper, Björk and Marius de Vries. It followed the No. 1 Take a Bow but peaked at No. 42 in spring 1995, while reaching No. 1 on the club chart. Its ambient sound and trippy refrain pointed to her next direction. "The song's trippy refrain, let's get unconscious baby, have the strong vibe of Björk's loopy poetry."
**I Want You (1995) - Marvin Gaye Cover with Massive Attack** : Her remake of Marvin Gaye's 1976 title track went straight to the Bristol trip-hop source. Producer Nellee Hooper brought in Massive Attack, who were co-credited as artists. It was a small hit that hinted at her future, ironically released on the ballad compilation Something to Remember. "Massive Attack themselves were brought in by producer Nelly Hooper and co-credited as artists on the track."
**You Must Love Me (1996) - Evita Oscar Winner** : Andrew Lloyd Webber and Tim Rice reunited to write this song specifically for the film and for Madonna. She performed it at the 1997 Academy Awards, and it won Best Original Song for Lloyd Webber and Rice. Though out of step with Top 40 radio, it reached No. 18 on fan sales. "The estranged pair even reunited to pen a new song You Must Love Me, expressly for the movie and for Madonna."
**Don't Cry for Me Argentina (1997) - Latin Dance Remix** : Her recording of the musical's signature song wasn't a radio hit until Warner Music issued a Latin dance remix, which peaked at No. 7 in March 1997. "that version peaked at number seven in March."
**Frozen (1998) - Lead Single from Ray of Light** : This dark, moody ambient track was not her first electronic experiment, but it was her first to climb the Hot 100. A month of fast-growing airplay before the retail release let it debut at No. 8 in the week ending March 21, 1998. Within two weeks it peaked at No. 2, blocked only by K-Ci & JoJo's three-week No. 1 All My Life. Its Chris Cunningham video won an MTV Video Music Award. "Frozen debuted in Billboard that week in March 1998, all the way up at number eight."
**Ray of Light (1998) - Title Track Built from Curtiss Maldoon** : The track comes from Sepheryn, an early-'70s song by British folk duo Curtiss Maldoon, who received songwriting credit. William Orbit had worked with Christine Leach, niece of the duo's David Atkins (Dave Curtis), on a Strange Cargo track; she sang her uncle's lyrics over Orbit's instrumental, leaving the first verse nearly intact. Madonna rearranged it, made the "ray of light" line the refrain and pushed her vocal to near-shouting. It reached No. 5 on the Hot 100. A 10-minute-plus full version was never released. Its video, directed by Jonas Åkerlund, won Video of the Year at the 1998 VMAs. "The adult almost shouted, but captivating singing was one of Madonna's boldest ever vocal performances."
**Swim (1998) - Orbit/Melvoin Co-Write** : A moody track William Orbit brought to the sessions, co-written with Susannah Melvoin, sister of Wendy Melvoin of Prince and the Revolution. "a moody track called Swim."
**Candy Perfume Girl (1998) - Cautionary Drug Song** : Another Orbit/Susannah Melvoin co-write he brought to the sessions, a cautionary tale about drugs. "a cautionary tale about drugs called Candy Perfume Girl."
**Little Star (1998) - Song for Lourdes** : A gentle, ethereal ballad dedicated to her infant daughter, built with Orbit's electronic touches. "remained fully grounded in Madonna's maternal sentence."
**Nothing Really Matters (1999) - No. 1 Club Hit** : A four-on-the-floor club track from Ray of Light. In March 1999 it topped Billboard's Club Play chart while Cher's Believe topped the Hot 100. "a four-on-the-floor club jam designed for thumping discos."
**The Power of Goodbye (1998) - Torch Song** : A torch song from Ray of Light that peaked at No. 11 in November 1998. "the torch song The Power of Goodbye came just shy of the top ten, peaking at number 11 in November."
**Mer Girl (1998) - Album Closer** : A gentle tone poem about birth and death that closes Ray of Light. She wrote it after visiting her mother's grave. It may contain the darkest, most visceral lyrics of her career. "Madonna wrote it after visiting her own mother's grave site."
**Beautiful Stranger (1999) - Austin Powers Single** : From Mike Myers' second Austin Powers film. It reached No. 19 during the TRL teen-pop summer. "scoring a number 19 hit from the movie called Beautiful Stranger."
**Music (2000) - Most Recent Hot 100 No. 1** : The title track of her Mirwais-produced album became her most recent No. 1 on the Hot 100. "becoming Madonna's last two-date, number 1 hit on the Hot 100."
**Don't Tell Me (2000) - Country-Inflected Electro** : An example of the stylistic range of her later hits. "the country-eluting electrode of Don't Tell Me."
**Hung Up (2005) - ABBA Sample** : A later hit built on an ABBA sample, showing the range of her post-'80s hits. "the oba sampling hung up."

## curiosities
**Background and Cultural Impact** : Born Madonna Louise Ciccone, she grew up a Catholic girl in Michigan and was once married to Sean Penn. She popularized mesh shirts, fingerless gloves and about a dozen other fashion trends. She more or less brought dance and club music back to radio after disco's collapse in the early 1980s, coming out of New York clubs with a post-disco brand of dance pop. "the woman who more or less restored dance and club music to the radio after the disco implosion of the early 1980s."
**1984 Interview - "To Rule the World"** : Asked in early 1984 what she hoped for in her professional life, she answered that she wanted to rule the world and said she had always been confident in herself. "What's left? To rule the world."
**17 Straight Top 10s - The Joe DiMaggio Comparison** : Her 1980s streak has been compared to fellow Italian American Joe DiMaggio's unbroken 1941 record of hits in 56 straight games. In the digital era, when album cuts can also chart on the Hot 100, the conditions to break her streak may never exist. "the conditions may never exist for Madonna's streak to be broken."
**Record for Most Career Top 10 Hits** : She has 38 career top 10 hits, 21 of them after her 1980s streak and 8 after Ray of Light, the most of any artist. "Madonna still holds the record for most career top 10 hits."
**Justify My Love - First Video Single** : After MTV banned the explicit Jean-Baptiste Mondino video, which shows her walking down a hotel hallway past barely clothed sex acts before acting out her own fantasies, she sold the clip by itself on VHS. It was the first video single ever, and it sold extremely well. "released the clip as the first ever video single."
**Sex Book (1992)** : A coffee-table book released with Erotica as Maverick's first releases. It contained arty montages, nudes, depictions of sexual fantasies and images of her with other celebrities, and was sold sealed in a wrapper, often behind the counter. It appeared at the peak of the AIDS crisis and drew harsh criticism. "It was perhaps the highest profile act of sex-positive feminism in history to date, issued right at the peak of the AIDS crisis."
**2016 Billboard Woman of the Year Speech** : At Billboard's 2016 Women in Music Awards, more than a year before the Me Too movement, she recalled the backlash to Erotica and Sex: every headline was damning, she was called a whore and a witch, and one headline compared her to Satan. She pointed to Prince wearing fishnets, heels and lipstick and noted he was a man. She said the experience left her paralyzed and that it took time to get on with her creative life. "This was the first time I truly understood that women really did not have the same freedom as men."
**Shift to Adult Contemporary Stateswoman** : Through the early-to-mid 1990s, the public rewarded her most for mainstream ballads such as This Used to Be My Playground, I'll Remember and Take a Bow, rather than provocation, while hip hop and R&B became the new center of pop. "Madonna was evolving into an adult contemporary stateswoman."
**Maverick Mogul** : She co-founded Maverick, a Warner Bros.-distributed record and media company that grew well beyond a vanity label, with Guy Oseary. It had multi-platinum success with Candlebox, Alanis Morissette and The Prodigy. "It grew into much more than a so-called vanity label."
**Evita and Motherhood** : She played Eva Perón in Alan Parker's Evita, her most high-profile film role. She became pregnant during filming with her first child by then-boyfriend Carlos Leon, a dancer and trainer from Havana, and gave birth to Lourdes Maria Ciccone Leon in October 1996, weeks before the film's release. "during the making of the film, Madonna became pregnant with her first child."
**Golden Globe and Oscar Performance** : Evita earned her some of the best reviews of her acting career and the Golden Globe for Best Actress in a Musical or Comedy. She wasn't nominated for acting at the 1997 Oscars but performed You Must Love Me, which won Best Original Song. "Madonna won the Golden Globe for Best Actress in a Musical or Comedy."
**Vocal Training from Evita** : Singing Lloyd Webber and Rice's material gave her the strongest vocal chops of her career, though not at the level of Celine Dion or Mariah Carey. "Months of training and performing the Broadway caliber songs had increased her range and refined her vocal technique."
**Following Donna Summer's Lead** : Like Donna Summer with I Feel Love a generation earlier, she made cutting-edge electronic music work as pop, just before turning 40, while pop was getting younger. "Like Donna Summer did, a generation before with I Feel Love, Madonna made cutting-edge electronic music viable pop."
**Ray of Light's Roots in 1990** : Her work with William Orbit began with his 1990 remix of Justify My Love, seven years before they made an album together. "the seeds of what would eventually become Ray of Light were already sewn by the end of 1990."
**1998 MTV VMAs - Video of the Year** : Madonna and director Jonas Åkerlund won Video of the Year for Ray of Light. Spice Girl Geri Halliwell, then 27, presented the award and called Madonna her childhood idol. Ray of Light won five VMAs, and a sixth went to Chris Cunningham's Frozen video. "called the 40-year-old Madonna My Idol Growing Up."
**First Grammy for a Recording** : At the 41st Grammys, Ray of Light won Best Pop Album, her first Grammy for a recording; her only previous Grammy was for a concert video. She brought William Orbit onstage and thanked him first. "First and foremost, I must thank William Orbit, without his vision and his brilliance. The album would not be."
**Spring 1999 - Women Over 40 Top the Charts** : In March 1999, with Britney Spears and the Backstreet Boys dominating the charts, Cher's Believe topped the Hot 100 and Madonna's Nothing Really Matters topped the Club Play chart. "the biggest pop song and biggest dance song in America were by a 52-year-old woman and a 40-year-old woman respectively."
**Later Career** : All of her albums in the 2000s and 2010s debuted at No. 1 or No. 2, and she remains as big a concert draw as any classic rock act. "All of Madonna's albums of the 2000s and 2010s have debuted at either number one or number two on the charts."

## albums
**Strange Cargo (1987–) - Ambient Album Series** : Since 1987, Orbit has released ambient techno albums under the Strange Cargo name, popular with listeners from new age to club music. He kept releasing new volumes alongside his remix work up to Ray of Light. One volume includes his collaboration with Christine Leach that became the basis of Madonna's Ray of Light. "Since 1987, Orbit had been issuing ambient techno albums under a series he called Strange Cargo."
**Ray of Light (1998) - Producer** : He produced Madonna's album over four and a half months, combining live instruments, computer sounds and obscure samples, and brought in songs from other projects, including Swim and Candy Perfume Girl, co-written with Susannah Melvoin. "Orbit was an ideal choice for Madonna's next album."

## songs
**Sepheryn - Strange Cargo Collaboration with Christine Leach** : Christine Leach reworked her uncle David Atkins' Curtiss Maldoon song into a track with Orbit, singing the original lyrics over his instrumental and keeping the first verse almost unchanged. Madonna later turned it into Ray of Light. "The instrumental track was Orbits and Leach sang Curtis Maldun's lyrics on top."
**Justify My Love Remix (1990)** : His progressive house remix of Madonna's 1990 single did well in clubs but got little radio play. "a kind of progressive house as they called it then, was well received in clubs but not commonly heard on the radio."
**I'll Remember Remix (1994)** : Included on the I'll Remember maxi single, his third Madonna remix after Justify My Love and Erotica. "his third Madonna single after remixing the songs Justify My Love and Erotica."

## curiosities
**London Origins and the Early-'90s Ambient Scene** : A British producer born and raised in London's Shoreditch district, he was one of the producers and DJs behind the ambient music scene around 1990. "Orbit was among the producers and DJs contributing to the ambient music scene at the turn of the 1990s."
**Shy at the Grammys** : Madonna brought him onstage to accept the Best Pop Album Grammy and joked about how quiet he was. "he does speak English, you'd never know it."

"who started in the group as a dancer."
**The Only Real Success of Electronica's First Wave** : By the end of 1997, Firestarter was the only major radio hit to come out of the electronica scene. "The prodigy were essentially the only serious success of electronics first wave."

# genre - Electronica

## curiosities
**An Industry-Made Label** : Around 1996, the music industry grouped trip hop, jungle, techno and big beat together as "electronica." Many of the artists distrusted the term, seeing it as invented, like "grunge." "won the artists found as suspicious and made up as grunge."
**Chris Norris - "Recycling the Future"** : In early 1997, New York magazine critic Chris Norris wrote that the industry, facing slumping sales and the decline of alternative rock, was desperate for a movement to replace grunge. "1997 will be the year Electronica replaces grunge as linguistic plague, MTV buzz, ad soundtrack, and runway garb."
**More Hype Than Hits** : Apart from The Prodigy, the first electronica wave produced no major radio hits by the end of 1997. Follow-ups by Tricky and Moby underperformed, and U2's attempt with Pop failed. The term already seemed dated by the early 2000s. "Electronica was already proving more hype than hit."

# genre - Trip Hop

## curiosities
**Bristol Sound in the Charts, 1995** : Driven by Nellee Hooper, Massive Attack, Portishead and Tricky, trip hop was popular with discerning listeners and appeared on UK and U.S. charts in 1995. Madonna drew on it for Bedtime Story and I Want You. "absolutely on trend for the rise of trip hop among discerning audiences."

# genre - Electronic Dance Music

## curiosities
**EDM Replaces "Electronica"** : The term caught on in the late 2000s. Michelangelo Matos, in his book The Underground Is Massive, explains that EDM appealed to scholars and critics because it wasn't tied to a specific style and didn't recall the late-'90s major-label gold rush. "because it had no connotation to specific styles like house or Tecno."
**EDM Goes Mainstream** : By the end of the 2000s, EDM was mainstream pop. Rihanna and Britney Spears adapted it into hits, and Lady Gaga's career built on Madonna's boldness combined with EDM. "the entire career of Lady Gaga is basically unthinkable, without the mashup of Madonna's stylistic boldness with the shimmer and thump of EDM."

# genre - Alternative Rock

## curiosities
**Post-Nirvana Wave** : After Nirvana and Pearl Jam, alternative bands with harder-edged material, from newcomers like Rage Against the Machine to college-radio veterans, went platinum in the mainstream. "Nirvana and Pearl Jam may have been the leading edge of the new rock."

# genre - Riot Grrrl

## curiosities
**Mid-'90s Female Rock Renaissance** : About five years after riot grrrl began in Olympia, Washington, Liz Phair and PJ Harvey were going gold. In 1995, Courtney Love's band Hole went platinum, a year after the death of her husband Kurt Cobain. "a female rock renaissance was in full swing."

# genre - Teen Pop

## curiosities
**Late-'90s Teen Pop Boom** : In 1997, the Spice Girls' Wannabe and Hanson's MMMBop both went to No. 1, starting a teen pop and boy band wave that by 1999 was led by Britney Spears and the Backstreet Boys. "The top renaissance of the late 90s kicked off in earnest in the early months of 1997."

# genre - Hip Hop and R&B

## curiosities
**New Center of Pop** : In the mid-'90s, hip hop and R&B became the center of pop, with a softer but still bold sexual agency heard in TLC and Boyz II Men. "The center of pop was shifting toward a softer but still bold form of sexual agency, informed by hip hop."

# label - Maverick

## curiosities
**Madonna's Label** : Madonna co-founded the label and media company, distributed by Warner Bros. Its first releases were Erotica and the Sex book. It went on to release Candlebox, Alanis Morissette's Jagged Little Pill, the With Honors soundtrack and The Prodigy's U.S. releases. "Maverick wound up issuing some of the top rock and pop of the decade."
**The Maverick Paradox** : While the public was pushing Madonna away from the cutting edge, she and partner Guy Oseary profited from radio-friendly versions of rock's more extreme and independent movements. "she and her colleague Guy Ossiri were profiting handsomely on pop-friendly versions of rock's more extreme and independent movements."
