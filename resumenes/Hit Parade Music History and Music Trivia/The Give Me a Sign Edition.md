# artist - ABBA

## members
Benny Anderson
Bjorn Olveus

## genres
Pop

## concerts
Eurovision Song Contest

## songs
**Waterloo (1974)** : Sweden's winning entry at the 1974 Eurovision Song Contest, which also served as the band's debut single. It achieved significant international success, topping charts in 10 countries across Europe and South Africa, and reaching number six in America in August 1974. "Waterloo topped charts in 10 countries across Europe, plus South Africa, and even reached number six in America in August of 1974."
**Dancing Queen (1977)** : ABBA's only number one hit in the United States, which they achieved in April 1977. This contrasted sharply with their extensive success in Europe, where they accumulated multiple platinum studio albums and numerous number one hits. "Dancing Queen was their only number one hit theme."

## curiosities
**Eurovision Winners 1974** : Crowned the victors of the annual Eurovision Song Contest held in Brighton, United Kingdom, in 1974. Their win secured their place in history as the "most beloved, most famous Eurovision winner ever." "not only go on to take the crown for their country, but go down in history as the most beloved, most famous Eurovision winner ever."
**US vs. European Success** : Experienced more modest success in the United States compared to their widespread popularity in Europe, where they scored multiple platinum studio albums and numerous number one singles. In the US, their chart performance was limited to one number one hit, "Dancing Queen," and over a half-dozen Top 40 singles on the Hot 100 over three years. "across Europe, Abba scored multiple platinum studio albums and strings of number one hits."
**English Language Proficiency** : While their manager, Stig Anderson, initially wrote many early lyrics, co-band leaders Benny Anderson and Bjorn Olveus improved their English sufficiently to write more of their own songs by the late 1970s. Their impressive command of English, learned in grammar school, disproves theories that language barriers hindered their US success, especially given their 18 consecutive Top 10 singles, including nine number ones, in the UK. "Abba's English is impressive, considering it was their second language, one they had learned in grammar school."
**Music First Approach** : The band, like many Swedish pop makers, prioritized the music and indelible melodies above all else. This approach often meant selecting English words that fit the song's meter rather than strictly adhering to natural prose, although their lyrics remained memorable. "for Swedish pop makers, the music always comes first."
**Timing of US Success** : The relatively limited but strong success in the US was largely attributed to timing. Following the group's split in the 1980s, individual members achieved hits on the Hot 100 that performed as well as or better than ABBA's 1970s singles, suggesting the broader market was more receptive to Swedish pop later. "In the end, Abba's strong but more limited U.S. success was probably largely timing."

## albums
**Happy Nation (European Debut)** : The band's original European debut album. US music executive Clive Davis sought to reissue this album for the American market under a new title and with an accompanying commercial follow-up single. "Davis wanted to reissue Ace of Base's European debut album titled Happy Nation in the American Market, but with a new title connected to a follow-up single, something commercial and immediate."
**The Sign (1994) - US Reissue of Happy Nation** : The American reissue of their European debut album, "Happy Nation." This album became the number one album of the year in 1994, selling seven million copies that year alone and eventually achieving nine times platinum certification in the US. "Its American album, also called The Sign, sold seven million copies in 1994 alone. It is now certified nine times platinum in the U.S. It was the number one album of the year."
**The Bridge (1995)** : Ace of Base's sophomore album, released in 1995. This period marked Denniz Pop's increasing collaboration with other writers and producers within his Cheiron stable, notably giving special attention to Max Martin. "By the time the group's follow-up album The Bridge emerged in 1995, Dennis Pop was beginning to partner more closely with other writers and producers in his Cheyron stable."

## songs
**All That She Wants (1993)** : Originally titled "Mr. Ace," this song was produced by Denniz Pop, who streamlined the demo by Jonas Bergren and Ulf Ekberg into a "light reggae beat." Its deliberately awkward English lyrics, particularly the phrase "another baby" (meaning another lover), paradoxically made the song catchier. It topped charts across Europe in 1993 and reached number two on the Hot 100 in late 1993, holding a top three position for nearly a dozen weeks, successfully navigating a US chart dominated by grunge, gangster rap, and R&B. "All that she wants topped charts across Europe in 1993 before finding its way to storied US music executive, Clive Davis."
**The Sign (1994)** : The band's second single in America, described by New Yorker writer John Seabrook as a "three-minute, thirty-second sonic thrill ride of Swedish funk." Produced by Denniz Pop, it featured a "double-track rhythm" and a "percolating mix." It ascended to number one on the Hot 100 in March 1994, holding the top spot for a month, then falling to number two for another month, before returning to number one for two additional weeks. "The Sign was a three-minute, thirty-second sonic thrill ride of Swedish funk."
**Beautiful Life (1995)** : The lead-off single from their sophomore album, "The Bridge," and notably, Max Martin's first production credit to appear on the Hot 100, reaching number 15 in December 1995. The song showcased early hallmarks of Martin's "maximalist pop sound" and production precision, with every element serving the tempo and music, a concept he termed "Melodic Math." "Beautiful life was not only the lead-off single on Ace of Base's sophomore album The Bridge, it was the first Max Martin production credit to appear on the Hot 100."

## curiosities
**American Breakthrough with Denniz Pop** : Their unexpected success in the US with "All That She Wants" and "The Sign," produced by Denniz Pop, was remarkable because their "Swedish Europop" sound managed to overcome the dominant grunge, gangster rap, and R&B trends prevalent on the 1993-94 charts. "what was remarkable about the American success of all that she wants was that the charts of 1993 and 94 did not sound much like Ace of Base's Swedish Europop."
**Dominance in Diverse Era** : Along with Denniz Pop and Cheiron Studios, Ace of Base achieved "the seemingly impossible" by dominating the 1994 charts with "pure Swedish Europop" amidst the prevailing sounds of grunge, gangster rap, and divas. "They dominated the year of grunge, gangster, and divas with pure Swedish Europop."
**Paving Way for Pop Renaissance** : The group played a crucial role in establishing Cheiron Studios and Sweden as "the base of operations for the pop renaissance" that swept across radio in the late 1990s, despite the band itself never replicating the blockbuster success of their debut. "the group helped make Cheyron and Sweden the base of operations for the pop renaissance that would overtake the radio in the final years of the 1990s."

## songs
**We've Got It Goin' On (1995)** : The debut single by the Backstreet Boys, which was their first collaboration with Denniz Pop and Max Martin. It was a "studious attempt to sound rhythmic and street," written by Swedish songwriters. While a Top 5 hit across Europe and the UK, it peaked at a "dismal number 69" on the Billboard Hot 100 in December 1995 in the US, partly due to MTV's reluctance to play their "bright and shiny" pop. "We've got it going on. Was the debut single by the Backstreet Boys."
**Quit Playing Games (With My Heart) (1997)** : This mid-tempo ballad became their breakthrough hit on the Hot 100 in summer 1997, produced and written by Max Martin. Its romantic style proved to be an "easier sell for American audiences" compared to their earlier R&B-influenced attempts, ultimately peaking at number two. "The Backstreet Boys had finally broken through on the hot 100 with quit playing games with my heart."
**As Long As You Love Me (1997)** : A "similarly romantic" song that followed the success of "Quit Playing Games (With My Heart)," this track notably spent a full year on Billboard's Radio Songs chart, further cementing their balladry appeal in the US. "as long as you love me, which wound up spending a full year on Billboard's Radio Songs chart."
**Everybody (Backstreet's Back) (1998)** : This self-referential track reached number four on the Hot 100 in May 1998. It signaled their success with the "uptempo white funk sound" that Max Martin had originally envisioned for them back in 1995, marking a significant chart comeback. "Their self-referential everybody, Backstreet's Back, reached number four on the hot 100 in May of 98."

## curiosities
**European vs. US Reception** : Their debut single, "We've Got It Goin' On," was a smash hit in Europe, but faced a lukewarm reception in the US, where boy bands were out of favor in 1995. Band member Howie Dorough recalled the "humbling" experience of having many fans at European airports but "nobody" back in America. "We'd have tons of fans at the airport when we'd leave Europe. And then back in America, there'd be like nobody. It was humbling."
**MTV's Rejection** : MTV, a major arbiter of "cool" in the 90s, rejected their video for "We've Got It Goin' On" because it didn't fit the network's preference for "dark and edgy" youth culture, contributing to the song's poor US chart performance. Andy Schoen, then chief programmer at MTV, recalled, "we looked at that Backstreet video and said, That doesn't feel right."
**US Breakthrough** : After earlier struggles, the band finally achieved a major US breakthrough in summer 1997 with the Max Martin-produced "Quit Playing Games (With My Heart)." "The Backstreet Boys had finally broken through on the hot 100 with quit playing games with my heart."
**Balladry Appeal** : The group found that mid-tempo balladry was an "easier sell for American audiences" compared to their earlier attempts at a "wannabe R&B" sound, such as in "We've Got It Goin' On." "It turned out that this sort of mid tempo balladry, not the wannabe R&B of We Got It going on, was an easier sell for American audiences."
