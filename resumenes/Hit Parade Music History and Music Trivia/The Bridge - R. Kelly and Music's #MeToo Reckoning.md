When The Beatles' "Come Together" reached number one, "Down on the Corner" was at number nine. "down on the corner."
**Green River (1969)** : This was one of Credence Clearwater Revival's 1969 singles that reached the number two position on the charts. "green river."
**Bad Moon Rising (1969)** : Another of CCR's 1969 singles that peaked at number two on the charts. "bad moon rising."
**Born on the Bayou (Year not specified)** : Despite the title of this hit song, which suggests a Louisiana origin, Credence Clearwater Revival actually hailed from El Cerrito, California. ## curiosities
**Unique Billboard Hot 100 Record** : Credence Clearwater Revival holds a unique record in Billboard Hot 100 history: they generated the most number two hits (five in total) without ever reaching the number one spot. This is a common piece of trivia for Billboard Chart enthusiasts. "Credence Clearwater Revival holds a unique record. They generated the most number two hits without reaching number one, 5 number two hits in total."
**Consistent Number Two Hits (1969-1970)** : The band scored their five number two hits within a tight timeframe between 1969 and 1970, consistently being blocked from the top spot by other classic and sometimes minor hits of the era. "Credence Clearwater Revival scored these five number two hits in a tight time frame from 1969 to 1970."
**Origins in El Cerrito, California** : Despite their distinctive "swamp rock" sound, often associated with the American South and reflected in song titles like "Born on the Bayou," Credence Clearwater Revival originated from El Cerrito, California, approximately 15 miles outside San Francisco. This geographical paradox is a notable aspect of the band's identity. "CCR actually hails from El Serito, California about 15 miles outside of San Francisco"
**Blocked from Number One** : Credence Clearwater Revival was notably prevented from reaching the number one spot on the Hot 100 by several contemporary hits. These blocking songs included classics like Sly and the Family Stone's "Everyday People," Simon and Garfunkel's "Bridge Over Troubled Water," and Diana Ross's "Ain't No Mountain High Enough," as well as other more "minor hits." "the hits by Sly, Simon and Garfunkel and Diana Ross all prevented Credence from ever topping the hot 100."
**John Fogerty's Musical Versatility** : John Fogerty, the band's leader, is characterized as an "ultimate American hitmaker" who blended elements of rock, country, pop, and R&B into his music. His songwriting produced "irrepressible songs that were instantly catchy." "their leader John Fogarty is this really interesting figure."

**Music Industry's Slow Response to Me Too** : The music business has notably lagged behind other content industries such as movies, publishing, and television in having a Me Too reckoning. This delay is attributed to factors like the historical "mythos of the underage girl" in rock and roll, the industry's strong emphasis on freedom of speech, and the unique challenges in legally proving allegations against artists. "what is it about music that has made it so slow to change, stubborn?"
**Industry Dilemma: Freedom of Expression vs. Allegations** : The music industry faces a complex dilemma when artists like R. Kelly are accused of serious crimes but not convicted in a court of law. This creates a "weird purgatory" where the industry must weigh its belief in "freedom of expression" and defending artists' rights against moral obligations regarding actual abuse allegations. "And this guy has not been convicted of a crime. So are we going to defend our artists' rights to sing about what they want?"
**Role of Fan Pressure and Documentaries in Industry Change** : Significant shifts in the music industry's handling of controversial artists, such as R. "The interesting thing about music is that it's kind of in the ether, it kind of surrounds you."
**Ethics vs. Catchiness in Music Consumption** : Listeners sometimes "blissfully forget" serious allegations against artists when presented with a "catchy record" or a "jam." "some things may be more important than the fact that this record is a jam."
