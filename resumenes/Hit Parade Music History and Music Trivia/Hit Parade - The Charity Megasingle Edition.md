## albums

## songs
**We Are the World (1985) - Four Weeks at No. 1** : For the week ending April 13, 1985, the song began a four-week run at the top of Billboard's Hot 100, which it reached in under a month. It was the first single in US history to be certified multi-platinum, for sales of four million, and global sales are estimated at more than 20 million. Heavy airplay helped make it a national event. In the week it reached No. 1, it was both the top seller and the most reported airplay record, which was unusual for a seven-minute charity record, since such records are usually big sellers but only modest radio hits. "It was the first single in US history to be certified multi-platinum for sales of four million."

**We Are the World (1985) - Recording Night at A&M Studios** : On the night of January 28, 1985, hours after the televised American Music Awards had brought dozens of music stars to Los Angeles, Ken Kragen, Harry Belafonte and producer Quincy Jones gathered some 50 recording artists at A&M Studios in Hollywood. Quincy Jones told the stars to "please check your egos at the door," a line that has since become a cliché. The participants ranged from music legends such as Ray Charles and Waylon Jennings to relative newcomers such as Sheila E. and Moonlighting actors such as Dan Aykroyd. "Quincy Jones instructed the stars to please check your egos at the door."

**We Are the World (1985) - Song Structure and Soloists** : Michael Jackson and Lionel Richie co-wrote the song as an anthem, while Bob Geldof and Midge Ure had written a Christmas carol. It adapted the Band Aid template with one key difference: a full traditional chorus, first sung by Jackson and Diana Ross just over a minute in, which returns later as a gospel-style refrain. The running time is 7 minutes 5 seconds, nearly double Band Aid's under-four-minute track. It features 19 American soloists in the first three minutes, compared with about a dozen British soloists on Band Aid's record. "series of solos in front, choir of angels in back."

**We Are the World (1985) - Solo Showcases** : Many of the singers took liberties with the melody in the American soul tradition. Notable pairings include Dionne Warwick followed by Willie Nelson, and a one-time-only trio of Kenny Loggins, Journey's Steve Perry and Daryl Hall. Cyndi Lauper delivers the bridge just before the big chorus; she was the second-youngest soloist in the room after Michael Jackson. Five more solos come later in the song from Bob Dylan, Ray Charles, James Ingram, Stevie Wonder and Bruce Springsteen, with the last two singing a duet. "This skyscraper comes not only with corner offices, but extra penthouses."

**We Are the World (1985) - Critical Debate vs. Band Aid** : Robert Christgau wrote in spring 1985 that the song was "a good, maybe great record," while he called Band Aid's single "a bad or terrible one." British critic Tom Ewing wrote that Jackson and Richie "play things a lot safer. It was so long and cumbersome." "by any reasonably objective critical standard, USA for Africa's We Are the World is a good, maybe great record."

**We Are the World (1985) - Short Shelf Life** : The song was certified quadruple platinum in April 1985 but had largely left the radio by June. On the day of Live Aid in July 1985 it was down to No. 92 on the Hot 100, two weeks from dropping off entirely. It closed the Philadelphia Live Aid show, where cameras showed thousands of concertgoers at JFK Stadium singing along. "the day of Live Aid in July 1985, World was down to number 92 on the Hot 100."

**We Are the World (1985) - 1986 Grammy Wins and Modern Airplay** : The song won Record of the Year and Song of the Year at the 1986 Grammys. In his acceptance speech Michael Jackson thanked God and Quincy Jones and said, "remember the children." Today it does decently as an oldie: terrestrial US radio played it nearly 400 times nationwide in 2016. "At the 1986 Grammys, We Are the World won record of the year and song of the year."

**We Are the World 25 for Haiti (2010)** : Ken Kragen and Lionel Richie planned a remake mainly for the song's 25th anniversary. It gained a cause after a magnitude 7.0 earthquake devastated Haiti in January 2010. It opened with vocals by Justin Bieber and the Pussycat Dolls' Nicole Scherzinger, plus a will.i.am rap break. It was the first major charity single of the iTunes era and debuted at No. 2 on the Hot 100, higher than the original's debut, driven by a quarter million first-week downloads. It got little airplay and left the Hot 100 within a month. "We Are the World 25 was the first major charity single of the iTunes era."

## curiosities
**Inspired by Band Aid** : Ken Kragen, the manager and fundraiser who led the project with Harry Belafonte, openly admitted that Band Aid inspired America's own multi-superstar charity single. "openly admitted that bandaid inspired America's own multi-superstar charity single."

**Canada Chart Sweep** : Canada was the only country where all three national charity singles reached No. 1: "Do They Know It's Christmas?", "We Are the World" and "Tears Are Not Enough." "Canada was the one country where all three of the national pride charity singles."

## songs
**Do They Know It's Christmas? (1984) - Origin** : In fall 1984, Bob Geldof was moved by a BBC TV report on the famine in Ethiopia. Within just over a week, he and his girlfriend Paula Yates had contacted Midge Ure of Ultravox. Geldof and Ure decided to release a single for the 1984 holiday season. They chose to write an original song instead of recording an existing Christmas carol, which might have required royalty payments. They adapted a song Geldof had been writing for the Boomtown Rats, originally called "It's My World," and added a Christmas melody by Ure. "they adapted a song Geldoff had been working on for the Boomtown rats, originally called It's My World."

**Do They Know It's Christmas? (1984) - Recruiting the Stars** : Geldof began phoning the leading British pop stars, starting with Sting of the Police and Simon Le Bon of Duran Duran, then Boy George of Culture Club, George Michael of Wham! and Bono of U2. All of them got solo showcases on the final recording. "Geldoff began phoning up all the leading British pop stars of the day."

**Do They Know It's Christmas? (1984) - Solo-Packed Structure** : There is no real chorus for more than 24 bars. In the first two minutes, 11 pop stars sing verses, solo or in pairs; Sting shares his lines with Le Bon and Spandau Ballet's Tony Hadley. Phil Collins gets close-ups at the drum kit and his own cymbal-crash entrance. The verses change cadence often enough that it is hard to picture one person singing them all. After almost two minutes, blue-eyed soul singer Paul Young delivers the refrain, which is sung only twice, around the short bridge "Here's to you, here's to them," before the final sing-along. "There is no real chorus for more than 24 bars, as a procession of pop stars take a line or two each."

**Do They Know It's Christmas? (1984) - Recording at Sarm West** : The stars arrived at Sarm West Studios in Notting Hill, West London, on a cold November day and were met by cameras outside the building and inside the studio. The single came out in early December, barely a week after the session. A music video and a making-of video were released almost simultaneously, much like Michael Jackson's Thriller video the year before. "they were greeted by cameras both outside the building and inside the studio itself."

**Do They Know It's Christmas? (1984) - Bono's Line** : Bob Geldof insisted that a wary Bono sing the now-infamous lyric ("thank God it's them") exactly as written. "the now infamous lyric that Acynical Bob Geldoff insisted the wary U2 frontman deliver as written."

**Do They Know It's Christmas? (1984) - UK and US Sales** : The single sold more than three million copies in the UK and topped the chart for five weeks. It remains one of the two best-selling singles in UK chart history, behind only Elton John's "Candle in the Wind 1997." In the US it sold 2.5 million by 1985 and peaked on the Hot 100 in mid-January 1985; a countdown at the time noted more than a million US copies sold as it climbed to No. 13. Its US airplay was limited because it came out only weeks before the holidays, and it left the Hot 100 within two months. "Do they know its Christmas sold more than three million copies in the UK alone and topped their chart for five weeks."

**Do They Know It's Christmas? (1984) - Perennial Holiday Airplay** : It closed the London Live Aid concert, a Christmas song played in July. In 2016 alone it was played 19,900 times on US radio, nearly all of that in the last two months of the year. It appears every year on Billboard's holiday airplay chart and sometimes reaches the holiday top 10 alongside "Jingle Bell Rock" and "Feliz Navidad." Tom Ewing wrote that it "has stuck it out better than I thought it would, mostly because it's become a record about Christmas, not a record about tragedy." "It was played a staggering 19,900 times in 2016 alone."

**Do They Know It's Christmas? Remakes** : Bob Geldof later produced three remakes. All of them briefly topped the British charts, but none is regularly played on the radio. "including the three remakes Bob Geldoff later produced in future years, all of which briefly topped the British charts."

# artist - George Harrison

## albums
**All Things Must Pass (1970) - Triple LP Chart-Topper** : Released within the first year after the Beatles split in spring 1970, it topped the charts in both the US and UK. It outsold Paul McCartney's solo debut McCartney and John Lennon's Plastic Ono Band, making Harrison briefly the most popular former Beatle. "his triple LP set All Things Must Pass was a chart-topper in both the US and UK."

**The Concert for Bangladesh (1971) - Grammy Album of the Year** : The live triple album was credited to "George Harrison and Friends" and released in late 1971. It opens with sitar master Ravi Shankar and sarod player Ali Akbar Khan. It reached No. 2 on Billboard's album chart and won Album of the Year at the 1973 Grammys, where Ringo Starr accepted the award. Releasing it was difficult because several record labels had to clear rights for their performers. It spent about nine months on the chart, the shortest stay among 1970s Album of the Year winners, and had dropped off the chart months before Grammy night. It had no big radio hits and was never certified platinum; Nielsen SoundScan shows only 163,000 copies sold, even after a 2005 CD reissue. Highlights include Billy Preston's "That's the Way God Planned It," Leon Russell's medley of "Jumpin' Jack Flash" and "Youngblood," and a full set by Bob Dylan. "Concert for Bangladesh was never certified platinum, and to this day, it's a modest seller."

## songs
**My Sweet Lord (1971) - First No. 1 by an Ex-Beatle** : The first No. 1 single by any former Beatle. It topped the charts in America, England and more than a dozen other countries at the start of 1971, which began the biggest year of Harrison's career. It was a blatant copy of an earlier Chiffons hit. "blatant ripoff of an earlier chiffon's hit My Sweet Lord."

**Bangladesh (1971) - Minor Hit Single** : A minor hit released before the album. It was a studio recording, not a track from the concert LP. "Harrison did score a minor hit single called Bangladesh, but it was a studio recording not taken from the concert LP."

## curiosities
**Inventing the Benefit Concert** : In August 1971, at the height of his post-Beatles success, Harrison organized the Concert for Bangladesh at Madison Square Garden in New York to help starving people in the former East Pakistan. It was the first large-scale benefit concert of its kind, nearly 15 years before Live Aid. "It was the first large-scale benefit concert of its kind, essentially inventing the form nearly a decade and a half before livin."

**Bangladesh Fundraising** : For a year and a half, spin-off projects kept raising money for Bangladeshi refugees: a quarter million dollars in the first year and about 12 million dollars in total. The spin-offs included a documentary film that was a solid box-office hit in 1972. "raising a quarter million dollars in the first year and ultimately some 12 million dollars."

**Why the Grammy** : The award is best explained as rewarding Harrison's good intentions and his lineup of stars. By contrast, Paul McCartney never won Album of the Year, with or without Wings, and John Lennon won it only after his death. "the recording academy was rewarding two things. Harrison's good intentions and his cavalcade of stars."

# concert - Live Aid 1985

## curiosities
**Two Cities, One Day** : Held on July 13, 1985, at Wembley Stadium in London and JFK Stadium in Philadelphia for Ethiopian famine relief. It featured about 70 superstars, was billed as a "global jukebox," and was watched by billions. Performances by Queen, U2, Mick Jagger and Tina Turner are now seen as legendary. London ended with "Do They Know It's Christmas?" and Philadelphia with "We Are the World"; those two singles had inspired the event. "the largest concert event in history, in two cities on a single day, July 13th, 1985."

# curiosity

**Get High on Yourself (1981)** : Film producer Robert Evans, known for Love Story, The Godfather and the 2002 documentary The Kid Stays in the Picture, was arrested in 1981 for buying $19,000 of cocaine. A court ordered him to produce a public service TV special. He recruited more than 50 famous friends, including Bob Hope, Andy Gibb, Henry Winkler and Scott Baio, to sing an anti-drug song with a children's chorus. NBC aired it as a week of video bumpers between programs, and it set the visual template for 1980s charity singles. "In 1981, Evans had been busted for purchasing $19,000 of cocaine and was ordered by a court to produce a public service TV special."

**1970s Album of the Year Winners** : Stevie Wonder's Innervisions, Fulfillingness' First Finale and Songs in the Key of Life; Simon & Garfunkel's Bridge Over Troubled Water; Paul Simon's Still Crazy After All These Years; Carole King's Tapestry; Fleetwood Mac's Rumours; Billy Joel's 52nd Street; and the Saturday Night Fever soundtrack. All were million-sellers, eight of nine topped the Billboard album chart, and all produced big hit singles, which made The Concert for Bangladesh the outlier of the decade. "All nine of these other winning albums were million or multimillion sellers."

**Radio Bart Parody (1992)** : A January 1992 Simpsons episode featured Sting and Krusty the Clown on the benefit single "We're Sending Our Love Down the Well." Showrunner Mike Reiss said in a DVD commentary that it was inspired specifically by "Voices That Care." It aired the same week Nirvana topped the Billboard album chart, after which the charity mega-single nearly disappeared for the rest of the 1990s. "This Simpsons musical charity parody premiered on TV the same week in 1992 Nirvana topped the Billboard album chart."

**What's Going On (2001) All-Star Cover** : A cover of Marvin Gaye's song produced by Jermaine Dupri, recorded before 9/11 for AIDS research, with proceeds later shared with the Red Cross after the attacks. Performers included Britney Spears, Christina Aguilera, Pink, Missy Elliott, Nas, Gwen Stefani and Jennifer Lopez. It barely reached Billboard's top 30 and sold poorly. "Recorded before the 9-11 attacks and originally meant to benefit AIDS research, the single was later shared with the Red Cross."

**Music Video as Catalyst** : The charity mega-single reached its final form in 1984 because the music-video era made the recording a rewatchable event. "What makes 1984 the moment when the charity mega-single achieves its final form was the fact that the music video era was in full flower."
