## albums
**Ray of Light (1998) - First Studio Album in Four Years** : Released March 3, 1998, Madonna's first studio album in four years debuted on the Billboard album chart at No. 2 for the week ending March 21, 1998. It was held from the top spot by the 10-million-selling Céline Dion-fronted Titanic soundtrack. Produced with William Orbit over four and a half months, the longest Madonna had ever worked on an album, it was built from live instruments like guitar, computer-generated sounds and obscure samples. It came out a few months before her 40th birthday and was hailed as a triumph. "When the album dropped on March 3rd, 1998, it was hailed as a triumph."
**Ray of Light (1998) - Sales and Chart Legacy** : The album went triple platinum in America by the end of 1998 and quadruple platinum by early 2000, making it Madonna's best-selling studio album of the 1990s. Globally it sold 16 million copies, her third-best studio album after True Blue and Like a Virgin and ahead of Like a Prayer. Its singles charted higher than anything by any artist from the electronica boom up to that point. The album gave Madonna a foundation for another decade and a half of hits. "Globally, the album sold 16 million copies."
**Ray of Light (1998) - 20th Anniversary Critical Reappraisal** : On the album's 20th anniversary, Lindsay Zoladz in The Ringer called it a record about the anxieties of existing in a female body. Rob Sheffield wrote that Madonna spends these songs celebrating her newborn daughter, mourning her long-lost mother, and reckoning with her messed-up adult self. "one of the rawest pop albums about motherhood that I can think of."
**Ray of Light (1998) - Emotional Intent** : In pre-release interviews Madonna said that while she loved club and electronic music, she found much of it clinical and remote. Her goal was to put emotion back into music that had begun as dance-floor reveries, even if that meant much of the album was not very danceable. "Her goal with ray of light was to put the emotion back in this music."
**Veronica Electronica (Unreleased) - Planned Ray of Light Remix Album** : Orbit and Madonna planned a remix-album follow-up to Ray of Light under this title. It was meant to include the unreleased full-length Ray of Light, which ran more than 10 minutes. The collection never got past the planning stage, but the title leaked to the press and became a mocking nickname for Madonna. "Veronica Electronica became Madonna's new unofficial snarky nickname."
**Like a Virgin (1984) - Chart-Topping Album** : One of three consecutive studio albums, with True Blue and Like a Prayer, that spent months at No. 1 on the Billboard album chart. It is her second-best-selling studio album globally. Its title track spent six weeks at No. 1 across 1984 and 1985, a career record later broken by Take a Bow. "all spent months at number one on the Billboard album chart."
**True Blue (1986) - Best-Selling Studio Album** : The album spent months at No. 1 on the Billboard album chart. It remains Madonna's best-selling studio album globally, ahead of Like a Virgin and Ray of Light. "her third best studio album overall, after True Blue and like a Virgin."
**Like a Prayer (1989) - Last Album of the 1980s Peak** : This was the last of three consecutive studio albums to spend months at No. 1. It produced Cherish, Oh Father and Express Yourself, which set the template for her next decade. Ray of Light's global sales edged it out. "the last album of her 1980s peak."
**The Immaculate Collection (1990) - Greatest Hits** : The greatest-hits album included the new single Justify My Love. "Justified was a new single, included on Madonna's greatest hits album, The Immaculate Collection."
**Erotica (1992) - First Maverick Release and Parental Advisory** : Released alongside the Sex book as the first output of Maverick, it was Madonna's first album to carry a parental advisory sticker. It was her most sexually bold album to that point and also her loudest embrace of club culture, setting radio-friendly pop to deep house beats. It ran a near-maximum CD length of 75 minutes. It received harsh criticism and broke her streak of chart-topping studio albums: it peaked at No. 2 for one week and left the top 10 in four weeks. It still went double platinum and produced several hits, including Deeper and Deeper. "Erotica topped out at number two for a single week, and it was out of the album chart top 10 in just four weeks."
**Bedtime Stories (1994) - Pivot to New Jack Swing and R&B** : The long-awaited follow-up to Erotica was presented as a turn toward New Jack Swing and R&B. It was Madonna's first work with collaborators such as TLC producer Dallas Austin. Its biggest hit was the traditional ballad Take a Bow, made with Babyface. Nellee Hooper's title-related single Bedtime Story, buried on the back half of the album, pointed to her next direction. "stories found Madonna working for the first time with such collaborators as TLC producer Dallas Austin."
**With Honors Soundtrack (1994) - Maverick Flop** : Maverick released the soundtrack to the Brendan Fraser and Joe Pesci movie With Honors. The album flopped about as badly as the film, but Madonna's single from it, I'll Remember, was a hit. "the album was about as big a flop as the movie."
**Something to Remember (1995) - Ballads Collection** : This holiday-season compilation of Madonna's ballads was assembled after the success of I'll Remember and Take a Bow to remind mainstream audiences of her romantic songs. Ironically, it also contained her trip-hop cover of I Want You made with Massive Attack. "a holiday season collection of Madonna's Ballads."
**Evita (1996) - Soundtrack** : The soundtrack peaked at No. 2 in the winter of 1997 and sold more than two million copies. It included the new song You Must Love Me and Madonna's recording of Don't Cry for Me Argentina. "peaking at number two in the winter of 1997 and selling more than two million copies."
**Music (2000) - Follow-Up to Ray of Light** : Madonna repeated, tweaked and expanded the Ray of Light formula, this time with French producer Mirwais Ahmadzaï. It did better than Ray of Light on the album chart by reaching No. 1, and its title track became her last No. 1 hit on the Hot 100 to date. "she essentially repeated, tweaked, and expanded the Ray of Light formula with her follow-up album Music."

## songs
**Frozen (1998) - Ray of Light Lead Single** : A dark, moody track that announced a different Madonna. It was not her first experiment with ambient electronic music, but it was her first to climb high on the Hot 100. It debuted at No. 8 for the week ending March 21, 1998, after a month of fast-growing airplay before the retail single came out, and reached its No. 2 peak within two weeks. Only K-Ci & JoJo's three-week No. 1 All My Life kept it from the top. Its Chris Cunningham video won an MTV VMA. "Frozen debuted in Billboard that week in March 1998, all the way up at number eight."
**Ray of Light (1998) - Title Track Built on a 1970s Folk Song** : The song is based on Sepheryn by early-1970s British folk duo Curtiss Maldoon, who received songwriting credit. Christine Leach, niece of Dave Curtiss (David Atkins) of the duo, had reworked her uncle's track into the Orbit collaboration Zephyr in the Sky on one of his Strange Cargo albums, with Orbit's instrumental and Leach singing the original lyrics. Madonna rearranged it, turned the "ray of light" line into the refrain and pushed the vocal harder, giving an almost shouted performance. As a single it entered the Hot 100 at its No. 5 peak three months after Frozen. The Jonas Åkerlund video won Video of the Year at the 1998 VMAs. "The adiled, almost shouted but captivating singing was one of Madonna's boldest ever vocal performances."
**The Power of Goodbye (1998)** : A torch song from Ray of Light that just missed the top ten, peaking at No. 11 in November 1998. "peaking at number 11 in November."
**Nothing Really Matters (1999)** : The album's four-on-the-floor club track. It topped Billboard's Club Play chart in March 1999, the same month Cher's Believe topped the Hot 100. "a four-on-the-floor club jam designed for thumping discos."
**Little Star (1998)** : A gentle, ethereal ballad dedicated to Madonna's infant daughter Lourdes. It made full use of Orbit's electronic touches while staying grounded in Madonna's maternal feeling. "remained fully grounded in Madonna's maternal sentence."
**Swim / Candy Perfume Girl (1998)** : Two songs William Orbit co-wrote with Susannah Melvoin, sister of Wendy Melvoin of Prince and the Revolution. He had worked on both for other projects before bringing them to Madonna. Swim is moody; Candy Perfume Girl is a cautionary tale about drugs. "a moody track called Swim and a cautionary tale about drugs called Candy Perfume Girl."
**Mer Girl (1998) - Ray of Light Closer** : A gentle tone poem reflecting on birth and death, with possibly the darkest and most visceral lyrics of her career. Madonna wrote it after visiting her mother's grave. "Madonna wrote it after visiting her own mother's grave site."
**Everybody (1983) - Debut Single** : Madonna's first single reached the top three on Billboard's Club Play chart by 1983. "Her first single, Everybody, reached the top three on Billboard's club play chart by 1983."
**Holiday (1984)** : By the time Madonna performed it on Dick Clark's American Bandstand in 1984, it was already a top 20 Hot 100 hit. Her first top 10 hit followed five months later. "it was already a top 20 hit on the Hot 100."
**Borderline (1984) - Start of the 17-Hit Streak** : This song began Madonna's record run of 17 consecutive top 10 hits. "Starting with 1984's Borderline, she strung together 17 consecutive top 10 hits."
**Like a Virgin (1984)** : It spent six weeks at No. 1 across 1984 and 1985, Madonna's career high until Take a Bow. "her career high watermark of six weeks set by Leica Virgin in 1984 and 1985."
**Cherish (1989)** : A No. 2 hit near the end of her top 10 streak, followed by Oh Father. "followed up the number two hit Cherish with the deeply personal ballad O-Father."
**Oh Father (1989) - End of the Streak** : A haunting elegy to Madonna's mother, who died when Madonna was a child. It peaked at No. 20, ending her five-year run of top 10 hits. "finally ending Madonna's five-year streak of top 10 smashes."
**Express Yourself (1989) - Shep Pettibone Remix** : A No. 2 hit in summer 1989. The version heard on radio and MTV was Shep Pettibone's remix, introduced in the song's lavish video, an homage to Metropolis directed by future Oscar nominee David Fincher. The remix became the accepted hit version and signalled Madonna's growing commitment to house music and club culture. "becoming the widely accepted hit version of the song."
**Vogue (1990)** : Madonna and Shep Pettibone reunited for an original single built on the structure of the Express Yourself remix. It became one of her signature No. 1 hits. "became one of Madonna's most iconic number one hits, The Deep House Classic Vogue."
**Hanky Panky (1990)** : A light single from the Dick Tracy soundtrack whose lyrics already carried S&M themes, as the Open Your Heart video had. "By 1992, these singles and videos would look like Disney product."
**Justify My Love (1990)** : A new single on The Immaculate Collection and the most dissonant track Madonna had released as a single. Its lyrics are a spoken-word "sex rap" with lines like "you put this in me," though she never curses. Top 40 radio played it mostly because it was Madonna. It went platinum at a time when even No. 1 singles might not go gold. Jean-Baptiste Mondino's black-and-white video shows Madonna moving down a hotel hallway past rooms of sexual activity before acting out her own fantasies; MTV banned it. William Orbit remixed it in a progressive-house style that clubs liked but radio rarely played. "It was the darkest and most radical single of Madonna's career to date."
**This Used to Be My Playground (1992)** : A stately ballad from A League of Their Own, the film Madonna co-starred in. It was her only No. 1 of 1992 and her last for nearly three years. "her last for nearly three years with the stately ballad this used to be my playground."
**Deeper and Deeper (1992)** : A top 10 hit from Erotica. "the top 10 club-centric classic, Deeper and Deeper."
**Bad Girl (1993)** : A lurid saloon ballad from Erotica and her first single since the start of her career to miss the top 30. "became her first single to miss the top 30 since the start of her career."
**Rain (1993)** : A graceful ballad from Erotica that peaked at No. 14 in September 1993, nearly a year after the album came out, and steadied her chart standing. That same week Janet Jackson's sexually frank If was in the top 5. "another graceful ballad, rain, was a sizable hit."
**I'll Remember (1994)** : A pulsing, nostalgic ballad from With Honors and her biggest hit of 1994. It reached No. 2 in May 1994 and went gold. The maxi-single included a William Orbit remix, his third Madonna remix after Justify My Love and Erotica. "Madonna's With Honor single reached number two in May of 1994 and went gold."
**Take a Bow (1994–95) - Longest-Charting Hit** : Co-written and co-produced with Kenneth "Babyface" Edmonds, producer for Whitney Houston and Boyz II Men. It spent 30 weeks on the Hot 100, 27 in the top 40, and seven weeks at No. 1 in early 1995, beating Like a Virgin's six. It also spent nine weeks at No. 1 on the Adult Contemporary chart, pushing her into Céline Dion territory. "take a bow wound up being the longest charting hit of Madonna's career."
**Human Nature (1995)** : A Bedtime Stories single in which Madonna answered her critics' moralism. "she directly addressed her critics' moralism."
**Bedtime Story (1995) - Björk Co-Write** : The third Bedtime Stories single, written by Nellee Hooper, Björk and Marius de Vries. Its refrain "let's get unconscious baby" reflects Björk's poetic style. It stalled at No. 42 on the pop chart in spring 1995 but was a No. 1 club hit. Its ambient sound fit the rise of trip hop. "peaking at number 42 in the spring of 1995."
**I Want You (1995) - Marvin Gaye Cover with Massive Attack** : A cover of the title track of Marvin Gaye's 1976 album, originally a No. 1 R&B and No. 15 pop hit. Nellee Hooper brought in Massive Attack, who are co-credited. It was a small hit but a sign of where Madonna was heading. "Madonna went right to the source of the Bristol TripHop sound."
**You Must Love Me (1996) - Evita** : Written for the film and for Madonna by Andrew Lloyd Webber and Tim Rice, who reunited after being estranged. It peaked at No. 18, driven by fan sales rather than radio. Madonna performed it at the 1997 Academy Awards, and it won Best Original Song. "expressly for the movie and for Madonna."
**Don't Cry for Me Argentina (1997)** : Madonna's recording of the musical's signature song did little on radio until Warner Music released a Latin dance remix. That version peaked at No. 7 in March 1997. "That version peaked at number seven in March."
**Beautiful Stranger (1999)** : From Mike Myers' second Austin Powers film, a No. 19 hit during the summer of TRL teen pop. "scoring a number 19 hit from the movie called Beautiful Stranger."
**Music (2000)** : Title track of the Mirwais-produced album and Madonna's last No. 1 on the Hot 100 to date. "its title track, becoming Madonna's last two date, number one hit on the Hot 100."
**Hung Up / Don't Tell Me - Later Eclectic Hits** : Later hits ranged from the ABBA-sampling Hung Up to the country-tinged electro of Don't Tell Me. "from the Abba sampling hung up to the country eluding electro of Don't Tell Me."

## curiosities
**Early Biography and Influence** : Born Madonna Louise Ciccone, a Catholic girl from Michigan, she came up in New York clubs in the early 1980s with post-disco electronic dance pop. She popularized mesh shirts, fingerless gloves and many other fashion trends, was married to Sean Penn, and largely brought dance music back to radio after disco collapsed. "the woman who more or less restored dance and club music to the radio after the disco implosion of the early 1980s."
**1984 Interview - "To Rule the World"** : Asked in early 1984 what she hoped for in her career, Madonna said she had always had confidence in herself and that what was left was to rule the world. "What's left? To rule the world."
**17 Consecutive Top 10 Hits - The DiMaggio Comparison** : From Borderline in 1984, Madonna had 17 consecutive top 10 hits, 16 of them in the top five, including Lucky Star, Like a Virgin, Material Girl, Open Your Heart, Like a Prayer and Cherish. The run is compared to Joe DiMaggio's 56-game hitting streak of 1941. Because radio singles and album tracks now both chart on the Hot 100, the record may never be broken. "she strung together 17 consecutive top 10 hits, a record that still stands."
**38 Career Top 10 Hits - All-Time Record** : Madonna has 38 top 10 hits, 21 after her 1980s streak and eight after Ray of Light. She still holds the record for most career top 10s. "Madonna still holds the record for most career top 10 hits."
**First-Ever Video Single - Justify My Love** : After MTV banned the Justify My Love video, Madonna released it as the first video single, a VHS tape containing only that clip. The cassette single, CD single and 12-inch also sold well and took the song to No. 1. "it sold like brown paper contraband."
**Sex Book (1992)** : A coffee-table book released with Erotica as Maverick's first output. It contained art montages, nude images, depictions of sexual fantasies and photos of Madonna with other celebrities. It was sold in a wrapper, often kept behind the counter, at the height of the AIDS crisis, and it drew harsh criticism. "literally sold in bookstores in a wrapper, often stocked behind the counter."
**Billboard Women in Music 2016 - Woman of the Year Speech** : Accepting Billboard's Woman of the Year award in 2016, more than a year before the Me Too movement, Madonna recalled the backlash to Erotica and Sex. She said she was called a whore and a witch, that one headline compared her to Satan, and noted that Prince could wear fishnets, high heels and lipstick because he was a man. She said she felt paralyzed and that it took time to recover. "This was the first time I truly understood that women really did not have the same freedom as men."
**Maverick Co-Founder and Mogul** : Madonna co-founded Maverick, a Warner Bros.-distributed record and media company, with Guy Oseary and was its flagship artist. It grew well beyond a vanity label, profiting from Candlebox, Alanis Morissette and The Prodigy, so she made money from mainstream versions of rock's more extreme movements just as the public pushed her away from the cutting edge. "It grew into much more than a so-called vanity label."
**Evita - Pregnancy, Golden Globe and Vocal Training** : Alan Parker's Evita, based on the musical that made Patti LuPone and Mandy Patinkin Broadway stars, was Madonna's highest-profile film role. She became pregnant during filming by her boyfriend Carlos Leon, a dancer and trainer from Havana, and gave birth to Lourdes Maria Ciccone Leon in October 1996, weeks before the film opened. She won the Golden Globe for Best Actress in a Musical or Comedy. Months of training for the Broadway-level songs expanded her range and improved her technique. "she was now equipped with the strongest chops of her career."
**1999 - Cher and Madonna Top the U.S. Charts** : In March 1999, while Britney Spears and the Backstreet Boys dominated the charts, Cher's Believe topped the Hot 100 and Madonna's Nothing Really Matters topped the Club Play chart. Ray of Light's mix of romantic lyrics and digital effects may have helped open the market for Believe. "the biggest pop song and biggest dance song in America were by a 52-year-old woman and a 40-year-old woman respectively."
**1998 MTV VMAs - Five Awards for Ray of Light** : Madonna and director Jonas Åkerlund won Video of the Year for Ray of Light. Spice Girl Geri Halliwell, then 27, presented the award and called the 40-year-old Madonna her idol. Ray of Light won five awards in total, and Chris Cunningham's Frozen video won a sixth. "called the 40-year-old Madonna My Idol Growing Up."
**41st Grammy Awards - First Grammy for a Recording** : In February 1999, Madonna won Best Pop Album for Ray of Light, her first Grammy for a recording; her only previous Grammy was for a concert video. She was visibly thrilled and brought William Orbit up to accept with her. "Madonna took home amazingly her first Grammy ever for a recording."
**Electronic Pop at Nearly 40 - The Donna Summer Parallel** : Like Donna Summer with I Feel Love a generation earlier, Madonna made cutting-edge electronic music work as pop, months before turning 40 and at a time when pop was getting younger. "Madonna made cutting-edge electronic music viable pop."
**Influence on Lady Gaga and EDM-Era Pop** : Lady Gaga's career is described as depending on Madonna's stylistic boldness combined with EDM. "the entire career of Lady Gaga is basically unthinkable without the mashup of Madonna's stylistic boldness with the shimmer and thump of EDM."
**Later Career Consistency** : Every Madonna album of the 2000s and 2010s debuted at No. 1 or No. 2, and she remains as big a concert draw as any classic rock act. "All of Madonna's albums of the 2000s and 2010s have debuted at either number one or number two on the charts."

## albums
**Strange Cargo Series (1987–) - Ambient Techno Albums** : Since 1987, the London producer, born and raised in Shoreditch, had released ambient techno albums under the Strange Cargo name, liked by fans from new age to club music. One installment featured Zephyr in the Sky with Christine Leach, the basis of Ray of Light. "Since 1987, Orbit had been issuing ambient techno albums under a series he called Strange Cargo."

## songs
**Justify My Love Remix (1990)** : Orbit's progressive-house remix was popular in clubs but rarely heard on radio. Madonna and Orbit would not work together directly for another seven years. "a kind of progressive house as they called it then."
**Zephyr in the Sky - Strange Cargo with Christine Leach** : Christine Leach adapted her uncle Dave Curtiss's song Sepheryn. Orbit provided the instrumental and Leach sang the lyrics, leaving the first verse almost unchanged. Madonna later rearranged it into Ray of Light. "Christine Leach borrowed her uncle's track and turned it into the Orbit collaboration Zephyr in the sky."

## curiosities
**Madonna Remixer Turned Producer** : Orbit remixed Justify My Love, Erotica and I'll Remember before Madonna and Guy Oseary chose him to produce Ray of Light. They spent four and a half months in the studio. "Orbit was an ideal choice for Madonna's next album."
**Shy Grammy Winner** : At the 1999 Grammys, Madonna brought Orbit up to share the Best Pop Album award. "dragged up her shy British collaborator to accept the gramophone with her."

# genre - Electronica

## curiosities
**Industry-Made Successor to Grunge** : Around 1996, the music industry grouped trip hop, jungle, techno and big beat under the label "electronica." In early 1997 Chris Norris wrote in New York Magazine ("Recycling the Future") that the industry was desperate to replace grunge. "1997 will be the year Electronica replaces grunge as linguistic plague, MTV buzz, ad soundtrack and runway garb."
**More Hype Than Hits** : By the end of 1997, no electronica act besides The Prodigy had a major radio hit. The term seemed dated by the early 2000s. "the word already seemed jargonny and passe by the early 2000s."

# genre - EDM

## curiosities
**A Neutral Umbrella Term** : In The Underground Is Massive, Michelangelo Matos explains EDM appealed because it carried no specific style connotation and avoided electronica's association with the late-'90s gold rush. By the end of the 2000s it was mainstream pop, adopted by Rihanna, Britney Spears and others. "because it had no connotation to specific styles, like house or Tecno."

# genre - Trip Hop

## curiosities
**Rise of the Bristol Sound (1995)** : Trip hop gained serious audiences and some chart success through Portishead, Tricky and Massive Attack. "Bedtime's story was absolutely on trend for the rise of TripHop among discerning audiences."

# genre - Riot Grrrl

## curiosities
**Mid-'90s Female Rock Renaissance** : About five years after riot grrrl began in Olympia, Washington, Liz Phair and PJ Harvey were going gold. Courtney Love's Hole went platinum in 1995 after the 1994 death of her husband Kurt Cobain. "a female rock renaissance was in full swing."

# label - Maverick Records

## curiosities
**Madonna's Multimedia Venture** : Co-founded by Madonna and Guy Oseary and distributed by Warner Bros., Maverick released Erotica and Sex first, then broke Candlebox, Alanis Morissette's diamond-selling Jagged Little Pill and The Prodigy's No. 1 The Fat of the Land. The With Honors soundtrack flopped. "Maverick wound up issuing some of the top, rock, and pop of the decade."

# concert - 1998 MTV Video Music Awards

## curiosities
**Ray of Light's Big Night** : Ray of Light won Video of the Year and five awards overall, and Frozen won a sixth. Geri Halliwell presented the top award. "In all, Ray of Light took home five MTV awards."

# concert - 41st Annual Grammy Awards

## curiosities
**Best Pop Album for Ray of Light** : Madonna's first Grammy for a recording, shared on stage with William Orbit. "Accepting the best pop album prize, Madonna made no effort to look blasey."

# concert - 1997 Academy Awards

## curiosities
**Madonna Performs You Must Love Me** : Her acting was not nominated, but her performance helped the song win Best Original Song for Andrew Lloyd Webber and Tim Rice. "she did perform You Must Love Me on the Telecast."

# curiosity

**The Late-'90s Pop Revival** : In early 1997, Wannabe and MMMBop started a teen-pop wave that pulled the charts toward younger artists, following a first half of the decade dominated by grunge and gangsta rap. "The pop renaissance of the late 90s kicked off in earnest in the early months of 1997."
**Mid-'90s Shift in Pop's Center** : Pop moved toward hip-hop-influenced sexual agency from acts like TLC and Boyz II Men. Alternative rock went mainstream in the wake of Nirvana and Pearl Jam, with bands such as Rage Against the Machine. "hip hop and R&B were taking over as the new center of pop."
