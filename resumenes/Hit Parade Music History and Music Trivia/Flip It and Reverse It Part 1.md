## songs
**Rump Shaker (1992) - Pharrell's Songwriting Debut** : Wreckx-n-Effect's single was Pharrell Williams' songwriting debut, at age 19. Teddy Riley takes a verse on the track and assigned Pharrell to write it. That verse contains some of the song's catchiest bits, including lines Pharrell borrowed from an earlier hit by the band DeBarge. "it served as the songwriting debut of 19-year-old Farell Williams."
**Right Here (Human Nature Remix) (1993) - Pharrell's Recording Debut** : The year after Rump Shaker, Pharrell made his recording debut on this Teddy Riley-affiliated remix of SWV's "Right Here," which mixes in the hook of Michael Jackson's "Human Nature." The voice chanting SWV's name on the track is Pharrell's. "the voice you hear chanting SWV's name, that's Farell Williams."
**Like I Love You (2002) - Justin Timberlake's First Solo Single** : The Neptunes wrote and produced Justin Timberlake's first solo single. It had the vibe of a young Michael Jackson but was quirkier, like a lush 80s Jackson song taken apart. Pharrell appeared in the video alongside Clipse, a rap duo also from Virginia Beach. "as if a lush 80s Jackson song had been deconstructed and stripped for parts."
**I Just Wanna Love U (Give It 2 Me) (2000) - Pharrell's Falsetto Chorus** : Pharrell sings the falsetto chorus on this Neptunes-produced Jay-Z track. Jay-Z liked the chorus so much that he lip-synced it in the video. It reached #11 pop and #1 R&B. "It would not be the last time Ferell's falsetto was heard on a chart-topping hit."

## albums
**In Search Of... (2002) - N.E.R.D Debut** : The debut album by Pharrell's alternative rock band N.E.R.D went gold about two weeks before the week ending November 16, 2002, when Missy Elliott's "Work It" peaked at #2. "Ferell Williams' alt rock band Nerd went gold with their debut album in search of."

## curiosities
**S.B.I. Demo Tape (1991)** : Pharrell was a member of the Virginia Beach high-school rap crew and production team S.B.I., alongside his cousin Timothy Mosley, his friend Melvin Barcliff (later Magoo), and his Filipino American friend Chad Hugo. The raw 1991 demo showed promise and reached Teddy Riley. "SBI stood for Surrounded by Idiots."
**Amicable Split from Timbaland** : Pharrell and his cousin Tim amicably broke up S.B.I. and agreed to pursue separate paths after high school. "Tim and Farell amicably broke up SBI and agreed to pursue their own paths."
**Kraftwerk Rock Hall Induction Speech (2021)** : Pharrell inducted Kraftwerk into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2021. He said millions of hip-hop fans, himself included, first heard Kraftwerk through Afrika Bambaataa. He also said he had met the late Florian Schneider and told him how much his music meant. "For many of us, we were influenced by craft work without even realizing it."
**Career Built on Normalizing the Strange** : Like Kraftwerk, Eurythmics and James Brown, Pharrell spent his career turning strange sounds into mainstream pop. Within that period he fronted an alternative rock band and scored hits as a front-line artist. "Ferel Williams' whole career is about normalizing the formerly Utre."
**Rebooting Justin Timberlake** : Pharrell (with "Like I Love You") and Timbaland (with "Cry Me a River") relaunched Justin Timberlake's career after his boy-band years. "Ferel Williams and Timberland had fully rebooted the young pop stars career."

## songs
**When Boy Meets Girl (1996) - Total** : In 1996 the Neptunes wrote and produced this track for Total, Sean Combs' protégé girl group. The beat was built on a sample, but its dry, brittle drums and stripped-down production pointed to the Neptunes' later style. "the dry brittle drums and stripped down production hinted at the Neptune's future direction."
**Lookin' at Me (1998) - Mase, First Top 10** : This 1997 Neptunes track was for Mase, a rapper in Puff Daddy's circle. Its minor-key piano and synth lines became another Neptunes signature. It was the final single from Mase's multi-platinum album Harlem World and peaked at #8 on both the pop and R&B charts in summer 1998. It was Pharrell and Chad's first official top 10 as front-line producers. "giving Ferell Williams and Chad Hugo their first official top 10 hit as front line producers."
**Superthug (1998) - Noreaga, the Defining Neptunes Sound** : This Noreaga hit was built on a Korg synthesizer melody programmed to imitate the clavichord, a medieval instrument. It reached only #15 R&B and #36 on the Hot 100, but it was very influential and established the Neptunes' sound. "that nagging, proudly synthetic sound, totally unnatural and in your face, cut through the radio like glass."
**Got Your Money (1999) - Ol' Dirty Bastard** : After Superthug, the Neptunes produced this top 10 hit for Wu-Tang Clan rapper Ol' Dirty Bastard. "a range of artists wanted a piece of Ferell Williams and Chad Hugo and their uncludtered, dry, brittle production sound."
**Shake Ya Ass (2000) - Mystikal** : The Neptunes' production on this top 20 hit greatly boosted the career of New Orleans rapper Mystikal. "whose career was massively boosted by the Neptune's immortal production on his top 20 hit Shake Your Ass."
**Caught Out There (1999) - Kelis** : Pharrell and Chad wrote this as a showcase for Kelis. It was a top 10 R&B hit and a top 10 pop hit in about six countries. "affirming their status as top tier producers."
**I Just Wanna Love U (Give It 2 Me) (2000) - Jay-Z** : Released less than six months after Timbaland's "Big Pimpin'," this Neptunes production featured the duo's clavichord-style synth lines and Pharrell's falsetto chorus. It reached #11 pop and #1 R&B, a bigger hit than "Big Pimpin'." "This track showcased not only the duo's clavacord synth lines, but also the falsetto voice of Ferell Williams."
**Like I Love You (2002) - Justin Timberlake** : The Neptunes masterminded this skeletal first single from Timberlake's debut solo album on Jive. "This skeletal songs masterminds were from Virginia Beach, Virginia, a pair who called themselves the Neptune's."

## curiosities
**High School Talent Show Discovery** : Teddy Riley reportedly saw an early version of the Neptunes, then a side project of Pharrell and Chad, at a high school talent show. "reportedly caught an early version of Farell and Chad's side project The Neptune's at a high school talent show."
**Signed by Teddy Riley as a Pair** : Teddy Riley said he signed the Neptunes for both members together, not for either one alone. "It wasn't because of Farell that I signed the Neptune's, and it wasn't because of Chad that I signed them. It was both of them."
**Guns for Hire in the Mid-90s** : Although the Neptunes broke through before Timbaland thanks to Riley's mentorship, by the mid-90s Pharrell and Chad were occasional hired producers still working out their sound. "Ferell and Chad found themselves occasional guns for hire."

## songs
**In the Meantime (1993) - Recording Debut** : Timbaland made his debut as a guest rapper on this late-1993 Jodeci track from Diary of a Mad Band. It was a deep cut, not a hit. "Timbaland made his debut as a guest rapper on Jodicy's late 1993 track in the meanwhile."
**Pony (1996) - Ginuwine** : Timbaland co-created this track with Static Major. "Pony is arguably ground zero for the quirky Virginia Beach sound."
**Up Jumps da Boogie (1997) - Timbaland & Magoo** : This single reached #12 pop and #1 rap, with vocals from both Missy Elliott and Aaliyah. "the number 12 pop, number 1 rap single Up Jump's DubuGee featuring vocals from both Missy Elliott and Aliyah."
**Clock Strikes (1998) - Knight Rider Sample** : For Timbaland & Magoo's second single, Tim sampled the theme from the 1980s NBC show Knight Rider. The beat spread so widely that Busta Rhymes used it for his remix of "Turn It Up / Fire It Up," which also reached the top 10 on the Knight Rider sample. "That beat went so viral, Rap of Busta Rhymes copped it for a remix of his song Turn It Up, Fire It Up."
**Are You That Somebody? (1998) - Aaliyah** : This is described as the ultimate showcase for Timbaland's beatmaking, built from stuttering guitar licks, beatboxing, a halting rap from Tim, and a sample of a baby cooing. "the ultimate showcase for Tim Beland's beatmaking genius."
**Big Pimpin' (1999) - Jay-Z featuring UGK** : Timbaland built this track around a flute sample from Egyptian artist Hossam Ramzy's "Khusara." It reached #18 on the Hot 100 and #6 R&B in summer 2000. "it was a paragon of Tim's clever way with an unconventional sample."
**Try Again (2000) - Aaliyah** : From the Romeo Must Die soundtrack, this was Aaliyah's first Hot 100 #1. Timbaland raps on it about giving her a beat. "on which producer Timbaland wrapped about giving her a dope beat to stumped."
**Get Ur Freak On (2001) - Missy Elliott** : On this track Timbaland pushed his use of global sounds well beyond "Big Pimpin'." "Tim made Big Pimpin seem almost conventional."
**Cry Me a River (2002) - Justin Timberlake** : Timbaland produced the second single from Timberlake's debut solo album. "The recording was a cathedral of weird, it lurched, gurgled, pained, and word, punctuated by synthesized orchestral bursts and a virtual choir."
**Work It (2002) - Missy Elliott featuring Timbaland** : This peaked at #2 on the Hot 100 in the week ending November 16, 2002, during the Virginia Beach producers' peak period. "everything they touched turned to platinum."

## curiosities
**Real Name and Family Tie** : Timbaland was born Timothy Mosley. He is Pharrell Williams' cousin, and they were in S.B.I. together in high school. "The Handiwork of the Man Born Timothy Mosley known professionally as Timberland."
**DJ Timmy Tim, Bedroom Studio Rat** : Even more of a studio rat than Pharrell, Tim set up a mixing board in his bedroom and worked on beats constantly when he wasn't DJing parties as DJ Timmy Tim. "The future Tim Beland was even more of a studio rat than Farell."
**The Emperor of Sound Memoir - New Jack Swing Admission** : In his memoir The Emperor of Sound, Timbaland admits he would probably have started making New Jack Swing tracks like Teddy Riley's if he could have afforded an E-mu sampler and an 808 drum machine. "if he could have afforded an E-MU sampler and an 808 drum machine."
**Named After a Hiking Boot** : DeVante Swing nicknamed Tim "Timbaland" after the Timberland hiking boot, which had become popular in Black neighborhoods in the early 90s. "nobody outside of Virginia Beach knew DJ Timmy Tim. Timbaland writes in his memoir. But everybody knew Timbalands."
**Underpaid Apprenticeship with DeVante Swing** : For nearly three years in DeVante Swing's circle in Teaneck, New Jersey, Tim and Missy built up studio hours and developed signature styles that never appeared on Jodeci records. They were underpaid and often ignored. "I'll always give credit to Devante for what I learned. The problem was that we were not consistently compensated."
**Overhearing Caught Out There** : While Timbaland and Missy were working in one room at Master Sound Studios in Virginia Beach, the Neptunes were recording Kelis next door. Two decades later, sharing a stage with Pharrell, Tim recalled listening through the door. "And I was like, oh man, I wish I had made that beat."
**"Baby Girl"** : Timbaland's affectionate nickname for Aaliyah was "Baby Girl." He wove a baby's cooing into "Are You That Somebody?" as a tribute to her. "a sample of a baby cooling that Tim wove into the rhythm in homage to Baby Girl, Alia."

## albums
**Supa Dupa Fly (1997) - Debut Album** : Elektra had signed and then shelved Sista in 1994. It now signed Missy as a solo artist under the name Missy "Misdemeanor" Elliott, with Timbaland as sole producer. The album was full of unpredictable arrangements and stuttering breakbeats, and Missy moved between singing and rapping within songs. Released in July 1997, it debuted at #3, went platinum quickly, produced four R&B and pop hits, and placed 6th in the 1997 Village Voice Pazz & Jop poll. AllMusic's Steve Huey called it "a boundary-shattering postmodern masterpiece." "they had rebooted hip hop in their oddball image."
**Da Real World (1999) - Second Album** : This album was full of hits, guests and attitude. Its biggest single was "Hot Boyz," and its lead single was "She's a Bitch." The transcript states that Missy produced all of its hits, and it describes her bond with Timbaland as stronger than ever. "Elliott's bond with Timbaland was as strong as ever."
**Miss E... So Addictive (2001) - Third Album** : Recorded with Timbaland within a year of Da Real World. Its lead single, "Get Ur Freak On," may have been the pair's most progressive track so far. "might well have been the most advanced progressive track the pair had produced yet."

## songs
**First Move - Fayze** : An early recording by Missy's Portsmouth girl group Fayze, produced by Timbaland. "This recording phases first move was produced by Tim Beland."
**Won't Waste You (1993) - Jodeci Guest Spot** : Missy had a guest spot on this track from Jodeci's Diary of a Mad Band. It was a deep cut, not a hit. "On that same Jodicy album, Diary of a Mad Band, Missy got a guest spot too on Won't Waste You."
**Brand New (1994) - Sista** : This hip-hop soul track in the style of Mary J. Blige was co-produced by DeVante Swing and Timbaland and sung by Missy. It reached only #84 on the R&B chart in late summer 1994, and Elektra shelved the Sista album. "Brand new reached a modest number 84 on the R&B chart in the late summer of 1994."
**The Things That You Do (Remix) (1996) - Gina Thompson** : As Missy shifted from singing to rapping, she contributed a guest verse with a nonsense lyric to this remix of Gina Thompson's single. It fits the rock and roll tradition of rhythmic gibberish like "A-wop-bop-a-loo-bop." "wasn't even really English."
**If Your Girl Only Knew (1996) - Aaliyah** : Missy wrote the lyrics and Timbaland produced. It reached #1 R&B and #11 pop in fall 1996. "the lyrics that revealed a woman's innermost thoughts, Aliyah loved it instantly."
**Steelo (1996) - 702** : Missy contributed to the debut album by R&B trio 702 and provided a featured rap on this single, which reached #12 R&B and #32 pop. "She even provided a featured rap on their number 12 R&B, number 32 pop hit, Steela."
**The Rain (Supa Dupa Fly) (1997)** : The album's centerpiece reworks Ann Peebles' 1973 Memphis soul hit "I Can't Stand the Rain," and its parenthetical gave the album its title. It was released only to radio, not as a retail single, so it couldn't chart on the Hot 100 and appeared only on the R&B chart as a B-side. Its influence came mostly from the Hype Williams video. "The Rain Super dupe Fly was only a modest hit... But its influence was profound."
**The Rain Video - Inflatable Suit** : In Hype Williams' video, Missy danced in an inflatable patent leather suit resembling a billowing trash bag, made to look even bigger by a fisheye lens. Instead of trying to fit the slim R&B diva mold, she made herself extra large and deliberately strange. Rolling Stone recently ranked it 16th among the greatest videos, between videos by Duran Duran and the White Stripes. "Missy made herself extra large, deliberately freaky and visually arresting."
**Sock It 2 Me (1997) - featuring Da Brat** : One of the four hits from Supa Dupa Fly. "including the Debrat featuring Socket Toomey."
**Hit 'Em wit da Hee (1997)** : A Supa Dupa Fly hit that built on the nonsense lyric Missy had introduced on the Gina Thompson remix a year earlier. "which built on the nonsense lyrics Missy had coined on the Gina Thompson remix just one year earlier."
**Make It Hot (1998) - Nicole Wray** : The first hit on Missy's label The Goldmind, which was distributed by Elektra. It reached #5 pop and #2 R&B for Nicole Wray, who recorded as Nicole and, like Missy, came from Portsmouth, Virginia. Missy produced most of Nicole's album. "it produced a hit quickly with Make It Hot."
**Where My Girls At (1999) - 702** : Missy co-wrote and produced this for 702, the trio that had helped her break through in 1996. It reached #4 pop and #3 R&B in summer 1999. "They took the Elliott Pend Where My Girls At, to number 4 pop, number 3 R&B in the summer of 1999."
**Hot Boyz (1999)** : Missy leaned further into sass and sexual frankness on this top 5 pop, #1 R&B hit from late 1999, the biggest single from Da Real World and her first top 10. "Missy doubled down on not only the sass, but the sexual freakiness."
**She's a Bitch (1999)** : The lead single from Da Real World used the expletive as both an insult and a compliment, and the "bitch" was often Missy herself. "used the titular expletive as both a term of scorn and admiration."
**Get Ur Freak On (2001)** : A mix of global sounds: Japanese phrases, bhangra beats, a sample of a German record that itself sampled Punjabi singer Master Dilbahar, and even a moment where Missy audibly spits. It reached #7 on the Hot 100 in June 2001, her second top 10 after "Hot Boyz." It ranked first among singles in the 2001 Pazz & Jop poll, and Rolling Stone's 2021 list of the 500 greatest songs ranked it 8th, just above the Beach Boys and Fleetwood Mac. "even after 20 years, the magazine said, it still sounds like the future."
**One Minute Man (2001)** : Released about a month after "Get Ur Freak On," this song about female sexual satisfaction reached #15. "one-minute man was a shameless ode to female sexual gratification."
**I Care 4 U (2001) - Aaliyah** : Missy wrote this track and Timbaland produced it. After Aaliyah's death, radio stations and fans focused on it without any push from the label. "With no prompting from the record label, radio stations and grieving fans honed in on a track in the middle of the album called I Care For You."
**Work It (2002) - featuring Timbaland** : Peaked at #2 on the Hot 100 in the week ending November 16, 2002. "work it by Missy Elliott featuring Tim Beland reached its peak of number two on the Hot 100."

## curiosities
**Meeting Timbaland** : Magoo introduced Tim to Missy, a singer from Portsmouth, Virginia, in the girl group Fayze. In his memoir, Tim describes her as someone the industry would overlook, and he saw her taking his beats apart in her head the way he did. "She was pulling the music apart and putting it back together in her head the same way that I did."
**Unshakable Confidence** : Missy was convinced she and Tim were destined for stardom, and she promoted his beats whenever she promoted Fayze. At her insistence, DeVante Swing signed Tim along with her group. "at Missy's insistence also signed up their beatmaker, Tim Mosley."
**Ambassador of the Virginia Beach Sound** : Missy grew up in the Hampton Roads area and worked as both artist and producer. She was Timbaland's professional partner and muse, and together they brought unusual sounds into the top 10. "smuggling in some of the most delightfully weird sounds ever to make the top 10."
**Superstar and Mogul** : After Supa Dupa Fly, Missy produced and sang guest parts for artists ranging from Melanie B to Whitney Houston. She then launched her own label, The Goldmind. Her sound was rooted in lyrics and attitude: women expressing sexual agency and confidence. "The Missy Elliott sound, such as it was, was rooted more in lyrics and attitude."
**Mutual Promotion with Timbaland (1996)** : After both left DeVante Swing (Missy first, then Tim), each talked up the other to industry contacts and helped get the other work. "Each was talking up the other to their acquaintances in the business, helping to get each other gigs."
**Grief After Aaliyah** : Aaliyah's death deeply affected Missy and Tim and weighed on their work for years. It tested their friendship. "The loss would test their friendship and dig an emotional hole that would take years to climb out of."

# artist - Aaliyah

## genres

## albums
**One in a Million (1996) - Second Album** : Having cut ties with R. Kelly and her first label, Aaliyah met Timbaland and Missy in a Detroit studio and asked for an edgy, off-center sound. Tim and Missy stayed in Detroit and wrote and produced about half the album. The title track, a second Tim/Missy hit, gave the album its name. It went double platinum and made Tim and Missy in-demand hitmakers. "I really want that edgy off-center style for my next album. I don't want to play it safe."
**Aaliyah (2001) - Self-Titled Third Album** : Released in July 2001 with several Timbaland collaborations, including "More Than a Woman." It debuted at #2 on the Billboard 200, her highest chart position to that point. After her death it rose to #1, just days before 9/11. "The self-titled Alia album rose to number one on the Billboard 200 just after the tragedy."

## songs
**If Your Girl Only Knew (1996)** : The first single from One in a Million, with a Timbaland beat and Missy lyrics. It reached #1 R&B and #11 pop in fall 1996. "The rumbling, loathing beat, the lyrics that revealed a woman's innermost thoughts, Aliyah loved it instantly."
**One in a Million (1996)** : The second Tim and Missy hit from the album, and its title track. "a second hit by Tim and Missy gave the Aliyah album its title, One in a Million."
**Are You That Somebody? (1998)** : Recorded in one night to meet a deadline for the soundtrack to Eddie Murphy's Dr. Dolittle. The beat sounded futuristic and nearly impossible to sing over, but Aaliyah's start-stop vocals held it together. Because of the "war against the single," it charted on airplay charts only: #4 on Hot 100 Airplay and #1 for eight weeks on R&B Airplay. It stayed on the charts for nearly eight months, became her most enduring hit, and confirmed Timbaland as the most cutting-edge producer of the time. "Alia's fluttery start-stop vocals floated above the beat and tied together are you that somebody."
**Try Again (2000)** : From the Romeo Must Die soundtrack, this was her first Hot 100 #1, with a Timbaland rap. "Alia scored her first ever Hot 100 number one with try it again."
**We Need a Resolution (2001)** : The lead single from Aaliyah. It sampled a duduk from a film score to give the track a Middle Eastern feel, continuing Timbaland's global theme from his work with Jay-Z and Missy. "continued the globe-trotting theme of Tim's recent work with Jay-Z and Missy Elliott."
**More Than a Woman (2001)** : A Timbaland track planned as a future single. Aaliyah shot its video in Los Angeles in August 2001. "a planned future single for which Alia shot a music video in Los Angeles in August 2001."
**Rock the Boat (2001)** : After the LA shoot, Aaliyah flew to the Bahamas to film this video. The fatal crash happened on the return trip. "it was on that trip coming back from the shoot that tragedy struck."
**I Care 4 U (2001)** : Written by Missy and produced by Timbaland, the two people whose biggest break in 1996 came through Aaliyah. Fans and radio picked it up after her death. "the two creators who'd gotten their biggest break in 1996 thanks to the teenager they called Baby Good."

## curiosities
**Platinum at 15 and the R. Kelly Marriage** : By 1996 Aaliyah had a platinum album at 15. She had also been briefly and secretly married, illegally and underage, to that album's mastermind, R. Kelly. "She'd also been briefly secretly married, illegally and underage to that first album's mastermind, R Kelly."
**Instant Family with Tim and Missy** : Tim and Missy liked Aaliyah immediately, and she felt like family to them. "She felt instantly like family to them."
**Film Debut - Romeo Must Die (2000)** : Aaliyah became a cross-media star, making her film debut alongside martial artist Jet Li in the action romance Romeo Must Die. "In 2000, Alia made her movie debut alongside martial artist Jet Li in the action romance Romeo Must Die."
**Plane Crash (August 25, 2001)** : Aaliyah and her entourage boarded an overloaded twin-engine Cessna that crashed shortly after takeoff from the Bahamas. The pilot and all eight passengers were killed. She was 22. "The pilot and all eight passengers were killed. Alia was just 22 years old."

## songs
**I Want Her (1987) - Keith Sweat** : A key Teddy Riley production. "the seminal 1987 Keith Sweat hit, I Want Her."

## curiosities
**Inventor of New Jack Swing** : In the mid to late 80s, the New York songwriter-producer pioneered New Jack Swing, a blend of R&B and rap production styles. "a blend of R&B and rap production styles that made hip hop palatable on pop and even black radio."
**Move to Virginia Beach** : In the early 90s Riley, a Harlem native, left New York City for Virginia Beach and set up a small studio in a residential neighborhood. This catalyzed the Virginia Beach scene, which barely existed before he arrived. "This would prove to be a catalyst to the entire Virginia Beach scene, which wasn't really a scene before Riley arrived."
**Mentoring the Local Teens** : The S.B.I. demo reached Riley, and he let the teenagers hang around his studio. Pharrell and Chad learned syncopated hooks and thumping beats from him. Magoo later told Red Bull Music Academy how much it mattered to have a superstar nearby. "Teddy was huge. He inspired us because having someone locally where you could ride by their studio was great."
**"We Were All Technicians"** : Riley's description to Red Bull Music Academy of the Virginia Beach crew. "we were all technicians in Virginia."

# genre - Virginia Beach Sound

## curiosities
**The "Tidewater Tick" Rhythm** : The Virginia Beach sound was syncopated and spacious, built from swooping, skittering, almost cartoonish sounds that Pharrell, Timbaland and Missy made into mainstream pop. Ginuwine's "Pony" is called its ground zero. "help pioneer the so-called tidewater tick rhythm sound."
**Retraining Pop Ears** : By 2001, the Virginia Beach producers had made even tracks as strange as "Get Ur Freak On" radio-friendly. "Missy, Timbaland, and by extension, the Neptune's had retrained pop fans' ears to embrace the weird."
**25 Years of Hits** : Pharrell, Timbaland and Missy shaped R&B, hip hop and teen pop for about a decade before 2002, and kept scoring hits into the 2010s. "Hitmakers who, through the confidence of their productions, changed the sound of chart bound pop and kept scoring hits deep into the 2010."
