# artist - Justin Timberlake

## labels

## albums
**Debut Album (2002)** : In the summer of 2002, Jive Records was preparing the debut album for Justin Timberlake, a high-priority new solo artist who had recently left a blockbuster boy band, aiming to establish his own distinct adult sound. "Jive Records was setting up the debut album from a very high priority new solo artist who had just emerged from a blockbuster boy band."

## songs
**Like I Love You (2002)** : This was Justin Timberlake's first single, chosen by Jive Records for its debut album. The track evoked the vibe of a young Michael Jackson but was notably quirkier, described as a lush 80s Jackson song deconstructed and stripped for parts. It was masterminded by The Neptunes, with Pharrell Williams even appearing in Timberlake's music video alongside rappers Clipse. "For the first single, they went with a track that had the vibe of a young Michael Jackson. But the song, like I love you, was much quirkier than that, as if a lush 80s Jackson song had been deconstructed and stripped for parts."
**Cry Me a River (2002)** : Justin Timberlake's second single, produced by Timbaland, demonstrated a spicy, avant-garde, and exceptionally catchy sound. The recording was characterized as a "cathedral of weird," with lurching, gurgling, and pained sounds, punctuated by synthesized orchestral bursts and a virtual choir. This track, along with "Like I Love You," significantly rebooted Timberlake's career, as nothing from his boy band past sounded similar. "The recording was a cathedral of weird, it lurched, gurgled, pained, and word, punctuated by synthesized orchestral bursts and a virtual choir."

## curiosities
**Career Reboot (2002)** : Following his departure from a blockbuster boy band, Justin Timberlake's solo career was entirely rebooted by the innovative production work of Pharrell Williams (with The Neptunes) and Timbaland. These two cutting-edge Virginia Beach producers crafted a bespoke adult sound for him, radically different from his past work. "Ferel Williams and Timberland had fully rebooted the young pop stars career."

## songs
**When Boy Meets Girl (1996)** : This track, produced and written by The Neptunes for Total, featured a beat built from a B.G. sample. Its dry, brittle drums and stripped-down production offered an early hint of the distinctive direction The Neptunes' sound would take in the future. "And though the beat on Totals when Boy meets Girl was built out of a BG sample, the dry brittle drums and stripped down production hinted at the Neptune's future direction."
**Lookin' at Me (1997)** : Produced for rapper Mase, this track further evolved The Neptunes' sound by incorporating minor key piano and synth lines, which became a signature element of their style. It was the final single from Mase's multi-platinum "Harlem World" album, peaking at number 8 on both the pop and R&B charts in the summer of 1998, marking their first official top 10 hit as frontline producers. "Its minor key piano and synth lines became another Neptune's sonic signature."
**SuperThug (1998)** : Produced for rapper Noreaga, "SuperThug" was pivotal in firmly establishing The Neptunes' signature sound. The track was built around a distinctive melody line created using a Corg synthesizer programmed to emulate a clavichord, producing a "nagging, proudly synthetic sound" that sharply cut through radio broadcasts. Although it reached number 15 on the R&B chart and number 36 on the Hot 100, its influence was profound, leading many artists to seek out Pharrell Williams and Chad Hugo's uncluttered, dry, and brittle production style. "Super Thug was built out of a melody line from a Corg synthesizer programmed to emulate the medieval instrument, the clavacord, that nagging, proudly synthetic sound, totally unnatural and in your face, cut through the radio like glass."
**Got Your Money (1999)** : This top 10 hit was produced by The Neptunes for Wu-Tang Clan rapper Ol' Dirty Bastard, demonstrating the widespread demand for their distinct production sound following the influence of tracks like "SuperThug." "including Wu Tang Clan rapper Olderty Bastard for whom the duo produced the top 10 hit Got Your Money."
**Shake Ya Ass (1999)** : The Neptunes' "immortal production" on this track significantly boosted the career of New Orleans rapper Mystikal, achieving a top 20 hit. This further solidified their status as sought-after producers. "And New Orleans rapper Mystical, whose career was massively boosted by the Neptune's immortal production on his top 20 hit Shake Your Ass."
**Caught Out There (1999)** : Written by Pharrell Williams and Chad Hugo as a showcase for Kelis (Colise Rogers), this song was an unconventional R&B track with the aggressive swagger of a hip-hop beef record and the raw crunch of alternative rock, serving as a diatribe against cheating men. Kelis, despite her sweet singing voice, "literally shouts the chorus." It became a top 10 R&B hit in 1999 and a top 10 pop hit in half a dozen countries worldwide, marking a significant artistic victory for both Kelis and The Neptunes and affirming their status as top-tier producers. "What out there was a diatribe against cheating men, an unconventional R&B track with the swagger of a hip-hop beef record and the crunch of alternative rock."
**I Just Wanna Love U (Give It 2 Me) (2000)** : Produced for Jay-Z, this track prominently featured The Neptunes' signature clavichord synth lines and Pharrell Williams' distinctive falsetto voice, who sang the chorus. Jay-Z himself was so fond of the chorus that he lip-synced it in the song's video. This song became an even bigger hit for Jay-Z than "Big Pimpin'," reaching number 11 pop and number 1 R&B. "This track showcased not only the duo's clavacord synth lines, but also the falsetto voice of Ferell Williams, who sang the chorus."
**Like I Love You (2002)** : The first single for Justin Timberlake's solo debut, The Neptunes produced this track to give him a unique adult sound. It was noted for its quirky, deconstructed 80s Michael Jackson vibe, and Pharrell Williams appeared in the song's video alongside Clipse. "This skeletal songs masterminds were from Virginia Beach, Virginia, a pair who called themselves the Neptune's."

## curiosities
**Early Mentorship (Early 1990s)** : Teddy Riley, having moved to Virginia Beach, mentored Pharrell Williams and Chad Hugo, allowing them to frequent his studio. Riley also reportedly saw an early version of The Neptunes perform at a high school talent show. They learned from him how to produce syncopated hooks and thumping beats, leading Riley to sign them as a "great production team." "Riley began letting the teenagers hang around his studio."
**Signature Sound Development (Late 1990s)** : By 1998, The Neptunes had definitively established their "unclustered, dry, brittle production sound" with tracks like Noreaga's "SuperThug," which used a Corg synthesizer emulating a clavichord. This sound became highly influential, making them in-demand producers across the music industry. "Suddenly, a range of artists wanted a piece of Ferell Williams and Chad Hugo and their uncludtered, dry, brittle production sound."
**Timbaland's Admiration (Late 1990s)** : While recording in Master Sound Studios in Virginia Beach, Timbaland overheard The Neptunes working on Kelis's "Caught Out There" in the adjacent studio and was greatly impressed by the beat, wishing he had created it himself. "And I was like, oh man, I wish I had made that beat. That was the beat. I didn't make it, but Ferell, I was like, oh this is crazy."
**Chart Weirdness and Reinvention (2000s)** : The Neptunes, alongside Timbaland and Missy Elliott, were credited with "retraining pop fans' ears to embrace the weird," demonstrating how initially unconventional sounds could become mainstream hits. They contributed to keeping the charts "weird" and reinventing the sound of pop for multiple generations. "by 2001, Missy, Timbaland, and by extension, the Neptune's had retrained pop fans' ears to embrace the weird."

## curiosities
**Virginia Beach Origins (Early 1990s)** : Born Timothy Mosley, Timbaland was a Virginia Beach high schooler who formed the rap crew/production team SBI (Surrounded by Idiots) with his cousin Pharrell Williams, Melvin Barcliff (Magoo), and Chad Hugo. Their raw 1991 demo tape, featuring fairly catchy beats, was eventually discovered by Teddy Riley. "This is a 1991 demo tape by SBI. **Studio Rat and DJ Timmy Tim (Early-Mid 1990s)** : After high school, Timbaland pursued his own path, becoming an avid studio enthusiast. He set up a mixing board in his bedroom and relentlessly worked on beats, while also DJing parties under the name DJ Timmy Tim. In his memoir, "The Emperor of Sound," he noted that he likely would have produced New Jack Swing tracks similar to Teddy Riley's if he had been able to afford an E-MU sampler and an 808 drum machine. "The future Tim Beland was even more of a studio rat than Farell. He set up a mixing board in his bedroom and worked on beats endlessly when he wasn't DJing parties as DJ Timmy Tim."
**Finding Missy Elliott (Early 1990s)** : Timbaland's beat-making hobby transformed into a professional partnership after his friend Magoo introduced him to an ambitious vocalist from Portsmith, Virginia, named Missy Elliott, who was singing in a girl group called Phase. He immediately recognized a kindred spirit in her ability to dissect and reassemble music. "Tim's beat making might have remained a hobby. If his pal Magoo hadn't introduced him to an ambitious vocalist from Port Smith, Virginia, who was singing in a girl group called Phase F-A-Y-Z-E. Her name was Missy Elliott."
**Apprenticeship with Devanté Swing (Mid 1990s)** : Following Missy Elliott's insistence, Devanté Swing signed Tim Mosley (Timbaland) to his production house, Debasement, moving him from Virginia Beach to an apartment near his Teaneck, New Jersey studio. Devanté also bestowed upon him the nickname "Timbaland" after the popular hiking shoe. For nearly three years, Timbaland toiled in Debasement, accumulating studio hours and developing unique sonic styles, although he was often unheralded, underpaid, and ignored by Devanté, leading to his eventual departure in 1996. "Nobody outside of Virginia Beach knew DJ Timmy Tim. Timbaland writes in his memoir. But everybody knew Timbalands. So the nickname stuck."
**Aaliyah's Request for a New Sound (1996)** : After cutting ties with R. Kelly and her first album's label, Aaliyah sought a completely new sound and approached Timbaland and Missy Elliott in a Detroit studio. She expressed her desire for their "edgy off-center style" for her next album, emphasizing that she did not want to "play it safe." "I really want that edgy off-center style for my next album. I don't want to play it safe."
**Night Rider Theme Sample (1997-1998)** : For a single from his "Welcome to Our World" album with Magoo, Timbaland ingeniously sampled the theme to the 80s NBC TV hit "Night Rider." This beat became so popular that rapper Busta Rhymes later incorporated it into a remix of his song "Turn It Up (Fire It Up)," which also achieved a top 10 chart position, showcasing Timbaland's talent for transforming unlikely sources into hits. "For his and Magoo's second single, he took No Kidding, the theme to the 80s NBC TV hit Night Rider."
**Admiration for The Neptunes' Work (Late 1990s)** : Timbaland, working in studio A at Master Sound Studios in Virginia Beach, recalled hearing The Neptunes working on Kelis's "Caught Out There" in studio B. He was so impressed by the beat that he expressed to Missy Elliott his wish that he had produced it. "And I was working in the studio A and then they had a studio B. And I walked to the back door, and I said, Missy, you got to hit it. And I was like, oh man, I wish I had made that beat. That was the beat."

# curiosity

**General Evolution of Hit Music** : The history of popular music consistently shows that what initially sounds "weird" or "off-kilter" eventually becomes normalized. This phenomenon is exemplified by artists like Eurythmics, James Brown, and Kraftwerk, whose groundbreaking sounds were first disruptive before becoming foundational to subsequent musical styles. "This is the story of hit music in general. Stuff that at first upends our notion of what popular songs are supposed to sound like then gets normalized."
**Virginia Beach as a Music Scene Catalyst (Early 1990s)** : Prior to Teddy Riley's relocation from Harlem in the early 1990s, Virginia Beach lacked a significant music scene, despite having raw local talent. Riley's establishment of a small studio in a residential neighborhood served as a pivotal catalyst, fostering the conditions for the unique "Virginia Beach sound" to emerge. "What makes Teddy Riley pivotal to this story is what he did in the early 90s, the native of Harlem, looking to get away from the rough and tumble of New York City moved to Virginia Beach, Virginia, setting up a small studio in a residential neighborhood. This would prove to be a catalyst to the entire Virginia Beach scene, which wasn't really a scene before Riley arrived."
**Debasement Production House (Mid 1990s)** : Debasement was a production house founded by Donald DeGrate, also known as Devanté Swing, the main songwriter for Jodeci.
