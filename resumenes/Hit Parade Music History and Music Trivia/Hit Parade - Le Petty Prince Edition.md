## albums
**Tom Petty and the Heartbreakers (1976) - Debut Album** : This self-titled debut album was released after Petty had spent half a decade with Mudcrutch and formed The Heartbreakers. Despite their Southern roots, the album featured songs that barely sounded American, characterized by clipped phrasing and three-chord directness, leading to it being briefly marketed as punk or punk adjacent. "Their self-titled debut album featured songs that barely even sounded American, with clipped phrasing and three-core directness."
**You're Gonna Get It (1978) - Sophomore Album** : This album became the Heartbreakers' first Top 40 album and was certified Gold in July 1978, just two months after its release. Its success was set up by the single "Breakdown." "It became the band's first top 40 album, and in July of 78, just two months after its release, we're gonna get it was Certified Gold."
**Damned The Torpedoes (1979) - Blockbuster Album** : Released by MCA Records after months of legal disputes with Petty, this album proved to be a blockbuster, eventually going triple platinum. It remains the Heartbreakers' biggest studio album and spent seven weeks at number two on the album chart, stuck behind Pink Floyd's "The Wall," with half its tracks becoming radio hits. "An eventual triple platinum classic, Torpedoes remains the heartbreakers biggest studio album as a band, and it plays like an instant greatest hits."
**Hard Promises (1981) - Follow-up Album** : This album was originally threatened to be titled "898" by Petty when MCA proposed raising its suggested retail price by $1.998. It was eventually released at the old price, under the title "Hard Promises," following Petty's successful legal battle over "Damned The Torpedoes." "The 1981 album was eventually released at the old price, under the title Hard Promises."
**Long After Dark (1982) - MTV Video Star Album** : By this album, Petty had leaned into his role as a video star. The album's sound echoed the synth-heavy style of early MTV new wave. "By the time of the Heartbreakers' next album, 1982's Long After Dark, Petty had leaned into his role as a video star."
**Full Moon Fever (1989) - Solo Debut Album** : Produced by Jeff Lynne, this album marked Petty's belated solo debut away from The Heartbreakers, though it featured half of the band as players. It became a quintuple platinum smash, sending four singles deep at pop radio and seven tracks deep at rock radio, making Petty a more omnipresent pop star than ever as he approached 40. "Produced by Petty's fellow Wilberry, Jeff Lin, 1989's Full Moon Fever, which, despite the lack of the word heartbreakers on the cover, nonetheless featured half of the band as players, was a quintuple platinum smash."
**Into the Great Wide Open (1991) - MTV Cred Album** : This album saw Petty reconvene The Heartbreakers and work again with Jeff Lynne. It achieved double platinum status and benefited from Petty's revived fortunes post-"Full Moon Fever." The title track doubled down on Petty's renewed MTV credibility, with a video starring Johnny Depp and Faye Dunaway. "The Great Wide Open album was a success, eventually going double platinum."

## songs
**Anything That's Rock And Roll (1977)** : This was Petty and The Heartbreakers' first hit to chart, not in America, but in the United Kingdom. It reached number 36 on the UK chart in the summer of 1977, the same summer Brits were enthusiastic about the Sex Pistols. "Indeed that song, anything that's rock and roll, was Petty and the heartbreakers first hit to make the charts, not here, but in the United Kingdom."
**American Girl (1977)** : This now classic song was ironically not a hit in America, never making the Hot 100 despite being the first single Shelter Records issued by the band. However, it reached number 40 in the UK, leading to Petty and The Heartbreakers making several live and televised appearances in England in 1977. "It is one of the great ironies of chart history that the now classic American girl was not actually a hit in America."
**Breakdown (1978)** : This song became The Heartbreakers' first American hit, scraping the Top 40 on the Hot 100 by reaching number 40 in the winter of 1978. It smoldered with an R&B groove, leading Petty to often segue it into Ray Charles' "Hit the Road Jack" during live performances in the late 70s. "That's when breakdown scraped the top 40 on the Hot 100, sneaking up to number 40."
**Don't Do Me Like That (1980)** : This boogie rock number was Petty and The Heartbreakers' first Top Ten hit, peaking at number 10 on the Hot 100 in the week ending February 2nd, 1980. Petty had briefly considered giving the song away to The J. Geils Band before it became a smash for his own band. "It was also the album that gave Petty and the heartbreakers their first top ten hit."
**Stop Draggin' My Heart Around (1981)** : A bluesy, smoldering number three smash in the summer of 1981, this duet with Stevie Nicks became the biggest hit of both Nicks' solo career and Petty's career. Producer Jimmy Iovine passed the song, originally penned by Petty and Mike Campbell for The Heartbreakers, to Nicks, who sang over Petty's original track. It was one of the first videos played on MTV on its launch day, August 1st, 1981, and went into heavy rotation. "The result was the biggest smash of Nicks' solo career and the biggest of Petty's, with or without the Heartbreakers."
**The Waiting (1981)** : This song, a top-to-bottom homage to The Byrds' sound, became a number 18 pop hit and a number one rock track in 1981. It showcased Petty's skill in taking inspiration from his heroes and turning it into a hit. "In 1981, Petty took the homage even further when he wrote a top-to-bottom homage to the Birds sound and scored a hit with it."
**Don't Come Around Here No More (1985)** : This psychedelic digital rock track, co-written and produced by Dave Stewart of Eurythmics, became a number 13 pop and number two rock hit in the spring of 1985. It featured electric sitar and cellos and was accompanied by a trippy Alice in Wonderland music video, which became Petty's most beloved MTV hit, marking his return to the charts with his most complex song to date. "A psychedelic digital rock track, co-written and produced by Dave Stewart of Eurythmics, punctuated by electric satar and cellos, and featuring a trippy, Allison Wonderland music video that became Petty's most beloved MTV hit."
**Jammin' Me (1987)** : Co-written with Bob Dylan and Mike Campbell, this snarky track became the lead single from The Heartbreakers' album "Let Me Up I've Had Enough." It reached number 18 on the Hot 100 in the summer of 1987 and spent a month atop Billboard's Album Rock chart. "The snarky Jammin' May. A song that reached number 18 on the Hot 100 in the summer of 1987 and spent a month atop Billboard's album rock chart."
**I Won't Back Down (1989)** : This song from "Full Moon Fever" was a number 12 pop hit and topped the album rock chart. It was one of three classic singles from the album to achieve this success, contributing to Petty's revived radio fortunes. "The number 12 pop hit I Won't Back Down."
**Running Down a Dream (1989)** : Featured on "Full Moon Fever," this song became a number 23 pop hit and topped the album rock chart, solidifying the album's success with multiple radio hits. "The number 23 pop hit running down a dream."
**Free Fallin' (1990)** : This number 7 smash from "Full Moon Fever" became Petty's first Top 10 pop hit in nearly a decade, and his highest charting hit ever (excluding the Stevie Nicks duet). When it peaked on the Hot 100 in January 1990, Petty found himself in a Top 10 alongside diverse artists, while its video, an ode to Los Angeles, featured skateboarders and goth kids. "And Petty's first top 10 pop hit in nearly a decade, the number 7 smash free fallen."
**You're So Bad (1990)** : This was the last radio hit pulled from "Full Moon Fever," peaking in the album rock Top 5 in early summer 1990, more than a year after the album's release. "The last radio hit pulled from Full Moon Fever, the number 5 rock hit You're So Bad, peaked in the album rock top 5 in the early summer of 1990."
**Learning to Fly (1991)** : This sparkling top 40 hit from "Into the Great Wide Open" reached number 28 in 1991. The song, along with its video directed by Julian Temple and starring Johnny Depp and Faye Dunaway, satirized the music video era and early 90s pop trends. "The album benefited from Petty's revived fortunes, post-Fulmoon fever, and from the album's sparkling top 40 hit, Learning to Fly, which reached number 28 in 1991."

## curiosities
**Loss of an Icon (October 2017)** : Tom Petty's passing was mourned by the music world, just under a month before the transcript was created. He was celebrated as one of the great singer-songwriters and torchbearers of rock. "It's been just under a month since the music world mourned the loss of one of the great singer-songwriters and torchbearers of rock, Tom Petty."
**Rock and Roll Hall of Fame Induction (March 2002)** : Petty and his band, The Heartbreakers, were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame two years prior to the 2004 ceremony where he helped induct George Harrison. "Two years before, in March of 2002, Petty and his band, The Heartbreakers, had been inducted into the hall."
**First-Ballot Rock Hall Member** : Tom Petty, along with Prince, was part of the exclusive club of performers inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame on their first ballot, an honor reserved for artists whose contributions are considered undeniably vital to the popular canon. "Petty and Prince were in a different category. Rock Hall members who get in on their first ballot from Chuck Berry to Pearl Jam form a very exclusive club."
**Early Life and Influences** : Born Thomas Earl Petty in Gainesville, Florida in 1950, he was a product of a Southern white family. He came of age musically in the late 60s and early 70s, synthesizing two decades of rock music that had already seen titans like Elvis Presley and Jimi Hendrix come and go. "One born in the 1950s, who broke on the charts at the very end of the 1979s and came to define their corners of American rock in the 1980s."
**Early Career with Mudcrutch** : Before The Heartbreakers, Petty spent half a decade in the Gainesville, Florida band Mudcrutch, which included future Heartbreakers Mike Campbell and Benmont Tench. Mudcrutch moved to Los Angeles, signed to Shelter Records, and released one single, "Depot Street," before breaking up in 1975. "Before he and the heartbreakers issued their self-titled debut album in 1976, Petty had spent half a decade in the Gainesville, Florida band Mudcrutch."
**Record Deal Struggles (1978-1979)** : Petty faced significant record deal issues in an 18-month period. Shelter Records, affiliated with ABC Records, switched distributors, but ABC demanded they retain Petty. He renegotiated with ABC, only for ABC to be sold to MCA Records. This led Petty to declare bankruptcy in May 1979 to escape his contract, arguing that his low royalty rate wouldn't allow him to repay MCA. "During this period, Tom Petty's greatest concern wasn't musical crossover or even the charts. It was his record deal."
**Record Label Resistance (1979-1981)** : Petty's declaration of bankruptcy in May 1979 to get out of his contract with MCA Records stemmed from his band's low royalty rate. After months of legal battles, MCA capitulated, granting Petty and The Heartbreakers a better deal and a higher royalty rate, realizing they had a smash album, "Damned The Torpedoes," in the works. This fight branded Petty as an artist unwilling to back down to label demands. "That's when Petty exercised the nuclear option. In May 1979, he declared bankruptcy to get out from under his contract."
**Age at Death** : Tom Petty passed away in his mid-60s. "Both left us too soon, Prince in his late 50s, Petty in his mid 60s."
**Relationship with the Music Industry** : Petty consistently fought categorization and the machinations of the music industry throughout his career, often engaging in battles with his record labels over artistic control and financial terms. "Each man fought categorization and the machinations of the music industry for the entirety of their careers."
**Influences and Songwriting Praised** : Producer Jimmy Iovine praised Petty's songwriting skill, producing three albums for him during a highly prolific period. Roger McGuinn of The Byrds famously quipped "when did I write that?" upon hearing "American Girl," highlighting Petty's ability to create homages to his heroes. "In an interview for VH1's Behind the Music, producer Jimmy Iovine praised Tom Petty's skill as a songwriter above all."
**Early MTV Stardom (1981)** : Petty's duet with Stevie Nicks, "Stop Draggin' My Heart Around," was one of the first videos played on MTV on its launch day, August 1st, 1981, and quickly went into heavy rotation, inaugurating Petty's "unlikely role as an early MTV star." "It thus inaugurated Petty's unlikely role as an early MTV star."
**Self-Imposed Exile for "Southern Accents" (Mid-1980s)** : In the mid-1980s, while Prince was dominating the charts, Tom Petty was in a self-imposed exile attempting to record a high-concept album called "Southern Accents." The album was intended as a meditation on his Confederate heritage, featuring songs like "Rebels" and its title track. "During this two year period, when Prince was hovering over the sound of pop and rewriting the rules for chart domination, Tom Petty was in self-imposed exile, attempting to record a high-concept album."
**Tour with Bob Dylan (1986)** : In 1986, Tom Petty and The Heartbreakers toured with Bob Dylan, gaining experience playing backup to a rock legend. Dylan later reciprocated by teaming with Petty and Mike Campbell to co-write "Jammin' Me." "In 1986, Petty and the heartbreakers mounted a tour with Bob Dylan, learning what it was like to play back up to a rock legend."
**Meeting Jeff Lynne (1987)** : Petty befriended Jeff Lynne of the Electric Light Orchestra in 1987. Lynne, known for emulating The Beatles' production, had been producing George Harrison's comeback album, "Cloud Nine," which led to Petty's involvement in the formation of The Traveling Wilburys. "In 1987, Petty befriended Jeff Lynn of the Electric Light Orchestra, the stately orchestral pop rock band that had spent the 70s emulating the production approach of Lynn's heroes The Beatles."
**Inspiration to a New Generation (Early 1990s)** : "Full Moon Fever" not only revived Petty's radio fortunes but also made him relevant to a new generation of rising Generation Xers, particularly on MTV. The "Free Fallin'" video, which served as an ode to his adopted home of Los Angeles, featured skateboarders and goth kids, showcasing his continued cultural impact. "The album not only revived Petty's radio fortunes, it made him relevant to a new generation of rising generation exers, particularly on MTV."

## albums
**For You (1978) - Debut Album** : Prince's very first album, released when he was 19, included the single "Soft and Wet," which reached number 12 on Billboard's Hot Soul Singles. The album showcased his pop omnivore tendencies, closing with the metal funk hybrid "I'm Yours," a clear indication that he would not be creatively boxed in. "His very first album, 1978's For You, led off with a single that read as contemporary R&B and even reached the number 12 position on Billboard's Hot Soul Singles, the lascivious, soft and wet."
**Prince (1979) - Self-titled Album** : This self-titled album spawned the hit "I Want to Be Your Lover," which reached number 22 on the album chart and went platinum. It demonstrated his ability to create sparkling, danceable soul numbers that were conversant with disco but not wedded to it. "And coming after Prince's 1979 self-titled album, the one that had spawned, I want to be your lover, reached number 22 on the album chart and went platinum."
**Dirty Mind (1980) - New Wave Rock Album** : Prince's third album marked a significant "left turn" in rock history, trading the contemporary R&B sound of his previous hits for a new wave rock sound. Critically acclaimed, with Robert Christgau famously stating, "Mick Jagger should fold up his penis and go home," it was not a commercial success, peaking below the Top 40 and selling poorly, not going gold for another four years. "In one of the great left turns in rock history, Prince's third album, Dirty Mind, traded in the Contempo R&B sound of his first two albums hits for essentially a new wave rock sound."
**1999 (1982) - Double Album** : Released in late 1982 by "Prince and the Revolution," this double album definitively proved that Prince could not be confined to any single sound. It featured the smash hit "Little Red Corvette," which broke barriers at both radio and MTV and showcased thick pulsating synthesizers alongside a classic rock chorus and guitar solo. "Corvette was a single from 1999, a double album released in late 1982 by the collective now calling itself Prince and the Revolution."
**Purple Rain (1984) - Magnum Opus Album** : The title track of Prince's 1984 debut movie and accompanying magnum opus album, "Purple Rain" was inspired by power ballads from rock titans like Bob Seger and borrowed chord changes from Journey's "Faithfully." It reached number two on the pop chart, number four on the R&B chart, and number 18 on the Rock Chart, becoming Prince's third straight single to hit all three charts. "His epic single Purple Rain, the title track of both his 1984 debut movie and its accompanying Magnum Opus album, was inspired by the power-ballot work of such rock titans as Bob Seeger."
**Around the World in a Day (1985) - Psychedelic Album** : This trippy, Sergeant Pepper-esque album was the first to be released on Prince's Paisley Park label. Its lead single, the psychedelic rock story song "Raspberry Beret," debuted concurrently with Petty's "Don't Come Around Here No More" and showcased Prince's continued musical evolution. "A trippy, sergeant pepper-esque around the world in a day, the first album on Prince's label, Paisley Park."
**Love Sexy (1988) - Acclaimed but Slowing Album** : While widely acclaimed, Prince's 1988 album "Love Sexy" only went gold, not platinum, and generated just one hit song, the minimalist funk rock jam "Alphabet Street." This indicated a potential slowing down in his chart-topping period after an epic run from 1982 to 1987. "His 1988 album Love Sexy, while widely acclaimed, only went gold, not platinum."
**Batman (1989) - Movie Soundtrack Album** : Prince offered to record the entire soundtrack for Tim Burton's "Batman" adaptation, assuming the persona of various film characters on each song. This album, along with its lead single "Batdance," went to number one on their respective charts, rejuvenating Prince's career. "Prince offered to record not just a song for Burton's future blockbuster, but the entire soundtrack."

## curiosities
**Loss of an Icon (April 2016)** : Prince's passing was mourned by the music world, just over a year and a half before the transcript was created. He was celebrated as a multi-genre, multi-instrumentalist, and prolific pop genius. "And it's been just over a year and a half since we lost the multi-genre, multi-instrumentalist, prolific pop genius, Prince."
**Rock and Roll Hall of Fame Induction (March 2004)** : Prince was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in his first year of eligibility, 25 years after his first recording, demonstrating his undeniable impact on popular music. "That night in 2004 was Prince's turn, which is how the two men came to be at the Waldorf Astoria stage on the same night."
**First-Ballot Rock Hall Member** : Prince, along with Tom Petty, was part of the exclusive club of performers inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame on their first ballot, an honor reserved for artists whose contributions are considered undeniably vital to the popular canon. "Rock Hall members who get in on their first ballot from Chuck Berry to Pearl Jam form a very exclusive club."
**Early Life and Musical Background** : Born Prince Rogers Nelson in Minneapolis in 1958, he was an African-American polymath descended from jazz musicians. His father was a pianist and his mother a jazz singer, shaping him into a "pop omnivore" who famously taught himself a range of instruments. "The latter, Minneapolis's favorite son, an African-American polymath descended from jazz musicians who broke on urban radio."
**Age at Death** : Prince passed away in his late 50s. "Both left us too soon, Prince in his late 50s, Petty in his mid 60s."
**Warner Brothers Contract (1977)** : At age 19, Prince signed an unusual contract with Warner Brothers Records in 1977. This contract not only granted him an unusual degree of creative control but also explicitly designated him for the label's pop roster, not its R&B one, a distinction that would profoundly shape his entire career. "When Prince signed to Warner Brothers records in 1977 at age 19, his contract not only called for an unusual degree of creative control, but also explicitly stated that he be part of the label's pop roster, not its R&B one."
**Relationship with the Music Industry** : Prince consistently fought categorization and the machinations of the music industry throughout his career, often pushing creative boundaries despite label expectations, as seen with his contract terms and genre-bending albums. "Each man fought categorization and the machinations of the music industry for the entirety of their careers."
**Testing Label Limits with "Dirty Mind" (1980)** : After achieving a pop breakthrough with "I Want To Be Your Lover," Prince immediately tested the limits of his power with Warner Bros. by veering away from his hit sound. His third album, "Dirty Mind," shifted from contemporary R&B to new wave rock, a move that alienated the public and led to poor sales, despite critical acclaim. "In his case, that didn't yet involve legal action against Warner Bros. But rather, veering away from the sound that had literally just given him a hit."
**Impact on MTV for Black Artists (Early 1983)** : Prince's video for "Little Red Corvette" was groundbreaking for black artists on MTV. For its first year and a half, MTV had presented itself as a "rock station" and was largely unwilling to play videos by black artists, often relegating Prince's earlier videos like "Controversy" or "1999" to the "wee hours." "Little Red Corvette" became one of two tracks (along with Michael Jackson's "Billie Jean") to enter heavy rotation, marking a significant breakthrough. "Little Red Corvette changed that in early 1983."
**Breaking Barriers on Rock Charts (1983)** : With "Little Red Corvette" peaking at number 17 on Billboard's Rock Chart (then called Top Tracks), Prince became one of the first black artists to place on a chart previously dominated by rock acts like Pink Floyd and ZZ Top, demonstrating his cross-genre appeal. "Peeking at number 17 on the Rock Chart with Little Red Corvette, Prince was one of the first black artists to place on a chart dominated by the likes of Pink Floyd and ZZ Top."
**"Imperial Period" of Chart Domination (1983-1987)** : The chart success of "Little Red Corvette" and Stevie Nicks' "Stand Back" (co-created by Prince) initiated Prince's "Imperial period," a time where everything he touched, including songs he gave to others, "turned to gold and platinum," cementing his pervasive influence on pop music. "The chart's success of both Little Red Corvette and its cousin single Stand Back kicked off Prince's Imperial period, where everything he touched turned to gold and platinum."
**Inspiration from Other Artists (1984)** : Prince was not above drawing inspiration from other artists' work, even across genres. His epic single "Purple Rain" was explicitly inspired by the power ballad work of rock titans such as Bob Seger, and he admitted to borrowing some of its chord changes from Journey's power ballad "Faithfully," even seeking clearance from Jonathan Cain. "Like Stevie Nicks, Prince was not above reaching to other artists' work for inspiration, even across genres."

# label - MCA Records

## curiosities
**Acquisition of ABC Records (Late 1970s)** : MCA Records, a major conglomerate, acquired ABC Records in its entirety in the late 1970s. This acquisition led to significant legal disputes with Tom Petty, who declared bankruptcy to get out of his contract, ultimately resulting in MCA signing Petty and The Heartbreakers to a better deal for the blockbuster album "Damned The Torpedoes." "But no sooner had he done that, then ABC was sold in its entirety to Major Conglomerate MCA Records."
**Disputes over Album Pricing (1981)** : MCA Records proposed raising the suggested retail price of Tom Petty and The Heartbreakers' follow-up album to "Damned The Torpedoes" by $1.998. Petty threatened to title the album "898" in protest, leading to the album ultimately being released at the original price under the title "Hard Promises." "Almost two years later, Petty would threaten to title the band's follow-up album 898 when MCA proposed raising his LP's suggested retail price by $1.998."

**Music Industry "Repatiousness" (Over Four Decades)** : Both Prince and Tom Petty, over four decades, found creative routes around the "repatiousness" (rapaciousness) of the music industry.
