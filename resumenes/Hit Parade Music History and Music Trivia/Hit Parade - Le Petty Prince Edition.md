## albums
**Full Moon Fever (1989) - Belated Solo Debut and Career Peak** : Petty's first solo album away from the Heartbreakers, made as he approached 40 after three years of touring, writing and recording with baby-boom rock legends. It was produced by fellow Wilbury Jeff Lynne and, despite the band's name being absent from the cover, featured half of the Heartbreakers as players. It went quintuple platinum, four singles deep at pop radio and seven tracks deep at rock radio, and opened with three now-classic singles that hit the top 40 and topped the album rock chart: "I Won't Back Down" (No. 12), "Runnin' Down a Dream" (No. 23) and "Free Fallin'" (No. 7). It spent the summer of 1989 just a few chart spots below Prince's No. 1 "Batman" soundtrack, revived Petty's radio fortunes and made him relevant to rising Gen Xers, especially on MTV. "Incredibly, this debut made him a more omnipresent pop star than ever."
**Wildflowers (1994) - Second Solo Album** : Petty's second-ever solo album came out in late 1994, just weeks after he won MTV's Video Vanguard Award. It led off with the stoner-rock jam "You Don't Know How It Feels," which became a No. 13 Hot 100 hit and helped push the album to triple platinum. It also included the hard rocker "Honey Bee," which Petty performed on Saturday Night Live with Dave Grohl on drums. "helped power Wild Flowers to triple platinum sales."

## songs
**I Won't Back Down (1989) - Full Moon Fever Opener** : The first of three classic singles from "Full Moon Fever." It reached No. 12 on the pop chart and topped the album rock chart. "The number 12 pop hit I Won't Back Down."
**Runnin' Down a Dream (1989)** : The second "Full Moon Fever" single. It peaked at No. 23 on the pop chart and topped the album rock chart. "The number 23 pop hit running down a dream."
**Free Fallin' (1989) - Highest-Charting Petty Hit** : The third "Full Moon Fever" single was Petty's first pop top 10 in nearly a decade and peaked at No. 7 in January 1990. Not counting the 1981 Stevie Nicks duet, it was his highest-charting hit ever, and it shared the top 10 with Michael Bolton, Technotronic, Jody Watley and Skid Row. Its video is an ode to Petty's adopted home of Los Angeles, featuring skateboarders and goth kids while Petty strums his guitar on the escalators of a mall. Prince later named it one of his favorite songs. "Petty's first top 10 pop hit in nearly a decade, the number 7 smash free fallen."
**Yer So Bad (1990) - Last Full Moon Fever Radio Hit** : The last radio single from "Full Moon Fever." It reached No. 5 on the album rock chart in early summer 1990, more than a year after the album came out. "more than a year after Fever dropped."
**You Don't Know How It Feels (1994) - Last Top 40 Pop Hit** : The lead single from "Wildflowers," described as a blissed-out stoner-rock jam. It reached No. 13 on the Hot 100, and in early 1995 it became Petty's last top 40 pop hit. By then he had kept scoring hits deep into his 40s. "you don't know how it feels became Petty's last top 40 pop hit."
**Honey Bee (1994) - SNL Performance with Dave Grohl** : A hard rocker from "Wildflowers." Petty performed it on Saturday Night Live in November 1994 with Dave Grohl on drums. This was only six months after Kurt Cobain's death, while Grohl was still mourning his Nirvana bandmate and before the Foo Fighters. "Grohl bashed out a memorable performance on Petty's Wild Flowers Hard Rocker, honeybee."

## curiosities
**Gainesville Origins** : Thomas Earl Petty was born in 1950 to a Southern white family from Gainesville, Florida. He became a mainstream rock radio hitmaker, came of age musically in the late 60s and early 70s, and died in his mid-60s. "a product of a Southern white family from Gainesville, Florida."
**First-Ballot Rock Hall Inductee - March 2002** : Petty and the Heartbreakers were inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in March 2002 on their first ballot, joining an exclusive club that runs from Chuck Berry to Pearl Jam. Their songs had been part of American culture and all over the Billboard charts for three decades. "Petty might well have been the easiest of all."
**Leading the 2004 Rock Hall Tribute to George Harrison** : At the March 2004 ceremony at the Waldorf Astoria, Petty helped induct his late friend George Harrison. He led the all-star performance of "While My Guitar Gently Weeps" that featured Prince's solo, and he was the only person on stage that night besides Prince who was himself a first-ballot inductee. "the performance was led by Tom Petty, who was at the ceremony that night to help induct his late friend Harrison."
**1979 Bankruptcy to Escape His Contract** : Within about 18 months, the band's contract changed hands twice. Shelter Records, affiliated with ABC Records, switched distributors, but ABC demanded the band stay with ABC. Petty renegotiated to protect his Shelter deal, and then ABC was sold outright to MCA Records, which was the last straw. In May 1979 he declared bankruptcy, arguing that the low royalty rate meant the band could never repay MCA; after months of legal maneuvering, MCA gave in and signed them to a better deal with a higher royalty rate. "That's when Petty exercised the nuclear option."
**The $8.98 Album Title Threat** : When MCA proposed raising his LP's suggested list price, Petty threatened to title the follow-up to "Damn the Torpedoes" "$8.98." The 1981 album was released at the old price as "Hard Promises." The 1979 bankruptcy fight was what first branded him as an artist who wouldn't back down to his label. "Petty would threaten to title the band's follow-up album 898."
**Early MTV Star** : The Stevie Nicks duet "Stop Draggin' My Heart Around" was among the first videos MTV played on its launch day, August 1, 1981, and went into heavy rotation. This began Petty's unlikely role as an early MTV star, and he won MTV's Video Vanguard Award in 1994. Prince recalled loving Petty's MTV appearances. "It thus inaugurated Petty's unlikely role as an early MTV star."
**Self-Imposed Exile (1983–1985)** : During the two years Prince dominated pop, Petty was in self-imposed exile, trying to record a high-concept album about his Confederate heritage. That album became "Southern Accents." "Tom Petty was in self-imposed exile, attempting to record a high-concept album."
**Touring as Bob Dylan's Backing Band (1986)** : In 1986, Petty and the Heartbreakers toured with Bob Dylan and learned what it was like to back a rock legend. Within a year, Dylan co-wrote "Jammin' Me" with Petty and Mike Campbell. "learning what it was like to play back up to a rock legend."
**Youngest Traveling Wilbury** : At 37, Petty was the youngest member of the Traveling Wilburys. He was more than a sideman: he co-wrote and sang lead on parts of radio hits like "End of the Line." "Petty, who at 37 was the youngest of the bunch."
**Elder Statesman Before 40** : By the end of the 80s, after years with his baby-boom heroes, Petty seemed to be settling into elder-statesman status early. That made his solo debut an odd move at the time. "Petty appeared to be settling into a fine doodish, arriving at Elder Statesman status early."
**Roger McGuinn's Reaction to American Girl** : Byrds leader Roger McGuinn reportedly joked, on first hearing "American Girl," that he couldn't remember writing it. "when did I write that?"
**Jimmy Iovine on Petty's Songwriting** : Producer Jimmy Iovine praised Petty's songwriting above all his other skills on VH1's "Behind the Music." Iovine produced three Petty and the Heartbreakers albums during one of Petty's most prolific songwriting periods. "producer Jimmy Iovine praised Tom Petty's skill as a songwriter above all."
**Kim Basinger in Mary Jane's Last Dance Video** : The video for "Mary Jane's Last Dance" featured Kim Basinger, once Prince's girlfriend, as a "Weekend at Bernie's"-style reanimated corpse. "featuring Prince's one-time paramour Kim Basinger as a weekend at Bernie's like reanimated corpse."
**Petty's Tribute to Prince (2016)** : Less than a week after Prince's death in 2016, and 18 months before his own, Petty told the New York Times the 2004 performance was thrilling. He also recalled that shortly before Prince died, he had been talking with Susanna Hoffs about "Manic Monday" and almost called Prince. "I'm starting to think you should just act on those things all the time."
**Posthumous Chart Return (October 2017)** : The week after Petty's death in October 2017, five of his albums re-entered the Billboard album chart. Among them was the Heartbreakers' "Greatest Hits," which reached a new peak of No. 2 after peaking at No. 5 in 1993. "five of his albums re-entered the album chart."

## albums
**Tom Petty and the Heartbreakers (1976) - Self-Titled Debut** : After Mudcrutch broke up, Shelter Records kept Petty, who then formed the Heartbreakers with Mike Campbell and Benmont Tench plus fellow Gainesville natives Stan Lynch and Ron Blair. Although nominally a Southern rock band at a time when the genre was charting well, they sounded nothing like the Eagles or the Allman Brothers. The debut's songs barely sounded American, with clipped phrasing and three-chord directness. "Their self-titled debut album featured songs that barely even sounded American."
**You're Gonna Get It! (1978) - First Top 40 Album** : Set up by the single "Breakdown," the sophomore album became the band's first top 40 album. It was certified gold in July 1978, two months after release, just as the band's contract troubles began. "It became the band's first top 40 album."
**Damn the Torpedoes (1979) - Blockbuster Breakthrough** : Produced by Jimmy Iovine, known for his work with Bruce Springsteen and Patti Smith, the album was held up by Petty's bankruptcy fight. MCA gave in partly because it knew the band was sitting on a smash. It went triple platinum and remains the Heartbreakers' biggest studio album as a band. It spent seven weeks at No. 2 behind Pink Floyd's "The Wall," and half its tracks were radio hits. "it plays like an instant greatest hits."
**Hard Promises (1981) - The $8.98 Fight** : Petty threatened to title this follow-up "$8.98" when MCA proposed raising the LP's suggested list price. It came out at the old price under the title "Hard Promises." Jimmy Iovine produced it at the same time as Stevie Nicks' "Bella Donna." "The 1981 album was eventually released at the old price, under the title Hard Promises."
**Long After Dark (1982) - Leaning Into Video Stardom** : By this album, Petty had embraced his role as a video star. Its single echoed the synth-heavy sound of early-MTV new wave, and its video had the band acting out an homage to "Mad Max" and "The Road Warrior." "Petty had leaned into his role as a video star."
**Southern Accents (1985) - Confederate Heritage Concept Album** : A high-concept meditation on Petty's Confederate heritage, recorded during a self-imposed exile. It includes songs like "Rebels" and the moving title track. Its lead single, "Don't Come Around Here No More," barely fit the concept. "a meditation on his Confederate heritage, punctuated by songs like Rebels."
**Let Me Up (I've Had Enough) (1987) - Dylan Co-Write** : The album followed the 1986 tour with Bob Dylan. Its lead single, "Jammin' Me," was co-written by Dylan, Petty and Mike Campbell. "co-wrote the lead single from the heartbreakers next album Let Me Up I've Had Enough."
**Into the Great Wide Open (1991) - Post-Full Moon Fever Reunion** : Petty reunited the Heartbreakers and returned to the studio with Jeff Lynne a year after "Full Moon Fever." The album went double platinum, the band's first multi-platinum album as a group since "Damn the Torpedoes." It benefited from Petty's revived fortunes and from the top 40 hit "Learning to Fly." "The first multi platinum album for the heartbreakers as a group since Dam the Torpedoes."
**Greatest Hits (1993) - First Compilation** : The band's first greatest hits album, released in late 1993. Unlike most compilations, its new bonus track, "Mary Jane's Last Dance," became one of the band's most beloved hits. The album peaked at No. 5 in 1993 and reached a new peak of No. 2 the week after Petty's death in October 2017. "the one Petty and the heartbreakers recorded became one of the band's most beloved hits."
**She's the One (1996) - Soundtrack** : A soundtrack album the band recorded in the 90s. It is cited as one of the great albums they kept making after Petty's last pop hit. "including 1996's Soundtrack to She's the One."
**Echo (1999) - Acclaimed Late-90s Album** : An acclaimed late-90s Heartbreakers album. "1999's Acclaimed Echo."
**Hypnotic Eye (2014) - First Ever No. 1 Album** : Buyers of 2014 tour tickets were offered the new album as an add-on, and so many took it that it debuted at No. 1. It was Petty's first No. 1 album ever, 35 years after "Damn the Torpedoes" stalled at No. 2 behind Pink Floyd. "Petty's 2014 chart-topper was his first number one ever."

## songs
**Anything That's Rock 'n' Roll (1977) - First Chart Hit, in the UK** : The band's first single to chart came in the United Kingdom, not the US, reaching No. 36 in summer 1977. That was the same summer the British were going mad for the Sex Pistols. "was Petty and the heartbreakers first hit to make the charts, not here, but in the United Kingdom."
**American Girl (1977) - Not a Hit in America** : The first single Shelter released by the band, it never made the Hot 100 and failed to win over US radio programmers or buyers. In the UK it reached No. 40, and the band made several live and TV appearances in England in 1977. Roger McGuinn reportedly joked about it: "when did I write that?" "It is one of the great ironies of chart history that the now classic American girl was not actually a hit in America."
**Breakdown (1978) - First American Hit** : The band's first US hit, scraping the Hot 100 top 40 at No. 40 in winter 1978. It smoldered with an R&B groove, and in late-70s concerts Petty segued from it into Ray Charles' "Hit the Road Jack." Grace Jones covered it as reggae on "Warm Leatherette" (1980), with Petty's blessing and a third verse he wrote just for her. "Petty's breakdown smoldered with an R&B groove."
**Don't Do Me Like That (1979) - First Top 10 Hit** : A boogie-rock number from "Damn the Torpedoes" that Petty had briefly considered giving to the J. Geils Band. It became the band's first top 10 hit, entering the top 10 the week ending February 2, 1980, one spot above Prince's "I Wanna Be Your Lover." Casey Kasem counted it down on American Top 40. "A boogie rock number, Petty had briefly considered giving away to the Jay Guiles band."
**Refugee (1979)** : A "Damn the Torpedoes" track from Petty's productive Iovine years, cited as an example of his tough-minded songwriting. "records as tough-minded as Refugee."
**The Waiting (1981) - Byrds Homage** : A full homage to the Byrds' sound. It was a No. 18 pop hit and a No. 1 rock track. "he wrote a top-to-bottom homage to the Birds sound and scored a hit with it."
**Don't Come Around Here No More (1985) - Psychedelic MTV Favorite** : The lead single from "Southern Accents" and Petty's first single in two years, barely tied to the album's concept. It was co-written and produced by Dave Stewart of Eurythmics, with electric sitar and cellos, and a trippy "Alice in Wonderland" video that became Petty's most beloved MTV hit. It reached No. 13 pop and No. 2 rock in spring 1985, entering the charts alongside Prince's "Raspberry Beret." "A psychedelic digital rock track, co-written and produced by Dave Stewart of Eurythmics."
**Jammin' Me (1987) - Written with Bob Dylan** : Co-written by Bob Dylan, Petty and Mike Campbell as the lead single from "Let Me Up (I've Had Enough)." It reached No. 18 on the Hot 100 in summer 1987 and spent a month at No. 1 on the album rock chart. "The snarky Jammin' May."
**Into the Great Wide Open (1991) - Satire of the Video Era** : The title track pokes fun at the music video era and the vagaries of early-90s pop fame. Its video, directed by Julien Temple and starring Johnny Depp and Faye Dunaway, satirized the current crop of teen rock idols. "doubled down on Petty's revived MTV cred."
**Learning to Fly (1991)** : The top 40 hit from "Into the Great Wide Open," reaching No. 28 in 1991. "the album's sparkling top 40 hit, Learning to Fly."
**Mary Jane's Last Dance (1993) - Greatest Hits Bonus Track** : A new track recorded for the band's first greatest hits album. Its laconic, yowling rock sat comfortably on the radio next to grunge. It peaked at No. 14 on the Hot 100 in early 1994, Petty's first top 20 since "Free Fallin'," and topped the album rock chart. Its video starred Kim Basinger as a reanimated corpse. "a leconic yowling rock cut that sounded comfortable on the radio next to Grunge."

## curiosities
**Marketed as Punk / New Wave** : The band was briefly marketed as punk, or at least punk-adjacent. Petty later joked that the label dressed them in skinny ties and scarves to look new wave. "his label would dress the band in skinny ties and scarves to further the perception that they were new wave."
**Glenn Tilbrook Remembers the Ramones Double Bill** : Squeeze leader Glenn Tilbrook recalled seeing the band on a double bill with the Ramones in London in 1977. He said they combined punk energy with real craft and drive. "they had a lot of sort of punk energy, but they had shoving shit and had it a real sort of craft to the band."
**No US Hit for a Year** : For about a year, nothing helped the band at home, even as they charted in England. Their first American hit didn't come until winter 1978. "For about a year though, nothing would help the heartbreakers in their homeland."

## albums
**For You (1978) - Debut Album** : Prince's first album opened with the contemporary R&B single "Soft and Wet," which reached No. 12 on Billboard's Hot Soul Singles. It closed with the metal-funk hybrid "I'm Yours," a sign he wouldn't be boxed in. It came out when Black artists from Aretha Franklin to Marvin Gaye were adapting to disco. "Prince was recording albums that incorporated a range of sounds."
**Prince (1979) - Self-Titled Second Album** : Home of the breakthrough "I Wanna Be Your Lover." It reached No. 22 on the album chart and went platinum. A deep cut from it, "I Feel for You," later became a smash for Chaka Khan. "reached number 22 on the album chart and went platinum."
**Dirty Mind (1980) - The Great Left Turn** : Right after his first hit, Prince swapped the contemporary R&B of his first two albums for an essentially new wave rock sound. The black-and-white cover shows him in only a long coat, a scarf and black briefs, standing in front of an upturned mattress. Critics loved it, and Robert Christgau wrote one of his shortest and most famous reviews. The public didn't: only "Uptown" charted (top five R&B), there were no pop hits, it peaked below the top 40, and it wasn't certified gold for another four years. "In one of the great left turns in rock history, Prince's third album, Dirty Mind."
**1999 (1982) - Double Album** : A double album released in late 1982, credited to the collective now called Prince and the Revolution. It confirmed once and for all that Prince couldn't be confined to one sound, and it produced "Little Red Corvette." "The two LP set affirmed, once and for all, that Prince could not be confined to any one sound."
**Purple Rain (1984) - Magnum Opus Soundtrack** : The album accompanying Prince's 1984 debut movie, credited to Prince and the Revolution. Its singles "When Doves Cry," "Let's Go Crazy" and "Purple Rain" each made the pop, R&B and rock charts. After Prince's death in April 2016, it re-entered the album chart at No. 2. "its accompanying Magnum Opus album."
**Around the World in a Day (1985) - First Paisley Park Album** : A trippy, "Sgt. Pepper"-esque album and the first on Prince's own label, Paisley Park. Its lead single was "Raspberry Beret." "the first album on Prince's label, Paisley Park."
**Sign o' the Times (1987)** : Part of Prince's 1982–1987 run of classic chart-conquering albums. That run went from "1999" through "Purple Rain" to this album. "from 1999 to Purple Rain to Sign of the Times."
**Lovesexy (1988) - Commercial Slowdown** : Widely acclaimed, but it only went gold and produced just one hit, the minimalist funk-rock jam "Alphabet St." It suggested Prince was slowing down. "while widely acclaimed, only went gold, not platinum."
**Batman (1989) - Full Film Soundtrack** : Prince offered director Tim Burton, working inside the Warner Bros. family on a Batman film starring Michael Keaton and Jack Nicholson, an entire soundtrack rather than a single song. On each track he took on the persona of a character, such as Batman, the Joker or Vicki Vale. The album went double platinum and held No. 1 on the Billboard album chart for six weeks, a month and a half. Tying himself to a box-office smash revived his career. "Prince offered to record not just a song for Burton's future blockbuster, but the entire soundtrack."
**Diamonds and Pearls (1991) - First New Power Generation Album** : The first album co-credited to his new backing band, the New Power Generation. It produced the Hot 100 No. 1 "Cream." "the first album co-credited to his new backing band, the new power generation."
**Love Symbol Album (1992) - Unpronounceable Title** : Titled with an unpronounceable male-female hieroglyph that came to be called the Love Symbol. Its lead single was "Sexy MF," and it also produced "7," his last top 10 hit under the name Prince. "that 1992 album proved Prince could still write a sturdy pop hit."
**Musicology (2004) - Comeback Album** : An acclaimed album that reached No. 3 on the album chart. It put Prince back on the cover of Rolling Stone and drove his highest-grossing tour since the "Purple Rain" heyday. "fueled Prince's highest grossing tour since his purple rain hay day."
**3121 (2006) - Debut at No. 1** : The album debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard album chart, his first No. 1 album in 17 years. It was named after the address of a mansion Prince stayed at in L.A., reaffirmed his funk-rock credentials, and produced the minor Hot 100 hit "Black Sweat." "reestablished his bonafides as a first-rate funk rocker."
**The Very Best of Prince - Posthumous No. 1** : In the two weeks after Prince's death in April 2016, 19 of his albums re-entered the Billboard album chart. This compilation topped the chart for a week. "The very best of Prince actually topped the chart for a week."

## songs
**Soft and Wet (1978) - Debut Single** : The lead single from "For You." A contemporary R&B song, it reached No. 12 on Billboard's Hot Soul Singles. "the lascivious, soft and wet."
**I'm Yours (1978) - Metal-Funk Closer** : The closing track of "For You." It was never a single, but this metal-funk hybrid showed he wouldn't be boxed in. "an Easter egg at the end of his album indicating that he would not be boxed in."
**I Wanna Be Your Lover (1979) - First Pop Crossover** : Prince's first top 40 single, a No. 11 pop hit and No. 1 R&B hit. It sat one spot below Petty's "Don't Do Me Like That" the week ending February 2, 1980. It is a sparkling, danceable soul song that knows disco without being tied to it, ribald in a playful way, with a witty, PG-rated pause in the chorus between "make you come" and "running." "A sparkling, danceable soul number that was conversant with disco but not wedded to it."
**Uptown (1980)** : A funk-rock track from "Dirty Mind." Warner Bros. got it onto Black radio and into the top five of Billboard's R&B chart. "Warner Bros. did manage to get the funk rock track Uptown onto black radio playlists."
**When You Were Mine (1980) - Unreleased Single, Later a Cyndi Lauper Track** : The best song on "Dirty Mind," a new wave rock song that was never a single and never charted. It remains one of Prince's most acclaimed songs. Three years later it appeared on Cyndi Lauper's "She's So Unusual," one of the top-selling albums of the mid-80s. "When You Were Mine remains one of Prince's most critically acclaimed songs."
**Controversy and 1999 - Late-Night MTV Only** : MTV played videos like "Controversy" and "1999" only in the wee hours, if at all. That was before "Little Red Corvette" broke through. "If MTV played Prince videos like Controversy or 1999 at all, it was only in the wee hours."
**Little Red Corvette (1983) - Breaking MTV and Rock Radio Barriers** : His biggest hit to that point, from "1999," with thick pulsing synths building to a classic rock chorus and a searing guitar solo. In early 1983 it was one of two Black artists' videos, with Michael Jackson's "Billie Jean," to go into heavy MTV rotation. It reached No. 6 on the Hot 100 in May 1983 and No. 17 on Billboard's Top Tracks rock chart, making Prince one of the first Black artists on a chart dominated by Pink Floyd and ZZ Top. It inspired Stevie Nicks' "Stand Back" and launched Prince's imperial period. "Little Red Corvette broke down barriers at both radio and MTV."
**When Doves Cry and Let's Go Crazy (1984)** : These two "Purple Rain" singles both made the pop, R&B and rock charts. They preceded the title track's own triple-chart run. "after when Dub's Cry and Let's Go Crazy."
**Purple Rain (1984) - Power Ballad with Journey's DNA** : The title track of the film and album, inspired by the power ballads of rock titans like Bob Seger. By Prince's own admission, it borrowed chord changes from Journey's "Faithfully," and he sought clearance from the band's Jonathan Cain. It reached No. 2 pop, No. 4 R&B and No. 18 rock, his third straight single on all three charts. "it borrowed some of its chord changes from the band Journeys Power Ballad Faithfully."
**Raspberry Beret (1985)** : The lead single from "Around the World in a Day," a psychedelic rock story song about a kid working part-time at a five-and-dime for a boss named Mr. McGee. It entered the Hot 100 and Top Rock Tracks at the same time as Petty's "Don't Come Around Here No More," and peaked at No. 2 pop and No. 40 rock. "Prince's colorful classic about a kid working part time at a five-in-dime for a boss named Mr. McGee."
**Kiss (1986) - No. 1 While Holding No. 2** : A boldly minimalist piece of lean funk sung in a falsetto that cut through the radio like glass. It hit No. 1 in spring 1986 while Prince's "Manic Monday," written for the Bangles, sat at No. 2. "a brazenly minimalist piece of lean funk singing in a falsetto that cut through the radio like glass."
**U Got the Look (1987)** : One of the chart-conquering singles of Prince's 1982–1987 run. "from Kiss to You Got the Look."
**Alphabet St. (1988)** : The only hit from "Lovesexy," a minimalist funk-rock jam. "the minimalist funk rock jam Alphabet Street."
**Batdance (1989) - Prince's Take on Sampling** : The capstone of the Batman soundtrack, a collage of movie dialogue samples held together by electronic beats and searing guitar. It was Prince's inspired, if strange, take on the sampling that defined emerging hip hop. It topped the Hot 100 and R&B chart in August 1989. "Prince's inspired, if strange, take on the sampling phenomenon that defined the emerging art form of hip hop."
**Cream (1991)** : A cool, strolling single from "Diamonds and Pearls." It gave Prince another Hot 100 No. 1. "the cool, strolling chart-topper, Cream."
**Sexy MF (1992) - Rap-Era Stall** : The foul-mouthed, wickedly catchy lead single from the Love Symbol album. It stalled at No. 66 on the Hot 100 and No. 76 R&B, showing that when Prince tried to match rappers' crudeness, chart results suffered even when the music was funky. "stalled at number 66 on the hot 100 and number 76 on the R&B chart."
**7 (1993) - Last Top 10 as Prince** : An early-1993 hit from the Love Symbol album that peaked, fittingly, at No. 7. It was his last top 10 hit released under the name Prince. "peaked appropriately enough at number seven on the hot 100."
**The Most Beautiful Girl in the World (1994) - Hit as the Symbol** : Released under his unpronounceable symbol name, it reached No. 3 on the Hot 100 and No. 2 R&B. It was his final top 10 hit. "It was the kind of move that would make Tom Petty proud."
**1999 (1999 Chart Re-Entry)** : Around the 1999 New Year celebrations, the song drew enough sales and airplay to briefly return to the Hot 100. It was its first appearance there since 1983. "briefly reappear on the Hot 100 for the first time since 1983."
**Black Sweat (2006)** : A minor Hot 100 hit from "3121." "it even generated a minor Hot 100 hit with Black Swap."

## curiosities
**Musical Family and Self-Taught Polymath** : Prince Rogers Nelson, born in 1958, was Minneapolis's favorite son and an African-American polymath descended from jazz musicians. His father was a pianist and his mother a jazz singer, and he famously taught himself a range of instruments. He died in his late 50s. "an African-American polymath descended from jazz musicians who broke on urban radio."
**Warner Bros. Contract Demanded Pop Roster** : As critic Michaelangelo Matos noted in Pitchfork, Prince signed with Warner Bros. in 1977 at age 19. His contract gave him unusual creative control and explicitly placed him on the pop roster, not the R&B one, a distinction that shaped his whole career. "explicitly stated that he be part of the label's pop roster, not its R&B one."
**Christgau's Dirty Mind Review** : Robert Christgau, dean of American rock critics, reviewed "Dirty Mind" in what is perhaps his shortest and most famous review. "Mick Jagger should fold up his penis and go home."
**First-Ballot Rock Hall Inductee and the 2004 Solo** : Prince was inducted in 2004, his first year of eligibility. His guitar solo on "While My Guitar Gently Weeps" ran more than three minutes into the song. "a jaw-dropping solo that takes over three minutes into the song and carries it to the heavens."
**Imperial Period** : The success of "Little Red Corvette" and "Stand Back" began a stretch in which everything Prince touched went gold and platinum, both songs he kept and songs he gave away. "everything he touched turned to gold and platinum."
**1984 Protégé Hit Factory** : In 1984 Prince wrote and produced hits for many artists: Sheila E.'s "The Glamorous Life" (No. 7), the Time's "Jungle Love" (No. 20) and Sheena Easton's "Sugar Walls" (No. 9). "Prince was busy writing and producing hits for a parade of artists."
**Writing as "Christopher"** : Prince wrote "Manic Monday" for the Bangles under the pseudonym Christopher. "a second song Prince penned under the pseudonym Christopher sat at number two."
**Nothing Compares 2 U - Biggest Hit He Didn't Record** : Sinéad O'Connor's 1990 cover of a song he wrote in 1985 for The Family became the biggest hit of his career not recorded by him. "the biggest hit of his career not recorded by himself."
**Name Change and "Slave" Protest (1993)** : In 1993 Prince changed his name to the unpronounceable symbol from his last Warner Bros. album cover. He appeared in public with "slave" written on his cheek to protest what he saw as indentured servitude under his major-label contract. To run out the clock, he released stockpiled vault material as Prince at a faster pace, while putting out more polished work on other labels under the symbol name. DJs called him "the artist formerly known as Prince." "He appeared in public with the word slave written on his cheek."
**Struggles with New Jack Swing and Hip Hop** : As pop and R&B moved toward new jack swing and hip hop in the 90s, Prince tried to keep up with mixed success. He couldn't go toe-to-toe on the charts with gangsta acts like Ice Cube and Dr. Dre. "As top 40 radio swung toward hip-hop, Prince did his best to keep up, with varying degrees of success."
**Rolling Stone Praise for Tom Petty (2004)** : Weeks after the Rock Hall performance, in the Rolling Stone cover interview, Prince said nothing about the Hall, his solo or the Beatles song, and instead praised Petty. "it was an honor to play with Tom Petty. Free Fallen is one of my favorite songs."
**Posthumous Chart Surge (April 2016)** : In the two weeks after his death in April 2016, 19 of his albums re-entered the Billboard album chart. "19 of his albums re-entered the Billboard album chart."
