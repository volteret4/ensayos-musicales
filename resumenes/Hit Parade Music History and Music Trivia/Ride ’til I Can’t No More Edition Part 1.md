## songs
**Old Town Road (2019)** : Lil Nas X's debut single, which blew up on streaming services and initially debuted on the Hot 100 Pop Chart, Hot R&B Hip Hop songs, and Hot Country songs. It was removed from the Hot Country Songs chart by Billboard, sparking a nationwide debate about its genre, race, and country music's overdue racial reckoning. The remix featuring Billy Ray Cyrus topped the Hot 100, set an all-time record for most streams in a week (143 million), and held the number one spot for 19 weeks, becoming the longest-running number one in Hot 100 history. "It set records for most weeks a top billboards are in B chart, the biggest week of streaming ever, the fastest single to be certified diamond, and the biggest chart record of all most weeks at number one in Hot 100 history."

## curiosities
**Record-Setting Hot 100 Run (2019)** : "Old Town Road" set an all-time Hot 100 record by spending 19 weeks at number one. This achievement surpassed Mariah Carey and Boyz II Men's "One Sweet Day," which had held the record for 23 years with 16 weeks at number one. "It still holds the Hot 100 record for the longest run at number one, 19 weeks."
**Billboard's Removal from Hot Country Songs (2019)** : In March 2019, Billboard removed "Old Town Road" from the Hot Country Songs chart, claiming it "does not embrace enough elements of today's country music to chart" despite incorporating country and cowboy imagery. This decision sparked a widespread debate in mainstream media regarding the song's genre, the role of race, and the need for a racial reckoning in country music. "Billboard removed Old Town Road from that chart. Lil Nas X fell prey to the same gatekeeper by Nashville that had excluded other country style hits by black artists."
**Coming Out as Gay at Number One (2019)** : In June 2019, Lil Nas X, at 20 years old, came out as gay on the last day of Pride Month while "Old Town Road" was in its 13th week at number one. This made him the first LGBTQ artist to come out publicly while having the number one song in America. "The 20-year-old Montero Hill therefore became the first LGBTQ artist to come out of the closet while having the number one song in America."
**Record Streaming Week (2019)** : The remix of "Old Town Road" featuring Billy Ray Cyrus set an all-time record for most streams in a single week, reaching 143 million streams. These "gargantuan" streams allowed the song to dominate the pop chart through the spring and summer of 2019. "Not only topping the Hot 100, but setting an all-time record for most streams in a week, 143 million."
**Blocking Major Artists on Charts (2019)** : "Old Town Road" held the top spot on the pop chart for so long in 2019 that it blocked singles by prominent artists such as Ed Sheeran, Justin Bieber, Shawn Mendes, and Taylor Swift, all of whose summer hits peaked at number two behind it. "The song's streams were so gargantuan that it held the top of the pop chart through all of the spring and most of the summer of 2019, blocking singles by Ed Sheeran, Justin Bieber, Shawn Mendes, and even the mighty Taylor Swift, all of their summer hits peaked at number two, stuck behind Old Town Road."
**Fastest Diamond Certification (2019)** : "Old Town Road" became the fastest single ever to be certified diamond, signifying its immense commercial success and popularity. "It set records for most weeks a top billboards are in B chart, the biggest week of streaming ever, the fastest single to be certified diamond, and the biggest chart record of all most weeks at number one in Hot 100 history."

## albums
**Modern Sounds in Country and Western Music (1962)** : Released in 1962, this album was conceived by Ray Charles to demonstrate that country and soul music were fundamentally "the same goddamn thing exactly." Recorded in Los Angeles, it emulated the "Nashville sound" with lush arrangements. The album spent an impressive 14 weeks at number one on the album chart, longer than any other LP in 1962 except the "West Side Story" soundtrack, and spawned multiple hit singles. "The concept, according to Brother Ray, was to prove that country and soul music were, quote, the same goddamn thing exactly, unquote."
**Modern Sounds in Country and Western Music Volume Two (Year not specified)** : A quick follow-up album to the highly popular first volume of "Modern Sounds in Country and Western Music." This second volume continued the concept of blending country and R&B, generating additional hit singles for Ray Charles. "The LP was so popular, Charles recorded a quick follow up, a second volume of Modern Sounds, which generated hits like Brother Ray's Take on the 30s country standard You Are My Sunshine, a number seven pop number one R&B hit, and the Hank Williams classic Your Cheat and Heart, which hit number 29 pop number 23 R&B."

## curiosities
**Philosophical View of Country and Soul (1962)** : Ray Charles firmly believed that country and soul music were intrinsically "the same goddamn thing exactly," and "the same thing, man," rooted in aligned cultures whether performed in a honky-tonk or on the Chitlin circuit. "As Charles saw it, weather played at a honky-tonk or on the Chitlin circuit, the cultures were aligned at the root."
**Exclusion from Country Charts (1962)** : Despite covering country standards, Ray Charles's singles from "Modern Sounds in Country and Western Music" did not appear on the country charts in 1962. This mirrored Lil Nas X's later experience, as Nashville in 1962 "taken as a given that Ray Charles did not belong in the country format."
**Later Country Chart Success (1980s)** : Ray Charles's music did not chart on Billboard's Hot Country Songs until the 1980s, when his entries primarily consisted of duets with established country artists like Willie Nelson and George Jones. "Ray Charles's music would not touch Billboard's Hot Country Songs Chart until the 1980s. When he finally did, it was mainly in duets with the likes of Willie Nelson and George Jones."

# artist - Bubba Sparxxx

## members
Warren Mathis

## identity
White man from Lagrange, Georgia

## genres
Hick Hop
Rap

## albums
**Dark Days Bright Nights (2001)** : His debut album, released in 2001, which All Music described him as "the redneck version of Eminem." The album blended Hick Hop lyrics with "thumping beats" provided by a black production team, including Timothy "Timbaland" Mosley and Organized Noise. "In 2001, Warren Mathis, a white man from Lagrange, Georgia, released his debut album, Dark Days Bright Nights, under the name Bubba Sparks."
**Deliverance (2003)** : His follow-up album in 2003, on which Bubba Sparxxx made the country tropes more pronounced. Despite strong reviews, the album achieved lower sales, and none of its singles managed to break into the top 40 on either the pop or R&B Hip Hop charts. "so on his follow up album Deliverance in 2003, Bubba made the country tropes more overt."

## songs
**Ugly (2001)** : Produced by Timbaland, this song's video famously depicted Bubba and his crew, both white and black, splashing in a muddy pig pen with actual pigs. It charted well at pop and hip hop radio, reaching number 15 on the Hot 100 and number 6 on the R&B Hip Hop chart, but did not chart on country radio. "The video for Bubba Sparks' Ugly, produced by Timbaland, featured Bubba and his crew, White and Black, splashing around in a muddy pig pen with actual pigs."
**Deliverance (2003)** : The title track from his 2003 album, where Bubba Sparxxx rapped over acoustic guitar licks, indicating a more direct integration of country sounds. "The album's title track found Bubba wrapping over acoustic guitar licks."
**Coming Round (2003)** : The most acclaimed track from the album "Deliverance," in which Bubba Sparxxx sampled the banjo and fiddle-led "Two See Coming Round the Bend" by the Yonder Mountain String Band and transformed it into a "banger." "on the album's most acclaimed track, Bubba sampled the banjo and fiddle-led two-seew coming round the bend by the Yonder Mountain string band."
**Ms. New Booty (2006)** : A "dirty South Jam" that became a breakthrough three years after "Deliverance," bringing Bubba Sparxxx into the top 10. Featuring a guest refrain from Atlanta Crunk Kings, the Ying Yang Twins, this catchy single hit number 7 on both the pop and R&B charts, yet contained "scarcely any country in it" beyond minor lyrical references. "Three years later, what finally brought Bubba Sparks into the top 10 was a dirty South Jam called Misnue booty."

## curiosities
**Connection to Colt Ford**: Bubba Sparxxx had an "arm's length connection" to Colt Ford through Ford's producer, Shannon Hootchens (Fat Sean), who had previously worked with Timbaland on Bubba's debut album. "Interestingly, Ford had an arm's length connection to the last major white rapper who'd attempted Hick Hop. Bubba Sparks."

# genre - Country

## curiosities
**Exclusion of Black Artists (Historical)** : Nashville historically acted as a gatekeeper, excluding country-style hits by Black artists from its charts. This was evident with Ray Charles in 1962 and again with Lil Nas X in 2019, sparking debates about racial bias and the need for a racial reckoning in country music. "Lil Nas X fell prey to the same gatekeeper by Nashville that had excluded other country style hits by black artists."
**Nashville Sound (1950s-1960s)** : A prominent stylistic movement that swept through country music, leading to "country-politan" records like Patsy Cline's "Crazy." This sound introduced a new level of sophistication to the genre and achieved crossover success on both pop and country charts. "Styleistically, Charles was emulating the famed Nashville sound that had swept through country music in the 50s and early 60s."
**Challenges for Black Artists on Charts (1970s-1980s)** : During this period, most country-flavored hits by Black artists, with exceptions like Charlie Pride, were typically confined to the Hot 100 and the Soul Chart, rather than being allowed onto the Hot Country Songs chart. "We talked about the challenges Black artists faced crossing over on the country charts in our Darius Rucker episode of Hit Parade earlier this year."

## curiosities
**Mixed Results for Crossover (2000s)** : In the 2000s, several acts attempted to launch rap careers from within country music, coining the term "Hick Hop," but the results were "decidedly mixed." They even coined a term for it, Hick Hop, but the results for Hick Hop were decidedly mixed."

This acceptance was facilitated when Hick Hop increasingly distanced itself "from actual rap production and black co-creators like Timbaland," making it easier to register on country radio. "The path from Bubba Sparks to Colt Ford to Jason Aldin exemplifies how a certain kind of rap became acceptable in country music."
