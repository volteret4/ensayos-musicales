## albums
**The Barbra Streisand Album (1963) - Debut LP and Album of the Year Grammy Winner** : Recorded in early 1963, when Streisand was still only 20. She avoided the rock and pop then on the radio and instead reinterpreted material ranging from Disney's "Who's Afraid of the Big Bad Wolf" to her beloved Harold Arlen composition "A Sleepin' Bee." She rejected Columbia's proposed title, *Sweet and Saucy Streisand*. The LP entered the charts in April 1963 and, despite having no radio singles, became a hit on the strength of her growing Broadway fame and a string of TV appearances, reaching No. 8 on the Top LPs chart by summer 1963. At the 1964 Grammys it beat the Singing Nun, Andy Williams' chart-topper *The Days of Wine and Roses* and Al Hirt's light-jazz LP *Honey in the Horn* for Album of the Year. That made Streisand, just weeks past her 22nd birthday, the youngest Album of the Year winner to that date, a record she held for more than 30 years until Alanis Morissette won in 1996. "Simply titled The Barbara Streisand album, she rejected Columbia's proposed title Sweet and Saucy Streisand."
**The Second Barbra Streisand Album (1963) - Blocked at No. 2 by the Singing Nun** : Streisand recorded this follow-up quickly. It reached No. 2 by fall 1963 while her debut was still in the Top 10, so both albums were in the Top 10 at once. In her audiobook she recalls that the Singing Nun kept it from reaching No. 1: "People just couldn't get that catchy refrain out of their heads, I guess." "Do you know who kept me from reaching number 1?"
**Funny Girl Original Broadway Cast Album (1964) - No. 2 Before the Beatles Arrived** : The cast album of Streisand's 1964 Broadway hit reached No. 2 on Top LPs. The Beatles' *A Hard Day's Night* soundtrack then arrived and held No. 1 for 14 straight weeks. "The Funny Girl cast album did manage to reach number two on top LP's before the Hard Days Night LP arrived and took the number one spot for 14 straight weeks."
**People (1964) - First No. 1 Album** : Columbia sent Streisand back into the studio to build a full LP around her Broadway single "People." The album arrived in fall 1964, knocked the Beatles off the top of the album chart and gave Streisand her first No. 1 LP. It stayed at No. 1 for more than a month, until just before the Christmas season. "Amazingly, it ousted the Beatles from the top of the album chart, giving Barbara Streisand her first number one LP."
**TV Special Soundtracks and Hit LPs (1964–1966) - String of Top Five Albums** : For two years, while she kept playing Fanny Brice on Broadway, Streisand released a string of hit LPs, including soundtracks to two TV specials. Every one of them made the Top 5 on the album chart. "All of them made the top five on the album chart."
**Christmas Album (1967) - Holiday LP Released Alongside a Studio Album** : Once her stage commitments ended, Streisand recorded both a regular studio album and a Christmas album and released them the same week in October 1967. The holiday album was the bigger success and topped Billboard's special Christmas LPs chart. Her regular albums, meanwhile, began missing the Top 10. "The holiday album was the greater success, topping Billboard's special Christmas LP's chart."
**Funny Girl Soundtrack (1968) - Film Debut Soundtrack** : The film version of *Funny Girl*, which launched her Hollywood career, was a box-office blockbuster and also produced a hit soundtrack. "And a hit soundtrack, it also gave Streisand her first Oscar in her debut film role."
**What About Today? (1969) - First All-Contemporary Pop Album** : Streisand's first album made up entirely of contemporary pop songs, an attempt to keep up as the charts moved away from pop standards. It included covers of "Frank Mills" from the musical *Hair*, Simon & Garfunkel's "Punky's Dilemma," and the Beatles' "Honey Pie." None of the singles charted. The LP peaked at No. 31, her lowest-charting album of the 1960s. "The What About Today LP peaked at number 31, Streisand's lowest charting album of the 60s."
**The Way We Were (1974) - Second No. 1 Album** : Helped by the title-song smash and by her Top 10 easy-listening single "All in Love Is Fair," a Stevie Wonder cover, the LP reached No. 1 on the Billboard album chart. It was her second No. 1 album, after *People* in 1964. "Helped push the way we were LP to number 1 on the Billboard album chart."
**A Star Is Born Soundtrack (1977) - Third No. 1 Album** : The soundtrack to Streisand's 1976 remake reached No. 1, her third chart-topping LP after *People* (1964) and *The Way We Were* (1974). It was driven by "Evergreen," the film's love theme. "The soundtrack to A Star Is Born in 1977."
**Greatest Hits Volume Two (1978) - Fourth No. 1 Album** : The success of the "You Don't Bring Me Flowers" duet with Neil Diamond pushed this 1978 compilation to No. 1 on the album chart, her fourth No. 1 LP after *People* (1964), *The Way We Were* (1974) and the *A Star Is Born* soundtrack (1977). "Her fourth number one LP, by the way, after people in 1964, the way we were in 1974 and the soundtrack to A Star Is Born in 1977."
**Wet (1979) - Water-Themed Concept Album** : A concept album in which every song had a water theme, including her future hit "Kiss Me in the Rain." Because of that concept, Streisand asked Paul Jabara and Bruce Roberts to add water lyrics to "Enough Is Enough," which led to the "No More Tears" ballad intro. "A concept album called Wet, in which all of the songs would have water themes."
**Ninth No. 1 Album (2009) - Upset Over Mariah Carey** : In late 2009 Streisand topped the Billboard 200 with her ninth No. 1 album, an upset over Mariah Carey. That made her the only artist to score a No. 1 album in five different decades. The episode plays her recording of "In the Wee Small Hours of the Morning." "Streisand became the only artist to score a number 1 album in five decades."
**Eleventh No. 1 Album (2016) - Six Decades of Chart-Toppers** : Streisand scored her 11th No. 1 album in 2016. That makes her the only act with No. 1 albums in six decades (1960s, '70s, '80s, '90s, 2000s and 2010s). It also stood as the record for most No. 1 albums by a woman until Taylor Swift's 12th in 2023. "Barbara Streisand scored her 11th number 1 album less than a decade ago in 2016."

## songs
**Happy Days Are Here Again (1962) - Debut Single** : Streisand's first single reworked the standard made famous during Franklin Delano Roosevelt's 1932 presidential campaign as a torch song, an approach that foreshadowed her entire career. She only began recording after her Broadway success, having originally wanted to act rather than sing. "Streisand's first single was a take on Happy Days are here again."
**Miss Marmelstein (1962) - Broadway Showstopper** : Streisand originated the role of Miss Marmelstein in the 1962 musical *I Can Get It for You Wholesale*, her Broadway debut. It was a supporting role to Harry Bogen, played by her then-boyfriend Elliott Gould. She sang the song while wheeling around the stage in an office chair, and her witty performance reportedly stopped the show every night. Her sensational debut arguably extended the golden era of Broadway on the charts by a year or two. "Reportedly stopped the show every night."
**A Sleepin' Bee (1963) - Harold Arlen Favorite** : Written by Harold Arlen, whom a boyfriend introduced her to and who became her favorite composer. The song comes from Arlen's relatively obscure musical *House of Flowers* and was made famous by actress Diahann Carroll. Streisand recorded it for her debut album. "She especially loved Arlen's relatively obscure musical House of Flowers."
**Who's Afraid of the Big Bad Wolf (1963) - Disney Reinterpretation** : Streisand reinterpreted the Disney song on her debut LP, part of her choice to avoid contemporary rock and pop. "She reinterpreted everything from Disney's Who's Afraid of the Big Bad Wolf."
**People (1964) - First Hot 100 Hit and First Top 5 Single** : This ballad about empathy and understanding from *Funny Girl* had built months of buzz during out-of-town runs and previews. Streisand recorded the single before the original cast album was even finished. It debuted on the Hot 100 a week after *Funny Girl* opened on Broadway in March 1964 and reached No. 5 twelve weeks later, while competing with Beatlemania and at a time when Broadway songs no longer reliably ruled the charts. In her memoir, Streisand says she doesn't think the lyric "people who need people are the luckiest people in the world" makes sense either. "People reached number five, becoming her first top five single."
**Second Hand Rose (1966) - Fanny Brice Cover** : Although her singles in this period were not big hits, this cover of a song made famous by Fanny Brice reached the Top 40 in early 1966. "She did crack the top 40 in early 1966 with a cover of Second Hand Rose, a song made famous by Fanny Bryce."
**Frank Mills (1969) - Hair Cover** : Streisand covered this song from *Hair*, the new Broadway sensation that was producing hit songs by 1969. The single did not chart. "She covered the hair song Frank Mills, but the single did not chart."
**Punky's Dilemma (1969) - Simon & Garfunkel Cover** : After Paul Simon and Art Garfunkel took over the charts in 1968, Streisand covered their song. It did not chart. "Barbara tried her hand at their Diddy punkies dilemma, but it too did not chart."
**Honey Pie (1969) - First Beatles Cover** : Her first attempt at covering the Beatles was Paul McCartney's music-hall-style song from the White Album. It did not chart. "She honed in on Paul McCartney's Music Hall-style Diddy from the White Album Honeypie."
**Stoney End (1971) - Comeback Hit by Laura Nyro** : Producer Richard Perry encouraged her to cover Laura Nyro's 1967 song, a completely new sound for Streisand. Its loose, soulful arrangement suited her voice and gave it a gospel-like tone. It was her first Top 40 hit in half a decade and reached No. 6 on the Hot 100 by early 1971. "The shambling soulful Stoney End proved an unusual but compelling fit for Streisand's voice, evoking a gospel-like tone."
**Where You Lead (1971/1972) - Carole King Cover, Twice in the Top 40** : Streisand admired this deep cut from Carole King's *Tapestry* and covered it. Her studio recording reached No. 40 in 1971. A live version, performed at a fundraiser for Democratic presidential candidate George McGovern, reached No. 37 in 1972. "Taking where you lead into the top 40 twice."
**The Way We Were (1974) - First No. 1 Single** : The title song from Sydney Pollack's period romantic drama, in which Streisand plays a political activist who becomes involved with a writer and war veteran played by Robert Redford. It was written by composer Marvin Hamlisch with husband-and-wife lyricists Alan and Marilyn Bergman, and its lyrics wistfully recall a cherished but untenable romance. Released in fall 1973, just before the film, it reached No. 1 on the Hot 100 for the week ending February 2, 1974, just as it earned an Oscar nomination, and stayed there three weeks. It was still in the Top 40 in early April when it won the Oscar for Best Original Song, and Casey Kasem counted it down as the No. 1 song of 1974 on American Top 40's year-end show. It was later referenced in Tom Hanks' *Big*, and Gladys Knight & the Pips covered it in 1975. "It's the song that solidified Streisand's reputation as your mom's favorite singer."
**All in Love Is Fair (1974) - Stevie Wonder Cover** : Streisand adapted this deep cut from Stevie Wonder's *Innervisions* into a single. It went Top 10 on Billboard's easy-listening chart and helped push *The Way We Were* LP to No. 1. "She adapted all in love is fair, a deep cut from Stevie Wonder's inner visions LP."
**Evergreen (1977) - Love Theme from A Star Is Born, Oscar for Songwriting** : Before this, Streisand had taken only a couple of minor co-writing credits. For *A Star Is Born* she taught herself guitar and came up with a few chords that she thought sounded like a song. She took them to songwriter Paul Williams, who wrote the lyrics. The song debuted on the Hot 100 in December 1976 and reached No. 1 by March 1977. Streisand and Williams won the Oscar for Best Original Song at the 1977 ceremony, announced by Neil Diamond. The win gave her a second Oscar and made her a consistent chart presence. "Streisand taught herself guitar and came up with a few chords that she thought sounded like a song."
**My Heart Belongs to Me (1977) - Post-Evergreen Top 5** : A spring 1977 single that showed her new consistency in the Top 40, peaking at No. 4. "Shot straight into the top 10 peaking at number four."
**You Don't Bring Me Flowers (1978) - Accidental Duet with Neil Diamond** : Neil Diamond co-wrote and recorded this heartbreak ballad, and Streisand recorded it in the same key on her own 1978 solo LP. An enterprising DJ spliced the two recordings into a duet. Inspired by that mashup, Diamond and Streisand recorded it properly as a duet, and the single went to No. 1 in fall 1978. Its success pushed *Greatest Hits Volume Two* to No. 1. "An enterprising DJ spliced together the two recordings to turn You Don't Bring Me Flowers into a duet."
**The Main Event/Fight (1979) - First Disco Single** : The song comes from the 1979 boxing satire and romantic comedy *The Main Event*, co-starring Ryan O'Neal, which critics panned but which was a box-office hit. It was written by Paul Jabara and was her first disco single, peaking at No. 3 on the Hot 100 in summer 1979. "Her first foray into disco."
**No More Tears (Enough Is Enough) (1979) - Duet with Donna Summer** : Paul Jabara and Bruce Roberts wrote the disco anthem "Enough Is Enough." Streisand, then working on the water-themed *Wet*, asked them to add water lyrics, so they added a ballad opening of more than a minute called "No More Tears." Jabara had persuaded her to work with Donna Summer, and legend has it the two sang so hard that Summer fell off her stool. The single debuted in October 1979 and took six weeks to reach No. 1. It also gave Streisand her first No. 1 on the disco chart (where she topped 31 years before Duck Sauce's song, to the week) and reached the R&B chart. It hit No. 1 months after the "disco sucks" movement and Disco Demolition Night. "The two vocal powerhouses sang so hard at one point Donna fell off her stool."
**Kiss Me in the Rain (1979) - Wet-Era Hit** : One of the water-themed songs from the *Wet* concept album, later a hit for Streisand. "Like for example her future hit Kiss Me in the Rain."

## curiosities
**Brooklyn Childhood - Born 1942** : Born in 1942 and raised in Brooklyn, Streisand was singing by fourth grade. As a young teen she went into Manhattan with a friend to see Broadway shows, preferring straight plays such as *The Diary of Anne Frank* and *Inherit the Wind* to musicals. She dreamed of acting on stage, not singing, and only warmed to musicals after seeing film versions such as *Kiss Me Kate* and *The King and I*. "Born in 1942 and raised in Brooklyn, Streisand started singing as early as 4th grade."
**Marlon Brando in Guys and Dolls - A Formative Moment** : At 13, at Brooklyn's Kings Theatre, Streisand was mesmerized by Marlon Brando, not much of a singer, in the 1955 film *Guys and Dolls*. Her memoir says it was one of the first times she thought musical theatre might be for her. Decades later the two became friends. "Streisand was mesmerized when she saw him on screen at age 13 at Brooklyn's King's Theatre."
**Nearly Missing Broadway's Chart Golden Era** : When Streisand was a teenager, Broadway musicals dominated the charts. By the time she was auditioning and singing in nightclubs in the early 1960s, that era was ending, though her 1962 debut arguably extended it by a year or two. "The golden era of the musical on the charts was nearing its end."
**Columbia Contract - Artistic Control at 20** : When she signed with Columbia Records, Streisand took a smaller advance in exchange for the right to choose her own material. That level of control was virtually unheard of for any artist then, let alone a 20-year-old woman, and it paid off with the 1964 Album of the Year Grammy. "Streisand accepted a smaller advance in exchange for the ability to choose her own material."
**Remembering Nothing of Her Grammy Night** : In her memoir Streisand says she remembers nothing of her big 1964 Grammy night, which was not televised. By spring 1964 she was back on Broadway in *Funny Girl*. "That she remembers nothing."
**Funny Girl on Broadway (1964) - Playing Fanny Brice** : The musical about Vaudeville-era actress Fanny Brice seemed tailored to Streisand, but its creators made her work hard for the role and spent months refining the book and songs. It opened in March 1964 as a smash, with "People" already a hit. She played Fanny Brice for about two more years. "The creators made Streisand sweat for the role."
**Burnout and Chart Slump (1967–1969)** : Her relentless schedule began to take its toll around 1967. Her regular albums started missing the Top 10, and from 1967 to 1969 none of her singles made the Top 40, as her move to Hollywood drew her attention away from the pop charts. "Between 1967 and 1969 none of her singles made the top 40."
**1969 Oscar Tie with Katharine Hepburn** : At the 1969 Academy Awards, Ingrid Bergman announced a tie for Best Actress between Katharine Hepburn (*The Lion in Winter*) and Streisand (*Funny Girl*), her first film role. Streisand opened her speech with her *Funny Girl* line, "Hello, gorgeous," and said she was honored to be in Hepburn's company. "The winner is a tie."
**Turning Down Future No. 1 Hits** : Streisand turned down at least two songs that became No. 1 hits for others: Helen Reddy's "Delta Dawn" and Maureen McGovern's "The Morning After," which also won the Oscar for Best Original Song at the 1973 Academy Awards. "She turned down at least two songs that eventually became number 1 hits for other singers."
**Bob Dylan's Intended Duet - Lay Lady Lay** : Bob Dylan wrote his 1969 Top 10 hit "Lay Lady Lay" intending to sing it as a duet with Streisand, but he never managed to reach her. "He never managed to get in touch with her."
**1972 Box-Office Run** : In 1972 she starred back to back in the romantic comedies *The Owl and the Pussycat* and *What's Up, Doc?*, both box-office smashes, while her music career was erratic. "Each a box office smash."
**A Star Is Born (1976) - Producer and Rock-Star Vehicle** : With clout at both the box office and on the charts, Streisand headlined and produced a musical remake of *A Star Is Born*, recasting it as a music-business story with Kris Kristofferson. She conceived it as a vehicle to give her the swagger of a contemporary rock star. It was the third-highest-grossing film released in 1976. "Conceived by her as a vehicle to give her the swagger of a contemporary rock star."
**Neil Diamond's Oscar Joke (1977)** : Presenting Best Original Song in 1977, her friend and fellow Brooklynite Neil Diamond said he had told Streisand he would read her name whatever happened, so if he read it she had truly won. He then announced "Evergreen." In her speech she said she had never imagined winning an Oscar for songwriting. "In my wildest dreams I never, never could ever imagine winning an Academy Award for writing a song."
**Catching Disco as It Crashed** : Streisand's disco hits came just as disco was collapsing commercially. "No More Tears" reached No. 1 months after the "disco sucks" movement and Chicago's Disco Demolition Night in summer 1979. She was also about to work with the Bee Gees, a group even more tied to disco. "Funnily enough, Barbara caught disco just as it was crashing in the pop marketplace."
**My Name Is Barbra - Memoir 40 Years in the Making** : Her autobiography, about 1,000 pages long, was published in November 2023 and became a bestseller. Jacqueline Kennedy Onassis, then a Doubleday editor, reportedly asked her to write a memoir in 1984. "Jacqueline Kennedy-Onazes asked Streisand to pen a memoir back in 1984."
**48-Hour Audiobook Narrated by Streisand** : Streisand narrates the audiobook herself in her Brooklyn accent, often riffing on her own text and analyzing her music. Discussing one recording, she points out the squeaks in the opening bars as the signature of arranger Peter Matz. "The audiobook's total running time is 48 hours."
**"I Never Went Into the Studio Thinking I've Got a Hit"** : In her memoir Streisand says she has little feel for hitmaking. She says she chose songs that were not especially commercial and does not remember which ones reached No. 1, though she still mentions being kept from No. 1 by the Singing Nun. "To this day, I don't even remember which songs reach the top spot."
**The Streisand Effect** : Her attempt to keep photos of her waterfront mansion off the internet gave rise to the term "the Streisand effect." "Go ahead, Google the Streisand effect."
**Hot 100 Career Totals** : Streisand has had almost four dozen Hot 100 hits, more than half of them in the Top 40. "And that voice appeared on almost four dozen hot 100 hits, more than half of them top 40 hits."
**Six Decades of No. 1 Albums** : She is the only act with No. 1 albums in six decades, from the 1960s through the 2010s. She held the record for most No. 1 albums by a woman (11) until Taylor Swift's 12th in 2023. "Streisand remains the only act to have achieved number 1 albums in 6 decades."
**Flattered by Duck Sauce** : Streisand later said she was "flattered" by Duck Sauce's 2010 club hit named after her. "She was, quote, flattered by the club song bearing her name."
**The Way We Were in Tom Hanks' Big** : In *Big*, Tom Hanks' character, a boy trapped in a man's body, proves his identity to his mother by singing "The Way We Were" to her over the phone. "Proves who he is to his mother by singing it to her over the phone."
**Nicknames** : She is known as "the voice" and "Babs." "We will take on the voice, aka Barbara Jones, aka Babs."

# curiosity

**A Star Is Born - Four Versions** : The story of a rising female star whose drunken male partner declines began as a 1937 film-industry tragedy starring Janet Gaynor. It was remade as a musical in 1954 with Judy Garland and turned into a music-business story in 1976 by Streisand with Kris Kristofferson. **American Top 40's No. 1 Song of 1974** : On American Top 40's 1974 year-end show, Casey Kasem named Streisand's "The Way We Were," written by Marvin Hamlisch, the year's No. 1 song. "The song that comes in at the number one position in our countdown of the 100 Top Hits of 1974."
**1964 Album of the Year Contenders** : The 1964 Grammy field for Album of the Year included Streisand's debut, the Singing Nun, Andy Williams' chart-topper *The Days of Wine and Roses*, and Al Hirt's light-jazz LP *Honey in the Horn*. Streisand won. "Trumpeter Al Hertz smash light jazz LP Honey in the Horn."
