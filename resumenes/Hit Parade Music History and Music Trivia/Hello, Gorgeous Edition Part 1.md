## albums
**The Barbara Streisand Album (1963)** : Recorded in early 1963, this LP saw Streisand eschew contemporary rock and pop sounds, instead reinterpreting standards from Disney's "Who's Afraid of the Big Bad Wolf" to Harold Arlen's "A Sleepin' Be." Streisand insisted on choosing her own material, accepting a smaller advance from Columbia Records for this unprecedented artistic control, and rejected the label's suggested title "Sweet and Saucy Streisand." Fueled by her Broadway fame and charming TV appearances, it hit number eight on the Top LPs chart by summer 1963. "The LP hit the charts in April 1963, and, despite a lack of radio singles, quickly became a hit."
**The Second Barbara Streisand Album (1963)** : This follow-up LP climbed to number two on the charts by fall 1963, while her first album was still in the top 10. It was ultimately blocked from reaching number one by The Singing Nun. "The second album got all the way to number 2. Do you know who kept me from reaching number 1? The singing Nanda Manikani, Kasson Alain, and something like that?"
**People (1964)** : This album was built around Streisand's hit single "People" from the Broadway musical "Funny Girl." Released in the fall of 1964, it achieved the remarkable feat of ousting The Beatles' "A Hard Day's Night" LP from the top of the album chart, becoming Streisand's first number one LP. It maintained the number one position for over a month, until just before the Christmas season. "Amazingly, it ousted the Beatles from the top of the album chart, giving Barbara Streisand her first number one LP."
**What About Today (1969)** : This album marked Streisand's first effort composed entirely of contemporary pop songs. Despite her attempts to cover popular tracks like Hair's "Frank Mills," Paul Simon and Art Garfunkel's "Punky's Dilemma," and The Beatles' "Honeypie," none of the singles from the album charted. The LP itself peaked at number 31, making it Streisand's lowest-charting album of the 1960s. "The What About Today LP peaked at number 31, Streisand's lowest charting album of the 60s."
**The Way We Were (1974)** : This album, featuring the hugely successful title track, was pushed to number one on the Billboard album chart. Its success was further bolstered by Streisand's cover of Stevie Wonder's "All in Love is Fair," which became a top 10 hit on Billboard's easy listening chart. "all in love is fair, a deep cut from Stevie Wonder's inner visions LP. Into a single that went top 10 on Billboard's easy listening chart and helped push the way we were LP to number 1 on the Billboard album chart."
**A Star Is Born (Soundtrack) (1976)** : This soundtrack accompanied Streisand's reimagining of the Hollywood classic as a musical business fable, where she not only headlined but also produced. The album featured the song "Evergreen," which she co-wrote, and contributed to the film becoming the third highest-grossing film released in 1976. This soundtrack was Streisand's third number one LP. "The project also gave Streisand clout in one other area as a songwriter."
**Greatest Hits Volume Two (1978)** : Following the immense success of the duet "You Don't Bring Me Flowers" with Neil Diamond, this compilation album ascended to number one on the album chart. It marked Streisand's fourth number one LP, following "People" (1964), "The Way We Were" (1974), and the soundtrack to "A Star Is Born" (1977). "In turn, that success sent Barbara's 1978 compilation, greatest hits volume two to number one on the album chart."
**Wet (1979)** : This was a concept album in which all of the songs were intended to have water themes. During its production, songwriter Paul Jabara and Bruce Roberts grafted a ballad section called "No More Tears" onto their fierce disco anthem "Enough Is Enough" to incorporate Streisand's water theme, leading to her duet with Donna Summer. "At the time, Strison was working on a concept album called Wet, in which all of the songs would have water themes, like for example her future hit Kiss Me in the Rain."
**My Name Is Barbara (2023)** : This long-awaited autobiography was released in November 2023 and became a best-selling memoir. Former First Lady Jacqueline Kennedy-Onassis reportedly asked Streisand to write it as early as 1984. The audiobook, narrated by Streisand herself, has a total running time of 48 hours and features Streisand often riffing extemporaneously on her own writing and dissecting her music. "How long awaited, reportedly former first lady and then double day book editor Jacqueline Kennedy-Onazes asked Streisand to pen a memoir back in 1984."

## songs
**Happy Days Are Here Again (1962)** : Streisand's first single, released in 1962, was a reimagining of the standard made famous during Franklin Delano Roosevelt's 1932 presidential run. Barbara transformed it into a torch song, a style that foreshadowed her entire career. "reimagined by Barbara in 1962 as a torch song. It foreshadowed Streisand's entire career."
**Miss Marmelstein (1962)** : Streisand originated this song as part of her role in the 1962 Broadway debut, "I Can Get It For You Wholesale." While it was a supporting role, her witty performance, which included wheeling around the stage in an office chair, reportedly stopped the show every night. "Streisand's witty performance, she sang the song Miss Marmelstein while wheeling around the stage in an office chair, reportedly stopped the show every night."
**People (1964)** : This poignant ballad about empathy and understanding debuted on the Hot 100 just a week after the Broadway opening of "Funny Girl" in March 1964. It had already generated months of buzz during out-of-town productions and previews. Recorded before the original cast album was complete, it became Streisand's first Hot 100 hit and her first top five single, reaching number five after twelve weeks. "The poignant ballot about empathy and understanding had already built months of buzz during out of town productions and previews for the show."
**Second Hand Rose (1966)** : Streisand cracked the top 40 in early 1966 with her cover of this song, which was famously performed by Fanny Brice, the vaudeville actress Streisand portrayed on Broadway in "Funny Girl." "those Streisand's singles were not massive hits, she did crack the top 40 in early 1966 with a cover of Second Hand Rose, a song made famous by Fanny Bryce."
**Frank Mills (1969)** : Streisand covered this song from the Broadway musical "Hair" for her 1969 album "What About Today," which was composed entirely of contemporary pop songs. However, her single did not chart. "For her 1969 album What About Today, Streisand's first album composed entirely of contemporary pop songs, she covered the hair song Frank Mills, but the single did not chart."
**Punky's Dilemma (1969)** : Following Paul Simon and Art Garfunkel's chart dominance in 1968, Barbara Streisand attempted to cover their song "Punky's Dilemma" for her 1969 album "What About Today." Despite the duo's popularity, Streisand's rendition did not chart. "Barbara tried her hand at their Diddy punkies dilemma, but it too did not chart."
**Honeypie (1969)** : Streisand covered this Music Hall-style ditty from The Beatles' "White Album," penned by Paul McCartney, for her 1969 album "What About Today." However, her take on the song, in an attempt to keep current, did not chart. "Perhaps unsurprisingly, she honed in on Paul McCartney's Music Hall-style Diddy from the White Album Honeypie. But yet again, Barbara's take did not chart."
**Stoney End (1970)** : Encouraged by producer Richard Perry, Streisand covered Laura Nyro's 1967 song, representing a truly new, soulful sound for Barbara that evoked a gospel-like tone. Released in 1970, "Stoney End" became Streisand's first top 40 hit in five years and climbed to number 6 on the Hot 100 by early 1971. "The shambling soulful Stoney End proved an unusual but compelling fit for Streisand's voice, evoking a gospel-like tone."
**Where You Lead (1971)** : Admiring Carole King's blockbuster album "Tapestry," Streisand covered its deep cut "Where You Lead." A studio recording of the song reached number 40 in 1971. Later, a live version performed by Streisand at a fundraiser for Democratic presidential candidate George McGovern reached number 37 in 1972. "And so Barbara covered Carol as well, taking where you lead into the top 40 twice, a studio recording reached number 40 in 1971, and a live version, performed by Barbara at a fundraiser for Democratic presidential candidate. George McGovern reached number 37 in 1972."
**The Way We Were (1973)** : This title song for the 1973 film of the same name was written by composer Marvin Hamlisch and songwriters Alan and Marilyn Bergman. Its lyrics were wistful, recalling an untenable romance with nostalgia and warmth. Released in fall 1973, it climbed the charts while the film dominated the box office. In February 1974, it topped the Hot 100 for three weeks, becoming Streisand's first-ever number one single. It remained in the top 40 until early April, winning the Academy Award for Best Original Song. "The title song for the movie The Way We Were was written by Skor composer Marvin Hamlish and husband and wife songwriters Alan and Marilyn Bergman. Its lyrics were wistful, recalling the highlights of a cherished but untenable romance with nostalgia and warmth."
**All in Love Is Fair (1974)** : Streisand adapted this deep cut from Stevie Wonder's "Inner Visions" LP into a single. It went top 10 on Billboard's easy listening chart and contributed to pushing "The Way We Were" LP to number one on the Billboard album chart. "Streisand herself was covering even more contemporary material. She adapted all in love is fair, a deep cut from Stevie Wonder's inner visions LP. Into a single that went top 10 on Billboard's easy listening chart."
**Evergreen (Love Theme from A Star Is Born) (1976)** : Streisand taught herself guitar and composed a few chords for this song, which Paul Williams then completed with lyrics. "Evergreen" defined the movie "A Star Is Born," where it was the love theme, and debuted on the Hot 100 in December 1976, climbing to number one by March 1977, her second number one single. Streisand, along with Paul Williams, was nominated for and won an Academy Award for the song in 1977, making her an Oscar-winning songwriter. "Evergreen, the love theme from a star is born, did for that movie what the way we were had done three years earlier. It was released with Rose Alongside and defined the movie."
**My Heart Belongs to Me (1977)** : This single, released in spring 1977, shot straight into the top 10 on the charts, peaking at number four. This marked a period where Streisand's singles more consistently cracked the top 40, solidifying her imperial chart presence after the success of "Evergreen." "A spring 1977 single for example, my heart belongs to me. Shot straight into the top 10 peaking at number four."
**You Don't Bring Me Flowers (1978)** : Originally co-written and recorded by Neil Diamond, and also recorded by Streisand in the same key on her 1978 solo LP, an enterprising DJ spliced the two recordings together to create a makeshift duet. Inspired by this, Diamond and Streisand went into the studio to re-record the song as an actual duet, which then shot to number one in the fall of 1978. "An enterprising DJ spliced together the two recordings to turn You Don't Bring Me Flowers into a duet."
**The Main Event / Fight (1979)** : This song was Streisand's first foray into disco, released from the boxing satire/romantic comedy "The Main Event," which she starred in with Ryan O'Neal. Despite critics panning the film, it was a box office hit, and the single peaked at number three on the Hot 100 in the summer of 1979. "Her first foray into disco. The main event fight peaked at number three on the Hot 100 in the summer of 1979."
**No More Tears (Enough Is Enough) (1979)** : This duet with Donna Summer was essentially a guaranteed smash, bringing together two of the biggest female pop stars at their imperial peak. Songwriter Paul Jabara convinced Streisand to record the song with Summer. Jabara and Bruce Roberts added a minute-long ballad section, "No More Tears," with water-themed lyrics to their fierce disco anthem "Enough Is Enough" to suit Streisand's "Wet" concept album. Debuting in October 1979, the single rose to number one on the Hot 100 in just six weeks, also topping the disco chart (Streisand's first time) and cracking the R&B chart. "The two biggest female pop stars each at her Imperial Peak at the time on one song."

## curiosities
**#1 Album in Five Decades Record (2009)** : In late 2009, when Streisand topped the Billboard 200 album chart with her ninth number one album, she became the only artist to score a number one album in five consecutive decades: the 1960s, 70s, 80s, 90s, and 2000s. "Streisand became the only artist to score a number 1 album in five decades."
**Long-Term Chart Success** : Streisand has scored chart-topping singles and blockbuster LPs since the 1960s, simultaneously becoming one of America's biggest movie and pop stars in the 70s. Her stage, screen, and pop chart fame developed concurrently, with her film and Broadway success boosting her music hits from the 1960s through the 1980s and beyond, proving adaptable with contemporary hitmakers from disco to rock. "She built her stage, screen and pop chart fame all at the same time."
**Grammy Award for Album of the Year (1964)** : At the 1964 Grammy Awards, Streisand's first album won Album of the Year, making her the youngest person to win the award to that date, just three weeks past her 22nd birthday. She had recorded the LP when she was still 20. This benchmark stood for over 30 years until Alanis Morissette won at a slightly younger age in 1996. "Amazingly Streisand won, becoming the youngest ever album of the year Grammy winner to that date, just weeks past her 22nd birthday."
**Most #1 LPs by a Woman (Until 2023)** : Streisand held the record for the woman with the most number one LPs in chart history with 11 number one albums, a milestone she reached with her 11th number one album in 2016. This record was surpassed by Taylor Swift in 2023 when Swift debuted her 12th career number one album. "Barbara Streisand scored her 11th number 1 album less than a decade ago in 2016."
**#1 Album in Six Decades Record (Ongoing)** : Streisand remains the only act to have achieved number one albums in six distinct decades: the 1960s, 70s, 80s, 90s, 2000s, and 2010s. Even as she nears her 82nd birthday and almost halfway through the 2020s, the possibility of her topping the album chart again is not ruled out. "Streisand remains the only act to have achieved number 1 albums in 6 decades, the 1960s, 70s, 80s, 90s, 2000s and 2010s."
**Autobiography Request by Jacqueline Kennedy-Onassis (1984)** : The idea for Streisand's autobiography, "My Name Is Barbara," which was finally released in November 2023, dates back to 1984, when she was reportedly asked to pen a memoir by former First Lady and then Doubleday book editor Jacqueline Kennedy-Onassis. "How long awaited, reportedly former first lady and then double day book editor Jacqueline Kennedy-Onazes asked Streisand to pen a memoir back in 1984."
**Disdain for Hitmaking (Memoir Claim)** : In her memoir, Streisand sheepishly claims to be obtuse when it comes to hitmaking, stating that she never chose songs for commercial appeal and didn't even remember which songs reached number one. She also confesses to still holding a grudge against The Singing Nun for blocking one of her albums from the top spot. "The songs I chose were not exactly commercial. I never went into the studio thinking I've got a record of hit."
**The Streisand Effect (Meme)** : Streisand became a political activist and even inspired a meme known as "the Streisand effect." This phenomenon describes how an attempt to conceal, remove, or censor information can have the unintended consequence of publicizing the information more widely, as happened when she tried to prevent photos of her waterfront mansion from appearing on the internet. "Go ahead, Google the Streisand effect, to find out how she tried to prevent photos of her waterfront mansion from appearing on the internet and stepped into some very online mishagos."
**Childhood Musical Theatre Influence** : Born in 1942 and raised in Brooklyn, Streisand started singing as early as 4th grade. However, in her early teens, she preferred straight plays to musicals, dreaming of being an actress rather than a singer. Her interest in musical performances only solidified after seeing movie musicals like "Kiss Me Kate" or "The King and I," having been mesmerized by Marlon Brando in "Guys and Dolls" at age 13. "She began dreaming of being on stage, but to act not to sing."
**Broadway Debut Sensation (1962)** : In 1962, Streisand's Broadway debut in "I Can Get It For You Wholesale," where she originated the supporting role of Miss Marmelstein, was a sensation. Her witty performance, which included singing while wheeling around the stage in an office chair, reportedly stopped the show every night. "Streisand's 1962 Broadway debut was a sensation."
**Creative Control at Columbia Records (1963)** : When Streisand signed with Columbia Records in 1963, she accepted a smaller advance in exchange for the unprecedented ability to choose her own material. This level of artistic control was virtually unheard of for an artist at that time, especially for a 20-year-old woman. "When she signed to Columbia Records, Streisand accepted a smaller advance in exchange for the ability to choose her own material, virtually unheard of control for an artist at that time, especially a 20-year-old woman."
**"Funny Girl" Role (1964)** : Streisand starred in the Broadway musical "Funny Girl," which opened in March 1964. The show, an adaptation of vaudeville-era actress Fanny Brice's life, seemed so tailored to Streisand's talents that its creators spent months tinkering with the book and songs to get them right for her, making her "sweat for the role." "The musical adaptation of the life of Vaudville-era actress Fanny Bryce seemed to be tailored to Barbara Streisand's talents."
**Chart Competition with Beatlemania (1964)** : In 1964, while Streisand was achieving her first Hot 100 top five single with "People" and her first number one LP with the "People" album, she was competing with the massive youth quake of Beatlemania. The Beatles commanded both the Hot 100 and the album chart for most of that summer with their song, movie, and soundtrack LP "A Hard Day's Night." "For another thing, the 22-year-old Barbara was competing with an even bigger 1964 chart, Youth Quake, Beatle Mania."
**Relentless Schedule and Pop Chart Dip (1967-1969)** : The relentless pace of Streisand's schedule, particularly her continued performances as Fanny Brice on Broadway, began to take its toll around 1967. After being freed from her stage commitments, her regular albums started missing the top 10, and none of her singles made the top 40 between 1967 and 1969. "The relentless pace of Barbara's schedule began to take its toll around 1967."
**Oscar Win for "Funny Girl" (1969)** : Streisand kicked off her Hollywood career with the 1968 movie version of "Funny Girl," which became a box office blockbuster and produced a hit soundtrack. In her debut film role, she won her first Oscar at the 1969 Academy Awards, sharing the Best Actress prize in an unprecedented tie with Katharine Hepburn. "At the 1969 Academy Awards, Barbara was given her statuette by screen legend, Ingrid Bergman, under unprecedented Oscar circumstances. The winner is a tie."
**Bob Dylan's Unfulfilled Duet Idea (1969)** : In 1969, Bob Dylan penned his top 10 hit "Lay Lady Lay" with the specific intention of singing it as a duet with Barbara Streisand. However, he never managed to get in touch with her to realize this collaboration. "And interesting footnote was that Bob Dylan penned his 1969 top 10 hit Lay Lay Lay with the intention of singing it as a duet with Streisand, but he never managed to get in touch with her."
**Turned Down Future #1 Hits (Early 1970s)** : By 1973, Streisand had yet to score a number one pop single and had turned down at least two songs that subsequently became number one hits for other singers. These included Helen Reddy's "Delta Dawn" and Maureen McGovern's "The Morning After," the latter of which also won the Oscar for Best Original Song in 1973. "She turned down at least two songs that eventually became number 1 hits for other singers, Helen Reddy's Delta Dawn. And Maureen McGovern's The Morning After."
**"The Way We Were" Cultural Permeation (Mid-1970s)** : The song "The Way We Were" so deeply permeated mid-1970s culture that it was ranked as the number one hit of 1974 by Casey Kasem on American Top 40's Year End Show. More than a decade later, the Tom Hanks movie "Big" referenced the song, with Hanks's boy trapped in a man's body singing it to his mother over the phone to prove his identity. "If there's one song most associated with Barbara Streisand, the way we were is probably it."
**Remaking "A Star Is Born" (1976)** : In 1976, Streisand took on the Hollywood classic "A Star Is Born," reimagining it as a music business fable and using it as a showcase for both her acting and singing. Co-starring Chris Kristofferson, this version conceived by Streisand aimed to give her the swagger of a contemporary rock star. The 2018 version starring Lady Gaga and helmed by Bradley Cooper is essentially a reboot of Streisand's. "Streisand's a star is born was conceived by her as a vehicle to give her the swagger of a contemporary rock star."
**Oscar Win for Songwriting (1977)** : Streisand taught herself guitar and came up with chords that became "Evergreen," for which Paul Williams added lyrics. At the 1977 Oscars, Streisand, along with Paul Williams, won the Academy Award for Best Original Song for "Evergreen," marking her second Oscar and her first for songwriting. Her friend and fellow Brooklynite Neil Diamond announced the win. "Evergreen not only gave Strison to her second Oscar as a song it finally made her an imperial chart presence."
**Duet with Donna Summer (1979)** : In 1979, Streisand collaborated with the reigning queen of disco, Donna Summer, on the single "No More Tears (Enough Is Enough)." Legend has it that during the studio recording, the two vocal powerhouses sang so intensely that Donna Summer fell off her stool at one point. "Legend has it that in the studio, the two vocal powerhouses sang so hard at one point Donna fell off her stool."
**Disco's Decline (1979)** : Streisand caught the wave of disco just as it was crashing in the pop marketplace. Her duet "No More Tears (Enough Is Enough)" hit number one several months after the "disco sucks" movement and Chicago's infamous Summer 1979 Disco Demolition Night event, making her foray into disco ironically timed. "Funnily enough, Barbara caught disco just as it was crashing in the pop marketplace."
**Upcoming Collaboration with The Bee Gees** : Streisand was about to connect with The Bee Gees, a group even more associated with disco. This collaboration was anticipated to be a "commercial salvation" for them and bring her to new pop heights. "When we come back, Babs and the Bee Gees prove a match made in heaven before Barbara goes back to Broadway."
