## albums
**Guilty (1980) - Bee Gees-Produced Blockbuster** : After meeting Barry Gibb around summer 1979, Streisand asked him to write some songs for her next album. Gibb instead offered to produce the entire album and write all the songs with his brothers, so Streisand only had to show up and sing. The result was essentially a Bee Gees album fronted by Streisand, with the hallmarks of the Bee Gees production style but steered toward lush, urban pop rather than dance music, adapted for "a singer with a Titanic voice" and an audience now allergic to falsetto and Studio 54 beats. The album had no title until late in recording, when Streisand asked for one more uptempo song, which became the title track. The cover showed Streisand hugging Barry Gibb, both in disco-era white, showcasing rather than hiding his involvement. It reached No. 1 on the album chart the same week "Woman in Love" topped the Hot 100. It became her top-selling studio album ever, quintuple platinum in the U.S. and a reported 15 million sold worldwide, and her biggest chart success not tied to a movie, Broadway show, or TV telecast. "Streisand's 1980 album would be, in essence, a Bee Gees album, fronted and personified by Barbara Streisand."
**Memories (1981) - Compilation Extending the Guilty Era** : A 1981 compilation that essentially prolonged the Guilty era. It collected a handful of Streisand's recent hits and added the new song "Comin' In and Out of Your Life," which reached No. 11 on the pop chart in early 1982. "In 1981, compilation called Memories, essentially prolonged the Guilty era."
**Yentl Soundtrack (1983) - Oscar-Winning Score** : Music from Streisand's directorial debut, the film Yentl. "The Way He Makes Me Feel" briefly cracked the Top 40 in late 1983. The music won the Oscar for Best Original Song Score, a now-defunct prize. In Best Original Song, however, both "Papa, Can You Hear Me?" and "The Way He Makes Me Feel" lost to Irene Cara's "Flashdance... What a Feeling." "The Yental music did win the Oscar for Best Original Song Score, a now defunct prize."
**Emotion (1984) - Attempt to Adapt to 80s Pop** : When Streisand returned to recording in 1984, pop had shifted past her style of balladry toward synth-driven techno-pop and new wave, and she tried to adapt. The lead single was the Jim Steinman power ballad "Left in the Dark." "When Barbara returned to recording in 1984, she tried to adapt."
**The Broadway Album (1985) - Return to Broadway Roots** : The album came almost five years after her last major hit LP. Rather than go deeper into MTV-style pop, Streisand brought Broadway to an album, although she never returned to the stage after the 1960s. Columbia fought the idea and wanted another contemporary pop album, but her contract still granted her creative control. The album covered composers from Rodgers and Hammerstein to George and Ira Gershwin, and its highlights were Stephen Sondheim songs, with Sondheim working directly with her on the lyrical arrangements. Released just before Christmas 1985, it reached No. 1 by early 1986 and went quadruple platinum, her best seller since Guilty. When it reached No. 1, it ejected the soundtrack to NBC's Miami Vice from the top spot. "Columbia, Streisand's label, fought the idea at first."
**Till I Loved You (1988) - Top 10 Platinum Album** : This 1988 album went Top 10 and platinum. Its title duet with then-boyfriend Don Johnson performed far less well. "Streisand's 1988 album also titled Till I Loved You When Top 10 And Platinum."
**Back to Broadway (1993) - Broadway Album Sequel** : The sequel to the 1985 Broadway Album debuted at No. 1 on the Billboard 200. Its success reflected the hardcore fanbase guaranteed to buy whatever she released. "The CD predictably debuted at number one on the Billboard 200 album chart."
**Higher Ground (1997) - Inspirational-Themed Album** : The album was loosely themed around inspirational music and featured a duet with her protégée Céline Dion. It debuted at No. 1 on the album chart. By the 1990s Streisand was a known quantity who could take years off between albums. "That CD also debuted atop the album chart."
**Guilty Pleasures (2005) - 25th Anniversary Reunion with Barry Gibb** : Streisand reunited with Barry Gibb on the 25th anniversary of Guilty for a sequel album of all-new material. It was a Top 5 seller on CD and produced the dance hit "Night of My Life." "Strysan, reunited with Barry Gibb for a sequel album of all new material titled Guilty Pleasures."
**Love Is the Answer (2009) - Upset Over Mariah Carey** : The album was released on the same day as Mariah Carey's highly anticipated Memoirs of an Imperfect Angel, and it took the No. 1 spot instead. Billboard's own editors admitted they were blindsided. It was her first No. 1 album of the 2000s and extended her chart-topping run into a fifth decade. "Billboard's own editors, who admitted they were blindsided by this outcome."
**Partners (2014) - All-Duets Album** : An album composed entirely of duets, many of them remakes of her earlier hits. It included "What Kind of Fool" reimagined with soul singer John Legend. It extended her No. 1 streak into a sixth decade and went gold instantly and eventually platinum, at a time when album sales were down across the board. "Partners went gold instantly and eventually platinum, remarkable at a time when album sales were down across the board."
**Encore: Movie Partners Sing Broadway (2016) - 11th No. 1 Album** : A sequel to Partners in which the duet partners were Hollywood stars moonlighting as vocalists, including Star Trek film star Chris Pine. Chart watchers expected Florida Georgia Line to take No. 1 that week, but Streisand, at age 74, did. As her 11th No. 1, it reset the record for women, a mark Taylor Swift would not surpass for another seven years. It also tied her with Bruce Springsteen, who also has 11 No. 1 albums. "At age 74, Strysan ruled the album chart once again."
**Walls (2018) - Anti-Trump Protest Album** : The album's lyrics were a political protest against then-President Donald Trump. A remix of "Don't Lie to Me" reached No. 8 on the club songs chart. "Whose lyrics were a political protest against then-president Donald Trump."

## songs
**Woman in Love (1980) - Fastest-Breaking Solo Single** : The first single from Guilty was chosen by Columbia as the album's sultriest song. It was co-written by Barry and Robin Gibb and described as a pseudo-feminist tone poem. Streisand found the lyrics inscrutable but sang it powerfully anyway. It was the fastest-breaking solo single of her career and hit No. 1 on the Hot 100 within two months, the same week Guilty topped the album chart. "It was an instant smash, the fastest breaking solo single of Barbara Streisand's career."
**Guilty (1980) - Duet with Barry Gibb** : The album's only song written by all three Gibb brothers. It was written late in the sessions when Streisand asked for one more uptempo song, and it has a yacht rock-style bounce. Barry sang it with Streisand as a duet, and it reached No. 3. "The album's only song penned by all three Gibb brothers."
**What Kind of Fool (1981) - Heartbroken Ballad Duet** : Another Barry Gibb duet from Guilty, which reached No. 10 in early 1981. It was the last wave of Streisand's imperial phase on the singles charts. In 2014 it was remade on Partners as a duet with John Legend. "It was the last wave of Streisand's Imperial phase on the single struts."
**Comin' In and Out of Your Life (1981) - Memories Compilation Single** : The new song added to the Memories compilation. It reached No. 11 on the pop chart in early 1982. "Rose to number eleven on the pop chart in early 82."
**The Way He Makes Me Feel (1983) - Yentl Single** : The song briefly cracked the Top 40 in late 1983. It was nominated for the Oscar for Best Original Song but lost to "Flashdance... What a Feeling." "Briefly cracked the top 40 in late 1983."
**Papa, Can You Hear Me? (1983) - Yentl Oscar Nominee** : A song from Yentl that was performed live by Donna Summer at the 1984 Oscar ceremony. It lost Best Original Song to Irene Cara's "Flashdance... What a Feeling." "Streisand's friend Donna Summer sang the movie's Papa Can You Hear Me Live."
**Left in the Dark (1984) - Jim Steinman Power Ballad** : A bombastic Jim Steinman power ballad that served as lead single of Emotion. It was Streisand's first video to appear on MTV. The pairing proved a less than ideal fit, and the song reached only No. 50 on the Hot 100. "Left in the dark, only reached number 50 on the Hot 100."
**Somewhere (1985) - Broadway Album First Single** : Streisand's cover of the West Side Story classic co-written by Stephen Sondheim and Leonard Bernstein. It was the first single from The Broadway Album. "Barbara covered the West Side Story classic, co-written by Sondheim and Leonard Bernstein."
**Putting It Together (1985) - Rewritten Sondheim Lyrics** : This song from Sondheim's Sunday in the Park with George was one of several famous songs for which Sondheim wrote new lyrics for Streisand. "Streisand even got Sondheim to write new lyrics for some of his most famous songs."
**Send In the Clowns (1985) - New Second Bridge** : The melancholy classic from A Little Night Music, previously a 1970s hit for Judy Collins. At Streisand's request, Sondheim wrote a new second bridge that musically filled in a plot point from the original show, and Streisand was the first person to sing it. "Sondheim wrote a new second bridge for Send In the Clowns that musically filled in a plot point from the original show."
**Till I Loved You (1988) - Duet with Don Johnson** : A duet recorded during Streisand's romance with Don Johnson and his only other Top 40 hit. It peaked at No. 25, by which time the couple had broken up. "It peaked at number 25 by which time he and Streisand had broken up."
**I Finally Found Someone (1996) - Duet with Bryan Adams** : A duet with Canadian rocker Bryan Adams from her third directorial film, The Mirror Has Two Faces. It reached No. 8 on the Hot 100 in December 1996, when she was 54. It was her first Top 10 hit in 15 years and her last Hot 100 hit. It also made the Top 20 on pop radio playlists alongside Toni Braxton, Hootie & the Blowfish, and "the Lannis Moore set" (as transcribed). "Her first top 10 hit in 15 years since the string of hits from her guilty album."
**Night of My Life (2005) - Neo-Disco Club Hit** : A neo-disco track from Guilty Pleasures that reached No. 2 on Billboard's Club Play chart. "Night of My Life, which reached a stunning number two on Billboard's club play chart."
**Don't Lie to Me (2018) - Walls Dance Remix** : A remix of this song from Walls reached No. 8 on the club songs chart. "A remix of Don't Lie to Me reached number eight on the club song's chart."

## curiosities
**Yentl (1983) - Directorial Debut and Golden Globe First** : With the clout to get any project greenlit, Streisand took a multi-year break from the pop charts to direct and star in her dream project. Yentl tells the story of an early 20th-century Jewish woman in Poland who disguises herself as a man to study the Talmud, and it was both a historical drama and a musical. Streisand won the Golden Globe for Best Director, the first woman to do so. She was not nominated for Best Director at the Academy Awards, a controversial snub compared to Greta Gerwig's omission for Barbie. "The first woman to win the Globe's Best Director Prize."
**Never Returned to the Stage** : Despite The Broadway Album, Streisand never performed on a Broadway stage again after the 1960s. "After the 60s, Streisand never trod the boards again."
**Romance with Don Johnson (1987-1988)** : The Broadway Album had knocked the Miami Vice soundtrack from No. 1. In late 1987 Streisand began dating Don Johnson, the show's star. The whirlwind romance lasted just under a year and produced their duet "Till I Loved You." "In late 1987, Don Johnson began dating Barbara Streisand."
**Forerunner of the Diva Era** : The vocal divas of the late 1980s and early 1990s, including Whitney Houston, Mariah Carey, and especially Céline Dion, were in a way all following Streisand, the rock era's premier athletic vocalist. By the 1990s Streisand focused on directing rather than competing with them. "Barbara Streisand, who was the rock era's premiere athletic vocalist."
**The Prince of Tides (1991)** : Streisand directed the film and co-starred with Nick Nolte. It was not a musical, but she recorded a soundtrack song that became a minor adult contemporary radio hit. The film received seven Oscar nominations, including Best Picture, but Streisand again was not nominated for Best Director. "Barbara helmed 1991's The Prince of Todd, co-starring herself with Nick Nulltie."
**SNL's Linda Richman and Surprise Cameo (1992)** : Mike Myers played performatively Jewish talk show host Linda Richman, who treated Streisand as an object of worship. In a 1992 sketch, Madonna played Richman's friend Liz, and Streisand surprised both Myers and Madonna with a cameo. The character accurately portrayed a significant chunk of her fanbase, a hardcore that gave her music career a solid foundation. "Barbara herself surprised both Myers and Madonna by making a cameo."
**Lampooned in Mrs. Doubtfire (1993)** : Robin Williams briefly imitated Streisand in the film. No one in the movie said whom he was imitating. "It was meant to be obvious and it was."
**South Park Villain (1998)** : Trey Parker and Matt Stone depicted Streisand as an outright villain in a 1998 episode. She grows into a Godzilla-sized monster and wrecks the town until she is stopped by Robert Smith of The Cure. "Streisand enlarges into a Godzilla-sized villain and wrecks the town, only to be stopped by Robert Smith of the cure."
**Most No. 1 Albums After 50** : By the 1990s she was a known quantity who could take years off between albums. She scored nearly half of her record number of No. 1 albums after age 50. "She would score nearly half of her record roster of number one albums after age 50."
**Hot Country Songs Chart Appearance (1999)** : A late-1999 duet with country star Vince Gill put Streisand on the Hot Country Songs chart. "A duet with country star Vince Gill put Barbara on the hot country songs chart."
**No. 1 Albums Across Six Decades** : Love Is the Answer (2009) extended her chart-topping run into a fifth decade. Partners (2014) extended it into a sixth decade. "Strysan extended that streak into a sixth decade, with her 2014 chart topper Partners."
**Kennedy Center Honors (2008)** : At her Kennedy Center Honors induction, Queen Latifah, Idina Menzel, and Beyoncé performed in her honor. "Several musicians performed in Barbara's honor, including Queen Latifa, Adina Menzel, and Beyoncé."
**Retreat from Live Performance and Politics** : Streisand has not performed live in several years. She continues to speak out about politics and about where she will go to live if Trump is re-elected. "She continues to speak out about politics, and where she will go to live if Trump is reelected president."
