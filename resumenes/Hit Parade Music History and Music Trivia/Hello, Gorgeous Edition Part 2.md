## albums
**Guilty (1980) - Bee Gees Collaboration** : This 1980 album, produced and written entirely by Barry Gibb and his brothers, was essentially a Bee Gees album fronted by Barbra Streisand, heavily featuring their production style. It was characterized as lush, urban pop, not traditional dance music, with Barry Gibb adapting his style for Streisand's powerful voice and an audience averse to falsetto and Studio 54 beats. "Streisand's 1980 album would be, in essence, a Bee Gees album, fronted and personified by Barbara Streisand."
**Guilty (1980) - Album Title Origin** : The album's title, *Guilty*, was determined near the end of recording after Streisand requested one more uptempo track. Barry Gibb collaborated with Robin and Morris Gibb to write the title track, which became the only song on the album penned by all three brothers, featuring a yacht rock style bounce and performed as a duet with Barry Gibb. "The song and the album would be called Guilty."
**Guilty (1980) - Chart Performance** : This album reached number one on the album chart in the same week that its lead single, "Woman in Love," topped the Hot 100. "the Guilty album reached number one on the album chart."
**Guilty (1980) - Independent Success** : The *Guilty* album represented an exceptional chart success for Barbra Streisand, being her biggest without the typical accompanying promotion from a movie, Broadway show, or TV telecast. Its popularity was driven purely by the strength of its songs and Streisand's established "Imperial Pop profile." "Her biggest chart success not accompanied by a movie, Broadway show, or TV telecast."
**Guilty (1980) - Sales and Hit Generation** : This album became Streisand's top-selling studio album of all time, achieving quintuple platinum sales in the U.S. and an estimated 15 million copies sold worldwide. It was also notable for generating the most hit singles of any of her albums. "Quinn Tupple Platinum sales in the U.S. alone, and a reported 15 million sold worldwide, it also generated the most hits."
**Memories (1981) - Compilation Album** : This 1981 compilation album served to extend the *Guilty* era, gathering several of Streisand's recent hit songs. It also included the new track "Coming In and Out of Your Life," which climbed to number eleven on the pop chart in early 1982. "essentially prolonged the Guilty era, collecting a handful of Streisand's recent hits and adding a new song, coming in and out of your life, that rose to number eleven on the pop chart in early 82."
**Yentl Soundtrack (1984) - Oscar Win** : The music from the *Yentl* soundtrack won the Academy Award for Best Original Song Score, a category that is now defunct. However, the individual songs "Papa Can You Hear Me" and "The Way He Makes Me Feel" did not win Best Original Song, losing to "Flashdance... What a Feeling." "the Yental music did win the Oscar for Best Original Song Score, a now defunct prize."
**Emotion (1984)** : This 1984 album featured "Left in the Dark" as its lead single, which marked Streisand's attempt at a "bombastic Steinman power ballad" and was also her first music video to air on MTV. "Serving as the lead single of Streisand's 1984 album Emotion"
**The Broadway Album (1985) - Concept and Origin** : After a nearly five-year drought of major hit LPs and a failed attempt at MTV-style pop, Streisand made a "counterintuitive move" by returning to her Broadway roots, not on stage, but by dedicating her next album to showtunes. This album, released in 1985, was appropriately titled *The Broadway Album*. "She called it, duh, the Broadway album."
**The Broadway Album (1985) - Creative Control** : Streisand's label, Columbia, initially resisted the concept of *The Broadway Album*, preferring a contemporary pop album. However, Streisand's contract ensured her creative control, allowing her to proceed with the Broadway-themed project. "Columbia, Streisand's label, fought the idea at first."
**The Broadway Album (1985) - Song Selection and Collaboration** : This album featured showtunes by various composers, including Rogers and Hammerstein and George and Ira Gershwin. A significant highlight was the inclusion of songs by Stephen Sondheim, who directly collaborated with Streisand on the lyrical arrangements. "Streisand recorded showtunes by a range of composers, from Rogers and Hammerstein to George and Ira Gershwin."
**The Broadway Album (1985) - Chart Success** : Released just before Christmas 1985, *The Broadway Album* ascended to number one on the album chart by early 1986 and eventually achieved quadruple platinum status. It became Streisand's best-selling album since *Guilty*, despite being musically very different from that previous blockbuster. "Released just before Christmas 1985, the Broadway album went to number one on the album chart by early 86 and ultimately went quadruple platinum."
**Till I Loved You (1988)** : This 1988 album, also titled *Till I Loved You*, reached the top 10 and achieved platinum certification. "Streisand's 1988 album also titled Till I Loved You When Top 10 And Platinum."
**Back to Broadway (1993)** : This 1993 album, a sequel to her 1985 *Broadway Album*, predictably debuted at number one on the Billboard 200 album chart, demonstrating the loyalty of her fanbase. "when Streisand issued a sequel to her 1985 Broadway album in 1993, simply titled Back to Broadway? The CD predictably debuted at number one on the Billboard 200 album chart."
**Higher Ground (1997)** : Released four years after *Back to Broadway*, this album was loosely themed around inspirational music and featured a duet with her "protege" Selene Dion. It also debuted at number one on the album chart. "called Higher Ground, featuring a duet with her protege Selene Dion. That CD also debuted atop the album chart."
**Guilty Pleasures (2005) - Sequel Album** : Released in 2005 to mark the 25th anniversary of *Guilty*, this sequel album reunited Barbra Streisand with Barry Gibb for all-new material. It was a top five seller on CD and produced the neo-disco dance hit "Night of My Life," which impressively reached number two on Billboard's Club Play chart. "Strysan, reunited with Barry Gibb for a sequel album of all new material titled Guilty Pleasures."
**Love Is The Answer (2009) - Unexpected Number One** : In late 2009, Barbra Streisand's album *Love Is The Answer* unexpectedly debuted at number one on the Billboard 200 album chart, surpassing the highly anticipated release from pop titan Mariah Carey (*Memoirs of an Imperfect Angel*), which came out on the same day. This achievement extended Streisand's chart-topping prowess into a fifth decade. "Barbara Strysan's love is the answer to take the number one spot instead."
**Partners (2014) - Duets Album** : This 2014 chart-topping album extended Barbra Streisand's streak of number one albums into a sixth decade. Building on her reputation as the "queen of hit duets," the album featured an entire collection of duets, including reimagined versions of her earlier hits, such as "What Kind of Fool" with John Legend. "Strysan extended that streak into a sixth decade, with her 2014 chart topper Partners."
**Partners (2014) - Sales Success** : The album *Partners* quickly achieved gold status and later went platinum, which was considered remarkable given the overall decline in album sales across the industry at that time. "Partners went gold instantly and eventually platinum, remarkable at a time when album sales were down across the board."
**Encore, Movie Partners Sing Broadway (2016) - Duets with Hollywood Stars** : In 2016, Barbra Streisand released *Encore, Movie Partners Sing Broadway*, a sequel album where she performed duets with Hollywood stars moonlighting as vocalists, including Chris Pine. "she released a sequel called Encore, Movie Partners Sing Broadway. This time, her duet partners were Hollywood stars, moonlighting as vocalists."
**Encore, Movie Partners Sing Broadway (2016) - Chart Upset** : The album *Encore, Movie Partners Sing Broadway* defied Billboard chart watchers' expectations by reaching number one upon its release, surpassing the anticipated top spot for Florida Georgia Line's latest album. At 74 years old, Streisand commanded the album chart once more. "At age 74, Strysan ruled the album chart once again."
**Walsh (2018) - Political Protest Album** : Her 2018 LP, *Walsh* (commonly spelled *Walls*), featured lyrics that served as a political protest against the then-president Donald Trump. Although she hadn't reached number one since 2016, a remix of its track "Don't Lie to Me" became another dance hit, peaking at number eight on the Club Songs chart. "Her 2018 LP, Walsh, whose lyrics were a political protest against then-president Donald Trump"

## songs
**Woman in Love (1980)** : Chosen by Columbia as the first single from *Guilty*, this sultry, pseudo-feminist tone poem was co-written by Barry and Robin Gibb. Barbra Streisand found its lyrics inscrutable but delivered a powerful vocal performance. "It was called Woman in Love."
**Woman in Love (1980) - Chart Performance** : This song was an instant smash, becoming the fastest-breaking solo single of Barbra Streisand's career. It topped the Hot 100 within two months of its release, concurrently with the *Guilty* album reaching number one on the album chart. "Within two months, Woman in Love was on top of the Hot 100, the very same week the Guilty album reached number one on the album chart."
**Guilty (1980)** : This duet with Barry Gibb reached number three on the charts, following the success of "Woman in Love." "Barbara and Barry's Guilty duet reached number three."
**What Kind of Fool (1981)** : Released in early 1981, this heartbroken ballad was another duet with Barry Gibb and reached number ten on the charts. It marked the final wave of Streisand's "Imperial phase" on the singles charts. "Another Barry Gibb duet reached number ten."
**Coming In and Out of Your Life (1982)** : This new song, included on the 1981 compilation album *Memories*, reached number eleven on the pop chart in early 1982. "coming in and out of your life, that rose to number eleven on the pop chart in early 82."

**Left In The Dark (1984)** : This "bombastic Steinman power ballad" was the lead single from Barbra Streisand's 1984 album *Emotion*. It was also Streisand's first music video to appear on MTV but only reached number 50 on the Hot 100, indicating that the collaboration with Jim Steinman was not an ideal fit. "left in the dark, only reached number 50 on the Hot 100."
**Somewhere (1985)** : This cover of the *West Side Story* classic, co-written by Stephen Sondheim and Leonard Bernstein, was chosen as the first single from *The Broadway Album*. "Barbara covered the West Side Story classic, co-written by Sondheim and Leonard Bernstein somewhere."
**Putting It Together (1985)** : For *The Broadway Album*, Stephen Sondheim wrote new lyrics for this song, originally from his show *Sunday in the Park with George*, specifically for Streisand. "Sondheim to write new lyrics for some of his most famous songs, including putting it together from his show Sunday in the Park with George."
**Send In the Clowns (1985)** : This melancholy classic from *A Little Night Music*, previously a 70s hit for Judy Collins, was recorded by Streisand for *The Broadway Album*, with Stephen Sondheim writing a new second bridge at her request. "the melancholy classic, Send In the Clowns, which had already been a 70s hit for Judy Collins."
**Send In the Clowns (1985) - New Verse** : At Streisand's specific request, Stephen Sondheim composed a new second bridge for this song, designed to musically articulate a plot point from the original show *A Little Night Music*. Streisand was the inaugural vocalist for this new verse. "Barbara was the first person to sing this new verse."
**Till I Loved You (1988)** : This duet with Don Johnson emerged from Streisand's whirlwind romance with the actor in late 1987. It became Johnson's only other top 40 hit and the title track of Streisand's 1988 album. "A duet with Streisand called Till I Loved You."
**Till I Loved You (1988) - Chart Performance** : The duet with Don Johnson peaked at number 25 on the charts, by which time their romantic relationship had ended. "It peaked at number 25 by which time he and Streisand had broken up."
**I Finally Found Someone (1996)** : This song, a duet with Canadian rocker Brian Adams, was recorded for the soundtrack of Streisand's 1996 film *The Mirror Has Two Faces*. It became a notable hit pop single for her in the mid-90s. "Barbara recorded a duet with Canadian rocker and frequent movie balader Brian Adams, titled I Finally Found Someone."
**I Finally Found Someone (1996) - Chart Performance** : Released in December 1996, this duet with Brian Adams reached number 8 on the Hot 100. At age 54, this marked Streisand's first top 10 hit in 15 years, breaking a drought since her hits from the *Guilty* album. "In December 1996, I finally found someone reach number 8 on the Hot 100, her first top 10 hit in 15 years"
**I Finally Found Someone (1996) - Pop Radio Success** : This duet not only reached number 8 on the Hot 100 but also made it into the top 20 on pop radio playlists, competing with popular artists like Tony Braxton, Hoodie and the Blowfish, and the Lannis Moore set. "Streisand's duet even made the top 20 on pop radio playlists alongside hits by Tony Braxton, Hoodie and the Blowfish, and the Lannis Moore set."
**I Finally Found Someone (1996) - Last Hot 100 Hit** : This duet with Brian Adams was Barbra Streisand's last single to appear on the Hot 100 chart. "While I finally found someone was Barbara Strysan's last hot 100 hit"
**Duet with Vince Gill (1999)** : A duet with country star Vince Gill in late 1999 landed Barbra Streisand a spot on the Hot Country Songs chart. "a duet with country star Vince Gill put Barbara on the hot country songs chart."
**Night of My Life (2005)** : This "neo disco banger" from the *Guilty Pleasures* album reached number two on Billboard's Club Play chart, demonstrating Streisand's continued relevance in dance music. "the neo disco banger, Night of My Life, which reached a stunning number two on Billboard's club play chart."
**What Kind of Fool (2014) - Reimagined Duet** : Originally a 1981 hit with Barry Gibb, this song was reimagined as a duet with soul singer John Legend for Barbra Streisand's 2014 *Partners* album. "her 1981 hit What Kind of Fool, originally appearing with Barry Gibb, was reimagined for the Partners album as a duet with Soul Singer John Legend."
**Don't Lie to Me (2018) - Remix Dance Hit** : A remix of this track from her 2018 album *Walsh* (commonly spelled *Walls*) became a dance hit, reaching number eight on the Club Songs chart. The song's lyrics were part of a political protest against Donald Trump. "when a remix of Don't Lie to Me reached number eight on the club song's chart."

## curiosities
**Early Career Versatility** : Barbra Streisand emerged as a Broadway star, movie star, and pop star almost simultaneously, showcasing a versatile talent early in her career. She initially struggled to adapt to contemporary music but found a successful approach in the mid-1970s that led to consistent hits. "How she emerged as a Broadway star, movie star, and yes, pop star, virtually simultaneously."
**Collaboration with Barry Gibb** : Barbra Streisand met Barry Gibb around summer 1979 and, impressed by his hit-making abilities, asked him to write songs for her upcoming album. Gibb responded by offering to produce the entire album and write all its songs with his brothers, requiring only Streisand's vocals. "Barry Gibb met Barbara Streisand. Impressed by Barry's hit-making skill, Barbara asked him if he would write some songs for her next album."
**Voice Description** : Barbra Streisand is described as having a "Titanic voice." "a singer with a Titanic voice"
**Guilty Album Cover** : For the cover of her *Guilty* album, Barbra Streisand photographed herself embracing Barry Gibb, with both dressed in disco-era white. This bold promotional choice openly showcased Gibb's involvement, despite Streisand being the primary artist listed. "Streisand photographed herself in a hug with Barry Gibb, both of them clad in disco-era white."
**Imperial Pop Profile** : Barbra Streisand maintained an "Imperial Pop profile" which contributed to the success of her *Guilty* album. "Barbara's own Imperial Pop profile."
**Directorial Debut - Yentl** : Leveraging her significant influence, Barbra Streisand took a multi-year hiatus from the pop charts to direct and star in her passion project, the 1983 film *Yentl*. The movie is a historical drama and musical about an early 20th-century Jewish woman in Poland who disguises herself as a man to study the Talmud, showcasing Streisand's diverse talents. "Streisand took a multi-year break from the pop charts, to direct and star in her dream project, The Movie Yental."
**Golden Globe for Directing 'Yentl'** : For her directorial debut *Yentl*, Barbra Streisand won a Golden Globe, becoming the first woman to receive the award for Best Director. Despite this recognition, she was controversially snubbed for an Academy Award nomination for Best Director, although the film's music did receive Oscar nominations. "For this film, her directorial debut Streisand won a Golden Globe, the first woman to win the Globe's Best Director Prize."
**Collaboration with Jim Steinman** : Upon her return to recording in 1984, Barbra Streisand attempted to adapt to contemporary sounds by collaborating with songwriter and producer Jim Steinman. Steinman was at the peak of his career, fresh off his chart-topping success with Bonnie Tyler's "Total Eclipse of the Heart." "She worked with iconoclastic songwriter producer Jim Steinman."
**First MTV Video** : Barbra Streisand's music video for "Left In The Dark" from her 1984 album *Emotion* was her first to be featured on MTV. "it was also her first video to appear on MTV"
**Never Returned to Broadway Stage** : After the 1960s, Barbra Streisand never performed on a Broadway stage again, instead choosing to bring Broadway material to her recordings. "after the 60s, Streisand never trod the boards again."
**Romance with Don Johnson** : In late 1987, Barbra Streisand began a whirlwind romance with actor Don Johnson, which lasted for just under a year. This relationship led to a duet, "Till I Loved You," which became Johnson's only other top 40 hit. "in late 1987, Don Johnson began dating Barbara Streisand."
**Willingness to Share Spotlight** : Barbra Streisand has shown a willingness to share the spotlight with other artists, as evidenced by her various duets and collaborations. "Never let it be said that Babs is unwilling to share the spotlight."
**Influence on "Titanic Divas"** : Barbra Streisand is identified as the "rock era's premiere athletic vocalist," influencing a generation of "Titanic Divas" in the late 1980s and early 1990s, such as Whitney Houston, Mariah Carey, and Selene D'Arm. "Barbara Streisand, who was the rock era's premiere athletic vocalist."
**Focus on Directorial Career in 1990s** : In the 1990s, Barbra Streisand prioritized her directorial career over competing with the new generation of "Titanic Divas." "by the 90s, Streisand was more focused on her directorial career, not competing with these Divas."
**Director of 'The Prince of Todd' (1991)** : Barbra Streisand directed and co-starred in the 1991 film *The Prince of Todd* (commonly spelled *The Prince of Tides*) with Nick Nulltie. Although not a musical, she recorded a song for its soundtrack that achieved minor success on adult contemporary radio. "Barbara helmed 1991's The Prince of Todd, co-starring herself with Nick Nulltie."
**Pop Culture Icon in 1990s** : In the 1990s, Streisand's status evolved beyond a mere celebrity to that of a significant pop culture icon, influencing various media. She was affectionately parodied by Mike Myers on *Saturday Night Live* as Linda Richmond, a talk show host who worshipped her. "the 90s was also when Streisand became more of a pop culture icon than an ordinary celebrity, for better and for worse."
**'The Prince of Tides' Oscar Snub (1992)** : *The Prince of Todd* (commonly spelled *The Prince of Tides*) received seven Oscar nominations, including Best Picture, but Barbra Streisand was controversially not nominated for Best Director, mirroring a similar snub for *Yentl*. "Prince of Todd's, seven Oscar nominations total, including Best Picture. But Barbara did not get a nomination for Best Director."
**Saturday Night Live Cameo (1992)** : Barbra Streisand made a surprise cameo appearance in a 1992 *Saturday Night Live* sketch featuring Mike Myers' Linda Richmond character and Madonna, who played Linda's friend Liz. "Barbara herself surprised both Myers and Madonna by making a cameo."
**Lampooned in 'Mrs. Doubtfire' (1993)** : In 1993, Robin Williams briefly and obviously lampooned Barbra Streisand in the hit movie *Mrs. Doubtfire*, famously quoting a line from "Don't Rain on My Parade." "Robin Williams briefly lampooned Streisand in the hit movie Mrs. Doubtfire."
**Depiction as Villain in South Park (1998)** : In a 1998 episode of the animated series *South Park*, creators Trey Parker and Matt Stone portrayed Barbra Streisand as an "outright villain" who transforms into a Godzilla-sized monster and destroys a town, ultimately being stopped by Robert Smith of The Cure. "South Park creators, tray parker and Matt Stone depicted Streisand as an honor. And outright villain in the 1998 episode of their animated series."
**Massive Cultural Figure and Loyal Fanbase** : Despite negative portrayals like in *South Park*, widespread pop culture references confirmed Barbra Streisand's status as a "massive cultural figure." Her music career benefited from an extremely loyal fanbase, accurately reflected by Mike Myers' Linda Richmond character, ensuring consistent sales for her releases. "these pop culture references to Streisand indicated what a massive cultural figure she had become."
**Established Chart Success in 1990s and Beyond** : By the 1990s, Streisand had become a "known quantity" on the charts, contrasting with the 1960s and 70s when each project had to prove itself. This allowed her to take multi-year breaks between albums, confident that her new releases would be well-received. Notably, she achieved nearly half of her record number one albums after turning 50. "by the 90s, she was a known quantity. She could take years off between albums and come back fairly secure that her latest output would be welcomed."
**Number One Albums After Age 50** : Barbra Streisand scored nearly half of her impressive total of number one albums after she turned 50, showcasing enduring chart prowess later in her career. "She would score nearly half of her record roster of number one albums after age 50."
**Top 10 Hit at Age 54** : At 54 years old, Barbra Streisand scored a significant chart achievement with "I Finally Found Someone," reaching number 8 on the Hot 100 in December 1996. This was her first top 10 hit in 15 years, dating back to the singles from her *Guilty* album. "at age 54, Streisand was scaling the charts again."
**Continued Charting After Last Hot 100 Hit** : Even after "I Finally Found Someone" marked her final Hot 100 hit, Barbra Streisand continued to record new music and successfully appear on various other Billboard charts, indicating she did not "settle quietly into the elder stateswoman phase of her career." "she did not settle quietly into the elder state's woman phase of her career."
**First Number One Album of the 2000s** : *Love Is The Answer* (2009) became Barbra Streisand's first number one album of the "Aughts," successfully extending her chart-topping abilities into a fifth consecutive decade. "This gave Strysan her first number one album of the Aughts, extending her chart topping prowess into a fifth decade."
**"Queen of Hit Duets"** : Barbra Streisand has a long history and reputation as the "queen of hit duets," which culminated in her 2014 album *Partners*, composed entirely of duets. "After her long history as the queen of hit duets"
**Chart Rule at Age 74** : At the age of 74, Barbra Streisand remarkably topped the album chart again with *Encore, Movie Partners Sing Broadway*, surprising industry experts and demonstrating her enduring appeal and the power of her fanbase, referred to as "the Barbara Hive." "At age 74, Strysan ruled the album chart once again."
**Record for Number One Albums for Women (2016)** : With *Encore, Movie Partners Sing Broadway* reaching number one, Barbra Streisand achieved her 11th number one album, resetting the record for women. This benchmark would not be surpassed by Taylor Swift for another seven years. "Strysan not only reset the record for women, the benchmark that Taylor Swift wouldn't surpass for another seven years."
**Tied with Bruce Springsteen for Number One Albums (2016)** : By securing her 11th number one album, Barbra Streisand tied fellow "elder singing legend" Bruce Springsteen, who also holds 11 number one albums. "She also tied her fellow elder singing legend Bruce Springsteen, who by the way also has 11 number one albums."
**Continued Recording and Charting Post-2016** : Since her last number one album in 2016, Streisand has persisted in recording and charting new material, including her politically charged 2018 LP *Walsh*. "Though she has not returned to number one since 2016, Strysan has continued to record and chart."
**Political Protest Album (2018)** : Barbra Streisand's 2018 album *Walsh* (commonly spelled *Walls*) featured lyrics that constituted a political protest against then-President Donald Trump. "whose lyrics were a political protest against then-president Donald Trump"
**Kennedy Center Honoree (2008)** : Barbra Streisand received the prestigious Kennedy Center Honors in 2008. During her induction, several notable musicians, including Queen Latifa, Adina Menzel, and Beyoncé, performed in her honor, highlighting her lasting influence on generations of vocalists. "Among the most prestigious of her awards was the Kennedy Center Honors."
**Hiatus from Live Performance & Political Activism** : Barbra Streisand has not performed live for several years but remains publicly active, frequently speaking out about politics. "Strysan, herself, has not performed live in several years. She continues to speak out about politics"
