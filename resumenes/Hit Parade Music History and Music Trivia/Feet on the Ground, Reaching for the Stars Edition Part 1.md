# artist - Kim Carnes

**Bette Davis Eyes (1981) - Return to No. 1 and Billboard's Top Song of 1981** : Kim Carnes' summer smash spent five weeks at No. 1 on the Hot 100 before Stars on 45, a dance medley of old pop songs, knocked it off for one week. # genre - Album-Oriented Rock

## curiosities
**AOR's Split-Personality Format** : AOR radio, such as Western Connecticut's I-95 in 1987, had its own set of hits that weren't intended for pop radio. Its foundation was the 60s and 70s rock canon: Led Zeppelin, the Steve Miller Band, Cream and Eric Clapton, Aerosmith, Boston and Heart. Next to these it played a steady diet of current rock hits from Richard Marx, Bob Seger, Tom Petty and the Grateful Dead. "AOR was a split personality radio format."
**Two for Tuesdays** : I-95's Tuesday feature played two songs back to back by the same classic rock band. "on I-95s two for Tuesdays when they would play a pair of songs back to back by the same classic rock band."
**Rock Tracks Chart Bands** : Billboard's Rock Tracks chart contained AOR hits by bands that never or only occasionally topped the pop charts, such as ZZ Top, Kiss, Mötley Crüe and Rush. "Bands that never or only occasionally made the top of the pop charts, like ZZ Top, Kiss, Motley Crew and Rush."

# curiosity

**Solid Gold - TV Countdown Show (1980–1988)** : A syndicated musical variety show that began as a one-off called Solid Gold '79, a countdown of the prior year's top songs. In New York it premiered on WPIX Channel 11 on March 1, 1980. Later in 1980 it became a weekly show, with the countdown as the centerpiece of each episode alongside the leotard-clad Solid Gold Dancers. It ran for most of the 1980s, through 1988. "Solid Gold was on TV for most of the 1980s through 1988."
**Solid Gold's Charts Came from Radio & Records, Not Billboard** : Solid Gold's countdown was based on the charts in Radio & Records magazine, a Billboard competitor focused on the radio industry, and the producers would fudge chart positions based on who was available to appear. "Solid Gold's producers would fudge chart positions based on who was available."
**Your Hit Parade - Radio and TV Countdown** : Your Hit Parade aired on TV in the 1950s and started as a radio show as far back as the 1930s, ranking songs by its own survey. "Your Hit Parade! The top tunes all over the melody, as determined by your Hit Parade survey."
**American Bandstand and Soul Train Countdowns** : These legendary music and dance TV shows also had countdowns, but unlike Solid Gold they didn't make the countdown the focus. "But they didn't make the countdown the focus of the show, the way Solid Gold did."
**AT40 Year-End Rankings Differed from Billboard's** : American Top 40's year-end countdowns used a different 12-month period than Billboard's official year-end rankings, so the positions differed. For 1983, both lists still agreed on The Police's Every Breath You Take as No. 1. "AT-40 used a different 12-month period to make its year-end countdown."
**The Hot 100 Beyond the Top 40** : Billboard's main pop chart, the Hot 100, contains hits that may never reach the Top 40 counted down on the radio. "I not only discovered there were more than 40 hits on the big pop chart. It was called The Hot 100."
**The Billboard Book of Top 40 Hits** : A reference volume used to check whether classic rock songs that weren't current hits had ever charted. "check my reference volume, the Billboard book of Top 40 hits, to see if they'd ever charted."
**Z100 and Gen X Radio in 1995** : By 1995 almost every radio market, from New York's Z100 to small-town stations, played Gen X alt rock, most of it recorded by Gen Xers. "It was all stuff catering to Gen Xers, most of it recorded by Gen Xers."
