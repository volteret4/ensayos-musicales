## concerts

## albums
**Love at First Sight (1977) - Urban Disco Attempt** : Although Warwick was mostly unmoved by disco, critic Danyel Smith describes her as pragmatic, and she tried a form of urban disco in the late 70s on this LP. Nothing from the album was a hit. The track "Early Morning Strangers" reunited her with lyricist Hal David, who had written so many of her 60s hits with Burt Bacharach. The song's other co-writer, Barry Manilow, turned out to be more pivotal for her disco-era career. "Nothing from this album was a hit."
**Dionne (1979) - First Platinum Album, Produced by Barry Manilow** : Barry Manilow, a singing superstar who was also a producer and arranger, produced this simply titled album. It became Warwick's first ever platinum album. Manilow found a disco-era sound that suited her, and the album produced the hits "Déjà Vu" and "I'll Never Love This Way Again." He made her current by keeping her near disco without making her a disco artist. "Manolo had made Warwick current by making her disco adjacent, not disco."

## songs
**Then Came You (1974) - Proto-Disco Thom Bell Smash** : Danyel Smith's book *Shine Bright* points out that Warwick was slightly ahead of the curve with this proto-disco smash produced by Thom Bell. Smith says the record has a "miraculous groove." It anticipated a wave of sophisticated disco-era hits by acts such as Marilyn McCoo and Billy Davis Jr. and Peaches & Herb. "Dion Warwick was, in one sense, slightly ahead of the curve."
**Early Morning Strangers (1977)** : This track from *Love at First Sight* was a reunion of sorts with Hal David, her 60s lyricist alongside Burt Bacharach. Its other co-writer was Barry Manilow, who would go on to produce her next album. "Early morning strangers was a reunion of sorts for Dion with Hal David."
**Déjà Vu (1979)** : This hit from the Manilow-produced *Dionne* album leaned closer to yacht rock than to disco. It peaked at No. 15 Pop and No. 25 R&B, and it reached No. 1 on Billboard's Adult Contemporary chart. "The hit Deja Vu was something closer to Yatra."
**I'll Never Love This Way Again (1979)** : This ballad from *Dionne* did even better than "Déjà Vu," reaching No. 5 Pop and No. 18 R&B. It sounded contemporary to the period but was not danceable. It was her last pop top 10 hit until "Heartbreaker" in 1983. "It sounded contemporary to the period, but not danceable."
**Heartbreaker (1983) - Barry Gibb Collaboration** : On this early-1983 hit, synthesizers were more prominent than on any of Warwick's earlier hits. The trend she was really joining, however, was working with the Bee Gees. The Gibb brothers were then writing and producing successful post-disco pop for veteran stars such as Barbra Streisand, and also wrote "Islands in the Stream" for Kenny Rogers and Dolly Parton. All three Gibb brothers wrote "Heartbreaker" and sang prominent backing vocals on it. The song returned Warwick to the pop top 10 for the first time since 1979. "For Dion Warwick, teaming with Barry Gibb was just good business."
**That's What Friends Are For (1985) - AIDS Benefit No. 1** : The song was written by Carole Bayer Sager and her then-husband Burt Bacharach. Rod Stewart first recorded it for the 1982 Henry Winkler–Michael Keaton comedy *Night Shift*, but his version was not a hit, which greatly disappointed the writers. Around 1985, Sager and Bacharach were invited to contribute to Warwick's next album, reuniting Bacharach and Warwick for the first time since 1971; their old grudge proved to be water under the bridge. Warwick loved the song but felt it needed backup, so she brought in Stevie Wonder, who opened the record with his signature harmonica. Elizabeth Taylor, a friend of Sager's and one of the earliest AIDS research advocates, visited the studio the day Wonder recorded, and Sager proposed making the single a benefit for Taylor's charity amfAR. Warwick then added Gladys Knight, and Elton John, who had recently played with Wonder, was brought in to give the chorus a final punch. Credited to "Dionne and Friends" and released just before the 1985 holidays, it reached No. 1 in January 1986 and stayed there four weeks. It was the biggest hit of her career and Billboard's No. 1 song of 1986, and it reportedly raised more than $3 million for AIDS research. "Together, these four singers were a mini USA for Africa, a super friends of pop."
**We Are the World (1985) - USA for Africa** : Warwick took part in the original USA for Africa recording and contributed a brief, warm solo. That same warmth carried over to "That's What Friends Are For." "The same warmth that Dion Warwick had brought to her brief solo on We Are The World was all over, that's what friends are for."
**You Really Started Something (2019)** : This club record reached No. 16 and opened her activity heading into the 2020s. "A number sixteen club record called You Really Started Something."

## curiosities
**Duet With Stevie Wonder on The Woman in Red Soundtrack** : In 1984, the year before "That's What Friends Are For," Warwick duetted with Stevie Wonder on his soundtrack album for *The Woman in Red*. That connection led her to bring Wonder onto the AIDS benefit single. "She had duetted with Wonder the prior year on his woman in red soundtrack album."
**Live Aid 1985 Appearance** : Warwick performed at Live Aid, though she was far less prominent that day than Patti LaBelle. "Dion Warwick was also at Live Aid, though she was far less prominent that day than Patti LaBelle."
**Queen of Twitter** : In the 2020s, Warwick became famous on Twitter for blunt, unfiltered posts about popular culture. Ego Nwodim parodied this persona affectionately on *Saturday Night Live*, and Warwick herself then appeared on SNL. "Dion has become the queen of Twitter."
**Mother's Day Livestream (May 2021)** : Now in her 80s, Warwick still sings. In May 2021 she livestreamed a Mother's Day concert of songs she had made famous nearly 60 years earlier. "She live streamed a concert just last May in 2021 for Mother's Day."
**Indifference to the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame** : In a mid-March interview on *Watch What Happens Live*, Warwick said she doesn't "give a damn" whether she is inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame. "She doesn't quote Give a Down. If she is inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame."

## albums
**First Take (1972)** : This album reached No. 1 on the pop album chart. No other R&B queen in this group did that again until Patti LaBelle's *Winner in You* in 1986. "The first of our R&B queens to do this since Roberta Flak's first take in 1972."
**Blue Lights in the Basement (1977) - Self-Produced Comeback** : Flack moved cautiously through the disco era and took two full years to return with this self-produced album. Tracks such as "Why Don't You Move In With Me" were perky and almost danceable. Like Warwick, though, she succeeded mainly with disco instrumentation on slow or mid-tempo songs rather than outright dance tracks. "Roberta Flack moved even more cautiously through the disco-era."
**Roberta Flack Featuring Donny Hathaway (1980) - Posthumous Duet Album** : Hathaway spent his final weeks recording with Flack, intending to release another full album of duets. He died by suicide in January 1979, before it was finished. A devastated Flack completed the album using whatever vocals he had recorded and released it in 1980 as a tribute to her friend. It produced two singles that each reached No. 8 on the R&B chart: "You Are My Heaven" and "Back Together Again." "He spent his final weeks recording with Roberta Flax with the intention of issuing another full album."
**Live and More (1980) - First Album With Peabo Bryson** : This concert-plus-studio album was Flack's debut with her new duet partner, Peabo Bryson, a soul balladeer from Greenville, South Carolina. It produced "Make the World Stand Still," a top 20 R&B hit the two wrote together. "His debut with Flax was a concert plus studio album Live and More."
**Oasis (1988)** : After a five-year hiatus, Flack returned in late 1988 with this album. Its title track topped the R&B chart for the last time in her career in early 1989. "Topping the R&B chart one last time in early 89 with Oasis."
**Running (2018)** : Her latest album, showing that she was still recording. "Her latest album Running came out in 2018."

## songs
**Why Don't You Move In With Me (1977)** : A perky, nearly danceable track from *Blue Lights in the Basement*. "Tracks like Why Don't You Move In With Me were perky and almost danceable."
**The Closer I Get to You (1977) - Duet With Donny Hathaway** : Released in the era of the "disco ballad" (such as the Bee Gees' "How Deep Is Your Love" and Peaches & Herb's "Reunited"), this track reunited Flack with Donny Hathaway. It reached No. 2 on the Hot 100 and No. 1 R&B. It became the pair's biggest duet hit ever, outcharting their 1972 hit "Where Is the Love." "Roberta Flack reunited with her duet partner, Donnie Hathaway, for a blue lights track that proved to be a mega smash."
**Where Is the Love (1972) - Flack/Hathaway Duet** : An earlier Flack and Hathaway duet hit that "The Closer I Get to You" later outcharted. At the 2010 Grammy Awards, R&B chart-topper Maxwell coaxed Flack onstage to duet with him on this song. "Maxwell even lured Flack out on stage to duet with him at 2010's Grammy Awards."
**Stay With Me (1980)** : This song anchored the elegiac *Roberta Flack Featuring Donny Hathaway* album. "This song, Stay With Me, anchored Flax Next album, which was a kind of elegy."
**You Are My Heaven (1980)** : A sparkling single from the posthumous Hathaway duet album that reached No. 8 R&B. "The sparkling You Are My Heaven."
**Back Together Again (1980)** : A gently funky single from the posthumous album that also reached No. 8 R&B. After Hathaway's death, its title became a grim irony. "The gently funky back together again. It's title now a grim irony."
**Make the World Stand Still (1980)** : Written by Flack and Peabo Bryson, this was a top 20 R&B hit from *Live and More*. "A top 20 R&B hit they wrote together, called Make the World Stand Still."
**I'm the One (1982) - Last Solo Pop Hit** : On this song, Flack sounded like an early version of Sade, a new kind of sophisticated pop. It reached the middle of both the R&B chart and the Hot 100. It was her last pop hit as a soloist; after it, most of her singles were duets. "She sounded like an early version of Shade, a new kind of sophisticated pop."
**Tonight, I Celebrate My Love (1983) - Peabo Bryson Duet** : This easy-listening favorite became so ubiquitous that people still hear it on the radio, in supermarkets, and in dentists' offices. Even so, it peaked only at No. 16 Pop, No. 5 R&B, and No. 4 Adult Contemporary. It stayed on the Hot 100 for 29 weeks, more than half a year spanning 1983 into 1984. Afterward, Flack began another long recording hiatus. "It was a remarkably long lasting hit, riding the Hot 100 for 29 weeks."
**Set the Night to Music (1991) - Last Top 10 Pop Hit Among the Four Queens** : This duet with reggae singer Maxi Priest reached No. 6. It made the reclusive Flack the last of the four R&B queens (Warwick, Flack, LaBelle, Khan) to score a top 10 pop hit. "It was the reclusive Roberta Flack. Who, in 1991, got all the way to number six."
**Killing Me Softly With His Song - Fugees Cover (1996)** : The rap trio the Fugees remade Flack's song in 1996 as a showcase for Lauryn Hill's vocals, with rapping from Wyclef Jean. Their version reached No. 2 on pop radio and pushed the Fugees' album *The Score* to No. 1. "Their version of killing me softly reached number two on pop radio."

## curiosities
**Slow, Self-Paced Recording Schedule** : Flack recorded on her own slower schedule throughout her career and took long breaks, including two years before 1977 and five years after 1983. Even so, she kept pace on the charts. "For an artist who now recorded on her own slower-paced schedule, Flack had nonetheless managed to keep pace on the charts."

## albums
**Beverly Hills Cop Soundtrack (1984) - High-Tech Makeover** : After signing with MCA in 1984, LaBelle wanted to move beyond her ballad-heavy style. She had watched synth-driven new wave production energize the work of Chaka Khan and her friends the Pointer Sisters, and thought, "I can do that." At age 40, working with a new production team on MCA's soundtrack for the Eddie Murphy film *Beverly Hills Cop*, she underwent a high-tech sonic makeover. Her two tracks, "Stir It Up" and "New Attitude," were featured prominently in the film. The soundtrack reached No. 1 on the pop album chart a month after "New Attitude" peaked in June 1985. "Patti LaBelle at age 40 underwent a high-tech sonic makeover."
**Winner in You (1986) - No. 1 Album** : Driven by "On My Own," this album reached No. 1 on the pop album chart. That made LaBelle the first of the four R&B queens to do so since Roberta Flack's *First Take* in 1972. The album was certified platinum and also produced "Oh, People," a top 10 R&B and top 30 pop hit. "LeBel's winner in you album also reached number one on the pop album chart."

## songs
**Music Is My Way of Life (1979) - Solo Disco Club Hit** : After leaving Labelle, LaBelle scored a real club hit as a solo artist. Having been a disco diva in training for most of her two-decade career, she finally fulfilled that destiny with this ride-cymbal-driven track. In May 1979, at the peak of Donna Summer and Gloria Gaynor, it reached No. 10 on Billboard's Disco chart. "Patty had been a disco diva in training for most of her two decade career."
**I Don't Go Shopping (1980)** : A stately piano-and-brass lament that reached No. 26 R&B. "The Stately Piano and Brass lament I Don't Go Shopping, a number 26 R&B hit."
**Shoot Him on Sight (1980)** : A mid-tempo rock-and-soul song originally intended for Jackson Browne and written by Dexter Wansel. "A mid tempo rock and soul song originally intended for Jackson Brown."
**If Only You Knew (1983) - First R&B No. 1** : This slow dance song was co-written by Dexter Wansel, Philly soul producer Kenny Gamble, and journeywoman Cynthia Biggs. It gave LaBelle her first R&B No. 1. Despite its success on Black radio, it missed the top 40 on the Hot 100. "Dexter Wonsol wound up giving LeBel her first R&B number one."
**Love, Need and Want You (1983) - Sampled in Dilemma** : In 2002, rapper Nelly and Destiny's Child singer Kelly Rowland interpolated this LaBelle R&B hit into their No. 1 smash "Dilemma," putting LaBelle back at No. 1 in a way. "Was interpolated by rapper Nelly and Destiny's Child Singer Kelly Roland into the number one smash dilemma."
**Stir It Up (1984)** : One of her two *Beverly Hills Cop* tracks. It reached the R&B top five and narrowly missed the pop top 40. "Stir It Up, which reached the R&B top five and just missed the pop top 40."
**New Attitude (1984/1985)** : The song doubled as LaBelle's new life philosophy. It was synthetic in sound yet authentic to her personality, a four-minute aerobics workout that also worked as a pop song. It reached No. 3 R&B and No. 17 on the Hot 100, her highest-charting song since "Lady Marmalade" a decade earlier, peaking in June 1985. "A four-minute aerobics workout that doubled as a pop song."
**On My Own (1986) - Duet With Michael McDonald** : Burt Bacharach and Carole Bayer Sager wrote and produced the song, and it was never meant to be a duet. LaBelle sang it well alone, but the songwriters felt it needed a male vocal partner. Their friend Michael McDonald, the former Doobie Brother and "yacht rock king," agreed to sing. He recorded his part in Los Angeles while LaBelle stayed in Philadelphia. The two singers did not meet until long after the song became a hit, on the night they performed it together on *The Tonight Show*; the transcript names "Baccarac" at this point, but the context indicates McDonald. It reached No. 1 on both the Hot 100 and the R&B chart, the biggest hit of either vocalist's career. "The song that took Patty LeBel to number one was never supposed to be a duet at all."
**Oh, People (1986)** : A top 10 R&B and top 30 pop hit from *Winner in You*. "Including the top 10 R&B Top 30 pop hit, O People."
**If You Asked Me To (1989) - James Bond Song** : LaBelle recorded this song for the 1989 James Bond film *Licence to Kill*. Céline Dion later turned it into a hit. "A song that, by the way, was later turned into a hit by Celine Dion."
**Amazed - The Masked Singer (2019)** : LaBelle performed a cover of Lonestar's country hit while hidden in a flower costume on *The Masked Singer*. Her identity was easy to guess even before the reveal. "It was not hard to figure out who was singing in that flower costume."

## curiosities
**Broadway Stint (1982)** : In 1982, LaBelle acted and sang on Broadway in the gospel musical *Your Arm's Too Short to Box with God*. "LeBel's brief stint on Broadway in 1982 acting and singing in the gospel musical."
**Black-Centered Fame Without Crossover** : By the early 80s, LaBelle had reached the height of Black-centered cultural fame without much interest in crossing over to pop. She had not had a serious pop crossover hit since "Lady Marmalade" in 1975. "Patty had seemingly reached the height of Black-centered cultural fame without much caring to cross over."
**Live Aid Philadelphia Set (July 1985)** : Live Aid's Philadelphia organizers gave LaBelle, a hometown artist, a prominent evening slot. Wearing a crown-like headdress, she performed six songs, one of the longest sets of the event. Besides her latest hits, she covered John Lennon's "Imagine" and Bob Dylan's "Forever Young," blending rock and soul as she had in her Labelle days. "She played a whopping six songs, one of the longest sets of the entire event."
**We Are the World Finale at Live Aid** : LaBelle had not taken part in the original USA for Africa recording. In the all-star finale of "We Are the World" at Live Aid, with roughly 100 people onstage, she stood out both visually and vocally. "Nobody missed Patti visually or vocally."
**Later Career** : In the 2010s, LaBelle appeared on *Dancing with the Stars* and *The Masked Singer*. She is described as a septuagenarian "energizer bunny." "Paddy Lebel is a septiginarian energizer bunny."

## albums
**Chaka (1978) - Solo Debut** : Within months of her breakout on "Stuff Like That," Khan teamed with super-producer Arif Mardin on this self-titled LP. It included "I'm Every Woman," written by Ashford & Simpson. "She teamed with super producer Arif Martin to record a self-titled LP, Shaka."
**I Feel for You (1984)** : An album on which electro-pop runs through every track, even the ballads. It established Khan as a central artist of 80s electro pop. "A sound that carried through on all of her I Feel For You LP's tracks, even the ballads."

## songs
**Stuff Like That (1978) - Quincy Jones Single** : Quincy Jones's single featured husband-and-wife duo Nick Ashford and Valerie Simpson alongside a very prominent Chaka Khan. It topped the R&B chart and just reached the pop top 20 in summer 1978. It served as Khan's de facto career coming-out, signaling her solo ambitions, much as Patti LaBelle had done. "Stuff Like That served as a de facto career coming out for Shaka Khan."
**I'm Every Woman (1978) - Solo Statement of Purpose** : Ashford & Simpson had struggled with the melody and lyric for months. When they gave it to Khan, she instinctively knew how to arrange it, placing vocal runs exactly where they belonged, such as the long "I'm" in "I'm Every Woman." Released in early fall 1978, it topped the R&B chart in just over a month and reached No. 21 pop, her best Hot 100 showing, with or without Rufus, in nearly three years. In 1992, Whitney Houston covered it for *The Bodyguard* soundtrack, copying Khan's arrangement and even briefly imitating Melle Mel. "Solo career launching songs don't get much more definitive than I'm Every Woman."
**What Cha' Gonna Do for Me (1981)** : A sleek synth-funk song that gave Khan another R&B No. 1 in 1981, during a run of solo LPs in the early 80s. "The sleek synth funk of what you're gonna do for me."
**I Feel for You (1984) - Biggest Pop Hit** : The song was originally a deep cut on Prince's self-titled 1979 album and had never been released as a single. Khan and producer Arif Mardin saw its potential while making her 1984 album and assembled an all-star team. Prince was invited but was too busy that *Purple Rain* year. Melle Mel, fresh off "White Lines" (1983), wrote a custom rap honoring Khan. Stevie Wonder played harmonica and cleared a sample of his 1963 No. 1 live hit as Little Stevie Wonder, "Fingertips Pt. 2." The famous stuttering "Chaka Khan" hook was partly an accident: Mardin had planned to put Mel's rap ("Chaka Khan, let me rock you") in the middle, but the sampler got stuck repeating her name, and Mardin kept it and moved it to the very beginning. Described as a hip-hop ambassador at a time when rap was still crossing over to pop audiences, it reached No. 3 on the Hot 100 and No. 1 R&B in November 1984. It was Khan's biggest pop hit ever. "One day the sampler got stuck repeating Khan's name."
**Through the Fire (1984)** : A ballad from *I Feel for You* that reached No. 15 R&B. "Like the number 15 R&B hit through the fire."
**Love Has Lost / Love Is Alive (1984) - Gary Wright Remake** : An acclaimed, kinetic remake of British soft rocker Gary Wright's 1976 hit. "Her acclaimed kinetic remake of British soft rocker Gary Wright's 1976 hit."
**Higher Love - Steve Winwood (1986)** : One year after the *I Feel for You* hits had run their course, Khan finally appeared on a No. 1 pop hit, singing the chorus of Steve Winwood's Grammy-winning chart-topper. "Khan finally appeared on a number 1 pop hit, when her unmistakable vocals graced the chorus of Steve Winwood's Grammy-winning chart-topper."
**I'll Be Good to You - Quincy Jones (1989)** : Quincy Jones brought Khan back for this 1989 R&B chart-topper, a remake of the Brothers Johnson song that also featured Ray Charles. "A brothers Johnson remake that also featured Ray Charles."
**All Good? - De La Soul (2000)** : The rap trio De La Soul invited Khan to sing on this song, which was a top 10 hit on Billboard's rap chart in 2000. "De La Soul invited Shaka Khan to sing on their hit All Good."
**It's Not Over (2013)** : A top 10 club hit that paired Khan with a singer and rapper. "Shaka Khan scored a top ten club hit in 2013 with It's Not Over."

## curiosities
**Smoothest Disco-Era Adaptation** : As the youngest of the four queens, Khan adapted best to peak disco. Her Rufus hits had gradually moved from funk toward disco. "The singer who seemed to adapt best to peak disco was the youngen, Shaka Khan."
**Everyone's Favorite Guest** : The run of *I Feel for You* hits made Khan rock and pop royalty and a sought-after guest vocalist. "Established Shaka as rock and pop royalty, everyone's favorite guest."

**Masterjam (1979) - Quincy Jones Reunion** : Chaka Khan's growing solo stardom caused tension within Rufus, but Quincy Jones managed to reunite the feuding factions for this album. It sounded less like the old Rufus and more like Jones's smooth "yacht soul." It extended the band's career but mainly boosted Khan's. "Producer Quincy Jones managed to reunite the warring factions in 1979 for the album Master Jam."

## songs
**At Midnight (My Love Will Lift You Up) (1977)** : A No. 1 R&B and No. 30 pop hit, showing the band's gradual move from funk toward disco. At this point the group was still billed as Rufus featuring Chaka Khan. "Khan's hits with Rufus had been edging away from funk and closer to disco bit by bit."
**Do You Love What You Feel (1979)** : Featuring lead vocals by both Khan and Tony Maiden, it topped the R&B chart at the very end of 1979. "With lead vocals by both Khan and Rufus member Tony Maiden, topped the R&B chart at the very end of 1979."
**Ain't Nobody (1983) - Last Hit** : The song grew out of keyboardist David "Hawk" Wolinski experimenting with the LinnDrum. He ran its beats through a sequencer, producing an off-kilter, enveloping rhythm in which the beat and synth lines seemed to fight or play off each other. He added a huge kick drum, which the band's human drummer, John Robinson, doubled in the studio. Khan holds back for two verses, snarling and purring, before the chorus explodes. It was Rufus's last hit, reaching No. 1 R&B in October 1983 and No. 22 on the Hot 100 two months later. It remains the band's most-played radio track, rivaled only by "Tell Me Something Good." "He ran the Lindrum's beats through a sequencer, which made the rhythm off-kilter and strangely enveloping."
**Tell Me Something Good** : The only Rufus song that rivals "Ain't Nobody" in radio play. "Rivaled only by telling me something good."

# genre - Disco

## curiosities
**Sink or Swim for Pre-Disco R&B Singers** : Black, gay, and especially female artists excelled at disco, and it made the careers of Gloria Gaynor and Donna Summer. For R&B singers who came before disco, though, it was sink or swim. "It was also sync or swim for a generation of R&B singers who predated disco."
**The Disco Ballad** : Slow songs with disco production, such as the Bee Gees' "How Deep Is Your Love" and Peaches & Herb's "Reunited," defined the late-70s disco ballad. Warwick and Flack both found success with this approach. "What really worked for Flack in this period was disco instrumentation on slow or mid-tempo songs."

# concert - Live Aid 1985

## curiosities
**Philadelphia Show and We Are the World Finale** : At the Philadelphia portion of Live Aid in July 1985, Patti LaBelle played a prominent evening set of six songs, including covers of "Imagine" and Bob Dylan's "Forever Young." # instrument - LinnDrum

## curiosities
**Seminal 80s Drum Machine** : The LinnDrum appeared on major hits by the Human League and Prince. It is grouped with other technology-born 80s hits, such as the Prophet-5 on Phil Collins's "In the Air Tonight" and the Roland CompuRhythm on Hall & Oates's "I Can't Go for That." On Rufus's "Ain't Nobody," Hawk Wolinski ran it through a sequencer. "Ain't nobody was born when Rufus keyboardist David Hawk Walinsky was playing around with what turned out to be a seminal piece of technology. The Lindrum."

# curiosity

**Shine Bright by Danyel Smith** : Critic Danyel Smith's book *Shine Bright* is a very personal history of Black women in pop. It is cited for its analysis of Dionne Warwick's disco-era pragmatism. "Shine Bright, a very personal history of black women in pop."
**Bacharach–Sager Back-to-Back No. 1s** : Four months after "That's What Friends Are For" peaked, Patti LaBelle reached No. 1 with "On My Own," also written by Burt Bacharach and Carole Bayer Sager. "You can't make up a coincidence like this, also by Bert Baccarac and Carol Bayer-Sager."
**Last of the Four Queens in the Pop Top 10** : Roberta Flack, not Chaka Khan or Patti LaBelle, was the last of the four to score a top 10 pop hit, with "Set the Night to Music" (No. 6, 1991). "It was the reclusive Roberta Flack."
