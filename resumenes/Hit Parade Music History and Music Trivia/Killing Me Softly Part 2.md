## albums
**Nightbirds (Year Unspecified)** : This album, by her group LaBelle, achieved top ten status and went gold, marking a triumph for the group. Subsequent albums by LaBelle, however, charted lower than their predecessor. "After the triumph of their Nightbirds album, which went top ten and gold, each subsequent album charted lower than its predecessor."
**Winner in You (Year Unspecified)** : This platinum-certified album reached number one on the pop album chart, making LaBelle the first of the R&B queens to achieve this since Roberta Flack's *First Take* in 1972. The album generated multiple hits, including "O People." "On my own was so popular, LeBel's winner in you album also reached number one on the pop album chart."

## songs
**Get You Somebody New (1976)** : This dance-funk single by her group LaBelle struggled to find success in the disco era, managing only a number 50 peak on the R&B chart. It demonstrated the group's difficulty in adapting after defining their rock-meets-funk style. "Lebel's strutting 1976 dance-funk single Get You Somebody New could only manage a number 50 R&B chart peak."
**Isn't It a Shame? (1977)** : This song was the closest her group LaBelle came to a disco ballad, though it was more accurately described as a saloon ballad. It peaked at number 18 on the R&B chart but failed to make any impact on the pop chart. "And 1977's Isn't It a Shame? The closest the group came to a disco ballad, though it was really more like a saloon ballad, managed a number 18 R&B peak but missed the pop chart entirely."
**Music Is My Way of Life (1979)** : This solo track solidified LaBelle's destiny as a disco diva, reaching number 10 on Billboard's Disco Chart in May 1979, coinciding with the peak of artists like Donna Summer and Gloria Gaynor. It demonstrated her successful adaptation to the disco sound in her solo career. "Patty's ride symbol jam reached number 10 on Billboard's Disco Chart."
**I Don't Go Shopping (Year Unspecified)** : This song is described as a stately piano and brass lament, which became an R&B hit, peaking at number 26. It represents the range of material LaBelle was recording in the early 1980s. "She was still recording a range of material from the Stately Piano and Brass lament I Don't Go Shopping, a number 26 R&B hit."
**Shoot Him on Site (Year Unspecified)** : A mid-tempo rock and soul song that was originally intended for Jackson Browne. Its songwriter, Dexter Wansel, ultimately gave LaBelle her first R&B number one with a different track. "To a mid tempo rock and soul song originally intended for Jackson Brown, shoot him on site."
**If You Only Knew (1983)** : This slow dance track became Patti LaBelle's first R&B number one hit, co-written by Philly Soul producer Kenny Gamble and Cynthia Biggs. Despite its success on Black Radio, it did not cross over into the pop Top 40, highlighting her focus on Black-centered cultural fame without much emphasis on pop crossover. "The songwriter of Shoot Him on Site Dexter Wonsol wound up giving LeBel her first R&B number one, the 1983 single If You Only New, a slow dance co-written with Philly Soul producer Kenny Gamble and Journey Woman Cynthia Biggs."
**Stir It Up (1984)** : One of two tracks LaBelle recorded for the *Beverly Hills Cop* soundtrack, this song received prominent placement in the 1984 film. It reached the R&B top five and narrowly missed the pop top 40. "Patti's two tracks for the Beverly Hills Cop soundtrack got prominent placement in the 1984 film, including Stir It Up, which reached the R&B top five and just missed the pop top 40."
**New Attitude (1984)** : This track, also from the *Beverly Hills Cop* soundtrack, charted better than "Stir It Up" and encapsulated LaBelle's newfound life philosophy. Described as a "four-minute aerobics workout that doubled as a pop song," it was synthetic in sound but authentic to her personality. It reached number 3 on the R&B chart and, significantly, number 17 on the Hot 100, marking her highest pop climb since "Lady Marmalade" a decade earlier. "New attitude was both synthetic, in sound, and authentic to Patti's personality, a four-minute aerobics workout that doubled as a pop song."
**Imagine (Year Unspecified)** : Patti LaBelle performed a cover of John Lennon's song at Live Aid in July 1985, as part of her set which paid tribute to rock legends. This performance highlighted her hybridization of rock and soul. "In addition to performing her latest hits, Patti paid tribute to rock legends like John Lennon with a cover of Imagine and Bob Dylan with Forever Young."
**Forever Young (Year Unspecified)** : Patti LaBelle performed a cover of Bob Dylan's song at Live Aid in July 1985, showcasing her ability to hybridize rock and soul while paying tribute to rock legends. This was part of one of the longest sets of the entire event. "In addition to performing her latest hits, Patti paid tribute to rock legends like John Lennon with a cover of Imagine and Bob Dylan with Forever Young."
**On My Own (1986)** : This duet with Michael McDonald, co-written by Burt Bacharach and Carol Bayer Sager, became a number one hit on both the Hot 100 and the R&B Chart, representing the biggest hit of both vocalists' careers. McDonald recorded his vocals from Los Angeles while LaBelle was in Philadelphia, and they did not meet until performing the song on *The Tonight Show* after it became a hit. "By then, on my own was number one on both the Hot 100 and the R&B Chart, the biggest hit of either vocalists' career."
**O People (Year Unspecified)** : A track from her platinum-certified album *Winner in You*, this song reached the top 10 on the R&B chart and the top 30 on the pop chart. It was one of several hits pulled from the successful album. "LeBel pulled more hits from the Platinum Certified album, including the top 10 R&B Top 30 pop hit, O People."
**If You Asked Me To (1989)** : Patti LaBelle recorded this song for the James Bond film *License to Kill*. The song was later turned into a hit by Celine Dion. "LeBel would continue to cast a shadow on pop in unexpected ways, including recording a song for a James Bond film, if you asked me to, from 1989's License to Kill."
**Love, Need and Want You (1983)** : This R&B hit was interpolated by rapper Nelly and Destiny's Child singer Kelly Rowland into their 2002 number one smash "Dilemma." The interpolation helped bring the song to a new generation and chart success. "Was interpolated by rapper Nelly and Destiny's Child Singer Kelly Roland into the number one smash dilemma."
**Amazed (2019)** : Patti LaBelle performed a cover of Lone Star's country hit while concealed in a costume on the television show *The Masked Singer*. "In 2019, performed a cover of Lone Stars Country hit Amazed, concealed in a costume on the show The Masked Singer."

## curiosities
**Departure for Solo Career (Year Unspecified)** : The flagging fortunes of her group LaBelle, whose albums charted progressively lower after *Nightbirds*, made Patti LaBelle's departure for a solo career "a virtual inevitability." She did not reunite with group members Nona Hendryx and Sarah Dash until the 2000s. "The group's flagging fortunes made Patty Lebel's departure for a solo career and a virtual inevitability."
**Broadway Stint (1982)** : In 1982, Patti LaBelle had a brief stint on Broadway, acting and singing in the gospel musical *Your Arms Too Short to Box with God*. This performance showcased her talents beyond the traditional music charts. "Patty had seemingly reached the height of Black-centered cultural fame without much caring to cross over."
**Age 40 Makeover (1984)** : At age 40, Patti LaBelle underwent a "high-tech sonic makeover" for the *Beverly Hills Cop* soundtrack after signing with MCA. This transformation, inspired by new wave production, helped her transition from her ballad-heavy lane into a new, energetic pop star persona. "So, teaming up with a new production team working on MCA's soundtrack to a forthcoming Eddie Murphy film called Beverly Hills Cop, Patti LaBelle at age 40 underwent a high-tech sonic makeover."
**Prominent Live Aid Performance (1985)** : Patti LaBelle was given a very prominent evening slot at Live Aid in Philadelphia in July 1985. She performed a whopping six songs, one of the longest sets of the entire event, looking regal in a crowning headdress and covering rock legends like John Lennon and Bob Dylan. "May your blades and always keep you. May your wishes are hard to come. May you always do for our best and never miss you."
**We Are the World Performance (1985)** : Patti LaBelle's most significant showcase at Live Aid came during the final all-star sing-along of "We Are the World," a song she had not participated in for the original USA for Africa recording. Despite nearly 100 people on stage, she stood out visually and vocally, "more than mak[ing] up for" her absence from the studio version. "Live on stage at Live Aid, she more than made up for that."
**"Septuagenarian Energizer Bunny" (2022)** : Described as a "septuagenarian energizer bunny" in 2022, Patti LaBelle continued to be active in the entertainment industry. In the last decade, she made appearances on *Dancing with the Stars* and performed on *The Masked Singer* in 2019. "Paddy Lebel is a septiginarian energizer bunny."

Manilow successfully crafted a "disco-era sound that suited her," leading to hit singles like "Deja Vu" and "I'll Never Love This Way Again." "Manolo manned the boards for Dion's next album, 1979's Simply Titled Dion, which became Warwick's first ever platinum album."

## songs
**Then Came You (1974)** : A proto-disco smash, described by critic Danielle Smith as having a "miraculous groove." This song, a collaboration with The Spinners, preceded a wave of sophisticated disco-era hits by artists like Marilyn McCoo and Billy Davis Jr., and Peaches & Herb. "Her 1974 Proto-Disco Tom Bell smash then came you with what Smith calls a miraculous groove."
**Early Morning Strangers (1977)** : A track from her 1977 LP *Love at First Site*, this song marked a reunion for Dionne Warwick with Hal David, the lyricist who penned many of her 1960s hits with Burt Bacharach. The song was also co-written by Barry Manilow, who would become a pivotal figure for Warwick in the disco era. "Nothing from this album was a hit, although early morning strangers was a reunion of sorts for Dion with Hal David, the lyricist who penned so many of Warwick's 60s hits with Bert Bakerak."
**Deja Vu (1979)** : This hit single from her album *Dion* was a solid success, reaching number 15 on the Pop chart, number 25 on the R&B chart, and number one on Billboard's Adult Contemporary Chart. The song was noted for being "closer to Yatra," representing the disco-era sound tailored for her by Barry Manilow. "The hit Deja Vu was something closer to Yatra. And a solid hit."
**I'll Never Love This Way Again (1979)** : This ballad, also from the album *Dion*, performed even better than "Deja Vu," reaching number five on the Pop chart and number 18 on the R&B chart. Barry Manilow produced this track, making Warwick's sound contemporary but not overtly danceable, positioning her as "disco adjacent, not disco." "The ballad I'll never love this way again, which reached number five, Pop. And number 18, R&B. It sounded contemporary to the period, but not danceable."
**Heartbreaker (1983)** : This hit marked Dionne Warwick's return to the pop top 10 for the first time since "I'll Never Love This Way Again" in 1979. Written by all three Gibb brothers (who also sang prominent backups), the song featured more prominent synthesizers than any of her previous hits, aligning with a trend of veteran superstars working with the Gibb brothers. "With Heartbreaker written by all three Gibb brothers, they even sing prominent backups on the track."
**We Are the World (1985)** : Dionne Warwick contributed a "great and underrated vocal" to this charity mega-single. She was present at Live Aid, although less prominent than Patti LaBelle, and this song was one of her charity achievements that year. "I've already extolled Dion's great and underrated vocal on We Are the World."
**That's What Friends Are For (1985)** : Credited officially to "Dion and Friends" (featuring Stevie Wonder, Gladys Knight, and Elton John), this benefit single for AIDS research was Warwick's crowning achievement of 1985. Originally recorded by Rod Stewart in 1982 without success, Warwick's version, co-written by Burt Bacharach and Carol Bayer Sager, soared to number one on the Pop chart in January 1986 for four weeks, becoming the biggest hit of her career and Billboard's number one song of 1986. It reportedly raised over $3 million for AIDS research. "Released just before the holidays of 1985, that's what friends are for, credited officially to Dion and friends. Climbed all the way to number one in January 1986 and stayed there for four weeks."
**It's Not Over (2013)** : This club hit reached the top ten, pairing Dionne Warwick with singer and rapper LaCray. It demonstrated her continued relevance and ability to score hits in later decades. "Shaka Khan scored a top ten club hit in 2013 with It's Not Over, which paired her with Singer and rapper La Cray."
**You Really Started Something (2019)** : This song became a number sixteen club record, indicating that Dionne Warwick was still achieving chart success as she kicked off the 2020s. "She kicked off the decade in 2019 with a hit song, a number sixteen club record called You Really Started Something."

## curiosities
**Ahead of the Curve (1974)** : Critic Danielle Smith noted that Dionne Warwick was "slightly ahead of the curve" with her 1974 proto-disco smash "Then Came You," which had a "miraculous groove" and presaged a wave of sophisticated disco-era hits. "In her new book, Shine Bright, a very personal history of black women in pop, critic Danielle Smith points out that Dion Warwick was, in one sense, slightly ahead of the curve."
**Pragmatic Approach to Disco (Late 1970s)** : While mostly unmoved by disco, Dionne Warwick was described as "pragmatic" by critic Danielle Smith. She did attempt "urban disco" in the late 1970s, as seen on her 1977 LP *Love at First Site*, though this album did not produce any hits. "Warwick was pragmatic Smith writes. She did try a form of urban disco in the late 70s, as on her 1977 LP Love at First Site."
**Reunion with Bacharach (1985)** : Dionne Warwick reunited with Burt Bacharach to contribute to her next album around 1985, having not recorded together since 1971. Their old grudge was described as "water under the bridge," leading to their collaboration on "That's What Friends Are For." "Remember, Bert and Dion had not recorded together since 1971. Sager brought them back together and their old grudge turned out to be water under the bridge."
**Elizabeth Taylor's Influence (1985)** : Actress Elizabeth Taylor, an early advocate for AIDS research and a friend of Carol Bayer Sager, visited the studio the same day Stevie Wonder was recording "That's What Friends Are For" with Warwick. Sager then proposed that the single serve as a benefit for Taylor's charity, Amphar. "Taylor was one of the earliest advocates for AIDS research. And Sager proposed that Warwick and Wonder's new single should serve as a benefit for Taylor's charity Amphar."
**"Mini USA for Africa" (1985)** : The supergroup assembled for "That's What Friends Are For" (Dionne Warwick, Stevie Wonder, Gladys Knight, and Elton John) was referred to as a "mini USA for Africa" and "super friends of pop." The song embodied kindness and empathy, especially towards those marginalized by HIV at an early stage of the AIDS epidemic. "Together, these four singers were a mini USA for Africa, a super friends of pop."
**"Queen of Twitter" (2019-present)** : Starting around 2019, Dionne Warwick became known as the "queen of Twitter," gaining popularity for her candid thoughts on popular culture. She was lovingly parodied on *Saturday Night Live* by Ego Nwodim, and Warwick herself later appeared on the show. "More famously, Dion has become the queen of Twitter, popping off with thoughts about popular culture tweeted by a woman with no fucks left to give."
**Rock and Roll Hall of Fame Indifference (2022)** : In a mid-March 2022 interview with *Watch What Happens Live*, Dionne Warwick revealed her indifference to being inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame, stating she doesn't "give a damn." This sentiment highlights her confidence in her own legacy, regardless of external validation. "Dion Warwick revealed that she doesn't quote Give a Down. If she is inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame."

## albums
**Blue Lights in the Basement (1977)** : This self-produced album marked Roberta Flack's return after a two-year hiatus during the disco era. It featured tracks like "Why Don't You Move In With Me" and the mega-smash duet with Donnie Hathaway, "The Closer I Get To You." "Roberta Flack moved even more cautiously through the disco-era, taking a full two years to come back with another self-produced album, 1977's Blue Lights in the Basement."
**Roberta Flack Featuring Donnie Hathaway (1980)** : Released as a tribute after Donnie Hathaway's suicide in January 1979, this album was completed by a devastated Roberta Flack using his recorded vocals. It produced two sizable singles in 1980, both number eight hits on the R&B chart: "You Are My Heaven" and "Back Together Again." "A devastated Roberta Flax completed the album with whatever vocals he had recorded for a 1980 release in tribute to her friend."
**Live and More (Year Unspecified)** : This album, a combination of concert and studio recordings, marked Roberta Flack's debut collaboration with new duet partner Peabo Bryson. It generated a top 20 R&B hit, "Make the World Stand Still," which they co-wrote. "His debut with Flax was a concert plus studio album Live and More that generated a top 20 R&B hit they wrote together, called Make the World Stand Still."
**I'm the One (1982)** : On this album, Roberta Flack's sound evolved to resemble an early version of Sade, categorized as a "new kind of sophisticated pop." The title track made the middle rungs of both the R&B chart and the Hot 100, and it proved to be Flack's last ever pop hit as a soloist. "On 1982's I'm the One, she sounded like an early version of Shade, a new kind of sophisticated pop."
**Oasis (1988)** : This album marked Roberta Flack's return to recording after a five-year hiatus. It ended her dry spell, with the title track topping the R&B chart in early 1989. "She wouldn't come back for five years, finally ending her dry spell in late 1988, topping the R&B chart one last time in early 89 with Oasis."
**Running (2018)** : This is Roberta Flack's latest album, released in 2018, demonstrating her continued activity in recording music. "Her latest album Running came out in 2018."

## curiosities
**Cautious Disco Approach (1970s)** : Roberta Flack moved more cautiously through the disco era, taking two years to release her self-produced album *Blue Lights in the Basement*. She found success by incorporating disco instrumentation into slow or mid-tempo songs rather than focusing on dance tracks, similar to Dionne Warwick's approach. "Meanwhile, Roberta Flack moved even more cautiously through the disco-era, taking a full two years to come back with another self-produced album, 1977's Blue Lights in the Basement."
**Donnie Hathaway's Suicide (1979)** : Roberta Flack's career path became "bumpier and much sadder" after her duet partner, Donnie Hathaway, committed suicide in January 1979. Hathaway, a gifted but troubled singer, was plagued with crippling depression and paranoid schizophrenia and had been recording with Flack for another album of duets that he would not live to see released. "He committed suicide in January 1979."
**Recording Hiatuses (1984-1988)** : After the success of "Tonight I Celebrate My Love," Roberta Flack went on another of her long recording hiatuses, not releasing new music for five years. She finally ended this dry spell in late 1988 with the album *Oasis*. "Figuring she probably couldn't top that, Roberta Flack went on another of her long recording hiatuses."

## albums
**Chaka (1978)** : Chaka Khan's self-titled solo LP, recorded with super producer Arif Mardin, capitalized on her growing solo profile outside of Rufus. The album featured the iconic single "I'm Every Woman," co-written by Ashford & Simpson, which served as her definitive solo career launch. "She teamed with super producer Arif Martin to record a self-titled LP, Shaka."
**Masterjam (1979)** : This album, by Rufus featuring Chaka Khan, was produced by Quincy Jones, who reunited the "warring factions" of the band despite internal tensions from Khan's rising solo stardom. The album's sound shifted from the "Rufus of old" to "Quincy's brand of smooth yacht soul," and it prolonged Rufus's career while primarily burnishing Khan's. "Producer Quincy Jones managed to reunite the warring factions in 1979 for the album Master Jam, which sounded less like the Rufus of old and more like Quincy's brand of smooth yacht soul."
**I Feel For You (1984)** : This album, produced by Arif Mardin, solidified Chaka Khan as a "centerpiece artist of 80s electro pop." The album's sound, characterized by sleek synth funk and innovative production, carried through all its tracks, including ballads like "Through the Fire" and her remake of Gary Wright's "Love Is Alive." "I Feel For You affirmed Khan as a centerpiece artist of 80s electro pop, a sound that carried through on all of her I Feel For You LP's tracks, even the ballads, like the number 15 R&B hit through the fire."

## songs
**At Midnight (My Love Will Lift You Up) (1977)** : This song with Rufus reached number 1 on the R&B chart and number 30 on the pop chart. It represented the group's sound "edging away from funk and closer to disco bit by bit," with the group still billed as "Rufus featuring Chaka Khan." "As on their 1977 number 1 R&B, number 30 pop hit at Midnight, My Love Will Lift You Up."
**Stuff Like That (1978)** : Billed to producer Quincy Jones, this single featured prominent vocals from Chaka Khan, alongside husband and wife team Nick Ashford and Valerie Simpson. It reached the top of the R&B chart and scraped the pop top 20 in the summer of 1978, serving as a "de facto career coming out" for Khan outside of Rufus. "The shot across the bow was a single build to producer Quincy Jones called Stuff Like That."
**I'm Every Woman (1978)** : Co-written by Ashford & Simpson, this song became Chaka Khan's definitive solo career launching single and a "statement of purpose." Khan instinctively arranged the track, adding vocal runs, such as the extended "I'm." Released in early fall 1978, it topped the R&B chart and reached number 21 on the pop chart, her best Hot 100 performance in nearly three years. "Solo career launching songs don't get much more definitive than I'm Every Woman, Shaka Khan's statement of purpose."
**Do You Love What You Feel (1979)** : This track from Rufus's *Masterjam* album featured lead vocals by both Chaka Khan and Rufus member Tony Maiden, and it topped the R&B chart at the very end of 1979. While it prolonged Rufus's career, its primary effect was to further burnish Khan's growing solo profile. "Do you love what you feel with lead vocals by both Khan and Rufus member Tony Maiden, topped the R&B chart at the very end of 1979?"
**What Cha' Gonna Do For Me (1981)** : A sleek synth funk track, this song became another R&B number one for Chaka Khan as she transitioned into her solo career in the early 1980s. It was part of a string of new LPs released over three years. "Dropping a string of new LPs over the next three years, including the sleek synth funk of what you're gonna do for me. Another Shaka Khan R&B number one in 1981."
**Ain't Nobody (1983)** : This track, Rufus's last hit and arguably their "tour de force," originated from keyboardist David "Hawk" Wolinski experimenting with the Lindrum drum machine. Wolinski ran the beats through a sequencer, creating an "off-kilter and strangely enveloping" rhythm, with a monster kick drum doubled by human drummer John Robinson. Khan's vocals build from snarling and purring to an explosive chorus. It reached number one on the R&B chart in October 1983 and a more modest number 22 on the Hot 100 two months later, remaining Rufus's most played radio track. "It was Rufus' last hit, and arguably their tour de force."
**I Feel For You (1984)** : Originally a deep cut from Prince's 1979 self-titled album, this song was remade by Chaka Khan and producer Arif Mardin in 1984 with an all-star team. It featured rapper Meli Mel doing a bespoke rap, Stevie Wonder playing harmonica and a sample from his 1963 hit "Fingertips Part 2." A sampler error repeating Khan's name was kept as its signature opening. This "hip-hop ambassador of a song" reached number 3 on the Hot 100 and number 1 on the R&B chart in November 1984, becoming her biggest pop hit ever and cementing her as a centerpiece of 80s electro pop. "I feel for you was on Prince's self titled 1979 album and had never been issued as a single."
**Through the Fire (Year Unspecified)** : A ballad from Chaka Khan's *I Feel For You* LP, this song was an R&B number 15 hit. It demonstrated how the electro pop sound of the album carried through even on its slower tracks. "Even the ballads, like the number 15 R&B hit through the fire."
**Love Is Alive (Year Unspecified)** : An "acclaimed kinetic remake" by Chaka Khan of British soft rocker Gary Wright's 1976 hit.
