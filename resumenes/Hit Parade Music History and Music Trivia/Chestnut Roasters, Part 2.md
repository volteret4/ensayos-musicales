## albums
**Make It Big (1984) - Second Studio Album and Commercial Breakthrough** : Wham!'s second album. On it George Michael and Andrew Ridgeley changed their sound from the one they had broken through with. Its lead single, "Wake Me Up Before You Go-Go," made the duo stars and gave them their first #1 in both the UK and the US. The next single was "Careless Whisper." "That album was called Make It Big, and this single did just that to Wam."

## songs
**Young Guns (1982) - UK Debut** : Wham!'s UK debut single, from before the duo changed their formula. It did not become a lasting perennial for the group. "including their UK debut 1982's Young Guns."
**Bad Boys (1983) - First US Hit** : Wham!'s first American hit, which peaked at only #60 on the Hot 100. Like "Young Guns," it did not become a lasting perennial for the duo. "which peaked at a lowly number 60 on the Hot 100."
**Wake Me Up Before You Go-Go (1984) - First Transatlantic #1** : A jitterbugging lead single from Make It Big and Wham!'s first #1 in both the UK and the US. It remains one of their most popular year-round radio staples. According to MRC data, DJs played it more than 60,000 times in 2020, about 5,000 spins a month nationwide. That is more radio play than "Last Christmas" gets. "it gave Wam their first number one on both sides of the Atlanta."
**Careless Whisper (1984) - Sax-Drenched Soul Ballad** : The single released right after "Go-Go," and Wham!'s other reliable golden oldie. In the UK it was treated as a George Michael solo single, but in the US it was credited to Wham! featuring George Michael. It was spun more than 52,000 times on US radio in 2020, more than "Last Christmas." "Considered a George Michael solo single in the UK, it was credited in America to Wam featuring George Michael."
**Everything She Wants (1984) - Double A-Side Partner of Last Christmas** : A slow-burning R&B lament released in the UK as a double A-side with "Last Christmas" just before the 1984 holiday season. The single stalled at #2 behind Band Aid. "paired with the slow-burning R&B lament everything she wants."
**Last Christmas (1984) - Holiday Canon Entry** : Recorded in 1984 by George Michael and Andrew Ridgeley. Guardian critic Laura Snapes called it "a high watermark of mid-80s British synth pop song craft." It is one of very few songs from the final decades of the 20th century to enter the holiday music canon, and it has been covered by many vocalists, most recently Ariana Grande. Its success was hard to predict in 1984 because it sounded nothing like the hits Wham! had broken through with. "It's been covered by countless vocalists over the years, including quite recently Ariana Grande."
**Last Christmas (1984) - Original Chart Performance and Belated UK #1** : In the US in 1984 the song did not chart at all, because it was released only as a B-side at a time when B-sides usually didn't chart. In the UK it was a double A-side with "Everything She Wants" and got stuck at #2 behind Band Aid's charity single "Do They Know It's Christmas?" At the end of 2020, in the same week Donny Hathaway's "This Christmas" first reached the US Top 40, the Official Charts Company announced that the 37-year-old song had finally hit #1 in the UK. "This 37-year-old song had finally topped the British chart for the first time."
**Last Christmas (1984) - Radio and Streaming Data** : It received about 40,000 US radio spins in 2020, fewer than "Go-Go" or "Careless Whisper." Almost all of those spins came in November and December, which works out to about 20,000 plays a month compared with about 5,000 a month for "Go-Go." On streaming services it is easily Wham!'s biggest hit because it appears on pre-programmed Christmas playlists. Its lifetime Spotify streams are about 40% higher than "Go-Go" and 60% higher than "Careless Whisper," and in 2020 it outstreamed "Go-Go" by 2 to 1. "Whereas the concentrated two month play for last Christmas is more like 20,000 plays a month."

## curiosities
**Whamageddon - A Game Built Around Avoiding Last Christmas** : The song feels so inescapable each holiday season that people invented a game, "Whamageddon," in which players try to avoid hearing it. Some people are still only now discovering the song. "So much so that they've created a game out of trying to avoid it?"

## songs
**Dynamite (Recorded at Age 12) - Origin of a Nickname** : A raucous rockabilly song she recorded at age 12. Because of it, and her height of 4'9", the industry nicknamed her "Little Miss Dynamite." "the industry nickname Brenda, Little Miss Dynamite."
**Bigelow 6200 - The Telephone-Number Song** : An early single, named after a telephone number, that showed the grit in her voice even at a young age. "Her singles, like the telephone number song Bigelow 6200, had remarkable grit."
**One Step at a Time (1957) - Cross-Format Airplay** : It earned her airplay on both Top 40 and country radio in 1957. "One step at a time, garnered airplay for Lee on both top 40 and country radio in 1957."
**Sweet Nothin's (1959) - Major Breakthrough** : Released at the end of 1959, it reached #4 on the Hot 100 and #12 on the R&B chart. It has a memorable opening, and Lee was 15 when it came out. Kanye West sampled that opening on "Bound 2" (2013), his song about his future wife Kim Kardashian. "Keep in mind, Lee was still only 15 years old."
**I'm Sorry (1960) - First #1** : A ballad of unrequited love produced by Owen Bradley, the pioneer of the Countrypolitan sound made famous by Patsy Cline. It reached #1 on the Hot 100 in July 1960. By 2020 it was spun fewer than 600 times on US terrestrial radio. "I'm Sorry, Top to the Hot 100 in July 1960."
**That's All You Gotta Do (1960) - Hit B-Side** : The assertive B-side of "I'm Sorry" reached #6 and showed her range. "Even its B side was a smash, and it showed Lee's versatility."
**I Want to Be Wanted (1960) - Second #1** : Her second #1, arriving three months after "I'm Sorry." It is an English adaptation of the Italian song "Per tutta la vita." By 2020 it was spun fewer than 200 times on radio. "an adaptation of an Italian song, Pertuta La Vita, that was anglicized as I Want to Be Wanted."
**Rockin' Around the Christmas Tree (1958/1960) - Third Time's the Charm** : Lee recorded it at 13, and it flopped in both the 1958 and 1959 holiday seasons. It was produced by Owen Bradley and written by Johnny Marks, who also wrote "Rudolph the Red-Nosed Reindeer" for Gene Autry. Once Lee was famous, Decca re-released it in November 1960. It entered the Top 40 in December and peaked at #14 during a four-week run, held back by the short chart runs typical of holiday songs. The record has teen-idol energy, Marks's lyrics about a Christmas party hop, a rockabilly sound that worked for both country and pop audiences, and Lee's sassy voice. "As Deca suspected, the third time was the charm for Rockin' Around the Christmas Tree."
**Emotions (1961) - Back in the Top 10** : A stately ballad that put her back in the Top 10 in early 1961, weeks after the Christmas single. "she was back in the top 10 with another stately ballad, emotions."
**Fool #1 - Part of the Top 10 Streak** : One of the classics from her 1960–63 run of Top 10 hits. "Classics like full number one, you can depend on me, and break it to me gently."
**You Can Depend on Me - Part of the Top 10 Streak** : Another classic from her early-1960s Top 10 streak. "Classics like full number one, you can depend on me, and break it to me gently."
**Break It to Me Gently - Later Remade by Juice Newton** : A classic from her early-1960s streak. In 1982 the country-pop crossover act Juice Newton remade it, and the remake reached #11 on the Hot 100. "returned to the Hot 100 in the 80s in a remake by then-hot country pop crossover act, Juice Newton, a number 11 hit in 1982."
**All Alone Am I (1962) - One of Her Last Big Pop Hits** : It reached #3 in late 1962, just before the British Invasion. "By the time Brenda Lee scored one of her last big pop hits in late 1962, the number three All Alone MI."
**Ride, Ride, Ride (1967) - Final Top 40 Pop Hit** : Her last Top 40 pop hit, released as hippie-era tastes moved away from her. "she scored her final top 40 pop hit with Ride Ride Ride."
**Nobody Wins (1973) - Country Smash** : A #5 country hit during her run as a Nashville hitmaker in the 1970s. "with such singles as the number five country smash Nobody wins in 1973."
**Big Four Poster Bed (1974) - Biggest Country Hit** : A twangy single that reached #4 on the country chart, her biggest country hit. "her biggest country hit, the Twangie Big Four poster bet, which reached number four."

## curiosities
**Childhood in Poverty and Early Start** : Born Brenda Mae Tarpley in 1944 into deep poverty in Atlanta, Georgia. She began singing professionally at five and appeared on television at 10. Her father died when she was eight, which made her the family's breadwinner. At 11 she signed with Decca Records and took the stage name Brenda Lee. "Brenda Mae Tarpley, born into deep poverty in Atlanta, Georgia in 1944, began singing professionally as young as five, and appearing on television at 10."
**Little Miss Dynamite - Four Foot Nine** : Lee never grew taller than 4'9". Her height and the song "Dynamite" earned her the nickname "Little Miss Dynamite." "Lee never grew taller than four foot nine."
**A Younger Parallel to Wanda Jackson** : While Wanda Jackson, the Queen of Rockabilly, was changing the role of women in rock and roll and country, Lee was doing much the same while nearly a decade younger. "Brenda Lee was doing much the same, nearly a decade younger than Wanda Jackson."
**A Girl Teen Idol** : By the start of the 1960s Lee was a fully established teen idol, at a time when most teen idols were boys. "at a time when most teen idols were boys."
**Dozen Top 10s Before Age 20** : From 1960 to 1963 she had a nearly unbroken streak of a dozen Top 10 singles that blended girl group, Countrypolitan, and teen pop, all before her 20th birthday. "a near-unbroken streak of a dozen top 10 singles that blended the ascendant modes of girl group, country-politin, and teen pop all before her 20th birthday."
**Fourth-Biggest Act of the 1960s** : According to chart historian Joel Whitburn's Hot 100 rankings, Lee was the fourth-biggest pop act of the 1960s. She ranked behind the Beatles, Elvis Presley, and Ray Charles and ahead of the Supremes. "And ranked fourth for that decade. Again, based just on her chart performance is Brenda Lee."
**1969 Pivot to Country** : Her music had long leaned toward country, so in 1969 she rebranded herself as a country singer and scored her first country hits since 1957. She remained a major Nashville hitmaker through the 1970s. "reprimoting herself as a country singer and scoring her first country hits since 1957."
**Country Collaborations** : She worked with the Oak Ridge Boys and Willie Nelson. Her last major hit came in 1984, a collaboration with George Jones. "She collaborated with luminaries in the field, such as the Oak Ridge Boys, Willie Nelson and in 1984, on her last major hit, with country legend George Jones."
**Country Music Hall of Fame (1997)** : She was inducted in recognition of her pioneering work with Owen Bradley and her years of country hits. She has been cited as an influence on country-pop crossover singers including Bobbie Gentry, Shania Twain, and Carrie Underwood. "in 1997, Brenda Lee was inducted into the country music hall of fame."
**Only Woman in Both the Country and Rock Halls** : She was inducted into the Rock and Roll Hall of Fame in 2002, which makes her the only woman honored by both the country and rock halls. "She remains the only woman to be honored, doubly, by both the country hall and the rock hall."
**Rock Hall Acceptance Speech** : In her speech, Lee credited Owen Bradley and rock and roll, and contrasted the Georgia cotton fields she came from with the Waldorf Astoria. "It's a long way from the Georgia cotton fields to the Waldorf Astoria and I can tell you that I owe it to Owen Bradley and most of all I owe it to rock and roll."
**Rockin' Around the Christmas Tree - Streaming-Era Chart Resurgence** : The song returned to the Hot 100 at #50 in 2013, then reached #30 in 2015, #27 in 2016, and #9 in 2018, its first time ever in the Top 10. In 2019 it climbed to #2, directly behind Mariah Carey's "All I Want for Christmas Is You." It returned to #2 for two straight holiday seasons and was expected to do so again in 2021. This made Lee a 21st-century Christmas queen alongside Carey. "And number 9 by 2018, its first ever appearance in the Billboard Top 10."
**Christmas Song vs. Her #1 Hits** : In 2020, US DJs played "Rockin' Around the Christmas Tree" more than 17,000 times, compared with fewer than 600 for "I'm Sorry" and fewer than 200 for "I Want to Be Wanted." It was streamed 177 million times, about 50 times as much as those two #1s combined. "On streaming services, it was played a stunning 177 million times last year."
**Possible First #1 in Six Decades** : In late 2021 the gap in streaming, sales, and airplay points between Lee's and Carey's songs was narrowing. Billboard staff speculated that Lee could challenge Carey by the mid-2020s, which would give the 77-year-old her first #1 in more than six decades. "Giving the 77 year old her first number one hit in over six decades."
**Still Performing** : A performance from 2016 shows Lee still active as a live performer. "This performance is from 2016."
