## albums
**Minute by Minute (1978)** : This studio album was the Dubie Brothers' first chart-topping LP and became their all-time biggest seller. It affirmed Michael McDonald's conversion of the group into a smooth pop and R&B crossover act, featuring "What a Fool Believes" as its centerpiece. "Minute by minute the Dubies Athe studio album would be their first chart topping LP and all-time biggest seller."
**Taking It to the Streets (1976)** : This LP marked Michael McDonald's rapid emergence as the group's most successful songwriter, as he penned the album's only Top 40 hits. The title track, a number 13 hit in the summer of 1976, foreshadowed the sleek R&B direction McDonald would soon take the band. "But by the 1976 LP taken it to the streets, McDonald was rapidly emerging as the group's most successful songwriter."

"McDonald penned the album's only Top 40 hits, including the title track of taking it to the streets, a number 13 hit in the summer of 1976."

"But the LP's centerpiece was a song McDonnellt had written with his friend Kenny Loggins and which Loggins recorded first."
## curiosities
**Band Name Origin** : The name "Dubie Brothers" was derived from a slang word for marijuana. "Their name, Dubie Brothers, was taken from the slang word for marijuana."
**Musical Transformation** : The Dubie Brothers underwent a remarkable evolution in the second half of the 1970s, shifting from an early 70s Southern Rock sound to smoother Pop and R&B, a transformation largely influenced by the arrival of Michael McDonald. "The Dubies Brothers' evolution in the second half of the decade, all the more remarkable."
**Grammy Awards (1980)** : The Dubie Brothers were significant winners at the 1980 Grammy Awards, taking home four gramophones, marking their transformation into pop stars. "40 years ago at the 1980 Grammy Awards, the night's big winners taking home four gramophones were abandoned originally formed 10 years earlier in San Jose, California that had transformed themselves into pop stars."

"McDonald was brought in at first to cover vocals for the ailing Tom Johnston, who had health issues that prevented him from touring with the band."

## curiosities
**21st Century Coining** : The name "Yacht Rock" is a 21st-century concoction, coined by a foursome of LA-based actors and writers at the dawn of the YouTube era. It was originally conceived for a deliberately cheap-looking online TV series. "The name Yacht Rock is a 21st century concoction coined by a forceum of L.A. based actors and writers at the dawn of the YouTube era."
**Original Definition** : As devised by its inventors, Yacht Rock was defined as smooth music, largely played by a network of LA bands and session musicians between 1976 and 1984, characterized by instrumental complexity and sounds evoking jazz and R&B. "As they devised it, Yacht Rock was smooth music, played largely by a network of LA bands and session musicians between the years 1976 and 1984, with instrumental complexity and sounds that evoked jazz and R&B."
**Commercial Success** : Even when limited to the concept intended by its framers, Yacht Rock music was commercially very successful, representing "the right music at the right time" on the charts. "Even if we limit the concept to what the Yacht Rock framers intended, one thing is absolutely true. This music was commercially very successful."
**Nautical Metaphor vs. Literal Interpretation** : The "Yacht" in Yacht Rock was originally intended as a metaphor for the high-end quality of the music and for playing music while on a boat, not literally *about* boats. However, on the internet, the nautical idea was often taken literally, contributing to its meme-ability and viral spread. "The Yacht was meant to be a metaphor about the high-end quality of the music, but on the internet, folks took the nautical idea literally, even though Riznar had named the music for playing while on a boat, not about the boat per se."
**Overused Term** : In the decade and a half since the name took off, it has become a retro pop genre that seems to encompass all of 70s and 80s soft rock and jazzy R&B, whether the term truly fits or not, making it a "slippery, overused term."
