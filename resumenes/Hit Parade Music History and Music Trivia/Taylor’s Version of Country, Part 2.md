## curiosities
**Early Career Award Show Shock** : In her early years, Taylor Swift frequently exhibited a "slack jawed look of shock" at award shows, for which she was often teased and pilloried. This look might have been genuine, as her 2009 VMA win was considered an upset, marking the first time a country video won any VMA prize. "Swift's look of shock when she won, a slack jawed look that she exhibited at multiple award shows in her early years for which she was often teased and pilloried might have been actual shock."
**Relationship with Country Music** : Swift's connection to country music was described as more than "careerist calculation," with critic Jody Rosen noting that Nashville is a "song town" and Swift is primarily a songwriter. She expressed love for both country and pop, navigating the genres adeptly even as she became a crossover sensation. "I love country and I love pop, she told me. I love them both."
**Monologue Song about Taylor Lautner (Circa 2010)** : While hosting NBC's Saturday Night Live, Swift composed and performed a short song (a "diddy") that alluded to her romantic involvement with actor Taylor Lautner, who was her co-star in the film Valentine's Day. This was part of a public "in joke" where fans knew many of Swift's songs were thinly veiled accounts of her real-life relationships. "And if you're wondering if I might be dating the werewolf from Twilight. I'm not going to comment on that."
**Roman-à-clef Songwriting Style** : Many of Swift's songs, particularly her hits, are "thinly veiled roman-à-clef" about her past and current love interests, often taking the form of "kiss-offs." This style blended a country tradition of "wounded kiss-off" songs with the "guided missile specificity of a rap era beef record," dating back to her 2006 debut album. "Her kiss-offs were targeted and they dated all the way back to her 2006 debut album."
**Joe Jonas Kiss-off** : Swift was notably "openly rough" toward Joe Jonas, who was her "first post-fame power-more" and lead singer of the boy band The Jonas Brothers. She addressed their breakup directly in the song "Forever & Always," featured on the *Fearless platinum edition*. "Swift was much more openly rough toward her first post-fame power-more."
**Critiques of Feminism and Damsel in Distress Narrative** : Feminists and cultural critics often "lambasted Swift for perennially centering men in her narrative, and for portraying herself as a damsel in distress." Despite these criticisms, Swift's theme of giving voice to those who felt belittled resonated powerfully with her youngest fans, even when her songs weren't about romantic relationships. "Feminists and cultural critics lambasted Swift for perennially centering men in her narrative, and for portraying herself as a damsel in distress."
**"Innocent" Performance at 2010 VMAs** : One year after the infamous Kanye West incident, Swift performed her new ballad "Innocent" at the 2010 MTV VMAs, clearly aiming the song at Kanye. She opened the performance by replaying a clip of his interruption from the previous year, a move that even her most supportive critics found "patronizing." "Swift's new song, Innocent, was a gentle ballad, clearly aimed at Kanye."
**Crossover Appeal Beyond Genre** : By the release of *Speak Now*, Taylor Swift had grown "bigger than any one genre," demonstrating unprecedented crossover appeal. Unlike artists such as Shania Twain or The Chicks, she managed to achieve massive pop success without being "spurned by the country audience," showcasing a unique ability to retain both fanbases. "Swift was now bigger than any one genre."
**Country Radio Challenges in the 2010s** : In the early 2010s, Team Taylor recognized that country radio would become an increasing challenge for female artists. The format was predominantly male-driven, with women artists becoming "positively scarce" by 2011 and 2012, and many hits featuring male-centric "cookie-cutter lyrics" about trucks and lake parties. "Country had long been a male-driven format. But by the early 2010s, women artists were becoming positively scarce."
**"Red" Album's Production Evolution** : While working on the title track "Red" with long-time producer Nathan Chapman, they realized that despite its country "bones and chord changes" and banjos, the song needed "something extra," specifically a "punch" characteristic of big Top 40 pop hits. This led them to bring in Max Martin to help achieve a "lift, a soar" in the production. "It needed the kind of punch that was the specialty of a maker of big top 40 pop hits."
**First Documented Official Pop Album (1989)** : In 2014, Taylor Swift declared her next project, *1989*, to be her "first documented official pop album," signaling a deliberate genre shift. The album was named after her birth year, symbolically representing her "genre rebirth," and involved new collaborators like executive co-producer Max Martin and Indie/PowerPop producer Jack Antonoff. "In 2014, Taylor Swift announced her next project would be her quote first documented official pop album in her words."
**New Round of Beefs with Kanye West** : After the success of *1989*, Taylor Swift became embroiled in "a new round of beefs with her old nemesis Kanye West." This ongoing public feud was a significant event, with Swift directly referencing West in her 2017 chart-topper "Look What You Made Me Do." "Taylor wound up in a new round of beefs with her old nemesis Kanye West."
**Embracing LGBTQ+ Support** : Taylor Swift eventually "came out as an unabashed LGBTQ+ supporter," overcoming her previous "fear of being too political" which she observed in figures like her friends and "forebears the chicks." She openly expressed this support in her 2019 song "You Need To Calm Down." "Taylor also came out as an unabashed LGBTQ plus supporter. Finally moving past her fear of being too political, like her friends and forebears the chicks."
**Country Audience Loyalty to Old Hits** : Even as Taylor Swift fully embraced pop, country radio stations played her new pop songs "very sparingly, usually not at all." However, the "country audience never quite forgot Swift's country heyday," with these stations continuing to play her older hits in "recurrent rotation," demonstrating a lasting loyalty. "But the country audience never quite forgot Swift's country heyday. As these stations continued to play her old hits in recurrent rotation."
**Unplanned Return to Country Radio** : During the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020, Taylor Swift released two albums, *Folklore* and *Evermore*, which represented a "sonic departure" towards Americana and Indie Pop. By "happenstance," each album generated a song—"Betty" from *Folklore* and "No Body, No Crime" from *Evermore*—that organically brought Swift back to country radio for the first time since the early 2010s. "This gradual, quiet return to country music, without trying hard at all to sound country, has, one might say, brought Taylor's career full circle."

## albums
**Fearless (2008)** : This album marked a significant period in Swift's "Country Years" and was revisited in 2021 with a re-recording called *Fearless, Taylor's Version*. It was a foundational album that brought young fans to country music. "On our last episode, we talked about Taylor Swift's Country Years, which are now being revisited in her 2021 re-recording of her 2008 album Fearless."
**Fearless Platinum Edition (2009) - Bonus Tracks Release** : Released in late October, this edition of the *Fearless* CD included a "half dozen new songs." Thanks to heavy track downloads, all six new songs debuted within the Top 40 of the Hot 100 in a single week, showcasing Swift's growing influence and fan engagement. "In late October, she issued a so-called platinum edition of the fearless CD that came packed with a half dozen new songs."
**Speak Now (2010) - Million-Selling Week** : This album opened with over 1 million copies sold in its first week, a feat that "stunned" the music industry and marked the first time a country album had achieved this since Garth Brooks's *Double Live* in 1998. It more than doubled the first-week sales of Kanye West's critically acclaimed *My Beautiful Dark Twisted Fantasy*. "Speak Now opened to over a million copies in its first week. The first country album to do so since Garth Brooks's Double Live in 1998."
**Red (2012) - Crossover Breakthrough** : Arriving weeks after its lead single, *Red* sold 1.2 million copies in its first week, surpassing *Speak Now*'s opening sales by 200,000. This album solidified Taylor Swift's status as an artist transcending genre boundaries and demonstrated her massive appeal to both pop and country audiences. "Within a few weeks, the Red album would arrive to 1.2 million copies sold in its first week. 200,000 more than Speak Now had opened to."
**1989 (2014) - Official Pop Debut** : Swift's next project was explicitly announced as her "first documented official pop album," named after her birth year to symbolize a "genre rebirth." This album opened with 1.3 million in sales, exceeding *Red*, and generated three Hot 100 number one hits, including "Shake It Off," "Blank Space," and "Bad Blood." "The 1989 album, which by the way, opened to 1.3 million in sales, even bigger than red."
**Reputation (2017) - Hip-Hop Influenced** : This album was described as "hip-hop fueled," marking another sonic departure for Swift. It included the chart-topping single "Look What You Made Me Do," which referenced her ongoing feud with Kanye West, and "Ready For It," on which Swift "started rapping herself." "New Year's Day, a piano ballad that closed her otherwise hip-hop fueled 2017 album Reputation, cracked the country top 40."
**Folklore (2020) - Pandemic Indie Pop** : Recorded during the COVID-19 pandemic, this album represented a radical change from Swift's usual release schedule, being one of two albums released in a single year. It showcased a "sonic departure" into the rustic sounds of Americana and Indie Pop, and organically led to the song "Betty" reaching the country radio top 40 and Hot Country charts top 10. "Titled Folklore and Evermore, the two discs were yet another sonic departure for Swift, a wash in the rustic sounds of Americana and Indypop."
**Evermore (2020) - Pandemic Indie Pop Companion** : The second album recorded by Swift during the COVID-19 pandemic in a single year, *Evermore* shared *Folklore*'s sonic departure into Americana and Indie Pop. It featured the collaboration "No Body, No Crime" with Haim, which also made the Hot Country top 10 and was still riding the country airplay chart weeks before the episode. "And from Evermore, the song No Body, No Crime, Swift's collaboration with the L.A. Sister Trio Hyme also made the hot country top 10."
**Fearless, Taylor's Version (2021) - Re-recorded Album** : This is Swift's re-recording of her 2008 album *Fearless*, released less than a week before the podcast episode was completed. It was confidently predicted to be the new number one album in America, continuing Swift's streak of every album since the original *Fearless* topping the charts. "Fearless, Taylor's version, arrived less than a week before we finished this episode of Hip-Head."

## songs
**You Belong With Me (2009)** : This song and its video were set to be the "most played U.S. radio hit" and a "Moonman trophy" winner at the 2009 VMAs, where it won Best Female Video. The video was a "spunky, fully realized depiction of its high school story" with Swift playing both a "mousey nerd" and a "bitchy brunette queen B." It was the first country video in MTV's history to win any VMA prize. "If the song You Belong With Me was spunky, its video was more so, a fully realized depiction of its high school story with Taylor playing both the mousey nerd pining for her handsome male friend, and the bitchy brunette queen B, whom he's currently dating."
**Jump Then Fall (2009)** : This song was the biggest track released on the *Fearless platinum edition*. It "cracked the top 10" of the Hot 100, demonstrating the immediate success and fan engagement for the new material included on the re-release. "The biggest track, Jump Then Fall, even cracked the top 10."
**Today Was a Fairy Tale (2010)** : This song made a "massive hot 100 debut" at number two, fueled by impressive opening sales of 325,000 digital downloads. It was seen by many fans as a sequel to "Love Story" and was notable for being Swift's first single to chart better on the Hot 100 than on Hot Country Songs, peaking at only number 41 on the country chart. "Today was a fairy tale, which felt to many fans like a sequel to Love Story entered the chart all the way up at number two, fueled by opening sales of 325,000 digital downloads."
**Picture to Burn (2006)** : This song was a number three country hit from Swift's debut album. It contained a "now regrettable lyric" about outing a "faithless ex-boyfriend," indicating an early example of Swift's characteristic roman-à-clef songwriting style. "Her number three country hit, Picture to Burn, contained a now regrettable lyric about outing a faithless ex-boyfriend."
**Forever & Always (2009)** : This track, featured on the *Fearless platinum edition*, was Swift's direct musical "dispatch" of Joe Jonas, the lead singer of The Jonas Brothers. It showcased her willingness to openly address her relationships and breakups in her music. "Swift dispatched with him on her fearless platinum edition track forever and always."
**Dear John (2010)** : Included on the *Speak Now* album, this was a "heart-rending tale of an age inappropriate romance" that was "unsubtly" titled. It was widely understood to be about guitarist and songwriter John Mayer, who dated Swift when he was 31 and she was 19, and Mayer later wrote his own response song, "Paper Doll." "No one mistook who that song was about."
**Mean (2010)** : A single from the *Speak Now* album, this song was directly aimed at "bullies and her own critics," reflecting a theme of giving voice to those who feel belittled. The song's plain title indicated its direct message and resonated particularly with Swift's youngest fans. "Not mincing words, the song was simply called Mean."
**Runaway (2010)** : Performed by Kanye West at the 2010 MTV VMAs, this song was his "brash response to the incident" with Taylor Swift the year prior. Its "unapologetic chorus" included the lines "let's have a toast for the douchebags" and "let's have a toast for the assholes," leading critics to describe it as both "unrepentantly rude or defiantly resilient." "Unrepentantly rude or defiantly resilient. Critics said it was both."
**Mine (2010)** : This song served as the lead single from the *Speak Now* album and reached number two on the Hot Country Songs chart. It was part of a string of country hits generated by the album, demonstrating Swift's continued success within the country genre despite her growing crossover appeal. "Both mine, the lead single... reached number two on Hot Country Songs."
**Back to December (2010)** : This hit, a "Taylor Lautner Memento," reached number three on the Hot Country Songs chart. It was a song Swift wrote about Lautner a year after their amicable breakup, continuing her tradition of using her music to reflect on her past relationships. "Back to December, her Taylor Lautner Memento reached number three."
**Sparks Fly (2011)** : This track from the *Speak Now* album achieved significant success on the country charts, reaching number one. It was one of several country hits that the album continued to generate as late as the fall of 2011, reinforcing Swift's presence in the genre. "As late as the fall of 2011 and the winter of 2012, Speak Now was still generating country hits. Spark's fly reached number one."
**Ours (2011/2012)** : A track included on the Deluxe edition of *Speak Now*, this song also topped the country chart. Its success, even in late 2011 and early 2012, highlighted the album's longevity and Swift's continued ability to produce country hits. "And hours, a track from the album's Deluxe edition, also topped the country chart."
**Begin Again (2012)** : This song from the *Red* album demonstrated Swift's continued ability to write "sturdy acoustic style material" and reached number three on the country charts. It was produced by her long-time producer, Nathan Chapman, showcasing her roots amidst her evolving pop sound. "She was still writing sturdy acoustic style material, like the future number three country hit Begin Again."
**State of Grace (2012)** : This lead track from the *Red* album had an "arena rock" sound, reminiscent of U2. It was produced by Nathan Chapman, indicating the diverse sonic landscape Swift was exploring on the album even before bringing in pop producers. "Or by contrast, the album's lead track, State of Grace, sounded like arena rock in the style of YouTube."
**Red (2012) - Title Track Production** : The title track of the album *Red* initially sounded good with banjos and had country chord changes, but Taylor Swift and Nathan Chapman realized it needed an extra "punch" from a big Top 40 pop hitmaker. This insight led to the collaboration with Max Martin for other songs on the album, marking a pivotal moment in Swift's genre evolution. "It still needed something extra. It needed the kind of punch that was the specialty of a maker of big top 40 pop hits."
**I Knew You Were Trouble (2012)** : Co-written and produced by Max Martin and Shellback for the *Red* album, this was a "classic Swift Romana clef" and "kiss and tell song," widely rumored to be about One Direction singer Harry Styles. Sonically, it was a dramatic departure, incorporating elements from Kelly Clarkson, a "roller coaster chorus," and a "dubstep breakdown," yet it impressively held together. "Because I knew you were trouble when you walked in."
**22 (2012)** : Co-written and produced by Max Martin and Shellback for the *Red* album, this "thumping" and "eclectic" song was seen as a sequel to Swift's earlier hit "15." It celebrated a year Swift called her "favorite of her adulthood," featuring dance pop beats and swirling production layered over her guitar strumming and vocals. "Then the thumping 22, a kind of sequel to Swift's earlier hit 15, celebrating a year she called the favorite of her adulthood was similarly eclectic."
**We Are Never Ever Getting Back Together (2012) - First Pop #1** : This "standout" track from *Red*, co-written and produced by Max Martin and Shellback, was inspired by Swift's description of a past relationship as "break up, get back together, break up, get back together, just hug the worst." Max Martin encouraged conversational verses. Ann Powers called it "pop punk," and it became Swift's "first ever number one pop hit," debuting at #72 on the Hot 100 before rocketing to #1 in one week with 623,000 downloads, a record for a female artist. "Max Martin suggested they write about it and he encouraged Taylor to make the versus conversational as much talking as singing."
**We Are Never Ever Getting Back Together (2012) - Country Radio Reception** : Despite its pop success, this song was given a country radio remix by Big Machine, replacing synthesizers and drum machines with mandolin, steel guitar, and snare drums, and debuted at #13 on Hot Country Songs. However, it quickly fell, becoming Swift's first officially promoted single not to reach the country airplay top 10. Later, a Billboard chart rule change artificially pushed it to #1 on the revamped Hot Country Songs chart for 10 weeks despite limited radio play. "It replaced Max Martin and Shellback's synthesizers and drum machines with Mandolin, Steel Guitar and snare drums."
**Highway Don't Care (2013)** : A duet with veteran country superstar Tim McGraw, this song was from McGraw's new album and was acclaimed as one of the year's best country singles. It reached number four on Billboard's Hot Country Songs chart and, significantly, number one on the Country Airplay chart. This marked Swift's last country airplay number one song, and after 2013, no solo Taylor Swift song would appear in the country top 10 for the rest of the decade. "It would be Swift's last country airplay number one song and after 2013, no solo Taylor Swift song would appear in the country top 10 for the rest of the decade."
**Shake It Off (2014)** : This song was one of three Hot 100 number one hits from the *1989* album, marking Swift's full transition into documented pop music.
**Blank Space (2014)** : A "witty follow-up single" from the *1989* album, this song was a "satire of her string of well-chronicled romances" and became one of three Hot 100 number one hits from the album. The lyrics self-referentially play on her public image. "The witty follow-up single, a satire of her string of well-cronricled romances, blank space."
**Bad Blood (2015)** : This song was a "beef song against Katy Perry" and became one of three Hot 100 number one hits from the *1989* album. Its release further fueled the well-publicized feud between the two pop stars. "And her beef song against Katy Perry, Bad Blood."
**Look What You Made Me Do (2017)** : This chart-topping song from the *Reputation* album directly referenced Kanye West, signifying a new round of hostilities between Swift and her "old nemesis." Its release was a significant moment in their public feud. "And so she, of course, referenced Kanye in her 2017 chart topper, look what you made me do."
**Ready For It (2017)** : Featured on the *Reputation* album, this song showcased a new stylistic direction for Swift, with her "kind of started rapping herself" on the track. This marked a departure into a more hip-hop influenced sound for her. "Taylor kind of started wrapping herself on hits like ready for it."
**Delicate (2017)** : This "kinetic hit" from the *Reputation* album saw Taylor Swift delving "deep into digital pop." It further demonstrated her willingness to experiment with electronic sounds and production techniques in her music. "And she went deep into digital pop on kinetic hits like Delica."
**You Need To Calm Down (2019)** : This "BOP" from Swift explicitly showcased her support for the LGBTQ+ community. It represented her public stance on political issues, moving past her previous reluctance to be overtly political. "And she sang about it on her 2019 BOP you need to calm down."
**New Year's Day (2017)** : This piano ballad served as the closing track on Swift's otherwise "hip-hop fueled 2017 album Reputation." Its quieter, more contemplative style allowed it to "crack the country top 40," demonstrating that country stations and audiences still embraced certain aspects of her music. "New Year's Day, a piano ballad that closed her otherwise hip-hop fueled 2017 album Reputation, cracked the country top 40."
**Better Man (2016)** : This song was penned by Taylor Swift for the country group Little Big Town. It was an example of Swift's continued songwriting contributions to other country acts even as her own career pivoted largely to pop. "As a songwriter, Swift also penned songs for other country acts, including Better Man for the group Little Big Town."
**Babe (2018)** : Written by Taylor Swift for the country duo Sugarland, this song featured Swift's own vocals, bringing her momentarily back to the country top 10 in 2018. It highlighted her continued occasional presence in the country music scene, often through collaborations. "And Babe for Country Duo Sugarland. The Sugarland hit featured vocals from Taylor herself, bringing her momentarily back to the country top 10 in 2018."
**Soon You'll Get Better (2019)** : This "tender ballad" was written and performed by Taylor Swift in collaboration with The Chicks. Its release brought both Swift and the trio "briefly back to the country charts," and was particularly significant for The Chicks as it marked their first country hit in more than a dozen years. "And in 2019, a tender ballad Swift wrote and performed with the chicks, soon you'll get better."
**Betty (2020)** : From her *Folklore* album, this song reached the country radio top 40 and the Hot Country Charts top 10. It was a testament to Swift's "gradual, quiet return to country music," achieving country chart success without explicitly trying to sound country, reflecting the rustic sounds of Americana and Indie Pop. "From Folklore, the song Betty reached the country radio top 40 and the hot country charts top 10."
**No Body, No Crime (2020)** : Featured on the *Evermore* album, this collaboration with the L.A. Sister Trio Haim also made the Hot Country top 10. It was still active on the country airplay chart just weeks before the podcast episode, signifying Swift's continued, organic presence in the country genre through her new soundscapes. "And from Evermore, the song No Body, No Crime, Swift's collaboration with the L.A. Sister Trio Hyme also made the hot country top 10."
**15 (2008) - Recreated in Early 30s** : Originally from *Fearless*, this song was recreated by Swift in her early 30s as part of *Fearless, Taylor's Version*. The song's lyrics reflect on the "wisdom that comes with age," with Swift singing about wishing she could tell her younger self what she knows now. "In the song's lyrics, Taylor talks about the wisdom that comes with age. Quote, when all you wanted was to be wanted, wish you could go back and tell yourself what you know now."

## songs
