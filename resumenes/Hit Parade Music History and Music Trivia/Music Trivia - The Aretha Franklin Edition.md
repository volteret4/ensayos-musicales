## songs
**Bridge Over Troubled Water - Paul Simon Cover with Billy Preston on Keys** : Aretha Franklin's hit cover of Paul Simon's song features keyboards played by the famous sideman Billy Preston. It was one of several songs by rock writers that Franklin covered and turned into a hit. The song was chosen as a tribute after her passing because Emily Lordi's New Yorker tribute described Franklin as a bridge builder. "On Ritha Franklin's hit cover of Bridge Over Troubled Water, the keyboards are played by Billy Preston."
**I Say a Little Prayer - Definitive Version** : The song was also recorded by other artists, including Dionne Warwick, but Franklin's version, with its "little vocal fire works", became the best-known one. "Aretha's became the iconic version for a reason."
**Respect (1967) - First Hot 100 Number One** : The song was written and first recorded by Otis Redding, but few people now think of it as his song. Redding himself said Franklin had taken it from him, and he meant it as praise, not anger. The track is known for its breakdown in the middle. It was her first number one on Billboard's Hot 100, and she didn't have another until 1987. "that girl stole my song. And he meant it admiringly."
**I Knew You Were Waiting (For Me) (1987) - Duet with George Michael** : This one-off pairing with George Michael was Franklin's only other pop number one, 20 years after "Respect". It reached number one on the Hot 100 and number five on the R&B chart. It was George Michael's first solo number one in America, not counting "Careless Whisper", which was billed there as a Wham! single. It was also Franklin's first number one since "Respect" in 1967. "it was George Michael's first solo number one hit, and Eurythus first number one since respect in 1967."
**(You Make Me Feel Like) A Natural Woman - Kennedy Center Honors Tribute to Carole King** : About three or four years before her death, Franklin performed the song at the Kennedy Center Honors in tribute to Carole King. "when she honored Carol King by doing that amazing performance of making feel like a natural woman."
**Eleanor Rigby - Paul McCartney Cover** : In the early 70s Franklin recorded a soulful cover of Paul McCartney's Beatles song. It was a hit on both the pop and R&B charts. "The other three songs were all covered by Franklin, and all were pop and R&B hits."
**Border Song - Elton John Cover** : One of Franklin's early-70s covers of songs by white rock songwriters. It was a hit on both the pop and R&B charts. "she recorded celebrated, soulful covers of songs by a string of white men from the world of rock."
**The Weight - Robbie Robertson / The Band Cover** : Franklin covered this song by Robbie Robertson of The Band in the early 70s. It was a hit on both the pop and R&B charts. "The other three songs were all covered by Franklin, and all were pop and R&B hits."

## curiosities
**Death of the Queen of Soul - August 2018** : For several days before her death the public knew she was ailing, so the news was less of a shock than some other recent losses in American music. She died on the same day Madonna, the Queen of Pop, turned 60. "she was ailing and she was not long for this world."
**Never a Number One Album in America** : Franklin had a couple of number two albums but never an American number one album. A posthumous number one in August 2018 would have been her first, much like David Bowie, who only reached number one when he died. Chart performance after her death would depend on streaming, which now counts toward the charts, and on what current artists, such as Drake, released that week. "She's had a couple of number twos, but she's never had a number one, which is kind of mind blowing."
**Only Two Hot 100 Number Ones** : Over her whole career Franklin topped Billboard's Hot 100 only twice: "Respect" in 1967 and "I Knew You Were Waiting (For Me)" with George Michael in 1987. "Aretha Franklin scored only two number one hits on Billboard's Hot 100 in her career."
**A Career Spanning Decades** : Her career covered so many decades that it ran from her 60s hits to her performances at Barack Obama's inauguration and the Kennedy Center Honors. "her career was so long-lasting and covered so many decades"
**Emily Lordi's New Yorker Tribute - The Bridge Builder** : Emily Lordi wrote a New Yorker tribute describing Franklin as a bridge builder. Her death also prompted pieces by Slate writers Jack Hamilton, Carl Wilson and Dan Kois. "Emily wrote about how Aretha was a bridge builder."
**Never Covered a Barry Gibb Song** : Franklin covered many rock writers' songs in the early 70s, but she never had a hit with a Barry Gibb composition. The Bee Gees' "How Can You Mend a Broken Heart" was famously covered by Al Green instead. "The Queen of Soul never scored a hit with a Barry Gibbs song."

# artist - Michael Jackson

## albums
**Number Ones - Bestseller Barred from the Billboard 200** : When Jackson died suddenly in the summer of 2009, his compilation "Number Ones" was selling better than any album in America, new or old. Billboard's rules then kept older "catalog" albums off the main Billboard 200, so it couldn't appear there at all. For about six weeks it sat at number one on Billboard's separate catalog album chart while outselling everything on the regular chart. Later that year Billboard changed the rule. "it was not allowed to appear on the album chart at all."

## curiosities
**The Death That Changed Billboard's Album Rules** : After the interest in Jackson's death died down in 2009, Billboard allowed any album to chart on the Billboard 200. That rule stayed in place for the following decade and opened the main chart to posthumous sales surges for Whitney Houston, Tom Petty and Prince. "Billboard finally changed the rule and basically made a new rule that any album could chart on the main flagship Billboard 200 album chart."

# artist - David Bowie

## albums
**Blackstar - Final Studio Album and Only US Number One** : Bowie's final studio album came out within about 24 to 48 hours of his death. Public mourning made it his only American number one album. Unlike the Prince compilation, it would have charted under either set of Billboard rules because it was a new release. "the collective mourning for Bowie led to Black Star being bizarrely enough, David Bowie's only number one album ever in America."

## curiosities
**No Number One Album Until His Death** : Bowie never had an American number one album during his lifetime, a situation compared to Aretha Franklin's. "Bowie somehow right up to his death and never had a number one album."

**The Warrior (1984) - Only Top 10 Hit** : Credited to "Scandal featuring Patty Smyth", this was the band's one and only top 10 hit, reaching number seven in the summer of 1984. There was reportedly friction between Smyth and the rest of the band, and everyone expected her to go solo. The "featuring" credit probably eased the tension in the band and certainly prepared the market for her solo career. "that hit was called The Warrior, scandal featuring Patty Smith, and it reached number seven in the summer of 1984."

## curiosities
**Breakup by the Late 1980s** : Scandal had broken up by the late 1980s, and Patty Smyth went solo as expected. "by the late 1980s Patty Smith had a solo career, scandal had broken up."

# curiosity

**Billboard's Catalog Album Rule (pre-2009)** : Before late 2009, Billboard kept older catalog albums that had dropped off the chart off the main Billboard 200. They could appear only on a separate catalog album chart. Michael Jackson's death exposed the problem, and Billboard changed the rule. "Billboard had a rule that albums that were old albums, catalog albums as they called them."
**Posthumous Chart Surges Under the New Rules** : After the rule change, albums by Whitney Houston, Tom Petty and Prince could all appear on the main chart after their deaths, and some reached number one, most notably Prince's. "from Whitney Houston to Tom Petty to Prince, all of these albums are allowed on the main chart."
**Streaming Now Counts Toward the Charts** : Since Whitney Houston's death earlier in the decade, streaming has started counting toward the charts. Streams can add up to lift an album, so a posthumous chart run depends on which album people stream and what else is charting that week. "streaming now counts for the charts."
**"Featuring" as a Contractual Device** : Before the modern featured-artist era, labels sometimes used "featuring" to single out a member already in a group, usually a singer being prepared for a solo career, not a special guest. Examples are "Wham! featuring George Michael", Diana Ross and the Supremes, and "Scandal featuring Patty Smyth". "They were some sort of weird contractual arrangement whereby the record label was using the word featuring to highlight an artist who's already in the group but is not actually a special guest."
**Pulling a Singer Out of the Group's Name** : Throughout pop history, a singer still in a group has been pulled out into the billing, mostly to prepare that singer for a solo career. "an artist, a singer usually, who is still a part of the group is pulled out of the group in the artist's name, mostly because they're preparing the artist for a solo career."
**"Shipped Gold, Returned Platinum" - Music Business Slang** : In the late-70s music business, a pre-sold record "shipped gold" or "shipped platinum", meaning it went out to stores with half a million or a million copies already ordered. The joke turned this around for the failed Sgt. Pepper soundtrack. "it already shipped out to stores with like half a million or a million copies."
