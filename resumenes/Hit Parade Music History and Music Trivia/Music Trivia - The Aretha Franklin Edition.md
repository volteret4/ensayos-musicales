# curiosity

**Billboard Chart Rules for Catalog Albums (Post-2009)** : Prior to a rule change in late 2009, Billboard had a policy preventing "catalog albums" (old albums that had fallen off the chart) from reappearing on the main Billboard 200 album chart, even if they were selling exceptionally well. This rule caused controversy when Michael Jackson's "Number 1s" outsold all other albums after his death but was confined to the separate catalog album chart. "Billboard had a rule that albums that were old albums, catalog albums as they called them... weren't allowed to reappear on the main Billboard 200 album chart."
**Billboard Rule Change (Late 2009)** : Following controversy surrounding Michael Jackson's posthumous chart performance, Billboard changed its rules in late 2009. The new rule permitted any album, regardless of age, to chart on the main flagship Billboard 200 album chart. This change subsequently benefited artists like Prince and Whitney Houston, whose albums charted after their deaths. "Billboard finally changed the rule and basically made a new rule that any album could chart on the main flagship Billboard 200 album chart."
**Impact of Streaming on Charts** : Contemporary chart mechanics differ from earlier decades, with streaming now counting toward an album's chart position. This evolution means that the aggregation of streams from various albums can significantly boost an album's overall chart performance, especially relevant for artists' music after their passing. "The charts work differently now... in that streaming now counts for the charts."
**Evolution of "Featuring" Credit** : Historically, the "featuring" credit has evolved beyond signifying a special guest. It was sometimes used in contractual arrangements or to highlight a member already within a group, often to prepare that artist for a solo career, as seen with Wham! featuring George Michael, and Scandal featuring Patty Smyth. "They were some sort of weird contractual arrangement whereby the record label was using the word featuring to highlight an artist who's already in the group but is not actually a special guest."
**Music Business Jargon - "Shipped Gold, Returned Platinum"** : In the 1970s music business, the phrase "shipped gold, returned platinum" was a sarcastic joke used to describe a record's massive commercial failure. It meant that a huge number of copies (e.g., half a million or a million) were initially shipped to stores, but most were returned unsold, indicating a spectacular flop, such as the "Sgt. Pepper's Lonely Hearts Club Band" soundtrack. "The joke in 1978 was the Sargent Pepper soundtrack, shipped gold and returned platinum."
