# artist - Television

## members
Tom Verlaine
Richard Lloyd

## genres
Punk Rock

## concerts

## curiosities
**First Band to Open CBGB to Rock Acts - March 1974** : Television's two frontmen, Tom Verlaine and Richard Lloyd, were the first to persuade CBGB owner Hilly Kristal to let them and similar bands play his country, bluegrass and blues club in the Bowery. Bands played for whatever money was collected at the door, and the bar kept all alcohol sales. Starting around March 1974, this turned the bar into the centre of a scene, so Television can be credited with starting the CBGB scene. "The two guys out front in this group were the first to persuade owner Hilly Crystal to let them and their ilk play."
**The Art Side of New York Punk** : Not all New York punk was loud and fast. Compared with the Ramones, a Television set at CBGB was a more artistic experience. "Maybe you'd catch a set by television, which of course was a lot more artsy."
**Credited With Starting the CBGB Scene** : Television is named as the New York group that started the scene at CBGB, in the Bowery, which the transcript describes as a dirty, crime-ridden part of the city. A Television recording from 1977 is presented as part of the story. "Television, the New York group that can be credited with getting some kind of scene started at CBGB down in the bowery, that awful, dirty crime-ridden part of New York, beginning in about March 1974."

# artist - Ramones

## concerts

## albums
**Ramones (1976) - Debut Album** : The Ramones' 1976 debut album came out of the New York CBGB scene. Its records later crossed the Atlantic to the UK and spread to Los Angeles, where they helped punk take hold quickly. "The Ramones from their 1976 debut album."

## curiosities
**First CBGB Show - August 1974** : When the Ramones first played CBGB in August 1974 they were "absolutely terrible," and nobody knew whether they were serious. Audiences were puzzled by the matching haircuts, the leather jackets, the shared last name, and the speed of their sets. "And how can you possibly play 15 songs in 20 minutes? Is this some kind of a joke?"
**Mistaken for Minimalist Art** : The Ramones were serious, but their goofiness was so unusual that CBGB's arty crowd took them as a grand minimalist artistic statement. The band never thought of themselves that way, and it made no difference. Today they are remembered as a straight-ahead buzzsaw punk band, which the transcript says is not entirely correct. "But because their goofiness was so unusual, they were accepted by the artsy crowd as some kind of grand artistic statement with a minimalist bent."
**Influence on LA Punk** : Once the Ramones and other New York bands started releasing records, Los Angeles adopted punk very quickly. "As soon as the remones and some of the other New York bands started releasing records, and as soon as a few sex pistol singles were spread around by the right people, LA took to punk very quickly."

## curiosities
**Bill Grundy's Today Show - December 1, 1976** : Queen were booked for Bill Grundy's live ITV teatime talk show Today but cancelled at the last second. People recall it was because a band member, possibly Freddie Mercury, needed a dentist for a toothache. Because the show was live, someone in Grundy's office called Malcolm McLaren, the Sex Pistols' manager, whose band had released their debut single a week earlier. McLaren reached the band at their rehearsal space and told them a limo was coming. "Someone in Bill Grundy's office got a hold of Malcolm McLaren, the manager of a new group called the Sex Pistols, who had just released their debut single a week earlier."
**The Drunken Limo Ride** : The Pistols, always keen to have a laugh, called friends to come along. They all piled into the limo with many bottles of wine, most of which were gone by the time they reached the studio. The band and their hangers-on were all quite drunk when they were escorted onto the set. "The pistols, always keen to have a laugh, called some of their friends, and they all piled into the limo with many, many, many bottles of wine, most of which were gone by the time they reached the studio."
**No Tape Delay - Three Minutes That Ended a Career** : Grundy was also known to drink before and during shows and did not want the band on his programme, so there was tension from the start. Today had no tape delay, so nothing could be censored before it reached millions of homes. The segment began at 6:15pm on Wednesday, December 1, 1976, and the band swore repeatedly on air. "By 6.18pm, Bill Grundy's TV career was over and everyone was talking about those filthy young men on the telly."
**The £40,000 Record Company Question** : On air, Grundy challenged the band about the £40,000 they had reportedly received from a record company. He suggested it contradicted an anti-materialistic view of life, which provoked the profanity that followed. "I was told that that group had received 40,000 pounds for a record company. Doesn't that seem to be slightly opposed to the anti-materialistic view of life?"
**National Outrage and the Birth of Mass UK Punk - December 2, 1976** : Families across Britain were having supper with the TV on when the swearing aired. The shock and outrage "hit the country like a nuclear bomb," and the next day every newspaper ran headlines about the scourge of punk rock. Alienated young people took the opposite lesson, and a scene of a few dozen people suddenly saw a massive influx of followers nationwide. "While all the respectable people tried to recover from this horror, of all those alienated young people thought, oh, this is all right. Let's have more of this then."
**Missed Saturday Night Live - December 1978** : The Sex Pistols were booked for the new NBC show Saturday Night Live in December 1978. Visa problems stopped them, and Elvis Costello replaced them at the last second. "In December 1978, the Sex Pistols were booked to play this new show on NBC called Saturday Night Live. However, they ran into visa problems and were replaced at the last second on the show by this geeky guy from England named Elvis Costello."
**Singles Spread to Los Angeles** : A few Sex Pistols singles, passed around by the right people, helped LA adopt punk quickly. "as soon as a few sex pistol singles were spread around by the right people, LA took to punk very quickly."

# genre - Punk Rock

## curiosities
**Dismissed as Noise** : While it was happening in the mid-1970s, punk was treated as a niche aberration for "weirdos and misfits and outliers," and purists expected it to disappear. It didn't, and it became a way of thinking that shaped music, film, visual art, literature, dance and politics. "It's just noise, said the rock purists, ignore it, it'll just go away. But it didn't."
**The Punk Ethos in Western Culture** : Punk grew into a way of thinking, acting, creating and presenting that changed much of Western thought. "The punk aesthetic, that screw you, I'm going to do it anyway, ethos, can be found virtually everywhere in society today."
**Socio-Political Backdrop of 1974** : The ideals of the 1960s had collapsed. Nixon resigned in disgrace, the Vietnam War was a disaster, the oil crisis had crippled economies, the Middle East repeatedly went to war, and the US and Soviet Union confronted each other in the Cold War. "This was a very turbulent time in Western society."
**New York's Decay in the Mid-70s** : New York was full of crime, corruption, drugs and racial tension, and was close to bankruptcy. When the city asked the federal government for help, it was refused. "When the city asked the federal government for help, they were told, nope, it's your problem. You fix it."
**Cheap Bowery Lofts Drew Artists** : Artists who couldn't afford good neighbourhoods moved to areas like the Bowery. A loft could house many people for about $150 a month, sometimes without a toilet, shower or winter heat. The cheap rents drew musicians, poets, writers, painters and sculptors into one area, where they mixed and collaborated. "You could get a lot of space for a lot of people for maybe $150 a month."
**New York Punk as Art Statement vs UK Punk as Politics** : New York punk grew from an arty scene that was left alone, while UK punk was driven by politics and the class system. "Well punk was more of an art statement in New York. It was fueled by politics and the class system in the UK."
**Britain in 1975 - Economic Collapse** : Some bomb-damaged buildings from the war were still standing. The oil crisis of 1973 had derailed the welfare state, manufacturing was declining, three-day work weeks were introduced, and unions from miners to grave diggers went on strike. The Bank of England spent its foreign reserves defending the pound and needed an IMF bailout. Scheduled rolling blackouts meant people had to cook ahead before the power was cut. "There was so much pressure on the pound that the Bank of England spent all of its foreign reserves trying to prop up the currency and needed a Greek-style bailout from the International Monetary Fund."
**Class System and Monarchy** : Advancement in Britain depended on birth rather than ability. During the Queen's Christmas message, activity in homes stopped, and some older people stood and saluted the TV until she finished. Unemployment soared, and millions resigned themselves to living on the dole. "Some older people would stand in front of the TV and salute, remaining that way until her majesty had finished speaking."
**A UK Scene of Only Dozens** : By the end of 1975, word of the New York scene had reached Britain. The young people who picked up on it numbered only in the dozens and were largely ignored until the Bill Grundy broadcast. "Now we're actually literally talking about groups of young people who numbered in the dozens. That's it. That's how small this scene was."
**Giving Power to the Powerless** : British punk went beyond music. It attacked the upper classes and the monarchy, set out to shock older generations, challenged established institutions and aimed to destroy the status quo. "In short, it gave a sense of power to the powerless."
**Manchester Heatwave and Crumbling Sewers** : During the hot summers that followed, Manchester's 100-year-old sewer system began to crumble, leaving much of the city with a lingering stench. "In Manchester, the 100-year-old sewer system began to crumble so much of the city had to deal with an awful lingering smell during those terrible heat waves."
**Winter of Discontent 1978-79** : The post-war consensus between left and right collapsed, and the political right blamed the welfare state for Britain's economic problems. The winter of 1978-79 brought nationwide strikes and the coldest weather in 15 years, with blizzards during blackouts and coal shortages. This turned punk into a genuine counterculture. "Not only was the country paralyzed by strikes, but it was the coldest winter in 15 years."
**Margaret Thatcher and Political British Music** : The Winter of Discontent ended the left's hold on power, and Margaret Thatcher became Prime Minister on May 4, 1979. Even as punk burned out, her tough policies kept much of British music political and critical of her for years. "Lady Thatcher's tough tactics made sure that a huge swath of British music remained very political and very critical of her for years to come."
**Toronto - Third Leg of the Triangle** : Canada was struggling in the mid-to-late 70s. The Parti Québécois won election in Quebec in 1976 seeking separation, Pierre Trudeau was seen as arrogant and unable to fix the economy, national debt exploded and interest rates rose. Yonge Street was seedy. The rape and murder of 12-year-old Portuguese immigrant Emanuel Jaques above a massage parlour shocked a city that saw itself as moral and where it was hard to get a drink on a Sunday. Toronto's small scene became part of a triangle with New York and London, with constant band traffic to New York, and UK bands touring North America had to play Toronto. "And Toronto ended up forming the third leg of a triangle that extended through both New York and London."
**Toronto Punk Character** : Toronto punk was less arty than New York's and less political or class-conscious than the UK's, but it could be very angry. There were other Canadian scenes in Hamilton, London, Winnipeg and Vancouver, but none as big as Toronto's. "But man, it could still be really, really angry."
**Los Angeles Punk** : LA had always attracted the alienated and the outcast, so punk landed there naturally. The recurring themes were alienation, hopelessness and a desire to "kick the crap out of the status quo" and the established music industry. LA also had its own version of CBGB, The Masque. "LA had always been a place for the alienated the outcast and the weird, so it was a natural place for punk to land."
**San Francisco - Most Eclectic Punk** : San Francisco punk combined the arty and angry sides of punk with the city's hippie drug culture and left-wing politics. Its experiments helped lay the groundwork for later rock, new wave and techno-pop. First-wave bands included the Avengers, the Nuns and the Dead Kennedys. "In fact, you can make an argument that San Francisco punk was the most eclectic of all the punk that we saw back then."
**Mid-70s Big Bang of Alternative Music** : Punk is presented as alternative music's "big bang" in the mid-1970s, the source of today's alt rock. "As far as alternative music is concerned, its big bang moment was in the mid 1970s, and we're still feeling the effects today."

# concert - CBGB

## curiosities
**From Bluegrass Bar to Punk Hub** : CBGB was a run-down country, bluegrass and blues club in the Bowery owned by Hilly Kristal. It began letting rock bands play for the door money while the bar kept alcohol sales. From about March 1974 it became a scene and a place where disaffected, alienated artists could hang out. "CBGB slowly became known as a place where disaffected and alienated artists of all kinds could hang out."
**The Room and the Originals** : The room was long and narrow, with a sparse stage and no dressing rooms. Radio offered little worth covering, and the left-wing, arty, anti-establishment crowd encouraged experimentation, so most acts played original material. "Because there wasn't a lot on the radio that was worth covering, most of the acts played original material."

# curiosity

**Perfect Storm Moments in Rock** : Major shifts in rock come when culture, politics, demographics, economics and technology collide. Examples given are Elvis in the 1950s, the Beatles on Ed Sullivan in 1964, the music video in the early 1980s, the first hearing of "Smells Like Teen Spirit," hip-hop, and mid-70s punk. "A bunch of things involving culture and politics, demographics and economics and science and technology all collide and mix in just the right way for something totally new and unexpected to be created."
**Music Video Transforms the Industry** : The arrival of the music video in the early 1980s transformed the music industry. "You might remember the appearance of the music video in the early 1980s, which transformed the industry."
**Smells Like Teen Spirit - First Listen** : Hearing "Smells Like Teen Spirit" for the first time is cited as a moment that signalled a change in music. "Or the time you heard smells like teen spirit for the first time, and immediately you knew that something whatever was coming next would be very, very different."
**Top AM Radio Songs of 1974** : The top songs of 1974 were Barbra Streisand's "The Way We Were," John Denver's "Sunshine on My Shoulders," and "The Entertainer," a piano instrumental from the film The Sting. Mainstream pop was out of touch with the state of the world. "The top songs in 1974 were The Way We Were from Barbara Streisand, Sunshine on My Shoulder from John Denver, and The Entertainer, an instrumental piano diddy from the movie The Sting."
**Bill Grundy - The Oprah of His Day** : In December 1976 the UK had only three TV channels (BBC One, BBC Two and ITV), so TV personalities were rare and very famous. Bill Grundy hosted the teatime talk show Today on ITV and had a long career on national TV. "Now Bill was the real thing, kind of like the Oprah of his day."
